CPC Certification Requirements (2026)

The CPC (Certified Professional Coder) from AAPC is the most-requested credential for physician-office and outpatient coding jobs. Here is exactly what it takes.

Eligibility

  • No formal degree required — but AAPC strongly recommends training in medical terminology, anatomy, and coding.
  • You must be an AAPC member to sit for the exam (membership bundled with most exam packages).
  • Bring current-year code books (CPT, ICD-10-CM, HCPCS) — the exam is open-book.

Exam format

DetailCPC exam
Questions100 multiple choice
Time4 hours
Passing score70%
FormatOnline proctored or in-person
AttemptsTwo attempts per exam purchase

Questions are case-based: you read an operative report or office note and select the correct code(s), including modifiers and sequencing.

12-week study plan

  1. Weeks 1–3: Terminology + anatomy review. Drill root words daily.
  2. Weeks 4–7: CPT sections in order — E/M, anesthesia, surgery, radiology, pathology/lab, medicine. Tab your book.
  3. Weeks 8–9: ICD-10-CM guidelines — sequencing, Z-codes, 7th characters.
  4. Weeks 10–11: Timed practice exams. Target: finish with 30 minutes to spare.
  5. Week 12: Review weak areas, rest the day before.

After you pass: CPC-A and CEUs

  • New certificants hold CPC-A (apprentice) until they document 2 years of experience or 800 verified hours.
  • Maintain the credential with 36 CEUs every 2 years plus AAPC membership.
  • Specialty credentials (e.g. CPC-P, CEMC) can raise pay — coders with 2+ credentials earn notably more.

Exam-day tips

  • Answer surgery/radiology cases first — they are worth the most points per minute.
  • Eliminate two wrong answers before guessing; there is no penalty for wrong answers.
  • Use the process of elimination on E/M questions — time and MDM level usually decide it.
