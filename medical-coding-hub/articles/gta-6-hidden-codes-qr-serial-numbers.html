GTA 6 Hidden Codes: QR, Serial Numbers and a Phone

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and Rockstar hid codes all over its trailers — not cheat codes, but numbers and symbols that open something, mean something, or point somewhere. Fans have spent three years decoding them frame by frame, and the best finds are the ones you can verify yourself in the game's own material. This guide collects the confirmed codes, separates them from the rumours, and tells you exactly where each one appears.

The most famous code is a QR code. At 17:31 of the 26-minute Extended Look, a poster outside the Jack of Hearts strip club advertises "Make It Rain Mondays" with a Peekhole logo — and the poster carries a working QR code. Scan it and it opens Rockstar's GTA 6 website. Fans read Peekhole as a parody of a subscription site, but Rockstar has not said so; what is certain is that the code is real, it scans even from a compressed video frame, and it leads somewhere Rockstar owns. It is the single most verifiable hidden code in the game, and it has been there since the footage first aired.

The revolvers and their serial numbers

The second code is stamped on steel. The Hawk and Little Morgan revolvers — the paired bonus guns from the Ultimate Edition, engraved J. Duval and L. Caminos — carry serial numbers 311006 and 291002. Both are dates. Vice City Stories shipped on October 31, 2006, and the original Vice City shipped on October 29, 2002. Rockstar stamped its own release calendar onto the guns and said nothing about it anywhere. Across two trailers, 155 images and the cover art, fifteen months of digging produced 22 finds of that kind, each with a named finder, and the serial numbers are the cleanest of them: a date, a game, and a perfect match, hiding in a place most players never look.

A phone number in plain sight

The third code is a phone number. In a getaway frame from Rockstar's own footage, a sign carries the number 305-555-PAWN — the 305 area code is Miami's, and the word is a pawn shop's name spelled out on a keypad. It joins a longer list of signs drawn from real Miami companies and venues: a beach concession contractor, two clubs, an Art Deco architect, a mud park in Charlotte County, a fuel chain and a phone code. These are the kind of details that reward pausing the trailer. A rule for reading them: appearing in a frame does not confirm a game mechanic. Rockstar shows what it wants to show, not a feature list, and a sign is a sign — not a promise that you can call the number in-game.

What is rumour, and what is confirmed

Not every "code" survives checking, and the honest guides say so. The Dexter-style apartment — a waterfront building in the Extended Look that fans think mirrors Dexter Morgan's home from the TV series — is a rumour: Dexerto reported the theory in August 2026, and Rockstar has not confirmed it is an intentional reference. The Tommy Vercetti tattoo — a lizard-style wall tattoo behind Jason in the second trailer that a Reddit user said matches the pattern Tommy wore in Vice City — is also unconfirmed. Valentina, by contrast, is confirmed to reappear: she was introduced in 2025 in GTA Online's Money Fronts update, and she shows up in the Extended Look hiring Jason and Lucia as private security for Andres de Leon. The trophy list hides flavour text too — "The Truth Is Out There" reads "What the hell just happened?" — but what unlocks each trophy is unconfirmed. The line is simple: if it scans, if it is stamped on a gun, or if it appears in the game's own material, it counts. If a fan spotted a resemblance, it is a theory until Rockstar or the game says otherwise.

More finds from the same hunt

The QR code and the serial numbers are the headline finds, but the same hunt turned up more. Lucia's shirt in Trailer 2 reads "I WENT DOWN ON A LEVIATHAN", a line fans have argued about ever since. A Pisswasser NEIN banner with bottles hangs behind Lucia in one scene — an alcohol-free version of a brand whose whole joke was its strength. The banknotes in a cash close-up carry Red Dead presidents on them, a cross-game nod from one Rockstar world to another. And seven signs are drawn from specific companies and venues: the Miami Beach concession contractor, two clubs, an Art Deco architect, a mud park in Charlotte County, a fuel chain and a phone code. Across two trailers, 155 images and the cover art, fifteen months of digging produced 22 finds of that kind, each with a named finder, and the best guides label every one with its status — confirmed, reported by an outlet, or a fan reading Rockstar has not confirmed.

That is why these codes matter. They are Rockstar's way of rewarding the players who look closely — a QR code that opens a website, serial numbers that spell out two release dates, a phone number that roots the fiction in a real city. None of them changes how the game plays. All of them change how it feels to watch it. And with the game now out, the hunt has moved from the trailers into Leonida itself, where the next codes are waiting in shop signs, licence plates and phone screens — if you are willing to pause and read them.
