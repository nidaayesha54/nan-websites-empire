Frequently Asked Questions

What is medical coding?
Medical coding translates healthcare services, diagnoses, and procedures into standardized codes (CPT, ICD-10, HCPCS) so providers get paid correctly and data stays consistent.
What is the difference between CPT and ICD-10 codes?
CPT codes describe what was done (the procedure or service). ICD-10 codes describe why it was done (the diagnosis). Claims need both to be paid.
How much do medical coders make?
In the US, certified medical coders earn roughly $45,000–$75,000 per year depending on certification, specialty, and state, with experienced specialty coders earning more. See our salary-by-state guide.
Is medical coding hard to learn?
It takes study and practice — most students prepare 4–6 months for the CPC exam. Anatomy, medical terminology, and coding guidelines are the core skills.
