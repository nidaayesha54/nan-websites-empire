Is GTA 6 Worth $80? The Honest Value Check

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 costs $79.99 for the Standard Edition and $99.99 for the Ultimate Edition — and for a lot of players, that is real money. The game launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, thirteen years after GTA 5, and the reviews are the best in the history of the medium: a 99 on Metacritic, 10 out of 10 from IGN, GameSpot and Game Informer, and no review below 90. But a perfect score is not the same as value for money, and eighty dollars is eighty dollars. This guide does the honest value check: the price, the hours, the score, the maths, and — most importantly — who should buy it now and who should wait.

The Price, Plainly

Standard is $79.99 and it is the complete game: the full Jason and Lucia story, the whole map of Leonida, all 91 main missions, and every system in the game. Nothing in the story, the map or the missions is held back for the more expensive version. Ultimate is $99.99 and adds 16 story-mode extras — cars, a boat, weapons, outfits and five shops — threaded through the story, plus one exclusive mission. Neither edition lets you play early, and both get the same pre-order bonuses if bought before November 20: the Vintage Vice City Pack and one free month of GTA+.

The $20 question is simple: if the extras matter to you, Ultimate is twenty dollars for sixteen items — and Standard owners can buy the same upgrade later for $20, so you can start with Standard and add the rest later for the same total. The game is $79.99, and everything else is optional.

The Hours: How Long Is It Really?

Rockstar has not published an official story length, so the numbers come from the people who played it. Screen Rant's reviewer finished the main story in about 50 hours and 42 minutes while skipping most side activities. Polygon finished in about 53 hours and puts the main story at 70 to 80 hours for most players. IGN's two reviewers logged about 70 and 115 hours with side content, Push Square put it at about 60 hours, and Rockstar North co-head Rob Nelson told The New York Times his own playthrough took around 80 hours.

The honest range is this: about 50 to 60 hours for a story-focused run, 70 to 80 hours for most players who do some side content, and 150 to 200-plus hours for full completion. The story is 91 main missions, and the world around it — fishing, pool, side jobs, radio, the phone and social media layer, the wanted system, the six regions — is the part that turns a 50-hour game into a 150-hour one. No other game this year offers that much content in a single purchase, and that is the core of the value argument.

The Score: What 99 Actually Means

A 99 on Metacritic ties the original 1998 Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time as the highest-rated video game of all time on the platform. Every review counted so far is positive, none below 90, and OpenCritic shows 100 percent of critics recommending the game. IGN called it "the single most impressive video game I have ever played." Game Informer gave it a perfect 10. Kotaku called it a masterpiece of open-world design.

Scores are not value, but they are a check against the worst case: a game that costs $80 and disappoints. GTA 6 does not disappoint the critics — the praise centres on the world's density, the story, the driving, the lighting and the sheer amount to do. The criticisms are real but narrow: the 30 fps cap, no mission replays, stiff motorcycles. None is a reason to wait for most players.

The Maths: Dollars Per Hour

Here is the value check in one number. At $79.99 for a 50-hour story, GTA 6 costs about $1.60 an hour. At 80 hours — the playthrough most players will actually have — it costs about $1.00 an hour. At 150 hours of completion, it costs about $0.53 an hour. Compare that with a cinema ticket, which buys you two hours for roughly $15, or a streaming subscription, which costs $15 a month whether you watch or not. By any per-hour measure, GTA 6 is one of the cheapest forms of entertainment you can buy — if you play it.

The catch is in the last four words: a game you do not play is worth nothing, and one you play for five hours and abandon costs $16 an hour. If you love open-world games and played GTA 5 for years, the value is extraordinary. If you do not like open-world crime games, no score will make it worth eighty dollars — the maths only works if the hours happen.

Who Should Buy Now, and Who Should Wait

Buy it now if you own a PS5 or Xbox Series X|S, you like open-world games, and you have time to play it this month — launch window is when everyone is playing and the secrets are fresh. Buy Standard, not Ultimate: the story is identical, and you can upgrade later for the same $20.

Wait if the price is the problem or you do not own the console yet. There is no PC version announced and no date for one — do not wait for a PC date that does not exist, but wait for a sale if $79.99 is too much today. Big games go on sale, and GTA 5 and Red Dead Redemption 2 both joined Game Pass years after launch. The game is not going anywhere, and the price will only fall.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Standard Edition enough?

Yes. Standard is the complete game for $79.99 — the full story, the whole map, all 91 missions. Ultimate adds 16 extras for $20 more, and you can buy the same upgrade later for the same price if you change your mind.

How long is the GTA 6 story?

About 50 to 60 hours for a focused run, 70 to 80 hours for most players with some side content, and 150-plus hours for full completion. Rockstar has not published an official length; these are reviewer playtimes.

Is GTA 6 really the best-reviewed game ever?

It holds a 99 on Metacritic, tying Ocarina of Time as the highest-rated game of all time on the platform, with no review below 90. That is a snapshot from launch week — but it is a remarkable one.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6 is worth $80 if you will play it — about a dollar an hour for most players, in the best-reviewed game ever made. Buy Standard, skip Ultimate unless the extras call to you, and play it now if you have the time. If the price is too much today, wait for a sale: the game is not going anywhere, and neither is the value.
