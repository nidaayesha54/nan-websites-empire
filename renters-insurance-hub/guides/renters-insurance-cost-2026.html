How Much Does Renters Insurance Cost in 2026?

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Renters insurance is one of the cheapest policies in all of insurance. In 2026, the average US renter pays about $14–$20 per month ($170–$240 per year) for a standard policy with $30,000 of personal property coverage, $100,000 of liability, and a $500 deductible. That's roughly the price of two streaming subscriptions to protect tens of thousands of dollars of belongings.

The national average, explained

Industry data for 2026 puts the national average annual premium at roughly $180. But "average" hides wide variation. A studio renter in Montana with $10,000 of coverage might pay under $8/month, while a renter in Mississippi with $50,000 of coverage and a $250 deductible could pay $30/month or more.

The three numbers that matter most in any quote:

  • Personal property limit — the max the insurer pays for your stuff. $30,000 is the most common default.
  • Liability limit — typically $100,000 to start; covers injuries or damage you're responsible for.
  • Deductible — what you pay out of pocket per claim. $500 is standard; $250 costs more, $1,000 costs less.

Renters insurance cost by state

Where you live is the single biggest price factor. States with frequent severe weather, high theft rates, or expensive litigation cost insurers more — and they pass that on. Here's how 2026 average monthly premiums stack up for a standard $30,000 / $100,000 / $500-deductible policy:

Even within a state, your ZIP code matters — dense urban areas with higher theft rates cost more than suburbs and rural areas. That's why two renters in the same state can see very different quotes.

7 factors that move your premium

1. Your state and ZIP code

As shown above, location dominates. Hurricane, tornado, wildfire, and theft exposure all feed the model insurers use for your area.

2. Coverage amount

Going from $10,000 to $30,000 of personal property raises the premium roughly 30%, but going from $30,000 to $50,000 adds only about 15–18% more. Higher limits get cheaper per dollar — so don't underinsure to save a few dollars.

3. Deductible

A $1,000 deductible typically saves about 12% versus $500; dropping to $250 raises the price about 18%. Choose the highest deductible you could comfortably pay tomorrow.

4. Liability limit

Bumping liability from $100,000 to $300,000 usually adds just $1–$2/month. It's the cheapest upgrade in the policy.

5. Credit-based insurance score

In most states, insurers use a credit-based score to price policies. Better credit can mean meaningfully lower premiums. (A few states, including California, restrict this practice.)

6. Claims history

Multiple claims in a few years mark you as higher risk. Filing only when a loss clearly exceeds your deductible keeps your record — and your rate — clean.

7. Building and safety features

Newer buildings, gated communities, deadbolts, smoke/CO detectors, and sprinkler systems can each shave a few percent off. Some insurers also discount for smart leak sensors and monitored security.

How to lower your renters insurance cost

  • Bundle with auto insurance — the classic multi-policy discount saves ~10% on renters and often on the auto side too.
  • Raise your deductible — if you have a $1,000 emergency fund, a $1,000 deductible is free money saved every month.
  • Ask about every discount — safety devices, claims-free history, autopay, paperless billing, and even certain professions or alumni associations.
  • Don't over-insure or under-insure — inventory your stuff with our belongings calculator so you buy the right amount, not a guess.
  • Shop every 1–2 years — loyalty rarely pays in insurance. Comparing 3–4 quotes takes an hour and routinely saves $50–$100/year.
  • Pay annually if you can — monthly billing fees add up; annual payment avoids them.

Cost FAQs

Is renters insurance worth it at $15/month?
For most people, yes. One stolen laptop ($1,200) wipes out nearly seven years of premiums. Add liability protection worth $100,000+, and the value is hard to beat. See the full math in Do I Really Need Renters Insurance?

Why is my quote higher than the average?
Usually it's your state, a low deductible, a prior claim, or a high-theft ZIP code. Our estimator breaks down which factors hit you hardest.

Can my landlord make me buy it?
Yes — most states allow landlords to require renters insurance in the lease, and many do. It costs you little and protects them from liability disputes.
