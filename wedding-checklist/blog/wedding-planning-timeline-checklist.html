Wedding Planning Timeline Checklist

Every task, in order, by month — plus the things couples always forget.

A wedding checklist only works if it's in the right order. This one is organized by when each task actually needs to happen — not by category. Work top to bottom and you'll never book things backwards.

12+ Months Out ☐

  • ☐ Set your total budget (written down, agreed by all contributors)
  • ☐ Draft your guest list
  • ☐ Choose and ask your wedding party
  • ☐ Research and tour venues (top 3)
  • ☐ Decide: planner, coordinator, or DIY

9–11 Months Out ☐

  • ☐ Book venue, sign contract
  • ☐ Book photographer and videographer
  • ☐ Start dress shopping
  • ☐ Book officiant
  • ☐ Create registry, send save-the-dates

6–8 Months Out ☐

  • ☐ Book caterer + tasting
  • ☐ Book florist
  • ☐ Book DJ or band
  • ☐ Order wedding dress
  • ☐ Bridesmaid dresses and suits ordered
  • ☐ Block hotel rooms, launch wedding website

1 Month Out ☐

  • ☐ Final headcount to caterer and venue
  • ☐ Seating chart complete
  • ☐ Confirm all vendors (times, contacts, final payments)
  • ☐ Tip envelopes prepared
  • ☐ Honeymoon packed, documents confirmed

Wedding Week ☐

  • ☐ Rehearsal + rehearsal dinner
  • ☐ Delegate: rings, tips, timeline, emergency kit — each to a named person
  • ☐ Confirm vendor meals with caterer
  • ☐ Rest, hydrate, eat breakfast on the big day

The 7 things couples forget most

  1. Marriage license timing. Some states have waiting periods; some licenses expire in 30–60 days. Check yours early.
  2. Vendor meals. Your photographer working 10 hours needs dinner — confirm the count with catering.
  3. A rain plan. Even for "definitely sunny" months. Especially for "definitely sunny" months.
  4. Tip envelopes. Prepare them before the week-of chaos and hand distribution to someone reliable.
  5. Comfortable shoes. Ceremony heels, reception flats. Your feet will thank you at hour six.
  6. Eating on the wedding day. Schedule it like a vendor appointment or it won't happen.
  7. A "do not disturb" buffer. The morning of your wedding is not the time for 40 well-meaning texts. Assign a gatekeeper.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important thing on a wedding checklist? +
Your budget. It's not the most exciting line item, but every other decision — venue, guest count, vendors — is a function of that number. Couples who set it first stay in control; couples who don't end up with surprise debt.
What do people forget on wedding checklists? +
The top forgotten items: marriage license timing, vendor meals, a rain backup plan, tip envelopes, comfortable shoes for dancing, and eating on the wedding day itself.
