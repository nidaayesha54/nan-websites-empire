Short-Term vs Long-Term Disability Insurance

Disability insurance comes in two flavors: short-term and long-term. They look similar on paper — both replace income when you can't work — but they cover completely different risks. Choosing the wrong one (or only one) can leave a costly gap. Here's how they differ and which you actually need.

Short-Term Disability Insurance (STD): The Basics

Short-term disability insurance replaces a portion of your income for a short period — typically 3 to 6 months, sometimes up to 1 or 2 years. Benefits usually begin quickly, after an elimination period of just 0 to 14 days, and replace about 60% to 70% of your salary.

STD is the policy that covers life's common interruptions: maternity leave, a knee surgery, a bad fracture, a short recovery from an illness. Many employers provide STD as a benefit, and it's inexpensive — individual policies run about $25 to $75 per month.

Long-Term Disability Insurance (LTD): The Basics

Long-term disability insurance is protection against the catastrophic scenario: an illness or injury that keeps you out of work for years. The elimination period is longer — commonly 90 days (30 to 180 days available) — and benefits can continue for 2, 5, or 10 years, or all the way to age 65 or 67.

LTD typically replaces 50% to 60% of income. It costs more — roughly 1% to 3% of annual income for individual policies — because it covers the far more expensive risk. The average LTD claim lasts nearly three years. See our full 2026 cost guide for detailed pricing.

Which One Do You Need?

If you can only afford one, choose long-term. Financial planners overwhelmingly recommend LTD first, because the financial damage of a multi-year disability dwarfs the cost of a few months off work. Your emergency fund can cover a short gap — it cannot cover three years of lost income.

That said, the two policies are designed to work together. In the classic setup, STD pays during the first 3 to 6 months, and LTD picks up when STD ends (matching the LTD elimination period). If your employer already provides STD, you only need to buy LTD. If your employer provides LTD with a 180-day elimination period, a small STD policy bridges those six months.

What Causes Most Disability Claims?

Contrary to what many assume, workplace accidents are a small minority of claims. The leading causes of long-term disability claims are musculoskeletal disorders (back pain, arthritis), cancer, cardiovascular conditions, and mental health conditions including depression and anxiety. Pregnancy and childbirth lead short-term claims. None of these care how careful you are — which is why the "it won't happen to me" assumption is the most expensive myth in insurance. Own-Occupation vs Any-Occupation: The Fine Print That Matters

Especially with LTD, check the definition of disability. Own-occupation coverage pays if you can't do your specific job — a surgeon with a hand tremor collects even if she could teach. Any-occupation coverage only pays if you can't do any job you're reasonably suited for. Own-occupation costs more but is dramatically better protection for skilled professionals. Many policies offer own-occupation for the first 2 years, then switch to any-occupation.

How the Two Work Together: A Real Example

Maria, 38, earns $72,000 as a nurse. She has employer STD (60% for 26 weeks) and buys individual LTD ($3,600/month, 90-day elimination, benefits to 65). After a back injury she can't work for 2 years. STD pays ~$3,600/month for the first 6 months. Her LTD elimination period (90 days) passes during that time, so LTD payments begin seamlessly at month 7 and continue until she recovers. Total protection: two full years of income. Without the LTD policy, her income would have stopped at month 6.
