GTA 6 Accessibility: No Easy Mode, but Plenty of Help

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

GTA 6 has no Easy mode. There is no difficulty setting anywhere in the game — no Easy, Normal or Hard to pick when you start, and no way to lower the challenge with a single switch. GTA 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and Rockstar's answer to difficulty is different: instead of one setting, the game spreads dozens of smaller helpers across its menus — combat assists, input helpers, narration, subtitles and visual options — and this guide lists every one of them, where it lives, and what it actually changes.

That matters because the question "is there an Easy mode?" is one of the most searched questions about the game, and the honest answer is more useful than a yes or no. There is no Easy mode, but there are tools that do most of what an Easy mode would do — and none of them lock you out of achievements or trophies.

No Difficulty Setting — These Take Its Place

The Gameplay tab holds the two settings that do the heavy lifting. Accessible Combat makes enemies weaker and lowers the damage you take, which turns the hardest shootouts into something far more forgiving. Reduced Vehicle Damage makes your car tougher, so chases and getaways stop ending in a smoking wreck. The game warns that both change the balance of play — and it is right, they do — but neither one locks you out of achievements or trophies, so there is no penalty for using them. If a mission is beating you, turn these on, finish it, and turn them off again if you want the harder version back.

Simplified Mini-Games does the same job for the fiddly parts: it simplifies the interactions for exercising, hotwiring and basketball, and other timing-based moments that can block a mission for players who struggle with rapid button presses. And the PlayStation Store listing for the game names 30 accessibility features in total, including an adjustable difficulty option listed as "Advanced" — the store's own wording for the same family of assists. The point is the same either way: the game has no difficulty menu, but it is not a game that refuses to help.

First Start: Three Choices Before You Play

Before the game begins, it asks you to set three things — and you can change all of them later in Settings, so nothing you pick here is locked in. Screen Narration reads supported menu text and interface options aloud as you navigate them; it is off by default, so turn it on only if you need spoken menu assistance. UI Text Size comes in Small, Medium, Large and X-Large, with Small as the default — Medium is the sensible pick if you play on a television from across the room. And Subtitles can be Off, Small, Medium or Large, with Off as the default.

Those three choices are only the start. The full subtitle and caption options live in the UI tab, split between two groups headed Scaling and Readability and Subtitles and Captions. There you can pick three subtitle sizes, turn on closed captions, add a background behind the text, and turn on speaker names so you always know who is talking — which matters in a game where radio chatter, phone calls and street conversations all carry story. Set subtitles to All Dialogue with the background on, and you will catch the lines the default setting hides.

Seeing and Hearing: Vision, Audio and Contrast

For blind and low-vision players, the most impressive features are the ones that talk back. Menu narration covers most menus and the interface, and a separate audio descriptions toggle adds spoken descriptions of what is happening on screen. A high contrast mode shows characters, enemies and interactable items in contrasting colours, so the things that matter stand out from the background instead of blending into it. Text size goes up to X Large, and the game also offers a basic screen reader, clear and large text options, and a high contrast setting — all named on the PlayStation Store's accessibility list for the game.

On the audio side, there is a tinnitus-reduction toggle that softens the ringing effect after explosions, a mono audio toggle, and separate volume sliders for sound effects, interface sounds, dialogue, music and media — so you can turn the music down without losing the voices, or the effects down without losing the radio. The DualSense controller's haptic feedback is adjustable through the accessibility settings too, which matters on PlayStation 5, where strong haptics can be tiring over a long session.

Hands and Timing: Tap Assist, Hold Assist and Aiming

The Gameplay tab has a group headed Accessibility, and it is where the motor and input helpers live: Tap Assist, Hold Delay, Hold Assist, Focus, Directional Button Actions, Aiming, Interact and Loadout. Tap Assist lets you complete actions that need repeated presses with a single tap of a button — the same job a macro would do, built into the game. Hold Assist and Hold Delay change how long you need to hold a button for actions like aiming, interacting and using nitrous, and there is a toggle option for aiming, interacting and nitrous so you can press once instead of holding.

Aiming itself is adjustable as well: the game offers targeting choices including partial lock-on, aim assist strength in four steps, and look and aim sensitivity — set to 7 by default — plus stick sensitivity and inversion. And if the in-game remapping is not enough, both consoles offer their own: use Custom Button Assignments in the PlayStation 5 accessibility settings, or button mapping in the Xbox accessibility settings, to move any action to any button you prefer.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does GTA 6 have an Easy mode?

No. There is no difficulty setting and no Easy mode. Use Accessible Combat and Reduced Vehicle Damage in the Gameplay tab instead — they make enemies weaker, reduce the damage you take and toughen your vehicle, and neither one affects achievements or trophies.

Where are the accessibility settings?

There is no single Accessibility tab. The Gameplay tab holds the Accessibility group (Tap Assist, Hold Assist, aiming, Simplified Mini-Games), the UI tab holds text size and subtitles, and the Display, Camera, Audio and Controller tabs hold the rest. Three choices — Screen Narration, UI Text Size and Subtitles — are also offered before the game starts.

Do accessibility settings lock achievements?

No. Accessible Combat, Reduced Vehicle Damage and the other assists do not lock you out of achievements or trophies. The game warns that they change the balance of play, but there is no penalty for using them.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6 has no Easy mode, but it is not a game that leaves players to struggle alone: Accessible Combat and Reduced Vehicle Damage do the job of a difficulty setting, Tap Assist and Hold Assist do the job of easier controls, and narration, subtitles, high contrast and tinnitus reduction cover seeing and hearing. Turn on what you need, leave the rest off, and play the game your way — the trophies will still be there.
