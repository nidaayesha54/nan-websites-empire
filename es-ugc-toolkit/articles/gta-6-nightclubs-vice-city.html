GTA 6 Nightclubs: What You Can Actually Do in Them

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and Vice City at night is everything the trailers promised: neon streets, packed clubs, music everywhere, and crowds that feel like a real city on a Friday night. The clubs are some of the most impressive places in the game — and also some of the most misunderstood. This guide explains what you can actually do inside GTA 6's nightclubs, which ones exist, and the one thing you definitely cannot do: own one.

That last point is the one that surprises the most players. In GTA Online, buying a nightclub and earning passive income from it was a whole way of playing. In GTA 6, that system does not exist. The clubs are places you spend money, not places that make it.

No Ownership, No Passive Income

Let us get the big fact out of the way first. GTA 6 has no nightclub ownership, no purchasable businesses of any kind, and no passive income. Post-launch reporting confirms there is no business ownership system in the game at all: no nightclubs to buy, no safe filling up while you play, no warehouse, no technicians. Garages are the only property a player can purchase. If you walk into a club expecting to buy it, you will be disappointed — and if a guide tells you how to earn money from a club, it is wrong.

That is a deliberate design choice, and it fits the story. GTA 6 is a tale about escape, not empire-building: Jason and Lucia are trying to get out of Vice City, not buy it piece by piece. The clubs exist as story locations and social spaces, not as investments. You go to a club to dance, to meet people, to do a mission — and then you leave, lighter in the pocket than when you arrived.

NINE1NINE: The Big One

The most famous club in the game is NINE1NINE, the multi-level Vice City nightclub where Jason and Lucia dance in the second trailer. Post-launch guides describe it as fully interactive: a dance floor, bars, dancers, and prompts to sit, dance and greet people. A private dance costs a reported 50 dollars in-game, and it is available solo or with Lucia — and, in a detail the game itself makes clear, it does not count as cheating in the relationship system. The Vault, a dance club with multiple rooms and different DJs, sits across the street from NINE1NINE, just to the west, and at least one main mission takes place there.

NINE1NINE is also where the game's DJing comes in. Rockstar's Extended Look showed DJing as a player activity, part of the activities montage alongside fishing, jet skiing, weight lifting, boating, canoeing and basketball. You can perform in the clubs — you just cannot own them. That is the line the game draws: participant, not proprietor.

Jack of Hearts and Delights Cabaret

The Jack of Hearts is the main strip club, owned by Boobie Ike, in central Crosstown, just south of your apartment. It is a traditional club with dancers on poles, and it is one of the more expensive places in the city: VIP dance treatments are reported to cost 1,000 dollars. Boobie Ike also co-runs Only Raw Records with Dre'Quan Priest, which ties the club into Vice City's music industry — the nightlife and the music business are the same world in GTA 6.

Delights Cabaret is the other end of the scale: a smaller, traditional strip club in Port Gellhorn, all the way to the west of the map, with more of a bar atmosphere. You first find it in Chapter 2, when Vicki introduces you to her boss there. It is the kind of place that feels like the middle of nowhere, and that is the point — Vice City's nightlife is not all downtown neon. Some of it is out in the dark, in the places the map barely reaches.

VYB and the Music Scene

The last club is VYB, in the northeast of Ocean Beach, directly south of your Act 4 home in Washington Beach. It has a traditional club atmosphere, with dance floors and high-class bars in the back — but it is not open to the public. VYB is part of Dre'Quan's story and is only used during a main mission in Act 4. You cannot walk in off the street, and that is worth knowing before you go looking for it.

All of this sits inside a music scene that Rockstar has built carefully. The Extended Look showed clubs packed with real crowds, a rooftop bar where Jason and Lucia talk, and a city that does not shut down after dark. There are parties that turn into missions, strip clubs with their own characters and backstories, and a radio landscape that includes a dedicated CircoLoco Records station for underground house and techno. The nightlife is not a side feature. It is one of the ways the game shows you what Vice City is.

How It Compares to 2002

Long-time players will remember the Malibu Club, Vice City's first ownable nightclub. In the 2002 game, Tommy Vercetti bought it for 120,000 dollars, and it generated up to 10,000 dollars per in-game day as an asset. That was a different game, with a different story — an empire-building story, where buying the city piece by piece was the point. GTA 6 tells the opposite story, and its clubs reflect that. You can dance in them, drink in them, DJ in them, and spend money in them. You cannot buy them. That is not a missing feature. It is the game telling you what it is about.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you buy a nightclub in GTA 6?

No. There is no nightclub ownership, no purchasable businesses and no passive income in GTA 6. Garages are the only property you can buy. Clubs are places to visit and spend money, not investments.

What can you do in GTA 6 nightclubs?

Dance, sit, greet people, buy drinks, pay for private dances (a reported 50 dollars at NINE1NINE, 1,000 dollars VIP at Jack of Hearts), and DJ as a player activity. Clubs are also story and relationship locations — private dances with Lucia do not count as cheating, and at least one main mission takes place in The Vault.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6's nightclubs are places to dance, DJ and spend money — NINE1NINE, Jack of Hearts, Delights Cabaret, The Vault and VYB — and they are some of the most alive places in Vice City. But they are not businesses: no ownership, no passive income, garages only. Go to the clubs to live in the city, not to buy it.
