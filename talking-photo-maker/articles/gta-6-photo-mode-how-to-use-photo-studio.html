GTA 6 Photo Mode: How to Use Photo Studio

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and it is one of the most photographed games ever made — not just by players, but by the game itself, which gives you a full photo studio for the first time in the series. Photo Studio, the game's name for photo mode, lets you move the camera anywhere, add your own lights, change the focus and the colours, and take a clean shot of Vice City at its best. This guide explains how to open it, what every tool does, and how the quick phone camera fits alongside it.

The neon streets, the Keys at sunset, the wildlife in the Grassrivers — the game is built to be looked at, and the tools let you look at it properly.

How to open Photo Studio

Photo Studio becomes available once you gain control of Lucia during the prologue, and it stays available for the rest of the game. Open the pause menu by pressing the Options button on PlayStation 5 or the Menu button on Xbox Series X|S, and select Photo Studio — on the PlayStation copy it sits between Help and Settings in the pause menu. The shortcut is faster: press Square on PlayStation or X on Xbox from the pause menu and you go straight in.

You can also activate Photo Studio during cutscenes, though the functions are limited there — some controls show as unavailable while a scene is playing. That is still useful: a cutscene is often the best-framed shot in the game, and the studio lets you reframe it, even if you cannot change everything about it. In free roam, everything is available, and you can try the tools without advancing any mission.

What the tools do

The tools fall into a few groups, and the lighting is the standout. The Camera tab lets you rotate and zoom in and out, change the camera movement speed, show a grid, tilt the roll for angled shots, and set the field of view up to 48.5 degrees. You can also disable depth of field, adjust the aperture and the focus distance, use a focus guide, and turn your character invisible — hide Jason or Lucia entirely and the shot becomes a landscape instead of a portrait.

The Effects tab handles the image itself: exposure, contrast, tint, bloom, highlights and shadows, saturation and sharpness, film grain, temperature, gamma, lens flare and chromatic aberration. The Lighting tab is the one that changes a flat shot completely: you can place several light sources — up to five — and change their colour, intensity and range, with presets including stage lights and camera flashes. A soft light on a face or a coloured glow behind a car is the difference between a snapshot and a photograph. The Vehicle tab toggles the lights on your vehicle, and the Overlay tab handles the vignette and the filter intensity, with a set of preset looks for when you want a style in one step rather than building it by hand.

Two limits to know

Photo Studio cannot change the time of day or the weather. The sun stays where you found it, and the rain stays where it found you — a limit that matters more for a skyline than for a close-up, because the light you get is the light the game gives you. If you see a shaft of evening light fall across a street, pause while it is there. The second limit is the cutscene one: some controls are unavailable while a scene plays, so a cutscene shot is a reframed shot, not a rebuilt one.

The studio is for framing, lighting and colour, not for directing the weather — work with the light you have and take the shot when the moment is right.

The phone camera: quick shots without pausing

For a quick snap without pausing, the phone's camera does the job. Press Up on the D-pad to bring out the smartphone and open the Camera app — it is the third icon in the first row of the home screen. The rear camera opens by default. On PlayStation 5, press R2 to take a photograph, L1 to turn the camera light on or off, L3 to toggle the camera grid, and R3 to switch to selfie mode. Zoom in and out with Up and Down on the D-pad, and cycle the filters with Left and Right.

Selfie mode is the fun part: press R3 to switch to the front-facing camera, then choose expressions with Left and Right on the D-pad and poses with Up and Down, and take the picture with R2. The phone camera offers 5x zoom, a composition grid, filters and posed selfies, and it is the tool for the shots you take in the middle of a mission, when pausing for the studio would break the moment.

Where your photos save

Photos saved using Photo Studio can be accessed in-game, inside Jason and Lucia's phone Gallery — the same place the phone camera's photos go. That is the in-game save: open the phone, open the Gallery, and your shots are there. Screenshots taken using the console's own capture feature are a different thing, and they save outside the game: on PlayStation 5 they go to the Media Gallery, and on Xbox Series X|S they go to the console's capture gallery.

When you are done setting up a shot, press the Hide UI button — Triangle on PlayStation or Y on Xbox — to take a clean picture with no menus on screen. Check the edges of the frame one last time before you press it: a menu is easy to hide, but a stray car or a bystander in the corner is in the photo forever.

FAQ

How do you open photo mode in GTA 6? Press Options on PlayStation 5 or Menu on Xbox to pause, then select Photo Studio — or press Square on PlayStation or X on Xbox from the pause menu to go straight in. It is available from the prologue.

Can you change the time of day in GTA 6 photo mode? No. Photo Studio has no control for the hour or the weather — the sun and the rain stay where you found them.

Where do GTA 6 photos save? Photo Studio shots and phone camera photos save to the in-game phone Gallery. Console captures save to the PS5 Media Gallery or the Xbox capture gallery.

The bottom line: GTA 6 Photo Studio is a real studio, not a screenshot button — five lights, full camera control and preset filters, all from the pause menu. Use the phone camera for quick shots, the studio for the ones you want to keep, and hide the UI before you press the shutter.
