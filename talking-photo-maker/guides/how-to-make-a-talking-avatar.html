How to Make a Talking Avatar in 6 Steps

Making a talking avatar takes about ten minutes once you know the workflow. The six steps below work with any talking photo AI — HeyGen, D-ID, CapCut, Vidnoz, or whichever tool you picked from our comparator.

Step 1 — Choose the right photo

The photo is 50% of your result. Use a front-facing portrait, eyes open and looking at the camera, even lighting on the face, and a simple background. Minimum 1024px on the long edge. Avoid sunglasses, heavy shadows across the face, extreme angles, or group shots — every one of those degrades lip-sync. A clean headshot-style photo beats a "cool" photo every time.

Step 2 — Write the script

Write like you talk, not like you write. Target ~75 words per 30 seconds, put the hook in the first 3 seconds, and mark pauses with ellipses (...) where the avatar should breathe. Don't write a script longer than 90 seconds for your first attempt — short scripts hide flaws. Our free script writer generates a formatted script with pause markers for any topic, tone and length in seconds.

Step 3 — Pick the voice

Match the voice to the audience: warm and conversational for trust, authoritative for B2B, playful for social media, calm for tutorials. Most tools let you preview voices — always preview with YOUR actual script, because the same voice sounds different on different text. Not sure? Take our 5-question voice picker quiz.

Step 4 — Generate the avatar

  1. Upload your photo to your chosen tool.
  2. Paste the script (use the "clean" version without stage directions).
  3. Select the voice and language.
  4. Choose output length/resolution — 1080p vertical (9:16) for TikTok/Reels/Shorts, 16:9 for YouTube.
  5. Hit generate and wait (usually 1–5 minutes).

Step 5 — Review and fix

Watch the full render before publishing. Check: does the mouth match the words? Does blinking look natural? Are there weird teeth or background shimmers? The most common fix is shortening the script — long sentences expose lip-sync flaws. If one section looks off, regenerate just that section rather than the whole video.

Step 6 — Export and publish

Export at your platform's native resolution, then add burned-in captions — most viewers watch muted, and captions double as a realism safety net. Add a hook text overlay in the first frame. Publish at your audience's peak hours, and disclose AI generation where the platform requires it.

5 rookie mistakes to avoid

  1. Side-profile or blurry photos — lip-sync falls apart. Front-facing and sharp, always.
  2. Robotic scripts — if it reads like an essay, it will sound like a robot. Contractions, short sentences, conversational.
  3. 90-second first attempts — start with 15–30 seconds until you trust the tool.
  4. Ignoring the watermark — plan for it (or pay to remove it) before you publish commercially.
  5. No captions — muted viewers scroll past; captions keep them.
