What Is Professional Tax?

Professional tax (PT) is a state-level tax levied on income earned from a profession, trade, calling or employment — despite the name, it is not limited to "professionals". Your employer deducts it from your salary and remits it to the state government. The maximum anyone can be charged is Rs 2,500 per year, fixed by Article 276 of the Constitution. Professional tax paid is deductible under Section 16(iii) of the Income Tax Act, reducing your taxable salary income.

States With No Professional Tax

Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Punjab, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu & Kashmir, Uttarakhand, Bihar, Goa and most Union Territories do not levy professional tax. If you work in one of these, your PT is zero.

Who Else Pays PT?

Self-employed professionals (doctors, CAs, lawyers, consultants, freelancers), traders, HUFs and businesses must enrol (PTEC) and pay PT directly — usually a flat amount up to Rs 2,500/year depending on the state.

FAQs

Is professional tax the same as income tax?

No. Professional tax is a state government levy deducted from salary; income tax is a central tax. PT paid reduces your taxable salary under Section 16(iii).

Why is February's PT Rs 300 in Maharashtra and Karnataka?

It's a "balancing figure": Rs 200 × 11 months + Rs 300 in February = Rs 2,500, the constitutional annual maximum.

Do I pay PT in two states if I relocate?

PT applies based on your state of employment. If you relocate mid-year, each state's slab applies for the months worked there; confirm with payroll so the annual cap isn't exceeded.

Does the employer need to register?

Yes — employers must obtain a PT registration/enrolment certificate (PTRC/PTEC) in states that levy it, and remit deducted PT monthly/quarterly.

What if my state isn't listed?

The calculator covers the states with the largest salaried workforces. Slabs for other PT states (Kerala, MP, Odisha, Assam etc.) follow the same Rs 2,500/year cap — check your state's commercial tax notification.
