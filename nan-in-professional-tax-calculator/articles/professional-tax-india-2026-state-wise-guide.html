Professional Tax in India 2026: State-Wise Slabs & Complete Guide

Professional tax is the state-level tax quietly deducted from millions of Indian salaries every month. It differs in every state, is capped at ₹2,500 a year by the Constitution, and is fully deductible from your income tax. Here's the complete 2026 state-wise picture.

What professional tax is

Despite the name, professional tax has nothing to do with being a "professional" — it applies to salaried employees, traders, freelancers, and business owners in the states that levy it. It is imposed by state governments under their Professions, Trades, Callings and Employments Acts, and Article 276 of the Constitution caps it at ₹2,500 per person per year. No state can charge more.

For salaried employees, your employer deducts it from your salary and deposits it with the state. You'll usually see it as "PT" or "Prof. Tax" on your payslip — typically ₹200/month (₹300 in February in some states, a balancing figure to hit exactly ₹2,500 for the year).

States with zero professional tax

A large part of North India levies no professional tax at all: Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana, Rajasthan, Punjab, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, and Jammu & Kashmir, plus most Union Territories. If you move from Mumbai to Delhi, that ₹200/month line simply disappears from your payslip.

Income-tax deduction: Section 16(iii)

Here's the part most employees miss: professional tax paid is fully deductible from your salary income under Section 16(iii) of the Income Tax Act. If you paid ₹2,500 in PT, your taxable salary drops by ₹2,500 — saving you roughly ₹500–₹780 in income tax depending on your slab. The deduction is available under both the old and new tax regimes, making it one of the few deductions that survive in the new regime.

Employer duties and penalties

  • Employers must register for PT in every state where they have employees, deduct monthly, and deposit with the state treasury by the due date.
  • Returns are filed monthly/quarterly/annually depending on the state.
  • Late payment attracts interest and penalties — the employer bears these, not you.
  • If your employer never deducted PT in a PT-levying state, the liability (plus interest) sits with the employer — but check your payslip, because "not deducted" sometimes means "deducted but not deposited."

PT for freelancers and the self-employed

Doctors, lawyers, CAs, architects, consultants, and traders in PT states must register and pay PT directly — usually a flat annual amount (often ₹2,500/year in Maharashtra for professionals). Freelancers working remotely for out-of-state clients are still liable based on where they work, not where the client sits.

How to check your own PT slip

  1. Open your latest payslip and find the "Professional Tax" / "PT" line.
  2. Compare it with your state's slab above for your salary level — break down your salary components first if you're unsure what counts.
  3. Check February: in Maharashtra and Karnataka the February deduction jumps to ₹300 — that's normal, it's the annual balancing figure.
  4. Confirm the annual total doesn't exceed ₹2,500 — it legally can't.
  5. At tax-filing time, make sure the full PT amount is claimed under Section 16(iii).

PT registration: what employers must do

If you run a business with employees in a PT-levying state, you need a separate PT registration in each state — one national registration doesn't cover you. The typical flow: apply on the state commercial-tax portal, get a PT registration certificate (PTRC) for deducting employee PT and a PT enrolment certificate (PTEC) for your own liability as an employer/professional, then deduct monthly and file returns (monthly, quarterly, or annually depending on the state and your wage bill). Multi-state employers most often trip up by registering in their head-office state and forgetting the branch states — each state's inspector can penalize separately.

Working in two states: which PT applies?

A common 2026 reality — you live in one state and work in another, or you transferred mid-year. The rule of thumb: PT follows the state of employment (where you render the service), not your home address. If you worked 7 months in Maharashtra and 5 in Karnataka, each state's slab applies to the respective period — and the ₹2,500 annual cap is per state, so a mid-year transfer can technically expose you to two caps. Keep both Form 16s; your tax consultant will apportion the Section 16(iii) deduction across the full ₹5,000 if that's what you paid.

Payment frequency differs by state

  • Monthly deduction: Maharashtra, Karnataka, West Bengal, Gujarat (the familiar ₹200/month pattern).
  • Half-yearly: Tamil Nadu and Kerala levy PT in two half-yearly slabs (April–September, October–March).
  • Annual: some states allow or require annual payment for the self-employed and professionals.

Whatever the frequency, the constitutional ceiling of ₹2,500 per person per year per state always holds.

PT on arrears, bonuses and variable pay

A question payroll teams hear constantly: does PT apply to arrears, annual bonuses, or incentives? The answer depends on the state, but the general principle is that PT is levied on salary/wages earned in the month — so a large arrears payout credited in one month can push that month's PT into a higher slab. (The same applies to an annual statutory bonus payout.) Maharashtra's structure handles this gracefully (the ₹200/₹300 monthly pattern caps out at ₹2,500 anyway), but in states with granular slabs like West Bengal, a bonus-heavy month can legitimately attract a higher monthly PT. Employers should run PT on the actual month of payment, not spread it retrospectively.

How PT appears in Form 16 and your ITR

Your Form 16 shows total PT deducted under the salary details — cross-check this figure against your payslips once a year; mismatches are surprisingly common when payroll software changes mid-year. When filing your ITR, the PT amount flows into the Section 16(iii) deduction automatically if your Form 16 is correct. One caution: if you changed jobs mid-year, both Form 16s may show PT — add them up and claim the combined total. And if your employer deducted PT but your Form 16 shows zero, raise it before filing: the deduction is only as good as the paperwork.
