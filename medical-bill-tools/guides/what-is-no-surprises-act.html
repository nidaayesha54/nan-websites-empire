What Is the No Surprises Act? Patient Protections Explained (2026)

Updated January 2026 · 5 min read

Before 2022, you could do everything right — go to an in-network hospital — and still get a crushing bill from an out-of-network anesthesiologist you'd never met. The No Surprises Act, effective January 1, 2022, made most of that illegal.

What the law bans

  • Surprise bills for emergency services. Even at out-of-network facilities, your cost is capped at in-network cost-sharing amounts.
  • Out-of-network balance billing at in-network facilities. Ancillary providers — anesthesiology, radiology, pathology, labs, neonatology — can't bill you the difference.
  • Air ambulance surprise billing. Your cost is limited to in-network cost-sharing.

Good faith estimates for the uninsured

If you're uninsured or self-paying, providers must give you a good faith estimate of costs before care. If the final bill is $400 or more above the estimate, you can start a patient-provider dispute through the federal process at cms.gov/nosurprises.

The big gap: ground ambulance. Ground ambulance services are not covered by the No Surprises Act. If you get a large ground ambulance bill, your options are negotiation, financial assistance, and state-level protections where they exist.

Notice and consent: the exception

In non-emergency situations, a provider can ask you to sign a notice and consent form agreeing to out-of-network charges — but the form must meet strict federal requirements (timing, content, language access). Consent is never valid for emergency care, and never for ancillary services like anesthesiology or labs. If you're unsure a form you signed counts, file a complaint anyway and let the regulators decide.

How to use your protections

  1. Don't pay first, check first. Run your situation through our No Surprises Act checker.
  2. Call the provider's billing department and state that you believe the bill violates the No Surprises Act.
  3. File a federal complaint: call 1-800-985-3059 or submit at cms.gov/nosurprises.
  4. Keep everything: the bill, your insurance card, EOB, and any forms you signed.

Common questions

Does the No Surprises Act cover ground ambulance?

No. Ground ambulance is the biggest gap in the law — it is not covered. Air ambulance is covered. You can still negotiate ground ambulance bills and ask about financial assistance.

How do I file a No Surprises Act complaint?

Call the federal No Surprises helpdesk at 1-800-985-3059 or file online at cms.gov/nosurprises. Keep copies of the bill, your insurance card, and any consent forms.

What if I already paid a surprise bill?

You may still be able to get a refund. File a complaint and contact your insurer — the law's protections apply whether or not you've paid yet.
