Medical Debt & Your Credit Report in 2026: What's Changed

January 2026 · 5 min read

Medical debt used to wreck credit scores over a $200 ER copay. The rules have changed dramatically — here's where things stand.

The big changes

  • Paid medical collections are gone. Once you pay a medical collection, it must be removed from your credit reports.
  • Small balances don't appear. Medical collections under $500 are no longer reported at all.
  • A one-year waiting period. Unpaid medical debt can't hit your report until it's at least a year old — giving you time to resolve billing errors and insurance issues first.
  • The CFPB pushed further. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau finalized rules to remove medical debt from credit reports used in lending decisions. Check consumerfinance.gov for the current status, as rules can face legal challenges.

What still shows up

Large unpaid medical collections (over $500, older than a year) can still appear. And while credit reports are cleaner, the underlying debt still exists — collectors can still contact you, and within the statute of limitations they can still sue.

How to dispute medical debt errors

  1. Pull your reports free at AnnualCreditReport.com.
  2. Dispute in writing with each bureau (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion) — describe the error and attach proof (paid receipts, EOBs).
  3. Dispute with the collector too, and request debt validation under the FDCPA.
  4. Escalate to the CFPB at consumerfinance.gov/complaint if bureaus don't fix verified errors.
Pro tip: many "medical debts" on credit reports are billing errors — wrong patient, already paid by insurance, or duplicate accounts. Run the original bill through our bill error checker before you assume you owe it.
