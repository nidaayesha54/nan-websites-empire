How to Read Your Medical Bill Line by Line

January 2026 · 5 min read

Hospital bills look like they're written in code — because they are. Here's how to decode yours in about 15 minutes.

1. Start with the summary, then get the itemized bill

The first bill you receive is usually a summary: total charges, insurance payments, adjustments, and "amount due." Call billing and ask for the itemized statement with CPT codes. This is the version where errors live — and where you'll find them with our bill error checker.

2. Learn the key line-item fields

  • Date of service — when the care happened. Watch for dates you weren't there.
  • Description — often cryptic ("LAB CHEM PANEL"). Ask billing to translate anything unclear.
  • CPT/HCPCS code — the 5-digit procedure code. You can look these up online to see what was actually billed.
  • Charges — the hospital's "chargemaster" list price, often far above what anyone actually pays.
  • Adjustments — the contractual discount your insurer negotiated. This is why the same procedure costs you less than the list price.
  • Patient responsibility — what you owe after insurance and adjustments.

3. Match the bill against your EOB

Your insurer's Explanation of Benefits (EOB) is the Rosetta Stone. For each line: does the provider's bill show the same procedure, the same date, and the insurer's payment? Mismatches are where billing errors hide — duplicate charges, wrong codes, missing insurance payments.

4. Red flags to circle

  • Charges for dates you weren't at the facility
  • The same test or procedure listed twice
  • Brand-name drugs when you received generics
  • "Miscellaneous" or vague line items with big dollar amounts
  • Anesthesia or facility fees that don't match your procedure time
