GTA 6 Car Paint: Mod Shops, Custom Paint and Prices

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Paint is the cheapest way to make a car yours in GTA 6 — and the most confusing, because the game splits the work across three different kinds of shop. A quick colour change lives in one place, an elaborate paint job lives in another, and the two fanciest garages in the game are locked behind the Ultimate Edition. This guide explains where each kind of paint lives, what each shop can actually do, and the one step to take before you spend a dollar on colour.

The key fact is that GTA 6 does not have one customisation shop that does everything. In GTA 5, a single chain — Los Santos Customs — handled resprays, upgrades and repairs in one menu. GTA 6 spreads that work out, and knowing which door to walk through is the difference between a ten-second respray and a full custom build.

Pay 'n' Spray: the quick colour change

Pay 'n' Spray is the fast option, and it is deliberately limited. Rob Nelson, who co-runs Rockstar North, told Famitsu that Pay 'n' Spray does repairs and a basic colour change — and that is all. Drive in with a dented car and a wanted level, drive out repaired and repainted in a plain colour. It is the shop for fixing mistakes, not for making statements.

That limit is the point. Pay 'n' Spray is everywhere, it is fast, and it asks nothing of you. But if you want anything beyond a basic respray — a two-tone finish, a custom wrap, a finish that turns heads — Nelson's answer is clear: you will need a tuning shop.

Mod shops: where the real paint lives

The elaborate work happens in the game's mod shops, which GTA 6 calls its customisation garages. Coastal Auto Care in Key Lento is one every copy of the game has, and it is the first one most players will find. Drive a car in, and the menu splits into six tabs: Pro Builds, Cosmetics, Bodywork, Performance, Wheels and Interior. Paint sits in Cosmetics, alongside the other visual work, while the mechanical upgrades live in Performance.

Each mod shop has its own speciality, according to creator previews, and most handle basic upgrades while some options are offered only at certain locations. That means the shop you use matters: the paint job you want may not be available at the shop nearest your safehouse. The game never publishes a full list of which shop sells what, so part of the fun — and the frustration — is learning the map of garages the way you learn the map of the city.

The two Ultimate Edition garages

The fanciest paint in the game lives behind the Ultimate Edition. Rockstar's edition description names two exclusive mod shops, and both are open only with that edition. Rideout Customs in Vice City promises to turn plain vehicles into showpieces with detailed interiors, exquisite rims and donk stylings — the raised, big-wheeled look that is pure Florida car culture. One-Eyed Willie's on Lake Leonida specialises in off-road modifications and hand-painted automotive artistry.

Both shops are open from Chapter 1, so there is no story wait for either of them. And if you bought the Standard Edition and change your mind, Rockstar says you can upgrade on the PlayStation Store at any time. The Ganado Retro Build, another Ultimate Edition extra, adds exclusive mods to Jason's Vapid Ganado pickup — but only in Chapter 1, so fit it early if you want it.

What the reviews say about customisation

Eurogamer's review says you can customise guns and cars to extreme ends, which is the strongest statement anywhere about how deep the system goes. The same review describes custom wrapping on the street-racing side missions as an example of vehicle customisation in action — wrapping a car for a race, not just repainting it for the street. And Kotaku's tips guide notes that garages bought around the map give more slots for cars and motorcycles, so you can keep customised bikes, cars and trucks instead of choosing between them.

What nobody has published is a price list. Rockstar has said nothing about what mods cost, and no launch guide has a full menu of prices either. Treat any price you see online as a report from one player's game, not a fact — prices may vary by car, by shop and by the work you choose.

Before you spend a dollar on paint

Do one thing first: register the car. Guides that have played the released game say to register a vehicle before upgrading it, because an unregistered car can be lost — impounded, destroyed or simply gone — along with everything you spent on it. The registration step ties the car to you, and it is the difference between a custom build you keep and a custom build you mourn.

After that, the order is simple. Use Pay 'n' Spray for repairs and quick colour changes. Use a mod shop for anything elaborate. And if you want the donk stylings of Rideout Customs or the hand-painted art of One-Eyed Willie's, remember they belong to the Ultimate Edition — decide whether the paint is worth the upgrade before you fall in love with a finish you cannot buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you change your car's paint in GTA 6?

Yes. Pay 'n' Spray handles repairs and a basic colour change, while mod shops handle elaborate paint jobs. Two more mod shops — Rideout Customs and One-Eyed Willie's — are exclusive to the Ultimate Edition.

Where is the first mod shop in GTA 6?

Coastal Auto Care in Key Lento is one every copy of the game has, and it is the first customisation garage most players will reach. Its menu splits into six tabs: Pro Builds, Cosmetics, Bodywork, Performance, Wheels and Interior.

How much does a paint job cost in GTA 6?

Rockstar has published no prices for paint or mods, and no launch guide has a full price list. Treat any price you see as one player's report, not a confirmed figure — and register the car before you spend anything on it.

The bottom line: paint in GTA 6 is split by design. Quick colour changes live at Pay 'n' Spray, real custom work lives at the mod shops, and the two showpiece garages belong to the Ultimate Edition. Register the car first, then choose your shop — and your colour — with the full picture in mind.
