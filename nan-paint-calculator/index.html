FAQs

How many litres of paint do I need for one room?

It depends on the wall area, coats and coverage. A typical 12 × 14 ft room with 10 ft walls needs about 11–13 litres for 2 coats on smooth walls (after doors and windows are subtracted, plus 10% wastage). Use the calculator above with your exact measurements — guessing is how people end up making a second trip to the shop.

What coverage should I use per litre?

Most interior emulsions in Pakistan cover roughly 120–150 sq ft per litre per coat on smooth, primed walls. New plaster and rough surfaces absorb more, so effective coverage drops to about 100 sq ft per litre. Always check the coverage printed on your paint can — it beats any online average.

Do I need primer?

Yes on new plaster, and strongly recommended when changing from a dark colour to a light one. Primer seals the surface, improves adhesion and usually means you need fewer finish coats. On previously painted walls in good condition, one coat of primer is optional but still helps the finish look even.

How many coats are enough?

Two coats is the standard for a durable, even finish. One coat is fine only for a refresh in the same colour on a wall in good condition. Budget three coats when going from dark to light, or when painting over stains or patched plaster.

Why add 10% wastage?

Roller absorption, cutting-in around edges, spills and future touch-ups all use extra paint. Buying 10% more is far cheaper than a second trip — and a different batch can dry a slightly different shade, which shows on the wall.

Should I buy all the paint at once?

Yes. Buy every can for one room at the same time, from the same batch if possible, and mix (box) the cans together before painting. That keeps the colour identical from wall to wall.
