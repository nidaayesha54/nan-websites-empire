GTA 6 Side Jobs That Pay: Towing, Deliveries and More

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Story missions are not the only way to earn money in GTA 6 — and for most players, they are not even the best way. The game is full of side jobs, from towing ATMs off their foundations to delivering packages across the state, and the payouts range from a few hundred dollars to $20,000 a run. GTA 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and this guide ranks every launch-verified side job by what it actually pays — and warns you about the ones that cost more than they earn.

The warning matters because most of the side-job lists online were written before launch, and they are full of jobs that do not exist. Delivery apps, food running, bike couriers, assassination contracts, passive businesses — all of it was speculation, and none of it has been confirmed in the released game. What follows is only what launch-era guides have verified inside the game, with the numbers labelled for what they are.

ATM Towing: The Job Hiding in Plain Sight

The most surprising side job in GTA 6 is also one of the most reliable: towing ATMs. Leonida has 454 ATMs, and 168 of them can be hooked up to a tow truck and pulled out of the ground, cash and all. It is a criminal job, not a legal one — pulling an ATM triggers the wanted system, so the payout comes with a police chase attached — but it is repeatable, it is available early, and it turns a vehicle most players ignore into a money-making tool.

The practical lesson is to treat ATM towing as a job with a cost. The cash inside is real, but so is the response: the police act on what they witness, and an ATM being dragged down the street is hard to miss. Do it quietly, do it at night, do it away from patrols — and bank the money at a working ATM before the next job, because pocket cash is lost when you die or get arrested, and it does not carry into Chapter 2.

Pilot Pete Deliveries: $20,000 a Run

The best-paying side job verified so far is Pilot Pete deliveries: $20,000 per delivery mission, when all seven packages land on time. It is a Chapter 4 Strangers and Freaks activity, Jason only, and the missions appear as the quest line progresses — seven packages per run, on a timer, across the state. It is the highest verified per-run payout outside the bank vaults, and it is the closest thing the game has to a repeatable high earner.

The catch is the unlock: Chapter 4 is late in the story, and the deliveries only appear as the quest line progresses, so this is not an early-game earner. Treat it as the endgame side job — the one that funds the garage, the customization and the formal wear once the story is nearly done. And remember that the numbers come from early launch-era guides and may change with patches, so check the payout on your own run before planning around it.

Hunting, Treasure Diving and Car Theft

Three more jobs fill out the verified list, and each one pays in a different way. Hunting pays through Bret Vogel, who paid Jason $500 for the first hunt in early-copy footage — and studying a Whitetail Doe through the scope showed a value of $220 to $380, with the animal tagged Pristine when loaded into the truck, which suggests condition matters to the price. Hunting matters more for the 100% checklist than for your balance, but it is real money, and it is available from Chapter 2.

Treasure diving pays through Kenton, the dive-charter captain in the Keys: shipwrecks hold chests, gold doubloons and heirlooms, and Kenton's dives pay around $1,400 per wreck with almost no risk, once they unlock after the "Island Dreams" mission. There are 33 underwater discoveries in total, tracked under Progress, Collections. And car theft pays through Moses in Port Gellhorn and Randy in western Vice City — steal specific vehicles, deliver them undamaged for a bonus, and earn several thousand dollars per car, with high-end vehicles paying more than mid-range ones.

What Does Not Pay — Yet

The other side of the list is the jobs that do not pay, or do not exist. Jobs in Chapter 1 pay very little, and whatever you earn there disappears when the chapter ends — so do not grind early jobs expecting to keep the money. Fishing pays nothing: it is catch and release, for fun and collectibles, not income. Mugging pedestrians brings in small amounts — some people carry less than $100 — and it can trigger the wanted system, with some victims pulling a gun and shooting back. And passive income has not been confirmed at all: no buyable business, no property income, no stock market, no investment system has been announced or found in the released game.

The rule is simple: if a guide promises a job, a business or an investment that no launch-era guide has verified inside the game, it is inventing it. The verified economy runs on cash, banks, fences and robberies — and the side jobs above are the honest list of what pays, in the order it pays.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best-paying side job in GTA 6?

Pilot Pete deliveries, at $20,000 per run when all seven packages land on time — but it is a Chapter 4 Strangers and Freaks activity, Jason only. Before that, treasure diving at around $1,400 per wreck and car theft at several thousand per car are the best verified earners.

Can you tow ATMs in GTA 6?

Yes — 168 of Leonida's 454 ATMs can be hooked to a tow truck and pulled out. It triggers the wanted system, so do it quietly and bank the cash before the next job.

Is there passive income in GTA 6?

Nothing confirmed. No buyable business, no property income and no stock market has been announced or found in the released game. Money comes from active work: missions, robberies, hunting, diving and car theft.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6 side jobs pay from $500 for a first hunt to $20,000 for a Pilot Pete delivery run, with ATM towing, treasure diving and car theft in between — and nothing passive, nothing invested, nothing bought. Work the verified jobs, bank the money, and ignore any guide selling you a business or an investment that Rockstar has never announced.
