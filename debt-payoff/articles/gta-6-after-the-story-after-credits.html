GTA 6 After the Story: What to Do After the Credits

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

The credits roll, the story ends — and then what? In GTA 6, the answer is: a lot. The story is only the beginning of the game, and the road to 100% completion is roughly 200 hours long, with 91 main missions behind you and a checklist ahead of you that only appears after the credits. GTA 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and this guide covers everything left to do after the story: the 100% checklist, the collectibles, the side activities, and the things you can only do once the story is done.

It matters because the story is shorter than the game. A focused run takes about 50 to 60 hours, and most players finish in 70 to 80 — but the completion checklist is roughly 200 hours, and the difference is everything the game hides outside the main missions. The players who stop at the credits miss more than half of what Leonida contains.

The 100% Checklist

The road to 100% completion is the biggest thing waiting after the credits, and it is a long one: roughly 200 hours, built from everything the game tracks. The list includes the 91 main missions, 39 gang operations, 22 bank jobs, 42 shooting range gold medals, 300 stunts, 33 underwater discoveries, and the animal study requirement — 100 animals to study, counted in the Progress menu's Animals section. The checklist only appears after the credits, so the first thing to do after the story is open it and see what is left.

Some of it you will have done already without noticing: the gang operations and bank jobs are part of the story's later chapters, the shooting range medals come from practice, and the animal studies accumulate as you explore. The rest is the real work — the stunts scattered across Leonida, the shipwrecks at the bottom of the sea, and the hunting and fishing that the story never required. Work through the checklist in the order that suits you, and treat it as a tour of the state rather than a chore: every item on it is a place you have not been yet.

Shipwrecks, Stunts and Side Activities

The underwater discoveries are the most satisfying part of the post-story game. There are 33 of them, mostly wrecks, tracked under Progress, Collections — and each one needs scuba gear, a metal detector and a trip to the southern waters. Kenton, the dive-charter captain in the Keys, gives you one wreck at a time: dive down, sweep the metal detector until it flashes green, dig up chests, gold doubloons and heirlooms, and bring them all to Kenton for cash. The first wreck turns up during the story mission Island Dreams, and the rest are yours to find after the credits.

The stunts are the other big block: 300 of them, scattered across the state, and each one is a small challenge — a jump, a landing, a speed run — that tests a different part of the driving. And the side activities fill the gaps: hunting at Mount Kalaga and in the Grassrivers, fishing that is catch and release, pool at bars, and the radio stations, the in-game phone and the social media layer that make Leonida feel alive. None of it is required by the story. All of it is the game.

Money After the Story

Money works differently after the credits. The big paydays — the bank vaults paying up to $205,000 at the end of Chapter 4 — are behind you, and the repeatable earners are the side jobs: Pilot Pete deliveries at $20,000 a run, treasure diving at around $1,400 per wreck, car theft through Moses and Randy at several thousand per car, and hunting at $220 to $380 a deer. The money you earn after the story is for the garage, the customization and the formal wear — the things the story never gave you time to buy.

And the money rules still apply: pocket cash is lost when you die or get arrested, so bank it at ATMs, stash spare money under the floorboards in Jason's bedroom, and keep the BuckMe account topped up. The savings account is the one the story teaches you to use, and it is the one that matters most after the credits, when there are no more mission payouts to replace what you lose.

What You Cannot Go Back To

One thing to know before you settle into the post-story game: missions cannot be replayed. Once a mission ends, its outcome is permanent — the choices you made, the money you earned, and the mistakes you made are all part of your playthrough now. If there is a mission you wish had gone differently, the only way back is a manual save made before it started, which is why the save habit matters so much: fifteen manual save slots, rotated after every mission, with one kept from before any tricky job.

That permanence is also what makes the post-story game feel different. The story is done, the choices are made, and what is left is the world — the state, the activities, the checklist, and the freedom to explore them at your own pace. There is no online mode at launch to wait for, and no DLC announced, so the post-story game is the game: Leonida, yours to finish.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does 100% completion take in GTA 6?

Roughly 200 hours, against 50 to 60 hours for a focused story run and 70 to 80 for most players. The checklist includes 91 missions, 39 gang operations, 22 bank jobs, 42 shooting range gold medals, 300 stunts and 33 underwater discoveries.

What should I do first after finishing GTA 6?

Open the 100% checklist — it only appears after the credits — and see what is left. Then work through the underwater discoveries with Kenton, the stunts across Leonida, and the side activities you skipped during the story.

Can I replay missions after the story?

No. Missions cannot be replayed in GTA 6, so a mission's outcome is permanent. The only way back is a manual save made before the mission started.

The Bottom Line

After the credits, GTA 6 is still only half finished: 200 hours of checklist, 33 shipwrecks, 300 stunts, and a whole state left to explore. Open the checklist, dive with Kenton, bank your money, and treat the post-story game as what it is — the rest of Leonida, waiting for you to finish it.
