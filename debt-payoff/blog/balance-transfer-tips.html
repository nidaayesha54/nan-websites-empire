Balance Transfer Cards: 5 Rules to Use Them Right

By NAN Marketing Agency · October 12, 2026 · 4 min read

A 0% intro-APR balance transfer card can pause interest for 12–21 months — every payment hits principal. Used right, it's one of the fastest legal ways to kill credit-card debt. Used wrong, it's a trap. Five rules:

1. Do the Transfer Math First

Transfer fees are typically 3–5%. Moving $6,000 at a 3% fee costs $180 — but saves ~$1,500 in interest over 18 months at 24% APR. Worth it. Moving $800 at 5%? Probably not.

2. Have a Payoff Plan for the Promo Window

Divide the transferred balance by the promo months: $6,000 ÷ 18 = $334/month. Set that autopay on day one. The card is a tool, not a vacation from payments.

3. Don't Add New Purchases

Most cards charge regular APR on new purchases while transfers sit at 0% — and payments may go to the 0% balance first. Lock the card in a drawer after the transfer.

4. Know What Happens at Month 19

When the promo ends, remaining balances jump to the regular APR (often 20%+). A single late payment can kill the promo early. Calendar the end date.

5. Don't Bother If You Can't Qualify Well

The best 0% offers need good credit. If you only qualify for short promos with high fees, the avalanche method or a consolidation loan may beat it — compare with our calculator.
