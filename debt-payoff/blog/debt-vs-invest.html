Should I Pay Off Debt or Invest?

By NAN Marketing Agency · October 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Extra money in hand, two good options. The answer is simpler than the internet makes it: compare guaranteed returns against probable ones.

The Simple Rule

Paying off a 24% credit card is a guaranteed 24% return — risk-free, tax-free. The stock market's long-run average is ~10% before inflation, with plenty of down years. So:

  • Debt above ~7–8% APR? Pay it off first. No investment reliably beats that.
  • Debt below ~5% APR? (like many mortgages) Investing starts to make mathematical sense — if you can handle the risk.
  • The gray zone (5–8%)? Split the difference or follow your gut; both choices are defensible.

The Big Exception: Free Money

Always grab your employer's 401(k) match first — it's an instant 50–100% return that beats any debt payoff. Contribute enough to get the full match, then attack high-interest debt with everything else.

Don't Forget the Buffer

Before either: keep that $1,000 mini emergency fund. Investing while one car repair away from new credit-card debt is building on sand.

The Bottom Line

Kill high-interest debt first (it's a guaranteed return), grab the 401(k) match no matter what, then invest. Run your payoff scenarios in our avalanche vs snowball simulator to see your finish line.
