Sora vs Runway: How Prompting Differs in 2026

Oct 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Copy-pasting the same prompt into Sora and Runway gives you two different videos — sometimes wildly different. The models have different architectures, training mixes and control surfaces. Here's how to write one master prompt and adapt it per model.

The core difference, simply

  • Sora excels at coherent long shots, complex scenes and natural physics — but offers fewer explicit controls. Your words carry more weight, so descriptive richness matters most.
  • Runway excels at stylization and offers granular controls (camera moves, motion brush, negative prompt fields). Structured, directed prompts with explicit camera language win here.

Same scene, two prompts

Master prompt (works as a base for both):

"Slow dolly in: a lone astronaut walking across a neon-lit rainy Tokyo street, reflections rippling in puddles. Mysterious atmosphere, cinematic photorealistic style."

Sora adaptation — expand the description, append negatives inline (no separate field):

"…Cinematic photorealistic, rain streaking past glowing signs, the astronaut's visor fogging slightly. Mysterious, filmic mood. Avoid: extra limbs, morphing face, watermark, flicker."

Runway adaptation — lean on camera controls + the negative field:

Main prompt: "Slow dolly in: a lone astronaut walking across a neon-lit rainy Tokyo street, reflections rippling. Mysterious, cinematic photorealistic."
Negative field: extra limbs, morphing face, watermark, flicker, blurry motion
+ set camera motion to a slow push-in for extra control.

Workflow: write once, adapt twice

  1. Write the master prompt with the 6-part formula using our Prompt Builder.
  2. Score it with the Prompt Grader — 75+ before adapting.
  3. Fork it: richer description + inline negatives for Sora; tighter prompt + negative field + camera controls for Runway.
  4. Generate both, compare, and keep the winner's phrasing as your template.
💡 Pro tip: Luma and Pika sit closer to Runway's control style (dedicated fields, camera moves), while Kling rewards Sora-style rich description. One master prompt, two adaptation patterns, covers all five major 2026 models.
