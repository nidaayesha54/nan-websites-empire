Camera Language for AI Video

📚 Lesson 2 of 3🟡 Intermediate⏱ 20 min

The fastest way to spot a beginner's AI video: the camera never moves. It's a static wide shot of something happening. Professionals direct the virtual camera with camera language — the shot vocabulary of filmmaking — and the difference is instant.

Lesson objectives

  • Master 14 camera moves and shots by name
  • Learn which moves fit which scenes (and which to avoid)
  • Combine camera + subject for directed, cinematic prompts

The core shot vocabulary

Camera moveWhat it doesBest for
Dolly in / outCamera glides toward or away from subjectReveals, emotional build-up
Tracking shotCamera moves alongside a moving subjectChases, journeys, sports
Aerial / drone sweepHigh sweeping view from aboveLandscapes, establishing shots
OrbitCamera circles around the subjectProduct shots, hero moments
Crane shotCamera rises or descends verticallyEpic scale, grand reveals
Low-angle tilt upLooking up at the subjectPower, awe, monsters
Macro close-upExtreme detail of a small thingTextures, insects, droplets
POV shotFirst-person viewpointImmersion, horror, action
HandheldSubtle natural shakeRealism, documentary feel
Whip panFast horizontal swingEnergy, transitions
TimelapseTime compressedClouds, cities, blooming flowers
Slow motionAction stretched in timeWater, hair, impacts
Static wide shotCamera locked, scene playsCalm scenes, dialogue-free moments
Rack focusFocus shifts between depthsReveals, layered scenes
💡 Pro tip: one camera move per prompt. "Dolly in while orbiting with a whip pan" confuses the model — it will pick one at random or freeze. Direct like a real DP: one move, executed well.

Matching moves to scenes

  • Product/ad shot → "slow orbit around the product, studio lighting, macro close-up on details"
  • Nature epic → "aerial drone sweep over misty mountains at dawn, timelapse clouds"
  • Character moment → "slow dolly in on her face, shallow depth of field, golden hour"
  • Action → "tracking shot alongside the runner, low angle, slow motion dust"
  • Horror/mystery → "POV shot creeping down the dark hallway, handheld shake, moonlight"

Common camera mistakes

  1. No camera direction at all — the model defaults to static. Always name a shot.
  2. Stacking moves — "dolly in, orbit, crane up, whip pan" = mush. Pick one.
  3. Wrong move for the mood — whip pans in a lullaby scene, timelapse on a portrait. Match energy.
  4. Forgetting the subject is also moving — camera move + subject action is the pro combo: "tracking shot alongside the galloping horse".
✏️ Exercise: Take the prompt "a lighthouse in a storm". Write three versions: one with an aerial drone sweep, one with a low-angle tilt up, one with a macro close-up of rain on the lamp glass. Generate all three — notice how the same subject becomes three different films.

Key takeaways

  • Always name a shot — static is the beginner's tell.
  • One camera move per prompt, matched to the scene's energy.
  • Camera move + subject action = the cinematic combo.
