Video Prompt Engineering 101: The 6-Part Formula

📚 Lesson 1 of 3🟢 Beginner⏱ 15 min

Video prompt engineering is the skill of writing text instructions that make AI video generators — Sora, Runway, Luma, Pika, Kling — render the scene in your head instead of a random approximation. In 2026 the models are powerful enough that the prompt, not the tool, is the difference between viral and forgettable.

Lesson objectives

  • Understand how text-to-video models interpret your words
  • Learn the 6-part prompt formula professionals use
  • Write your first pro-level prompt with the Prompt Builder

How AI video models read your prompt

Text-to-video models don't "understand" your prompt like a human director. They map words to visual patterns learned from millions of captioned clips. Three things matter:

  1. Early words weigh most. Put your subject and action in the first sentence. Details buried at word 80 barely register.
  2. Concrete beats abstract. "Melancholic" is vague; "a lone figure under a flickering streetlamp in rain" gives the model something to render.
  3. Filmmaking vocabulary works. Models trained on film captions respond to "dolly in", "golden hour", "shallow depth of field" far better than casual descriptions.

The 6-part prompt formula

Every strong AI video prompt contains six blocks, in this order:

#BlockExample
1Subject — who/what, specificallya tiny baby dragon with emerald scales
2Action — the motion verbsplashing playfully in a rain puddle
3Camera — shot + movementslow dolly in, low angle
4Lighting — light quality and timegolden hour sunlight, long shadows
5Style — the visual lookcinematic photorealistic, 8K detail
6Mood — the feelingjoyful, heartwarming atmosphere

Assembled:

"Slow dolly in, low angle: a tiny baby dragon with emerald scales splashing playfully in a rain puddle. Golden hour sunlight, long shadows. Joyful, heartwarming atmosphere. Rendered in cinematic photorealistic style, 8K detail."
💡 Pro tip: 30–60 words is the sweet spot. Under 15 words and the model improvises; over 90 and later details get ignored. Our Prompt Grader scores your prompt on all six blocks.

Before vs after

Beginner: "a dragon in the rain" → random dragon, random rain, static camera, flat light.

Engineered: "Macro close-up: a tiny baby dragon shaking rainwater off its emerald scales, droplets flying in slow motion. Neon city glow reflecting on wet pavement. Playful mood, Studio Ghibli-inspired anime style." → a directed shot with motion, light and style locked.

The 7th block: negative prompts

Professionals add a negative prompt — what the model must avoid: extra limbs, morphing face, watermark, blurry motion, flicker. This single line fixes most "cursed AI video" artifacts. Lesson 3 covers negative prompts in depth.

✏️ Exercise: Take one of your old prompts (or write "a cat on a beach"). Score it with the Prompt Grader, note the weakest block, then rebuild it with the Prompt Builder and grade again. Aim for 75+.

Key takeaways

  • Subject + action first — models weigh early words most.
  • Use the 6-part formula: subject, action, camera, lighting, style, mood.
  • 30–60 words, filmmaking vocabulary, concrete details.
  • Add a negative prompt to block artifacts.
