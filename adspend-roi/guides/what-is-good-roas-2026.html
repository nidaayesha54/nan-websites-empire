What Is a Good ROAS? 2026 Benchmarks by Platform & Industry

By AdSpendROI · Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

"Is my 3.2x ROAS good?" It's the most-asked question in paid advertising — and the honest answer is: it depends on your margins. This guide gives you real 2026 benchmarks by platform and industry, then shows you how to find the one number that actually matters: your target.

The short answer: 4:1 is the classic benchmark

The advertising industry has long cited 4:1 ($4 revenue per $1 ad spend) as a healthy ROAS. It's a reasonable rule of thumb — but treating it as gospel is dangerous. A 2x ROAS on a 90%-margin digital course is wildly profitable, while a 5x ROAS on a 15%-margin dropshipping product can still bleed cash.

The number that matters: YOUR break-even ROAS

Forget averages. Compute your break-even ROAS — the minimum ROAS where you don't lose money:

Break-even ROAS = Price ÷ (Price − Costs)

Example: a $60 product with $25 in costs needs $60 ÷ $35 = 1.71x to break even. Your target should sit 20–50% above break-even for real profit.

How to improve a weak ROAS

  1. Creative first. 80% of Meta/TikTok performance is creative. Test 3–5 new hooks per week.
  2. Raise AOV. Bundles, upsells, and free-shipping thresholds lift ROAS without touching ad costs.
  3. Fix the landing page. A 1% conversion-rate lift is a 1% ROAS lift — free money.
  4. Segment by intent. Separate brand, retargeting, and cold prospecting. Blended ROAS hides winners and losers.
  5. Kill losers fast. Set a kill rule (e.g., 2x your target CPA with no sales) and obey it.

FAQ

The commonly cited healthy benchmark is 4:1 — $4 of revenue for every $1 of ad spend. But good ROAS depends entirely on your margins and business model.

For high-margin digital products, 2x ROAS can be very profitable. For low-margin ecommerce with 20% margins, 2x ROAS loses money. Always compare against your break-even ROAS.
