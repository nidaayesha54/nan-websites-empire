ROAS vs ROI: What's the Difference for Advertisers?

By AdSpendROI · Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

ROAS and ROI get thrown around interchangeably in marketing meetings. They're not the same — and confusing them is how "profitable" campaigns quietly lose money. Here's the clean breakdown.

Definitions

  • ROAS (Return on Ad Spend) = Revenue from ads ÷ Ad spend. A gross metric. Example: $4,000 revenue on $1,000 spend = 4x ROAS (400%).
  • ROI (Return on Investment) = (Profit) ÷ Total investment × 100. A net metric. Example: $4,000 revenue, $1,000 ad spend, $2,000 product costs → $1,000 profit on $3,000 invested = 33% ROI.

The critical difference

ROAS ignores costs. ROI includes them. That's the whole story. A campaign can show a beautiful 4x ROAS and still lose money if margins are thin — because ROAS never asked what the product cost you.

A real example where ROAS lies

You sell a $50 product. Costs per order: $20 product + $8 shipping + $3 fees = $31. You spend $1,000 on ads and generate $4,000 in revenue (80 orders).

  • ROAS: $4,000 ÷ $1,000 = 4x — looks great!
  • Reality: 80 orders × $31 cost = $2,480 in costs. Profit = $4,000 − $1,000 − $2,480 = $520. ROI = $520 ÷ $3,480 = 15%.

Still profitable — but far less exciting than "4x ROAS" suggests. Now imagine costs of $38/order: profit drops to −$40. Same 4x ROAS, losing money.

When to use each

Use ROAS when…

  • Optimizing campaigns inside Meta, Google, or TikTok (platforms report it natively)
  • Comparing creative, audiences, or campaigns quickly
  • Setting automated bid targets (tROAS bidding)

Use ROI when…

  • Deciding whether to scale, keep, or kill a channel
  • Reporting to a boss, client, or investor
  • Comparing ads against other investments (email, SEO, inventory)

The practical workflow

  1. Calculate your break-even ROAS from your margins.
  2. Set your campaign ROAS target 20–50% above break-even.
  3. Optimize daily on ROAS inside the ad platform.
  4. Review weekly on profit/ROI — that's the number that pays the bills.

FAQ
