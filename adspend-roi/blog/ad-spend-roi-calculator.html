Ad Spend ROI Calculator: How to Measure True Profit From Your Ads

By AdSpendROI · Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

Your dashboard says 4x ROAS. Champagne? Not yet. ROAS tells you revenue per ad dollar — but ad spend ROI tells you whether the business actually made money. This guide shows you the formula, a worked example, and how to use our free calculators to get the real number in seconds.

Ad spend ROI vs ROAS: quick refresher

  • ROAS = Revenue ÷ Ad spend (gross — ignores costs)
  • Ad spend ROI = Profit ÷ Total investment × 100 (net — includes everything)

Read the full breakdown in our ROAS vs ROI guide.

The ad spend ROI formula

Ad Spend ROI = (Revenue − Ad Spend − Other Costs) ÷ (Ad Spend + Other Costs) × 100

"Other costs" = everything required to fulfill the orders the ads generated: COGS, packaging, shipping, payment fees, returns, and any agency/freelancer fees tied to the campaign.

Worked example

A skincare brand spends $2,000 on Meta ads, generating $8,000 in revenue (160 orders at $50).

Line itemAmount
Revenue$8,000
Ad spend−$2,000
Product cost (160 × $12)−$1,920
Shipping (160 × $5)−$800
Fees (160 × $2)−$320
Profit$2,960
  • ROAS: $8,000 ÷ $2,000 = 4x
  • Ad spend ROI: $2,960 ÷ $5,040 = 58.7%

Both positive — a genuinely good campaign. But notice how 4x ROAS "feels" like 300% returns while true ROI is 59%. That's the gap that matters.

What is a good ad spend ROI?

  • Below 0%: losing money — pause and diagnose.
  • 0–20%: technically profitable, but fragile. One bad week wipes it out.
  • 20–50%: healthy for most ecommerce and lead-gen businesses.
  • 50%+: strong — this is where scaling makes sense.

Three levers that lift ad spend ROI fastest

  1. Raise average order value. Bundles and post-purchase upsells add revenue with zero extra ad cost.
  2. Cut fulfillment costs. Renegotiate shipping, reduce packaging, lower return rates.
  3. Improve creative. On Meta and TikTok, creative is the targeting. Better hooks = cheaper clicks = higher ROI.

FAQ

Ad spend ROI = (Revenue − Ad spend − All other costs) ÷ (Ad spend + All other costs) × 100. Unlike ROAS, it accounts for product costs, shipping, and fees.

A positive ROI above 20–30% is generally healthy for paid advertising. Anything above 0% means the ads are profitable; below 0% means you're losing money.
