GTA 6 Crashing in the Prologue? Fixes That Actually Work

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S — thirteen years after GTA 5, and the biggest entertainment launch of the year. IGN's review, watched 7.44 million times on launch day, calls the game a masterpiece. But the opening hours have a real problem: GTA 6 keeps crashing and freezing in the prologue, on both PS5 and Xbox. Players report repeated crashes during the first missions, and because the game's autosave is unreliable, a crash can cost you real progress.

Rockstar has not commented on the crashes, and there is no single universal fix. What does exist is a short list of verified workarounds that have helped players get through the prologue — gathered from launch-day reporting and community fixes. Work through them in order, from easiest to most involved.

What Is Happening in the Prologue

The crashes hit during the opening missions — the game freezes, stutters, then closes, or the console locks up and needs a restart. It happens on base PS5 and on Xbox, and it is not tied to one mission: players report it at different points in the first hours. The frustrating part is what a crash costs you. GTA 6's autosave only fires after a mission ends, and the prologue never autosaves at all — so a crash mid-mission can send you back to the start of the prologue, or further than you would like.

That is why the first rule of surviving the prologue is not a technical fix at all: save manually. Pause, go to Game, then Save Game, after every mission — and keep two or three of your fifteen manual save slots in rotation. If the game crashes, you reload a save made minutes ago instead of hours ago. Streamer JasonTheWeen lost around ten hours of progress to crashes before the manual-save habit caught on, and that story is now the standard warning for every new player.

First: Install Every Update

Before anything else, make sure both the game and the console are fully updated. GTA 6 needs PS5 system software 26.07-14.23.00 to run properly at launch, and Rockstar has been shipping game updates through launch week. On PS5, highlight GTA 6 on the home screen, press Options, and choose Check for Update. Then check the console itself under Settings, System, System Software, and install anything waiting there. On Xbox, open My Games & Apps, go to Manage, then Updates, and install everything pending for GTA 6 and the console.

Updates matter more than they usually do here, because the launch-day build is the one with the most reported crashes. If you preloaded the game days before launch, your copy may not have the latest patch yet — the console does not always fetch it on its own.

Do a Full Restart — Not Rest Mode

After updating, do a full restart of the console. On PS5 that means powering the console fully off — not rest mode — waiting a few seconds, and turning it back on. On Xbox, restart the console from the power menu rather than putting it to sleep. Rest mode keeps the game suspended in memory, which is exactly the state a crash leaves behind; a full restart clears it.

This sounds too simple to work, but it is the step every other fix builds on — do it after every update, and again if the game crashes a second time.

PS5 Safe Mode: Clear Cache and Rebuild Database

If crashes continue on PS5, the next verified step is Safe Mode. Turn the console fully off, then press and hold the power button until you hear the second beep — about seven seconds — and connect your DualSense controller with a USB cable. In the Safe Mode menu, choose Clear Cache and Rebuild Database. This clears the console's temporary data and rebuilds the storage database, which fixes corrupted cache entries that can make games crash on load.

This step does not delete your games, your saves, or your settings. It takes a few minutes, and the console restarts when it is done. It is the standard PlayStation repair step for exactly this kind of problem, and it is the fix most often reported as working when updates and restarts have not.

Xbox: Quit the Game Fully Before Relaunching

On Xbox, the equivalent step is simpler: fully quit the game before you launch it again. Highlight GTA 6, press the Menu button, and choose Quit. Then relaunch from the home screen. Like rest mode on PS5, the Xbox quick-resume state can hold onto whatever was broken when the game crashed — quitting fully forces a clean start.

If the game crashes again right after a clean launch, restart the console itself as well, and double-check that both the game and the console have no pending updates.

Protect Your Progress While Crashes Continue

Until the crashes stop, treat manual saving as part of playing. Save after every mission, rotate two or three of your fifteen slots so a bad save never strands you, and save again before any mission that looks difficult. Remember that autosaves only appear in the Load Game menu — never in the Save Game menu — and that the prologue never triggers an autosave. The fifteen manual slots are your safety net; use them.

It is also worth keeping a separate save slot from before any mission you are about to start. If a mission softlocks or a crash corrupts your progress, that slot is the only way back without replaying hours.

What Not to Do

Do not delete the game and reinstall it as a first step. The install is 212 GB, the download takes hours, and a reinstall rarely fixes crash problems that updates and cache clears do not. Do not download "crash fix" tools or pay anyone for a "fixed" copy — there is no legitimate fix file, and those downloads are a classic malware trap.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the GTA 6 prologue crash a known issue?

Yes. Crashes and freezes in the opening prologue are widely reported on PS5 and Xbox since launch day, November 19, 2026. Rockstar has not commented publicly, and there is no universal fix yet — the workarounds in this guide are the best verified steps available.

Will clearing the cache delete my saves or games?

No. Clearing the cache and rebuilding the database in PS5 Safe Mode does not delete your games, your saves, or your settings. It only clears temporary data and rebuilds the storage index.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6's prologue crashes are real, annoying, and — for now — without an official fix. Update the game and your console to the latest versions, restart fully instead of using rest mode, clear the cache and rebuild the database on PS5, or fully quit and relaunch on Xbox. And while the crashes last, save manually after every mission: it is the one step that guarantees a crash costs you minutes, not hours.
