GTA 6 Safehouses: You Can't Buy One — Here's Where You Live

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and it changed one of the oldest rules in the series: you cannot buy a home. In every GTA before it, a safehouse was something you earned or bought — a place to save, change clothes, and store cars. In GTA 6, homes arrive through the story, one per chapter, and they leave the same way. Jason and Lucia are fugitives, and fugitives do not shop for houses. This guide covers the four homes the story gives you, what you can actually do inside them, and the one kind of property you can buy instead.

That matters because the old habit will waste your money. Players who go looking for a house to buy in GTA 6 will find nothing for sale — and the garages that are for sale are a different thing entirely. Knowing which is which saves you from hunting for a property screen that does not exist.

Why You Can't Buy a Home

Rob Nelson, who co-runs Rockstar North, said it plainly to the creators who visited the studio: you can buy different garages as you progress through the game, but you cannot buy safehouses in GTA 6, because it "doesn't make sense for basically what's happening with Jason and Lucia." They are fugitives. The police are actively looking for them. A fugitive does not sign a mortgage.

Instead, homes arrive when the plot hands you a key, marked with their own safehouse icon on the map, and they change as the story moves — much like the camps in Red Dead Redemption 2. You do not own your safety in this game. You borrow it, one chapter at a time, and when the chapter ends, the home goes with it.

The Four Homes, Chapter by Chapter

The first home is the stilted Keys house in Chapter 1. Jason starts out at Brian Heder's green wooden house on the shore in Key Lento, in the Leonida Keys, living there rent-free in return for helping with Brian's shakedowns. Trailer 2 opens with Jason on the roof, fixing shingles with a hammer. Lucia moves in after she gets out of prison, and the house becomes their base in the early story — a bed, a porch, a boat within reach, and a basketball hoop bolted under the deck that you can actually shoot hoops on. It is gone for good after the mission "Home Sweet Home".

The second home is the Starlet Motel room in Chapter 2, on Historic Route 628 outside Port Gellhorn — the room from the first trailer, where Jason and Lucia trade the game's most quoted exchange: "Trust?" — "Trust." It is a cheap, run-down roadside motel, and it fits: a low-profile hideout for two people on the run. You lose it in "Checking Out", the mission that ends the chapter.

The third home is the Bayside apartment in Chapter 3: apartment 201 at Bayside Apartments on Catalan Boulevard, lent by Boobie Ike in the mission "Welcome to Vice City". This is the flat fans called Lucia's apartment before launch — it turns out that was one home all along. The fourth home comes in Chapter 4, when the mission "Ocean Beach" moves the pair to the beach — which building it is has not been confirmed yet, so treat any name you see for it as unconfirmed.

What You Can Do at Home

A safehouse in GTA 6 is more than a save point. Jason and Lucia share one home, and each keeps their own schedule inside it — walk in and you may find your partner asleep, cooking, or already out. You trim your own hair and beard there, and any length is available on the spot; the house handles maintenance, while the barber handles fashion. The fridge is stocked with consumables — during one hands-on session, Rob Nelson grabbed an apple, ate it for a health tick, and binned the core. Personal vehicles are kept at the safehouse, and both characters can own several; a stolen car joins them only after you pay to register it.

The home is also where the money rules live. Before the BuckMe bank account opens, spare cash goes under the floorboards in Jason's bedroom — and pocket cash is lost when you die or get arrested, so the floorboards are not decoration. The safehouse sits inside the world rather than behind a loading screen: you walk in through the front door, the street keeps living outside the windows while you lie low, and you can decorate the place while you are there.

Garages Are the Only Thing for Sale

If safehouses are borrowed, garages are the purchase that matters. Finish "Daily Driver" in Chapter 2 and Vice City Real Estate opens on the in-game phone, with eight garages for sale. A garage is cheap storage where a stolen car waits until you can afford to register it — and it is the only property in the game with a price tag. Two more come with the editions: the Shore Court Garage in Ocean Beach, a one-car garage with a weapon locker and a stolen-goods drop from the pre-order Vintage Vice City Pack, and the Paradise Garage in Watson Bay, built for the '67 Vapid Dominator Buggy, from the Ultimate Edition.

The rule is simple to remember: homes come from the story, garages come from the phone. Do not look for a house to buy, and do not skip the garages — in a game where your cars are your money, a garage is the one safe place to keep them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you buy a safehouse in GTA 6?

No. Safehouses arrive through the story, one per chapter, and cannot be bought. Rockstar's Rob Nelson confirmed it: buying a home would not make sense for two fugitives on the run.

How many safehouses are there in GTA 6?

Four are known, one for each of the first four chapters: the Keys house, the Starlet Motel room, the Bayside apartment, and the Ocean Beach home. Each one is lost when its chapter ends.

Where do you save the game in GTA 6?

At a safehouse, with a manual save — Pause, Game, Save Game — using one of fifteen manual save slots. The autosave only fires after a mission ends, so save by hand after every mission.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6 gives you four homes and sells you none of them: a stilted house in the Keys, a motel room in Port Gellhorn, an apartment in Bayside, and a beach home in Chapter 4 — each one borrowed from the story, each one gone when the chapter ends. Live in them, trim your hair, eat from the fridge, stash your cash under the floorboards, and buy garages instead: in Leonida, a garage is the only property with your name on it.
