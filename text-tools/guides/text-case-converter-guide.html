The 6 Case Styles Explained

1. UPPERCASE

ALL LETTERS CAPITALIZED. Use sparingly — for short warnings, acronyms (NASA, FBI), or button labels. Avoid full sentences: studies show all-caps text is harder to read and feels like shouting. On social media, all-caps posts get lower engagement.

2. lowercase

all letters small. Common in modern branding (think "iphone" style aesthetics), URLs, email addresses, and hashtags. Never use for formal writing.

3. Title Case

Every Major Word Capitalized. The standard for headlines, blog post titles, book titles, and YouTube video titles. AP style says to capitalize words of 4+ letters plus all nouns, verbs, and adjectives.

4. Sentence case

Only the first word capitalized, like a normal sentence. Best for body text, meta descriptions, and email subject lines — it reads most naturally.

5. aLtErNaTiNg case

Random-looking mixed case for memes and playful social posts. Fun, but never professional.

6. iNVERSE case

Flips every letter's case — mostly a novelty, occasionally useful for fixing accidental caps-lock typing.

Case Style Cheat Sheet

  • Blog headlines: Title Case
  • Email subjects: Sentence case (higher open rates)
  • SEO titles: Title Case or Sentence case — Google doesn't care, but readers click Title Case more
  • Social media: Sentence case for posts, Title Case for announcements
  • Buttons/CTAs: Short UPPERCASE ("BUY NOW") or Title Case ("Buy Now")

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Title Case?
Title Case capitalizes the first letter of every major word: 'The Quick Brown Fox'. It is standard for headlines, book titles, and blog post titles.
Is UPPERCASE bad for SEO?
Google treats uppercase and lowercase the same for ranking, but all-caps headlines get fewer clicks and look spammy to readers.
