7 Dog Nutrition Myths Busted — What Science Actually Says

Dog nutrition is drowning in confident claims from people selling dog food. Here are seven of the most persistent myths — and what veterinary nutrition science actually says about each.

Myth 1: "Grain-free diets are healthier"

Reality: There's no evidence most dogs benefit from grain-free diets. Dogs digest cooked grains like rice and barley perfectly well, and the FDA has investigated a potential link between grain-free diets (especially those heavy in peas and lentils) and dilated cardiomyopathy (DCM), a serious heart condition. Unless your vet diagnoses a genuine grain allergy — which is rare — grains are fine.

Myth 2: "Dogs are carnivores and need meat-only diets"

Reality: Dogs are omnivores. Over thousands of years alongside humans, dogs evolved extra copies of the gene for digesting starch. A meat-only diet is actually deficient in several nutrients dogs need. Balanced commercial foods include plant ingredients for good reason.

Myth 3: "By-products are junk filler"

Reality: "By-product" sounds gross, but it mostly means organ meats — liver, kidney, spleen — which are nutrient-dense and are exactly what wild canids eat first. Named by-products (e.g., "chicken by-products") from reputable manufacturers are nutritious. The vague unnamed ones ("meat by-products") are where quality gets murky.

Myth 4: "Raw diets are what nature intended"

Reality: Raw diets carry documented bacterial risks — Salmonella and E. coli — for both dogs and the humans handling the food. Studies consistently find most homemade raw recipes are nutritionally unbalanced. "Natural" doesn't mean safe or complete; most veterinary nutritionists recommend against raw feeding.

Myth 5: "Table scraps are fine as long as the dog likes them"

Reality: Occasional plain scraps are okay, but regular table scraps unbalance the diet and add stealth calories — a leading cause of canine obesity. Worse, many human foods are toxic (see our complete toxic foods list). Keep extras under 10% of daily calories with our treat calculator.

Myth 6: "The first ingredient tells you everything"

Reality: Ingredients are listed by pre-cooking weight, so "fresh chicken" first looks great but is mostly water. After cooking, its real contribution drops down the list. The AAFCO adequacy statement and guaranteed analysis matter far more than ingredient order theater. (More in our dog food buying guide.)

Myth 7: "Expensive food is always better"

Reality: Price tracks marketing budgets more than nutrition. Many mid-range foods meet identical AAFCO nutrient profiles as premium bags. Judge a food by its adequacy statement, your dog's condition (coat, stools, energy, weight), and your budget — not the price tag.

The takeaway: Good dog nutrition is boring — a complete-and-balanced food, fed in the right amount (see our portion calculator), with treats kept in check. The myths are exciting; the science works.
