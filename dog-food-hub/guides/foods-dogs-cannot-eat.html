Foods Dogs Cannot Eat: The Complete List

In this guide:
  • Extremely dangerous — can be fatal
  • Serious — vet visit needed
  • Mild — upset stomach likely
  • Surprising safe foods
  • What to do in an emergency

Dogs will eat almost anything — that's the problem. Many everyday human foods are toxic to dogs, and some can kill even in small amounts. Bookmark this page, and share it with everyone in your household (kids and guests are the usual culprits).

🔴 Extremely Dangerous — Can Be Fatal

Call your vet or poison control immediately if your dog eats any of these — don't wait for symptoms.

FoodWhy it's deadly
Xylitol (sugar-free gum, candy, some peanut butters, toothpaste)Causes a massive insulin release → liver failure. Even small amounts can kill a small dog.
Chocolate (especially dark/baking)Theobromine toxicity: vomiting, seizures, heart failure. Darker = deadlier.
Grapes & raisinsCan cause sudden kidney failure — even a few grapes. Toxic dose is unpredictable.
Macadamia nutsWeakness, vomiting, tremors, hyperthermia; can be fatal in severe cases.
AlcoholSame effects as in humans but far faster: coma, respiratory failure, death.
Cooked bonesSplinter into sharp shards that can perforate the stomach or intestines.
Onion & garlic (all forms: raw, cooked, powdered)Destroy red blood cells → anemia. Toxicity builds up over repeated small doses.

🟠 Serious — Vet Visit Needed

  • Caffeine (coffee, tea, energy drinks) — restlessness, rapid heart rate, tremors
  • Fatty foods (bacon grease, fried foods) — can trigger pancreatitis, a painful and dangerous inflammation
  • Raw dough with yeast — expands in the stomach and produces alcohol as it ferments
  • Avocado — persin can cause vomiting and diarrhea (the pit is also a choking hazard)
  • Cherries, peaches, plums — pits contain cyanide compounds and can block the intestines
  • Salt in large amounts — salt poisoning from play dough, soy sauce packets, or rock salt
  • Nutmeg — hallucinations, increased heart rate, seizures in larger amounts
  • Raw potatoes & green tomatoes — contain solanine, toxic in quantity

🟡 Mild — Likely Stomach Upset

  • Dairy (milk, cheese, ice cream) — most adult dogs are lactose intolerant; expect gas and diarrhea
  • Corn on the cob — the corn is fine, but the cob can cause a life-threatening intestinal blockage
  • Spicy foods — stomach pain, gas, diarrhea
  • Too many fatty table scraps — even non-toxic scraps in quantity cause vomiting and pancreatitis risk
  • Raw eggs / raw fish — bacterial risk and (in fish) potential parasites; occasional raw egg is debated, but cooked is safer

🟢 Surprising Safe Foods (in moderation)

Not everything from your plate is off-limits. These are generally safe as occasional treats (remember the 10% treat rule):

  • Plain cooked chicken, turkey, or lean beef
  • Carrots, green beans, cucumber, and plain pumpkin (great low-calorie treats)
  • Apple slices (no seeds), blueberries, watermelon (no seeds/rind), bananas
  • Plain cooked rice and plain peanut butter (xylitol-free — always check the label)
  • Plain cooked eggs

🚨 What to Do If Your Dog Eats Something Toxic

  1. Stay calm and act fast. Note what was eaten, how much, and when.
  2. Call your vet immediately — or the ASPCA Animal Poison Control Center at (888) 426-4435 (a consultation fee may apply).
  3. Do NOT induce vomiting unless a vet specifically tells you to — it can make some poisons worse.
  4. Bring the packaging to the vet so they know the exact ingredients and amounts.
Prevention beats treatment: keep purses, backpacks, and counters clear; teach a solid "leave it" and "drop it"; and make sure guests and kids know the rules — most poisonings happen when someone shares "just a bite."

Everyday Items People Forget Are Dangerous

Beyond the food list, a few household items catch owners off guard. Sugar-free products beyond gum — mints, protein bars, and even some medications — often contain xylitol, the single most dangerous sweetener for dogs. Compost piles grow molds that produce tremorgenic mycotoxins, which cause seizures. Cocoa mulch in gardens contains the same theobromine as chocolate. Unbaked bread dough was listed above, but it deserves repeating: the ASPCA receives a spike of these calls every holiday baking season. And nicotine products — cigarettes, vapes, and pouches — are rapidly absorbed and can be fatal to small dogs. When in doubt, assume it's unsafe until your vet says otherwise.

A Final Word: When "Wait and See" Is Wrong

The most dangerous reaction to a poisoning scare is hoping symptoms never appear. Many toxins — grapes, xylitol, antifreeze — cause irreversible organ damage hours before visible symptoms show up. By the time a dog looks sick, treatment is harder, more expensive, and less likely to succeed. If your dog may have eaten something on the red or orange lists above, call your vet right away even if your dog seems perfectly fine. A phone call costs nothing; waiting can cost everything.

Note: Feeding guidelines are estimates — consult your vet for your dog's specific needs. If you suspect poisoning, contact your vet or poison control immediately.
