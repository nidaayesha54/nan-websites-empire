GTA 6 Clips: Record on PS5 and Xbox Without a Capture Card

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and within hours the feeds were full of clips: heists gone wrong, stunt jumps, sunsets over Vice City. Every one of those clips was recorded on a console, and most of them were recorded without any extra hardware at all. The PS5 and the Xbox both have capture built in, and if you know which buttons to press, you can save almost anything that happens in Leonida — then get it onto your phone and turn it into a video worth sharing.

That matters because the obvious way to record gameplay — a capture card between the console and a computer — costs money, needs cables, and is overkill for most players. The consoles already record everything you do in the background. The trick is knowing how to keep the moment you want, how long each console will save, and what Rockstar's own rules say about posting the footage once you have it.

Recording on PS5: The Create Button Does Everything

On a PS5, everything starts with the Create button on the DualSense controller, the small button to the left of the touchpad. Press it while GTA 6 is running and you get three choices: Take Screenshot, Start New Recording, and Save Recent Gameplay. Save Recent Gameplay is the one you will use most, because the console records your play in the background all the time — you can save anything from the last 15 seconds up to the last hour, depending on how you set it up. Start New Recording begins a fresh recording that can run for up to one hour.

Set it up before you need it. Go to Settings, then Captures and Broadcasts, then Captures, and choose your video clip format — including whether your microphone or voice chat audio is included, which you need to decide before you record, not after. You can also change what a short press of the Create button does, under Shortcuts for Create Button: Standard opens the menu, while Easy Screenshots and Easy Video Clips capture with a single press or a double press. Saved clips live in the Media Gallery on the PS5 home screen, and you can copy them to an external USB drive, or turn on Auto-Upload so they appear in the PlayStation App on your phone.

Recording on Xbox: The Share Button and the Guide

On Xbox Series X|S, the quickest method is the Share button on the controller: one press takes a screenshot, and pressing and holding it records a clip of what just happened. For more control, press the Xbox button to open the guide, then press X to save the last 30 seconds, or press View to choose a longer stretch, up to the last 2 minutes. For a planned recording, open the guide and go to Capture and share, then Start recording — those clips can run up to 10 minutes on the console's internal storage, or up to 1 hour on an external drive, depending on the resolution and how much space is free.

The settings live under Profile and system, then Settings, then Preferences, then Capture and share, where you pick the clip resolution and where captures are saved. To capture in 4K HDR, the console has to be outputting 4K, and HDR captures follow from HDR being on. Captures upload to the Xbox network automatically, and you can pull them down in the Xbox app. One note from Microsoft's capture page is worth reading before you start: saving and sharing screenshots on Xbox consoles requires Game Pass, and game clips cannot be captured while streaming with remote play.

What Rockstar Allows — and What Gets Taken Down

Recording the clip is only half the job. Posting it is governed by Rockstar's policy on posting copyrighted Rockstar material, last updated on January 10, 2025, and it draws a clear line. Non-commercial fan videos of your own play are allowed. What gets a takedown notice is footage from before the game's release date — Rockstar says any posting of in-game footage from copies obtained before the release date will be taken down, regardless of how the copy was obtained, including early unboxing videos — and videos that show only isolated cutscenes or the ending.

There is one more limit, and it comes from the consoles rather than Rockstar. Sony's capture guide says plainly that some scenes are blocked and will not be included in your video clips, and each game decides which scenes those are. As of launch, neither Rockstar, PlayStation nor Xbox had said which GTA 6 scenes, if any, are blocked — so treat the console behaviour as the general rule rather than a promise for this game. If a moment you recorded comes out with a gap in it, a blocked scene is the likely reason, not a broken console.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a capture card to record GTA 6?

No. The PS5 Create button and the Xbox Share button record clips with nothing extra plugged in. A capture card only makes sense if you want longer recordings routed to a computer for editing, overlays or livestream production.

How long a clip can each console save?

On PS5, Save Recent Gameplay keeps from the last 15 seconds up to the last hour, and a new recording runs up to 1 hour. On Xbox, the guide saves the last 30 seconds with X or up to the last 2 minutes with View, and a started recording runs up to 10 minutes on internal storage or 1 hour on an external drive.

Can I post my GTA 6 clips online?

Yes, for non-commercial fan videos of your own play. Rockstar's policy takes down footage from before the release date and videos that show only isolated cutscenes or the ending.

The Bottom Line

Recording GTA 6 costs nothing and needs no extra hardware: press Create on PS5 or Share on Xbox, save the moment, and pull the clip onto your phone through the PlayStation App or the Xbox app. Set your capture settings before the big moment, not during it — and keep Rockstar's rules in mind before you post, because the clip you can record is not always the clip you can share.
