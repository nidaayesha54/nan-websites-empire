How to Apply for UPSC Exams Online 2026

The complete evergreen guide: OTR registration, filling the application form, fee payment, DAF for the mains stage, admit card download, and mistakes to avoid.

Where UPSC Applications Happen

Every UPSC examination — Civil Services, Engineering Services, NDA, CDS, CAPF and others — is applied for online only through the official portals: upsconline.gov.in and upsc.gov.in. There is no offline application route. The two concepts every aspirant must master are OTR (One Time Registration) and, for exams with a mains stage, the DAF (Detailed Application Form). This guide walks you through the full process for 2026.

Step 1: Complete Your OTR (One Time Registration)

OTR is the foundation of the UPSC online system. You register once with your personal details and then reuse that profile for every examination you apply to. It is mandatory — you cannot open an examination application form without completing OTR first.

1
Visit upsconline.gov.in and open the One Time Registration section.
2
Enter your details — name, date of birth, a valid email ID and mobile number. Keep these accessible; OTP-based verification uses them.
3
Verify and submit — complete any OTP or verification steps and save your OTR credentials safely. Do this well before any notification's last date.

Step 2: Fill the Examination Application Form

Once the notification for your chosen exam is out (for example, the UPSC ESE 2027 notification covering 480 engineering posts), log in with your OTR profile during the application window and fill the form carefully:

  • Personal & educational details: Enter exactly as per your certificates. Name spelling, date of birth and category must match your documents — mismatches cause problems at the DAF and document-verification stages.
  • Photo and signature: Upload a recent photograph and signature strictly as per the official specifications given in the notification (format, size and background). Rejections at this step are common and entirely avoidable.
  • Exam centre preference: Choose your preferred cities in the order requested; centres are allotted on a first-come basis in many exams, so apply early.

Step 3: Pay the Application Fee

The application fee is paid online through the portal during submission. Fee amounts and exemption rules differ by examination and category — check the official notification for the exact fee rather than relying on memory of previous years. After successful payment, save or print the confirmation page; it is your proof of submission. If the payment status shows as pending, wait for confirmation rather than paying again — duplicate charges are harder to resolve.

Step 4: Submit the DAF (for Mains-Stage Exams)

For examinations like Civil Services and Engineering Services, qualifying the preliminary stage is only half the process. Qualified candidates must then file the Detailed Application Form (DAF) online before the mains:

  • The DAF collects full personal information, educational history, employment details, service or cadre preferences, and other particulars the Commission needs for the mains and interview stages.
  • Fill it with the same care as the original application — the DAF feeds directly into the interview panel's briefing.
  • Watch the official portal for the DAF window; missing it means losing your mains eligibility even after qualifying the prelims.

Step 5: Download the Admit Card

The e-admit card (hall ticket) is released on upsc.gov.in and upsconline.gov.in, usually 2–3 weeks before the examination. No physical admit card is sent by post. Download it early, check your name, exam city, date and shift, and carry a valid photo ID along with the printout on exam day.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Leaving OTR to the last day: Portal congestion near deadlines is real. Complete OTR as soon as you decide to appear.
  • Name/DOB mismatches: Keep application entries identical to your matriculation certificate and ID documents.
  • Wrong photo/signature specs: Follow the notification's specifications exactly — not "close enough".
  • Ignoring the DAF: Prelims qualifiers who miss the DAF window cannot sit the mains.
  • Trusting third-party sites for dates: Always confirm notifications, dates and fees on upsc.gov.in or upsconline.gov.in. Track new openings on the NAN Jobs Portal homepage.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is UPSC OTR registration?
One Time Registration (OTR) on the UPSC online portal upsconline.gov.in creates your permanent profile once; you then use it to apply for every UPSC examination without re-registering.
Can I apply for a UPSC exam without OTR?
No. OTR is mandatory on the UPSC online portal — you must complete it before you can fill any examination application form.
What is DAF in UPSC exams?
The Detailed Application Form (DAF) is filled online by candidates who qualify the preliminary/similar stage, before the mains examination, with full personal, educational and service-preference details.
How do I pay the UPSC application fee?
The fee is paid online through the UPSC portal during application submission. Check the official notification for the exact fee and exemption rules for your category.
Where do I download the UPSC admit card?
The e-admit card is released on upsc.gov.in and upsconline.gov.in, roughly 2–3 weeks before the exam; no physical hall ticket is sent. Download it early and verify all details.
