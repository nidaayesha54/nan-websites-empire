What Are Termination Benefits in Malaysia?

Termination benefits (also called retrenchment or severance benefits) are the minimum lump-sum payments an employer must make when an employee's contract of service ends for reasons other than misconduct, resignation or retirement. They are set out in the Employment (Termination and Lay-Off Benefits) Regulations 1980, made under the Employment Act 1955.

The Statutory Formula

Benefit = (Basic Monthly Salary ÷ 26) × Days' Wages per Year × Years of Service
10 days per year for <2 years · 15 days per year for 2–5 years · 20 days per year for 5+ years.
An incomplete year is counted pro-rata to the nearest month.

Worked Example

Basic salary RM 4,000, 6 years of service: daily wage RM 4,000 ÷ 26 = RM 153.85; tier = 20 days (5+ years); benefit = RM 153.85 × 20 × 6 = RM 18,461.54 — before any notice pay in lieu.

Key Rules

  • Applies after 12 months of continuous service with the same employer.
  • Based on the ordinary rate of pay — basic salary ÷ 26; allowances are generally excluded.
  • Not payable for dismissal on misconduct grounds, voluntary resignation, retirement, or when the contract is renewed on equal or better terms.
  • Employers must give the statutory notice period (4, 6 or 8 weeks by tenure) or pay in lieu.
  • For retrenchment, employers must notify the Labour Office (JTKSM) at least 30 days ahead and follow the LIFO (last-in-first-out) principle within each job category.
  • Compensation for loss of employment is generally income-tax exempt up to RM 10,000 per completed year of service.

FAQs

Does the benefit use gross salary or basic salary?

The law uses the ordinary rate of pay: basic monthly salary divided by 26. Allowances, overtime and bonuses are excluded.

I worked 3 years and 7 months — how is it counted?

The incomplete year is pro-rated to the nearest month: 3 + 7/12 = 3.58 years at the 15-day tier (2–5 years).

Do foreign workers get termination benefits?

The Regulations cover employees under a contract of service in Malaysia. In practice, foreign workers are typically retrenched first (before locals) under the foreign-first rule, and entitlements depend on their contract and EA 1955 coverage.

Can my employer pay more than the minimum?

Yes — the Regulations set the floor. Many companies offer enhanced VSS/MSS packages above the statutory rate, especially for senior staff.

What if I was retrenched but not paid?

File a complaint with the nearest JTKSM (Department of Labour). Unfair dismissal claims can be referred to the Industrial Court.
