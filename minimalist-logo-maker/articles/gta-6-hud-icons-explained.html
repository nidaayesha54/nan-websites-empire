GTA 6 HUD: Every Icon on Screen, Explained

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and it rebuilt the heads-up display from the corners inward. The bottom-left arc that tracked health and armor in GTA V is gone, replaced by slim horizontal bars in the opposite corner — and most of the screen stays empty until the game decides you need information. If you have ever stared at a new icon and wondered what it means, this guide is the full legend: every bar, star, evidence icon and minimap element, and when each one appears.

The first rule of the new HUD is that it hides. Walk down a street with nothing happening and the screen holds nothing but the minimap. No health bar, no ammo counter, no wanted stars. The interface switches on when it is needed and fades the moment the need passes. A firefight shows health and focus while energy disappears; behind the wheel of a car, none of the bars remain, only the minimap and the ammo count. Once you know that, the screen stops feeling empty and starts feeling honest: if something is showing, it matters right now.

The three bars, top left

Three bars live in the top left. Pink with a heart is health — and it carries a small vertical notch about a quarter of the way along, which the footage does not explain: it could be a regeneration threshold or a segment boundary. Mint with a lightning bolt is stamina, which drains wherever effort happens — running, swimming, cycling, even paddling counts as effort. Purple with an eye is focus, the heir of Dead Eye from Red Dead Redemption: slow-down and target highlighting, topped up through side activities. Health and stamina gains fade if you stop training, while focus gains are permanent. While swimming or diving, an oxygen meter joins them. None of these bars are permanently on screen — cruise around with no threat nearby and the meters vanish entirely, leaving just the minimap.

Six wanted stars and the evidence icons

The top right corner gathers three blocks at once: the wanted stars, the evidence icons beneath them, and the ammo count next to a weapon silhouette. The wanted scale has six stars, where GTA V stopped at five, and they render in at least four states: grey and unfilled, hollow white, filled white and red. Rob Nelson explained the hollow white ones to IGN: the police know a crime took place but do not know who committed it. Below the stars sits a row of round icons, and it is the most consequential change in the HUD. Each icon is a trait the police are hunting you by. Six kinds turned up across the showcase: a security camera, a paired silhouette, a clothes hanger, a face, a pistol and a car. Fix each one and you break the trail — change your outfit to clear the clothes icon, change your hairstyle or wear a mask to beat the face icon, switch vehicles or repaint at a Pay 'n' Spray to clear the car icon. A hat or glasses is not enough; the game wants a full outfit change, and it suggests keeping spare clothes in your vehicle's trunk.

The minimap and the money

The minimap sits in the bottom left, and behind the wheel it takes the shape of a rounded rectangle rather than a circle. Your route to a waypoint is drawn as a pink line, and a live distance readout ticks down as you drive, dropping from something like 1.62 miles to 0.80 as you close in. A small arrow inside a black circle marks where you currently are. Police pursuits change it completely: cops no longer appear as dots on the minimap. Instead, while police are searching for you, the whole map tints red-pink to mark the boundaries of the search perimeter, so you can see how far you need to travel to break out of it — and the focus ability can reveal nearby officers on the minimap when you need them. Money gets its own corner too: the cash wallet shows the physical money you are carrying, while BuckMe shows the money stored in your bank account, and the weapon wheel now has its own items tab for healing and utility items. The bottom centre and right edge hold contextual button prompts and stacked action verbs that appear only when the situation calls for them.

One more piece of the interface only shows up when you need it: the wardrobe you can reach mid-firefight. Changing your outfit during a chase is how you clear the clothes evidence icon, and the game lets you do it without finding a shop — the same screen that tracks your clothes, your vehicle and your face lets you swap the evidence away. The bottom centre and right edge hold contextual button prompts and stacked action verbs that appear only when the situation calls for them, such as entering a vehicle or starting a conversation, and they vanish again when the moment passes. That is the design philosophy of the whole HUD: everything is hidden until it matters, and then it is right where your thumb already is.

One more honest note: some of the interface is still unconfirmed. Rockstar has not fully explained the Police Profiling and Criminal Profile systems, several reported HUD customization options remain unconfirmed, and the existence of a dedicated 2D/3D minimap toggle is unconfirmed — a 3D minimap has been shown, but Rockstar has not confirmed a toggle for it. Positions and labels could still shift, and the names fans use for the icons are not Rockstar's own terminology, so the exact names and functions could differ in the final game. What is confirmed is the layout: three bars, six stars, evidence icons, a minimap that tints red when you are hunted, and a screen that stays empty until it has something to tell you.

Learn the legend once, and the screen that looked empty starts talking to you.
