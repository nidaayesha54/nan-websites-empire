5 AI Editing Tricks That Cut My Edit Time in Half

October 2026 · 5 min read

I timed it: a standard 8-minute talking-head video took me 4 hours to edit manually last year. With these five AI features, the same video takes under 2 hours — and the quality is higher, not lower. Here are the tricks ranked by actual time saved.

1. Text-based cutting (saves ~60 min)

The single biggest upgrade. Instead of scrubbing a timeline, you delete sentences in the transcript and the video cuts itself. Removing a rambling 40-second tangent becomes a two-second highlight-and-delete. Descript and Premiere's text panel both do this well. Tip: do your structural cuts in transcript view first, then switch to the timeline for fine-tuning — never the other way around.

2. Silence & filler removal (saves ~35 min)

AI silence detection finds every pause over your threshold and every "um" and "uh" in one pass. Set the threshold to 0.5s, run it, then skim for cuts that feel too tight — about 10% need restoring. What used to be an hour of razor-blade tedium is now a 5-minute review.

3. Auto-reframe for multi-platform (saves ~25 min)

Shooting once and publishing to YouTube (16:9), TikTok (9:16), and Instagram (1:1) used to mean three manual reframes. AI subject-tracking reframe handles 90% of shots; you only fix the ones where the framing drifts. Tip: shoot with extra headroom — it gives the AI room to track without cropping faces.

4. Caption presets (saves ~20 min)

Build one caption style preset — font, size, stroke, karaoke highlight color, safe-zone position — and apply it to every project in one click. Consistency is a branding win and a time win. Pair it with our caption timing calculator when you are working from a script.

5. Template timelines (saves ~15 min per video)

Save a project template with your intro, lower thirds, music bed, caption track, and export settings. Every new video starts from the template instead of a blank timeline. This is not strictly "AI," but it compounds with the AI features above — the whole pipeline becomes assembly, not construction.

The honest caveat

AI saves time on mechanics, not decisions. You still need to watch the final cut as a viewer and fix pacing, hooks, and story — the parts no model can judge for you. Build that review into your process with our editing checklist generator.
