AI Video Editing Workflow: 5 Phases from Script to Publish

Updated October 2026 · 8 min read

Tools do not make creators fast — repeatable processes do. AI video editors remove the tedious 80% of editing, but without a workflow you still lose hours to decision fatigue. This is the 5-phase process that works for solo creators across YouTube, TikTok, and client work. Generate a project-specific version anytime with our editing checklist generator.

  1. Phase 1 — Script & plan
  2. Phase 2 — Capture clean
  3. Phase 3 — AI rough cut
  4. Phase 4 — Human polish
  5. Phase 5 — Publish & package

Phase 1 — Script & plan (before you press record)

Every painful edit starts with a weak plan. Write the script with the hook first: the first 5 seconds decide retention, so open with a question, a bold claim, or mid-action footage — never an intro. Note B-roll moments, on-screen text, and cut points directly in the script. For shorts, plan 3 value beats and nothing more. A 20-minute planning session saves a 2-hour edit.

Phase 2 — Capture clean (garbage in, garbage out)

AI cannot fix bad source material. Record in the highest resolution your export needs, lock exposure and white balance, and treat audio as the priority: a $20 lav mic beats a $2,000 camera with bad sound. Record 10 seconds of room tone for noise removal, and clap or mark takes so the transcript stage finds them easily. Clean capture is the cheapest "AI" upgrade there is.

Phase 3 — AI rough cut (let the machine do the boring part)

This is where AI earns its keep. Run this sequence in order:

  1. Transcribe & text-edit: delete filler words, false starts, and dead takes by deleting transcript text.
  2. Silence removal: auto-cut pauses over 0.5s, then skim the timeline for anything the AI was too aggressive with.
  3. Auto captions: generate, review names and jargon, then style. See the caption workflow.
  4. Smart assembly: auto-reframe for 9:16, auto B-roll suggestions, beat-sync for music-driven cuts.

A talking-head video that took 3 hours to rough-cut manually now takes about 20 minutes.

Phase 4 — Human polish (the 20% AI cannot do)

AI handles mechanics; humans handle meaning. Watch the rough cut at 1.5x speed on your phone — the device your audience uses — and fix what the AI missed:

  • Pacing: no shot longer than 4 seconds in short-form, 8 seconds in long-form without a visual change.
  • The hook: rewatch the first 3 seconds as a stranger. If you would scroll, reshoot or re-cut it.
  • Audio levels: normalize to -14 LUFS for YouTube, keep music 12–15 dB under voice.
  • Color: fix white balance first, grade second. Natural skin tones beat trendy LUTs.
  • Retention graph check: every dip is a cut the audience rejected — tighten or remove.

Phase 5 — Publish & package (the video is half the product)

The upload package matters as much as the edit: SEO title with the keyword up front, a description with timestamps and links, a thumbnail readable at 10% size, chapters for long-form, and platform-native exports (9:16, 1:1, 16:9 from one timeline). Schedule at your audience's peak hours, reply to early comments within the hour, and archive the project with clear labels — today's B-roll is next month's content.
