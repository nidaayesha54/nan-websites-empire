How to Auto-Caption Videos: The 2026 Workflow

Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

Around 85% of social video is watched without sound. Captions are no longer a nice-to-have — they are the difference between a scroll-past and a watch-through. The good news: AI transcription made captioning a five-minute job. This guide walks through the full workflow: transcribe, review, style, and burn in.

In this guide
  1. Step 1 — Transcribe with AI
  2. Step 2 — Review the transcript
  3. Step 3 — Time the captions
  4. Step 4 — Style for retention
  5. Step 5 — Burn in or export SRT

Step 1 — Transcribe with AI

Upload your video to any modern AI editor and hit auto-caption or transcribe. CapCut, Descript, Premiere Pro, and Kapwing all generate word-level timestamps in under a minute for a typical short. Accuracy on clear English speech now exceeds 95% — but "exceeds 95%" still means several errors per video, which brings us to step 2.

Step 2 — Review the transcript (the step everyone skips)

AI consistently mishears three things: names and brands, numbers and dates, and domain jargon. Read the transcript once at 1.5x speed while watching. Fixing five words now beats a comment section roasting your "captions" later. This single review pass is what separates professional-looking captions from sloppy ones.

Step 3 — Time the captions for readability

Good caption timing follows three rules:

  • 5–7 words per caption on mobile — never a wall of text.
  • Minimum 0.8 seconds on screen — faster than that and viewers cannot read it.
  • Break at natural pauses — split at sentence ends and breaths, not mid-phrase.

If you are captioning from a script rather than a recording, use our caption timing calculator to generate timestamped segments and export an SRT file before you even open your editor.

Step 4 — Style captions for retention

Styling is where captions become a retention tool instead of an accessibility checkbox:

  • Word-by-word highlighting (karaoke style) keeps eyes locked to the screen — the dominant style on TikTok and Reels.
  • High contrast always: white or yellow text with a black stroke or background box. Never thin white text on a bright sky.
  • Position with intent: keep captions clear of faces, and in 9:16 leave room for the platform UI (top ~15%, bottom ~25%).
  • Emphasize keywords in a second color — benefits, numbers, and punchlines.
  • Match brand font across every video so viewers recognize your content in half a second.

Step 5 — Burn in or export SRT

Burned-in captions (rendered into the pixels) are the standard for TikTok, Reels, and Shorts — they always show and carry your styling. SRT sidecar files are better for YouTube long-form, where viewers can toggle captions and YouTube indexes the text for search. When in doubt for short-form: burn them in.

Quick checklist

  1. Transcribe with AI (CapCut, Descript, Premiere, Kapwing).
  2. Fix names, numbers, and jargon — one full review pass.
  3. Keep 5–7 words per caption, ≥0.8s on screen, break at pauses.
  4. Style: karaoke highlight, high contrast, keyword emphasis, safe zones.
  5. Burn in for short-form; export SRT for YouTube.
