Why logo typography matters

Typography carries up to 90% of a wordmark logo's personality. The right font pairing makes a brand feel trustworthy, modern or luxurious before a single word is read — the wrong one makes it forgettable. These free tools put professional type decisions within reach of any founder or designer.

Frequently asked questions

What is font pairing for logos?

Font pairing is the art of combining two typefaces — usually a distinctive headline/logo font with a clean supporting body font — so your brand looks cohesive. Good pairs share a mood but contrast in style, like a bold serif headline with a neutral sans body.

What are the best fonts for logos in 2026?

Designers keep returning to versatile workhorses: Montserrat, Playfair Display, Poppins, Bebas Neue and Inter for modern brands; Garamond and Bodoni-style serifs for luxury. The best logo font depends on your brand personality — take our free brand font quiz to find yours.

How many fonts should a logo use?

One or two. Most professional logos use a single distinctive typeface, sometimes paired with a second supporting font for taglines. Three or more fonts almost always looks messy and unprofessional.

Should a logo font be serif or sans serif?

Serif fonts signal tradition, trust and luxury; sans serifs feel modern, clean and tech-friendly. Neither wins universally — match the style to your audience and industry, and read our serif vs sans serif guide for examples.
