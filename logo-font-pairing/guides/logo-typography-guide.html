The Logo Typography Guide

A logo is typography under a microscope. These are the fundamentals professional designers check on every wordmark — explained simply, with practical steps you can apply today.

On this page

  • Kerning & tracking
  • Hierarchy
  • X-height & legibility
  • Spacing & clear space
  • Case & customization

01 Kerning & tracking: the invisible polish

Tracking is the uniform space between all letters. Kerning is the custom spacing between specific letter pairs (like A-V or T-o). Default font spacing is designed for paragraphs, not logos — so every professional wordmark gets manual kerning.

How to kern a logo

  1. Set the wordmark large (at least 200px) so spacing flaws are visible.
  2. Flip it upside down — this stops your brain from reading and lets you judge pure spacing rhythm.
  3. Adjust the worst pairs first: AV, AW, LT, To, Ya, and round-to-straight pairs like O-T.
  4. Aim for equal visual area between letters, not equal measured distance — round letters need tighter spacing than straight ones.

Tight tracking (slightly negative) usually looks more premium in a wordmark; loose tracking feels airy and modern. Pick one mood and stay consistent.

02 Hierarchy: what gets read first

Most logos contain two text elements — the brand name and a tagline or descriptor. Hierarchy tells the eye which matters. Create it with contrast:

  • Size: the brand name should be dramatically larger — at least 2× the tagline.
  • Weight: bold headline + regular or light tagline is the classic formula.
  • Case: a title-case or mixed-case wordmark with an ALL-CAPS tracked-out tagline underneath is a proven combination.
  • Color: one element in full ink, the other in a muted tone or single accent.

Never let the tagline compete with the name. If both elements shout, neither is heard.

03 X-height & legibility

The x-height is the height of lowercase letters (like the "x") relative to capitals. Fonts with a large x-height — Inter, Poppins, Roboto — stay legible at tiny sizes, which is exactly what a logo needs for favicons, app icons and social avatars.

The legibility checklist

  • Test the wordmark at 32px wide. If you can't read it, simplify.
  • Check it in pure black on white — no color or effects should be load-bearing.
  • Watch for confusable glyphs: I/l/1, O/0, rn/m, c/e at small sizes.
  • View it at arm's length and squint — the overall shape should still read clearly.

04 Spacing & clear space

Professional logos define a clear-space rule: a minimum empty margin around the logo, usually measured in the height of one letter (often the cap-height of the "x" or the logo's "o"). Nothing — no text, no graphics, no page edge — may enter this zone. It guarantees the logo always breathes, no matter where a partner or printer places it.

Also define a minimum size: the smallest width at which the full logo may be reproduced, plus a simplified small-size variant for anything below it.

05 Case & customization

All caps wordmarks feel established and authoritative but sacrifice the distinctive word-shapes that aid recognition. Lowercase feels friendly and modern. Title case is the safe middle ground.

Whatever you choose, customize at least one letter: tighten a ligature, round a terminal, dot an "i" with a brand color, or merge two letters. That single modification is often what makes a wordmark trademarkable — raw, unmodified fonts generally can't be protected.
