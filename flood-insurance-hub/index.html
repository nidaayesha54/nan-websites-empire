Flood Insurance FAQs

Quick answers to the questions homeowners ask most.

Does homeowners insurance cover flood damage?

No. Standard homeowners insurance policies exclude flood damage. You need a separate flood insurance policy through the NFIP or a private insurer.

How much does flood insurance cost on average?

The average NFIP premium is about $700 per year, ranging from roughly $450/year in low-risk zones to $2,000+/year in high-risk coastal zones. See the full 2026 cost breakdown.

Is flood insurance required if I have a mortgage?

If your home is in a high-risk flood zone (Zone A or V) with a federally backed mortgage, your lender must require it. Elsewhere it's optional. Check your risk.

How long does flood insurance take to go into effect?

NFIP policies have a 30-day waiting period (with exceptions for loan closings). Many private insurers offer 10–15 day waits. Compare NFIP vs private.

What is the difference between NFIP and private flood insurance?

NFIP is federal, with standardized rates and caps of $250K building / $100K contents. Private insurance can offer higher limits, shorter waits, and sometimes lower premiums in low-risk zones.

Do I need flood insurance if I'm not in a flood zone?

Probably worth considering: over 25% of NFIP claims come from outside high-risk zones, and low-risk policies are the cheapest. Read the full guide.
