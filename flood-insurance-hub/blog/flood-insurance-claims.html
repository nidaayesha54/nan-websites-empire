How to File a Flood Insurance Claim: Step-by-Step

October 12, 2026 · 7 min read

The water has receded and the damage is real. What you do in the next 24–72 hours determines how smoothly — and how fully — your flood insurance pays out. This guide walks through the entire claims process for both NFIP and private flood policies, so you can move from chaos to check with confidence.

Step 1: Contact Your Insurer Immediately

Call your insurance agent or the carrier's claims line as soon as it's safe. For NFIP policies, your agent files the initial loss notice. Do not wait — after major flood events, adjuster schedules fill up fast, and delays can slow your payout by weeks.

Have ready: your policy number, contact information, and a brief description of the damage.

Step 2: Document Everything Before Cleanup

This is the step most people botch. Before you move, discard, or clean anything:

  • Photograph and video every room — walls, floors, furniture, appliances — from multiple angles. Include close-ups of water lines on walls.
  • Photograph damaged items where they are before moving them outside.
  • Keep samples of damaged materials: carpet squares, wallpaper, upholstery swatches.
  • Save receipts for emergency repairs, temporary housing, and replacement purchases.
  • Make a room-by-room inventory of damaged belongings with approximate age and value. Your phone's notes app is fine.
Don't throw things away yet. Adjusters need to see damaged property. If health authorities require disposal, photograph items thoroughly first and keep a disposal log.

Step 3: Prevent Further Damage

Your policy requires you to mitigate additional loss: pump out standing water, remove wet drywall and insulation (after documenting), run dehumidifiers, and tarp damaged areas. Keep every receipt — reasonable mitigation costs are typically reimbursable.

Step 4: Meet the Adjuster

An adjuster will inspect the property and prepare a damage estimate. Be present, walk them through every affected area, and share your photo documentation and inventory. Ask questions — understanding their estimate helps you spot anything missed.

Step 5: File Your Proof of Loss (NFIP)

NFIP requires a signed Proof of Loss within 60 days of the flood (extensions are sometimes granted after catastrophes). This is your sworn statement of the damage and amount claimed. Your adjuster usually helps prepare it — review it carefully before signing. Private insurers have their own forms and deadlines; confirm yours immediately.

Step 6: Review the Settlement Offer

Compare the offer against your documentation. If it's short:

  • Request an itemized explanation of the adjuster's estimate.
  • Submit supplemental documentation (contractor estimates, additional photos).
  • For NFIP, you can request a reinspection or file an appeal. For private policies, use the carrier's dispute process — and your state's department of insurance if needed.

Common Claim Mistakes That Cost Homeowners Thousands

  1. Cleaning up before documenting. No photos = weak claim.
  2. Missing the Proof of Loss deadline (60 days for NFIP).
  3. Assuming basements are covered. NFIP covers almost nothing in basements — know this before you finish yours. See our coverage calculator for the gaps.
  4. Forgetting contents coverage is separate. Building and contents have separate limits and deductibles under NFIP.
  5. Not keeping receipts for emergency expenses and mitigation.
  6. Accepting the first offer without review. Supplemental claims are normal and expected.

What Flood Insurance Doesn't Cover (Know Before You Claim)

Filing goes smoother when you know the boundaries upfront. Standard NFIP policies typically do not cover:

  • Damage in basements beyond structural elements (no finished walls, floors, or personal property below grade, with narrow exceptions)
  • Additional living expenses — hotel bills and restaurant meals while displaced (many private policies do cover these)
  • Landscaping, decks, patios, fences, and outdoor property
  • Currency, precious metals, and valuable papers
  • Moisture, mildew, or mold damage that could have been avoided with reasonable mitigation
  • Living expenses and business interruption

This is why reviewing your policy before flood season — and considering private coverage for the gaps — matters so much. Our coverage calculator flags the basement and excess-limit gaps automatically.

Prepare Before the Next Storm

The best claim is one you're ready for: keep a home inventory (photos + values, stored in the cloud), know your policy number and agent's direct line, and understand your deductibles and limits now — not mid-crisis.
