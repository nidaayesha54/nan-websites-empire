Beneficiary Allocation Planner

Beneficiary designations override your will — a stale form can send your 401(k) to an ex-spouse. List each account, assign percentages, and validate that every account totals exactly 100%. Your plan saves on this device.

Per stirpes vs per capita — what happens if a beneficiary dies first? Per stirpes ("by branch"): the deceased beneficiary's share passes to their children. Example: you leave 50% each to two children per stirpes; one dies leaving two kids — those grandkids split the 50%.

Per capita ("by head"): the deceased beneficiary's share is divided among the surviving named beneficiaries. In the same example, the surviving child would receive 100%.

Most families with grandchildren choose per stirpes to keep each branch's share intact. Your account's beneficiary form is where you make this election — ask the plan administrator.

Frequently asked questions

What does per stirpes mean? +
Per stirpes ("by branch") means if a beneficiary dies before you, their share passes to their own children — keeping inheritance within each family line.
What does per capita mean? +
Per capita ("by head") means a deceased beneficiary's share is divided among the surviving named beneficiaries, not passed to the deceased person's children.
Do beneficiary percentages have to add up to 100%? +
Yes — each account's designations must total exactly 100%, or the form may be rejected or trigger disputes. This planner validates every account for you.
