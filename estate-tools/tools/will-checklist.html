Frequently asked questions

What documents do I need for estate planning? +
At minimum: a will, financial power of attorney, healthcare proxy/advance directive, and HIPAA authorization. Most people also need beneficiary reviews, guardian designations for minor children, and a digital asset plan.
How often should I update my will? +
Review every 3–5 years and after major life events: marriage, divorce, births, deaths, moving states, or big asset changes. Outdated documents cause as many problems as missing ones.
Is this checklist a substitute for a lawyer? +
No. It helps you organize and prepare, but documents must comply with your state's laws to be valid. An estate attorney review is strongly recommended — many offer flat-fee packages.
