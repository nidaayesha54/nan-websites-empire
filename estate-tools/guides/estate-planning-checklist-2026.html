Estate Planning Checklist 2026: Every Document You Need

Estate planning isn't about wealth — it's about making sure the right people can act when you can't, and that your wishes are honored when you're gone. Here's the complete inventory, in the order most people tackle it.

The core four documents

  1. Last will and testament — distributes probate assets, names an executor and guardians for minor children.
  2. Financial power of attorney — authorizes someone to manage money and property if you're incapacitated.
  3. Healthcare proxy + advance directive — names a medical decision-maker and states treatment wishes.
  4. HIPAA authorization — lets your chosen people actually talk to your doctors.

These four cover the scenarios families actually face. A revocable living trust is a valuable fifth for many — it avoids probate for funded assets — but it's not mandatory for everyone.

Beneficiaries: the hidden will

Retirement accounts, life insurance, and POD/TOD accounts bypass your will entirely. Outdated beneficiary forms are the #1 estate planning failure — review them after every marriage, divorce, birth, or death. Use the beneficiary planner to map them all.

Guardians and family

Name guardians for minor children and backups. Plan for pets. If anyone in the family has special needs, a special-needs trust preserves their benefit eligibility — leaving money outright can disqualify them from assistance.

Digital assets

Password manager emergency access, crypto key storage, social media legacy contacts, and where your photos live. Without a plan, digital assets are simply lost — no one can guess your passwords.

Final wishes

Burial or cremation, service preferences, organ donation. Writing these down is a gift: grieving families shouldn't have to guess.

Keeping it current

Review everything every 3–5 years and after major life events. Then work through the interactive will checklist — it tracks your progress and prints a summary for your files and your executor.
