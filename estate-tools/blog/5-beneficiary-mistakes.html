5 Beneficiary Mistakes That Disinherit the Wrong People

Beneficiary forms override your will — yet most people fill them out once and never look again. These five mistakes show up in estate attorneys' offices every week.

1. The ex-spouse still on the form

The classic. Divorce decrees divide property, but a 401(k) with your ex still named as beneficiary goes to your ex in many states. Update every account after divorce — immediately.

2. No contingent beneficiaries

If your primary beneficiary dies before you and no contingent is named, the account falls into probate — the exact outcome the designation was meant to avoid. Always name backups.

3. Percentages that don't total 100%

A surprising number of forms are rejected or disputed over arithmetic. Use the beneficiary planner to validate every account sums to exactly 100%.

4. Never electing per stirpes

If a child beneficiary dies before you, do you want their share to go to their kids (per stirpes) or to your surviving children (per capita)? The default varies by institution — make the election deliberately, not by accident.

5. Naming minor children directly

Minors can't manage large sums; courts appoint guardians and restrict access. For significant amounts, name a trust for the child's benefit instead — and see the full estate planning checklist for how it fits together.
