What Probate Really Costs — and How to Avoid It

Probate is the court process of validating a will and distributing assets. It's not the nightmare some claim — but it's rarely cheap, fast, or private. Here's the honest accounting.

What probate typically costs

  • Attorney fees: 2–4% of the estate in many states (some states set statutory fee schedules — California's, for example, is notoriously expensive).
  • Court and filing fees: a few hundred to a few thousand dollars.
  • Executor compensation: often 2–4% as well, or "reasonable" compensation set by the court.
  • Total: 3–7% of the estate value is a common range — $15,000–$35,000 on a $500,000 estate.

How long it takes

Simple estates: 6–12 months. Contested or complex estates: 1–3 years. During that time, assets are largely frozen — heirs wait, bills accumulate, and everything happens on the public record.

How to avoid it (legally)

  1. Revocable living trust — assets titled in the trust pass to beneficiaries with no court involvement. The gold standard for probate avoidance.
  2. Beneficiary designations — retirement accounts, life insurance, POD bank accounts, and TOD brokerage accounts all bypass probate. Keep them current with the beneficiary planner.
  3. Joint ownership with right of survivorship — common for married couples' homes and accounts.
  4. Small-estate procedures — most states offer simplified processes for estates under a threshold (often $50,000–$184,000).

Avoiding probate doesn't mean avoiding planning — it means planning better. Start with the interactive will checklist and make sure every asset has a clear path to the right person.
