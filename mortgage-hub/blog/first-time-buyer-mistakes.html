7 First-Time Homebuyer Mistakes That Cost Thousands

Buying your first home is exciting — and expensive when you get it wrong. Here are the mistakes that cost first-timers the most money.

1. House-Hunting Before Pre-Approval

Falling in love with a home you can't finance wastes everyone's time — and in competitive markets, sellers won't even look at offers without a pre-approval letter. Get pre-approved first.

2. Draining Every Dollar for the Down Payment

Putting 20% down but leaving zero emergency fund is dangerous. One roof leak or job hiccup and you're in trouble. Keep 3–6 months of expenses in reserve after closing.

3. Ignoring the True Monthly Cost

Principal & interest is only part of the payment. Tax, insurance, PMI, and HOA can add 30–50% more. Calculate the full payment before making an offer.

4. Not Shopping Lenders

The CFPB found that failing to comparison-shop costs borrowers thousands. Get at least three Loan Estimates on the same day and compare.

5. Opening New Credit Before Closing

That new car or furniture on credit can tank your approval — lenders re-check credit before funding. Freeze all big financial moves until after closing day.

6. Skipping the Inspection to "Win" the Bid

Waiving inspection in a hot market can hide $20,000+ in repairs. At minimum, pay for an inspection for your own information even if you won't ask for repairs.

7. Maxing Out the Approved Amount

Approval is a ceiling, not a target. Budget 1% of the home's value per year for maintenance, and leave breathing room for rate resets, tax hikes, and life.

Do This Instead

Find your real budget, get pre-approved, shop three lenders, and keep reserves. Boring — and it saves thousands.
