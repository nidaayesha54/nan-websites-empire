How Much Mortgage Can I Afford in 2026?

The question every buyer asks first — and the one lenders answer with a simple rule: the 28/36 rule. Here's exactly how it works, with real numbers at 2026 rates.

The 28/36 Rule

  • Front-end ratio (28%): your total housing cost — mortgage payment, property tax, insurance, PMI, HOA — should not exceed 28% of your gross monthly income.
  • Back-end ratio (36%): all your monthly debts including housing should not exceed 36% of gross monthly income.

Example: $120,000 annual income = $10,000/month gross. Max housing payment = $2,800/month. If you have $500/month in other debts, your total debt ceiling is $3,600.

Worked Example at 6.5%

With $120,000 income, $80,000 down, 6.5% on a 30-year loan, and ~$600/month in tax + insurance, you can afford roughly a $500,000–$530,000 home. Run your own numbers with our affordability calculator.

What Lenders Actually Check

  • DTI ratio — the 28/36 rule above; some loan programs allow up to 45–50% back-end DTI.
  • Credit score — 620+ for conventional, 580+ for FHA (with 3.5% down).
  • Reserves — 2–6 months of payments in the bank after closing.
  • Employment history — typically 2 years of stable income.

Common Mistakes

  1. Maxing out the approval. Being approved for $600K doesn't mean you should spend it — leave room for maintenance (~1% of home value per year) and life.
  2. Forgetting the "extras." Tax, insurance, PMI, and HOA can add $500–$1,500/month beyond principal & interest.
  3. Ignoring rate buydowns. Paying points to lower your rate can beat a bigger down payment — compare both.
