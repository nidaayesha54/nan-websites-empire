FHA vs. Conventional Loans

The two most common mortgage types in the US — but they serve very different buyers. Here's an honest comparison.

Side-by-Side Comparison

FeatureFHA LoanConventional Loan
Min. down payment3.5%3–5%
Min. credit score580 (3.5% down); 500 (10% down)620
Mortgage insuranceMIP: 1.75% upfront + annual (often for loan life)PMI: drops at 20% equity
Loan limits (2026)Lower (county-based)Higher (conforming limits)
Best forFirst-time buyers, lower creditGood credit, 20% down

When FHA Wins

  • Credit score below 680 — FHA rates are often better for lower scores.
  • Small down payment with limited savings — 3.5% gets you in the door.
  • Higher DTI — FHA allows back-end ratios up to ~50% in some cases.

When Conventional Wins

  • 20% down — no mortgage insurance at all, the cheapest long-term option.
  • PMI drops off — conventional PMI cancels at 20% equity; FHA MIP often lasts the life of the loan.
  • Higher-priced homes — conforming loan limits exceed FHA limits in most counties.

The MIP Trap

FHA's mortgage insurance premium includes 1.75% upfront (usually rolled into the loan) plus an annual premium that, for most borrowers, never drops off. On a $400,000 loan that can mean $150+/month forever — often making conventional cheaper within a few years even with PMI at the start.

Bottom Line

FHA is the best door-opener; conventional is usually the best long-term deal. If you start FHA, plan to refinance into conventional once you hit 20% equity.
