When to Refinance Your Mortgage

Refinancing can save you tens of thousands — or cost you money if you get the timing wrong. The entire decision comes down to one number: your break-even point.

The Break-Even Rule

Break-even (months) = Closing costs ÷ Monthly savings.

Example: $8,000 in closing costs, $350/month savings → break-even in ~23 months. If you'll stay in the home longer than that, refinancing wins. Calculate yours here.

The 1% Guideline

A common rule of thumb: refinance when you can drop your rate by at least 1 percentage point. At today's balances, even 0.5% can be worth it if closing costs are low — always run the break-even math instead of guessing.

Good Reasons to Refinance

  • Lower rate — the classic reason; reduces payment and total interest.
  • Drop PMI — if your home appreciated past 20% equity, refinancing can kill PMI.
  • Shorten the term — 30-year to 15-year builds equity dramatically faster.
  • Cash-out — tap equity for renovations (but this resets your debt clock).

Traps That Wipe Out Savings

  1. Resetting the term. Refinancing a 27-years-remaining loan into a new 30-year loan restarts the interest-heavy early years — you can pay more total interest despite a lower rate.
  2. Rolling costs into the loan. "No-cost" refinances just hide the costs in a higher rate or bigger balance.
  3. Refinancing too often. Each refinance has costs; serial refinancers often never break even.

Checklist Before You Refinance

  • Break-even under 24 months?
  • Staying in the home past break-even?
  • Compared at least 3 lenders?
  • Checked that the new term doesn't extend your payoff date (unless intentional)?
