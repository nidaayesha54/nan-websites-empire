CRNA Salary Guide 2026: What Nurse Anesthetists Really Earn

Published October 12, 2026 · 7 min read · Data updated for 2026

Certified Registered Nurse Anesthetists remain the highest-paid nursing specialty in the United States — and it is not close. The national median CRNA salary sits around $214,000 in 2026, roughly 2.5× the median RN salary. But "median" hides enormous variation: your state, experience, practice setting, and call burden can swing total compensation by $100,000 or more.

This guide breaks down exactly what CRNAs earn — and what drives the differences.

Highest-paying states for CRNAs

Geography is the biggest lever on CRNA pay. West Coast states, the Northeast, and Alaska/Hawaii lead — driven by cost of living, union strength, and independent-practice laws.

Pay by experience level

CRNA pay grows steadily with experience, and the curve is steeper than in bedside nursing:

  • 0–2 years (new grad): ~92% of median — roughly $180K–$200K
  • 3–5 years: ~100% of median — the benchmark years
  • 6–10 years: ~108% — specialization and efficiency pay off
  • 11–15 years: ~114% — senior roles, leadership stipends
  • 16+ years: ~118% — top of most employed scales; locums can exceed this

What boosts total compensation beyond base salary

Base salary is only part of the story. These adders routinely push total comp $30K–$80K above base:

  • Call pay: in-house call stipends ($1,000–$2,000+ per 24h) add up fast
  • Overtime: paid at 1.5× — and anesthesia overtime is common
  • Locum tenens: travel CRNA contracts pay $200–$300+/hour; top earners clear $350K
  • Sign-on bonuses: $25K–$75K is common in competitive markets
  • Student loan repayment: many rural hospitals offer $50K–$100K+ packages
  • Benefits: 8–12 weeks PTO, full health coverage, and 5–8% retirement matches are standard in employed roles

CRNA salary growth outlook

Demand for CRNAs keeps climbing: an aging population needs more surgeries, anesthesia groups face staffing shortages, and CRNAs deliver anesthesia at lower cost than physician-only models — making them the economical choice for hospitals and surgery centers. Independent practice authority continues to expand state by state, which supports both pay and autonomy. Bottom line

A CRNA career reliably delivers $200K+ with strong benefits, geographic flexibility, and genuine clinical autonomy — the best compensation package in nursing by a wide margin.
