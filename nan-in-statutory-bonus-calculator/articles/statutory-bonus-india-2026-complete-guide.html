Statutory Bonus in India 2026: Complete Guide — Eligibility, Formula & Deadlines

India's Payment of Bonus Act, 1965 (now subsumed in the Code on Wages, 2019) guarantees eligible employees a bonus of 8.33% to 20% of wages every year — even if the company made no profit. Here's who qualifies, how it's calculated, and when it must be paid.

What statutory bonus is

Statutory bonus is not a gift from your employer and not the same as a performance bonus. It is a legal right: establishments covered by the law must pay eligible employees a bonus every accounting year, calculated from the establishment's profits. The key twist that surprises many workers: the minimum bonus of 8.33% is payable even if the company made zero profit (with narrow exceptions for new establishments). The bonus recognizes that workers share in the value they help create.

The law applies to establishments employing 20 or more workers on any day during the accounting year — factories and most other establishments. Once an establishment is covered, it stays covered ("once covered, always covered"), even if headcount later falls below 20.

Who is eligible (and who is not)

You are eligible for statutory bonus if all of these are true:

  • Your monthly salary/wage (basic + DA) is ₹21,000 or less.
  • You worked at least 30 working days in that accounting year.
  • You are employed in a covered establishment (20+ workers).

Not eligible: employees earning more than ₹21,000/month (though employers often pay them ex-gratia or contractual bonuses voluntarily); apprentices; employees of certain exempt establishments (e.g. LIC, hospitals under Section 32, some non-profits); and employees dismissed for fraud, riotous or violent behaviour, or theft of company property.

Note the important nuance: the ₹21,000 figure is an eligibility ceiling, not the calculation base. Earning ₹20,000/month makes you eligible — but your bonus is still calculated on a capped figure (explained below).

The bonus formula, with examples

Annual bonus = Bonus % × (Calculation-base monthly wage × 12)
Bonus % ranges from 8.33% (minimum) to 20% (maximum), depending on the establishment's allocable surplus.

The minimum 8.33% equals roughly one month's salary — that's why many employees simply call it their "Diwali bonus" even though the law doesn't tie it to any festival.

The ₹21,000 and ₹7,000 ceilings explained

Two different ceilings confuse everyone — here's the distinction:

  • ₹21,000/month — the eligibility ceiling. Earn more than this and the Act doesn't cover you at all.
  • ₹7,000/month — the calculation ceiling. Even if you earn ₹18,000/month, your bonus is calculated as if you earned ₹7,000/month — unless the applicable state minimum wage is higher than ₹7,000, in which case the minimum wage becomes the base.

Example: if your state's minimum wage for your category is ₹9,000/month, your minimum annual bonus = 8.33% × (₹9,000 × 12) = ₹8,997. Always check your state's current minimum wage notification — it can raise your bonus substantially above the ₹6,997 floor.

When bonus must be paid

The employer must pay the annual bonus within 8 months of the close of the accounting year. For companies following the April–March financial year, that means by 30 November. The government can extend this in exceptional cases, but the total extension cannot exceed two years.

Bonus must be paid in cash — not as goods, vouchers, or adjusted against other dues (except specific lawful deductions). Employers must maintain registers (Forms A, B, C) and file an annual return (Form D) with the Labour Inspector.

Disqualification and deductions

An employee can be disqualified from bonus for the year if dismissed for fraud, violent behaviour, or theft/misappropriation of company property. Separately, the employer may deduct from bonus: customary bonuses already paid during the year (like puja bonus), interim bonus advances, and amounts to recover losses from employee misconduct.

What the Code on Wages changes

The Code on Wages, 2019 consolidates the Payment of Bonus Act with three other wage laws. The bonus framework is substantially retained: the ₹21,000 eligibility limit, the 8.33%–20% range, and the calculation ceilings continue. One practical change to watch: the Code's broader definition of "wages" can shift the calculation base for PF and gratuity, and employers should confirm the bonus base under the Code's rules as enforcement rolls out state by state.

How to claim unpaid bonus

  1. Confirm eligibility: salary ≤ ₹21,000/month, 30+ days worked, 20+ worker establishment.
  2. Calculate your range with our bonus calculator — know your 8.33% floor before you ask.
  3. Write to HR citing the Payment of Bonus Act / Code on Wages and the 8-month payment deadline.
  4. Approach the Labour Inspector if the employer doesn't pay — inspectors can examine the Forms A–D registers.
  5. Raise a dispute before the Labour Court/Tribunal if needed. Claims should be made promptly; don't let years pass.

How the bonus percentage is actually decided: allocable surplus

The 8.33%–20% range isn't arbitrary — it comes from the company's allocable surplus. Here's the simplified mechanics: the employer computes gross profit per the Act's schedules, subtracts prior charges (depreciation, taxes, returns on capital), and arrives at the available surplus. A fixed share of that — 60% for companies, 67% for others — becomes the allocable surplus to be shared with workers.

  • If allocable surplus covers 8.33% of wages → workers get 8.33% (the minimum, paid even at zero profit).
  • If it stretches to 20% → workers get up to 20%.
  • If it would exceed 20%, the excess is "set on" (carried forward up to 4 years); shortfalls are "set off" against future surpluses. This carry-forward is why bonus can stay stable across good and bad years.

Statutory bonus vs ex-gratia vs performance bonus

Statutory bonusLegal right under the Act; 8.33%–20%; eligibility ≤ ₹21,000/month
Ex-gratiaVoluntary payment by employer (often to staff above ₹21,000); no legal formula
Performance/incentive bonusContractual or policy-based; tied to targets, not the Act

Many payslips show "bonus" that is actually ex-gratia. If you earn under ₹21,000 in a covered establishment, you have a statutory claim on top of — or instead of — whatever the company voluntarily pays. And customary festival payments (like puja bonus) given during the year can be adjusted against the statutory bonus, so ask HR for the split before assuming you're getting both.

New establishments: the 5-year exemption

Genuinely new establishments are exempt for their first 5 years (provided they meet the loss conditions) — the law doesn't burden a startup with bonus in year one. But the exemption is narrow: it doesn't apply to existing businesses that merely rebrand, and after the exemption window, normal rules apply. If your employer claims "we're a new company, no bonus," check the incorporation date and whether the exemption conditions are actually met.
