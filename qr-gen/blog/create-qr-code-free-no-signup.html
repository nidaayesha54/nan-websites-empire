Why most "free" QR generators aren't really free

Many popular QR tools use a freemium trap:

  • Email walls: they hold your code hostage until you hand over your address — which lands you on a marketing list.
  • Dynamic-code lock-in: they generate "dynamic" codes routed through their servers. Stop paying, and your printed codes die.
  • Watermarks & low-res: the free download is blurry or branded; the clean version costs $10/month.
  • Tracking: every scan is logged, and your data (including WiFi passwords) sits on their servers.

The fix is simple: use a generator that creates static codes locally. Static means the data lives inside the code itself — no server, nothing to expire, nothing to track. That's what QRGen does: open your browser's developer tools and watch the network tab while generating. Zero requests.

Static vs. dynamic QR codes: which do you need?

Static (what QRGen makes): the destination is baked into the pattern. Free forever, private, unchangeable. Perfect for WiFi credentials, business cards, menus, packaging — anything where the target rarely changes.

Dynamic: the code points to a redirect service, so you can change the destination later and track scans. Useful for large campaigns — but you're renting, not owning. If the service shuts down or you stop paying, every printed code breaks.

Rule of thumb: if you don't need scan analytics or editable destinations, static is better in every way — free, private, permanent.
