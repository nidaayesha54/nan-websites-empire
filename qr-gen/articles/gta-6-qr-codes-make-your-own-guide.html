Make GTA 6 QR Codes That Fans Actually Scan

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 did something no game had done quite like this: it put QR codes on billboards, posters and promo boxes inside the game world, and the codes actually work. Point a real phone at one and it opens a real website. The AudaCity codes in the November 10 pre-load trailer decode to a short link that lands on Rockstar's GTA 6 page, and the Peekhole poster outside the Jack of Hearts strip club in the Extended Look carries a working code too. Players are scanning the scenery now, which means a QR code of your own is no longer a gimmick — it is a link people will actually follow.

This guide is for creators: streamers, fan artists, map makers and anyone running a GTA 6 page. It explains what to link, how to make a code that scans reliably, where to put it, and the one mistake that makes a fan QR code look exactly like a scam.

Why GTA 6 fans scan QR codes now

Rockstar trained its audience to scan. The pre-load trailer, released November 10, squeezes a waterfront stunt into about 30 seconds, and at around six seconds in, pink QR codes sit on a stack of boxes beside Lucia. Fans pulled the frame, decoded the squares, and found the same short link in both: a link that opens Rockstar's GTA 6 page with the pre-load video and pre-order links. The Peekhole poster in the Extended Look works the same way. A code in the game world, scanned with a real phone, opens a real page.

That matters for your code because the audience already trusts the format. A QR code on a GTA 6 fan poster or a stream overlay does not look strange anymore — it looks like part of the game. The flip side is that scammers know this too, which is why the rules at the end of this guide matter as much as the how-to.

What to link

Link to something the scanner can use in ten seconds. The best choices are the second trailer, a fan map of Leonida, your best clip, your channel, or a piece of fan art with the artist credited. A code that opens a trailer is useful on a poster at a gaming night; a code that opens your channel is useful on a stream overlay; a code that opens a fan map is useful on a printout at a LAN party. Pick one destination per code. A code that opens a page full of links is a code nobody follows twice.

Do not link to a download. There is no PC version, no APK and no free copy of GTA 6, so any QR code that promises one looks exactly like the malware traps already circulating — fake installers, fake betas and wallet drainers. A fan code that points at a download will be reported as a scam even if your intentions are good, and it will be, because the destination is the same shape as the real scams.

How to make one that scans

Any free QR generator will do the job, and the code itself takes seconds. What takes care is the design around it. Use high contrast — black squares on a white background scan best, and pink-on-black, the AudaCity look, only works if the contrast is strong and the print is sharp. Leave a clear quiet zone, an empty border at least four squares wide, around the code. Print it big enough for the distance: a code on a poster meant to be scanned from two metres away needs to be at least 10 centimetres across, and a code on a phone screen overlay needs to be big enough to read at a glance.

Then test it before you publish. Scan it with two different phones, one iPhone and one Android if you can, in the lighting where it will actually be used. A code that scans on your desk under a lamp and fails in a dim room is a code that fails. If you use a dynamic QR code — one where the destination can be changed later — keep the service free and check that the link still works every month, because a dead code is worse than no code.

Where to put it

Posters and flyers are the obvious home, and GTA 6's own marketing shows why: the codes in the game sit on promo boxes and posters, at eye level, where people are already looking. A stream overlay works too, in a corner that does not cover the game, shown for a few seconds at a time rather than permanently. On social media, put the code in the image itself, not in a link in the bio, because the whole point is that the phone camera does the work. On merch, print it on the back of a shirt or the inside of a sticker sheet, where it survives washing and handling.

Label it. A short line under the code — "Scan for the trailer" or "Scan for the Leonida map" — doubles the scan rate, because people scan what they understand. An unlabelled code is a mystery, and mysteries get ignored.

FAQ

Do QR codes in GTA 6 really work? Yes. The AudaCity codes in the pre-load trailer decode to a short link that opens Rockstar's GTA 6 page, and the Peekhole poster code in the Extended Look works the same way.

What should a fan QR code link to? A trailer, a fan map, a clip, a channel or a piece of fan art. One destination per code, and never a download — there is no PC version or APK, so a download link reads as a scam.

How big should a printed QR code be? At least 10 centimetres across for scanning from two metres away, with a clear empty border around it, and always test-scan it on two phones before you print.

The bottom line: GTA 6 made QR codes part of the game, so a fan code that points at something real — a trailer, a map, a clip — will get scanned. Make it big, label it, test it twice, and never point it at a download.
