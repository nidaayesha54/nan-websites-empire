FPSC Written Test 2026 — Pattern, Syllabus & Passing Marks

Applying for an FPSC post? This guide explains how FPSC screening tests work — the MCQ pattern, what the syllabus covers, negative marking, and the stages after the written test.

✅ Updated October 24, 2026
Home » Federal Government Jobs » Articles

The Federal Public Service Commission (FPSC) runs recruitment for federal government posts through a structured process: advertisement, online application, screening test, and interview. For most general recruitment posts, the first hurdle is a written screening test — usually a multiple-choice paper. This guide explains the standard pattern, what the syllabus typically covers, how shortlisting works, and how this process differs from the CSS examination. Read it alongside the advertisement for your specific post, because the exact syllabus and rules for each post are published there.

Honesty flag: this is a general guide to how FPSC tests work, not a test schedule for any specific post. The exact syllabus, number of questions, time limit and passing standard for your post are published in its advertisement and on fpsc.gov.pk — always check those, because they vary by post.

The Screening Test Pattern

For general recruitment, FPSC's screening test is normally a multiple-choice question (MCQ) paper. Questions cover the subject area of the post — for example, law for a legal post, medicine for a medical post, or computer science for an IT post — plus English and general knowledge. The paper is designed to shortlist candidates for interview, not to test everything about the subject. The number of questions, total marks and time allowed are stated in the advertisement and on the admit slip for each post, so they differ from one recruitment to another.

Negative Marking — Read the Instructions

FPSC MCQ screening tests generally carry negative marking for wrong answers. The exact deduction is stated in the test instructions on your admit slip and on the question paper itself. The practical consequence is simple: do not guess blindly. Attempt questions you are confident about, make educated attempts where you can eliminate options, and leave questions you genuinely do not know if the penalty makes guessing a losing strategy. Many candidates lose marks by answering every question regardless of the penalty.

Passing Marks and Shortlisting

There is no single published passing mark that applies to every FPSC post — the qualifying standard is set per recruitment, and shortlisting for interview is normally done on merit order, with a limited number of top candidates called for interview against each vacancy. That means your target is not a fixed percentage but a rank high enough to be shortlisted. Strong subject preparation matters more than general knowledge alone, because the subject portion usually dominates the paper.

Stages After the Written Test

1
Result: FPSC publishes the screening test result on fpsc.gov.pk. Qualified candidates are shortlisted for the next stage.
2
Descriptive exam or psychological assessment: some posts add a descriptive paper or a psychological assessment before the interview. Check your advertisement to see if your post has one.
3
Interview: shortlisted candidates appear before the Commission with original documents — CNIC, degrees, experience certificates, domicile and NOC where applicable.
4
Final merit: FPSC recommends successful candidates to the hiring department, and appointment follows departmental verification.

FPSC Screening Test vs CSS Exam — Do Not Confuse Them

The CSS examination is a separate, much larger exam: compulsory and optional written subjects over several days, followed by a psychological assessment and a viva voce interview. It has its own schedule, syllabus and rules. The general recruitment screening test described in this guide is a post-specific MCQ test for individual advertised posts, such as lecturers, assistant directors or inspectors. If you applied through a consolidated advertisement for a specific post, you are in the general recruitment stream, not the CSS stream.

How to Prepare

Start from the syllabus in your advertisement, not from generic guess papers. Revise your subject area thoroughly, practise MCQs under timed conditions, and keep English and general knowledge fresh. Download your admit slip from fpsc.gov.pk as soon as it is available, check your test centre and reporting time, and carry your original CNIC to the test. Keep your application number and fee receipt safe — you will need them if any query arises.

Reminder: test schedules, admit slips and results for FPSC posts are published on fpsc.gov.pk only. Dates circulated on social media before FPSC publishes them should be treated as rumours.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the FPSC screening test pattern?

FPSC screening tests for general recruitment are typically MCQ-based written tests covering the post's subject area plus English and general knowledge. The exact syllabus, number of questions and time limit are published in each advertisement and on the FPSC website.

Does the FPSC written test have negative marking?

FPSC MCQ screening tests generally carry negative marking for wrong answers, as stated in the test instructions for each post. Read the instructions on your admit slip carefully, and avoid blind guessing.

What happens after the FPSC written test?

Candidates who qualify the screening test are shortlisted for the next stage, which is usually an interview before the Commission, and in some cases a descriptive examination or psychological assessment. Final selection is based on merit.

Is the FPSC written test the same as the CSS exam?

No. The CSS examination is a separate, much larger exam with compulsory and optional subjects and its own schedule. FPSC general recruitment screening tests are post-specific MCQ tests for individual advertised posts.
