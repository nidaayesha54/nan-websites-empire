How to Choose a Nursing School in 2026

By NursingSchoolsHub · Updated October 2026 · 9 min read

Choosing a nursing school is one of the highest-stakes education decisions you can make. The program you pick determines how much debt you carry, how well you pass the NCLEX, and how smoothly you land your first RN job. This guide gives you a practical checklist — the same factors admissions counselors and working nurses say actually matter.

1. Accreditation comes first

Before anything else, confirm the program is accredited by either ACEN (Accreditation Commission for Education in Nursing) or CCNE (Commission on Collegiate Nursing Education). Accreditation means an independent body reviewed the curriculum, faculty, and clinical training — and found them adequate.

Why does this matter so much? Many employers, especially hospitals and the military, only hire graduates of accredited programs. Graduate schools (for MSN or DNP degrees) generally require an accredited bachelor's. And some state boards won't let you sit for the NCLEX if your program lacks proper approval. If a school can't clearly name its accreditor, walk away.

2. Check the NCLEX first-time pass rate

The NCLEX-RN is the licensing exam every graduate must pass. A school's first-time pass rate — the percentage of graduates who pass on their first attempt — is the single best public measure of program quality.

Look for rates of 85% or higher, and ask for three to five years of data, not just the best single year. Many state boards of nursing publish pass rates by school, which makes verification easy. A program trending downward, or sitting below 80% for multiple years, is a warning sign no marketing brochure can offset. Our NCLEX pass rate decoder explains exactly how to read these numbers.

3. Evaluate clinical placements

Nursing can't be learned from lectures alone. Every program requires hundreds of hours of clinical rotations in hospitals, clinics, and community settings. The quality and reliability of these placements vary enormously.

Ask each school: Which hospitals do students rotate through? Does the school guarantee placements, or do students arrange their own? How many clinical hours are built into the curriculum, and in which specialties? Programs with strong hospital partnerships give you better training — and often a direct pipeline to job offers.

4. Do the real math on cost

Tuition is only part of the picture. Add fees, books, uniforms, transportation to clinicals, and lost income if you cut work hours. Then compare the total against your expected starting salary as an RN.

Community college ADN programs can cost under $15,000 total, while private BSN programs frequently exceed $80,000. Neither price automatically means better outcomes — plenty of affordable programs post excellent NCLEX rates. Run your numbers through our cost comparator and aim for total debt below one year of starting salary.

Don't skip the FAFSA. Federal aid, state grants, and nursing-specific scholarships (from hospitals, foundations, and programs like HRSA) can cut your out-of-pocket cost dramatically. Apply before comparing sticker prices.

5. Match the program to your life

The "best" nursing school is the one you can actually finish. Be honest about your constraints:

  • Working full-time? Look for part-time, evening/weekend, or hybrid tracks.
  • Already have a bachelor's? Accelerated BSN programs compress nursing school into 12–18 months.
  • Already an RN or LPN? RN-to-BSN bridge programs are mostly online and designed for working nurses.
  • Location-bound? Clinicals must be in person, so even "online" programs need to place you near home.

Still unsure which path fits? Our program finder quiz matches your goals, budget, and schedule to the right program type in under two minutes.

6. Compare ADN vs BSN for your goals

Both degrees lead to the same RN license and the same NCLEX exam. An ADN takes two years and costs far less; a BSN takes four years but is preferred by most hospitals and required for leadership, military, and graduate-school paths. Many nurses start with an affordable ADN, work as RNs, and complete an RN-to-BSN online with employer tuition help. Read our full ADN vs BSN breakdown before deciding.

7. Visit, ask, and verify

Before applying, tour the campus or attend a virtual info session and ask pointed questions: What is the first-time NCLEX pass rate for the last three years? What percentage of graduates have RN jobs within six months? What tutoring and remediation exist for struggling students? How are clinical placements assigned? Honest programs answer directly; evasive ones tell you everything you need to know.

The bottom line

Accreditation, NCLEX pass rates, clinical quality, true cost, and life fit — evaluate every program on these five factors and you'll avoid the expensive mistakes. Take your time, verify the data with your state board of nursing, and choose the program that sets you up to pass the NCLEX and thrive as a nurse, not just the one with the glossiest brochure.
