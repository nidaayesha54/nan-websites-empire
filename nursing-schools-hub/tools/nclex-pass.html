NCLEX Pass Rates: What They Mean

The single most important quality signal for a nursing program — explained, then decoded for any school you enter.

Why pass rates matter

Graduating from nursing school isn't enough — you must pass the NCLEX-RN exam to get licensed and work as a nurse. A program's first-time pass rate (the share of graduates who pass on their first attempt) tells you how well it actually prepares students. Schools report these rates to their state board of nursing, and most states publish them publicly.

Key insight: a high NCLEX pass rate usually means strong teaching, good clinical placements, and solid student support. A low rate — especially over several years — is the clearest warning sign a program has problems.

Decode any pass rate

What pass rates mean — the bands

  • 90%+ — Excellent. Top-tier programs. Consistently high rates here mean the curriculum, faculty, and clinicals are all working.
  • 85–90% — Strong. A solid, reliable program. Most well-regarded schools land in this range.
  • 80–85% — Acceptable, ask questions. The program clears most state minimums, but ask what support exists for students who struggle.
  • Below 80% — Caution. Many states flag or sanction programs that stay under 80%. Ask the school for a multi-year trend — one bad year differs from a bad pattern.
  • Below 75% — Red flag. Steer clear unless the school shows clear, documented improvement and explains what changed.

Beyond the number: 4 questions to ask

  1. Is the rate trending up or down? Ask for 3–5 years of data. A single great year after years of mediocrity deserves skepticism — and so does a sudden drop.
  2. How many students took the exam? A 100% pass rate means less when only 8 students tested. Look for rates based on 50+ test-takers.
  3. What happens if students fail? Good programs offer free remediation, tutoring, and re-test coaching. Ask directly.
  4. Is the program accredited? ACEN or CCNE accreditation plus a healthy pass rate is the gold standard. Pass rates without accreditation still carry risk.
