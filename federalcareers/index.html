How to Apply

Five steps to land your government job.

Target a Career Ladder

Look for announcements marked 'career ladder' (e.g., GS-9/11/12) — you enter at the lower grade and promote automatically as you gain experience.

Apply Through USAJOBS.gov

Every federal career starts with a USAJOBS.gov application. Build one strong federal resume and tailor it with keywords from each announcement.

Learn the GS Pay Scale

Grades GS-1 to GS-15 set your salary band; steps within each grade reward tenure. Locality pay can add 15–40% in high-cost cities like DC or San Francisco.

Complete Clearance Paperwork Early

Career-track roles in defense, intelligence and law enforcement need security clearances. Start SF-86 paperwork and gather 10 years of history early to avoid delays.

Plan Long-Term Growth

Federal careers offer predictable promotions, the TSP retirement plan (with 5% agency match), pensions after 5+ years, and the Senior Executive Service (SES) for top leaders.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about government hiring.

What is a federal career ladder?

A career ladder position (e.g., GS-9/11/12) lets you join at the entry grade and earn non-competitive promotions to the full grade as you demonstrate performance — no new application needed.

What is the TSP?

The Thrift Savings Plan is the federal 401(k)-style retirement plan. Agencies automatically contribute 1% of salary and match up to 5%, making it one of the best retirement benefits anywhere.

How do federal promotions work?

Most promotions are time-in-grade based: one year at each grade up to GS-12, then competitive promotion to GS-13 and above through merit promotion announcements.

What is the Pathways Program?

Pathways offers internships and recent-graduate positions that convert to permanent federal jobs — the main pipeline for students and graduates entering government service.

Can I switch agencies mid-career?

Yes — once you have competitive status (typically after 3 years), you can transfer between agencies or apply to merit promotion announcements government-wide.

Do federal employees get pensions?

Yes — under FERS, employees with 5+ years of service earn a pension annuity, plus Social Security and TSP savings, forming a three-part retirement system.
