Pathways Programs 2026: Federal Internships and Recent Graduate Jobs on USAJOBS

The Pathways Programs are the federal government’s front door for students and recent graduates: paid internships while you study, structured one-year programs after graduation, and a prestigious fellowship for advanced-degree holders — all with a real path to permanent federal employment.

Breaking into federal service as a student or recent graduate has a dedicated system: the Pathways Programs. Created to replace the old Student Career Experience Program, Pathways consists of three separate programs offering Schedule D excepted-service appointments — a hiring lane designed specifically to move students and recent graduates into federal careers. If you are in school now or graduated within the last two years, this is the single most important federal hiring system to understand.

Internship Program: Earn While You Learn

The Internship Program places currently enrolled students into paid positions across federal agencies. Unlike the unpaid internships common in the private sector, these are real federal appointments with real paychecks. The critical number is 480 hours: complete that much work (or 320 with a waiver for high performers), finish your degree, and your agency can convert you into the competitive service without you competing against the general public. The Department of Justice, the Department of Energy, the VA, and dozens of other agencies all run Pathways internships — posted on USAJOBS.gov under student hiring paths.

Recent Graduates Program: Your Career Launchpad

The Recent Graduates Program is a structured one-year developmental experience with at least 40 hours of interactive training, a mentor outside your chain of command, and an Individual Development Plan. It moves you through grade levels quickly as you prove yourself. Veterans delayed by service get up to six years after degree completion to apply.

Presidential Management Fellows: The Premier Track

The PMF Program is the federal government's flagship two-year leadership development program for advanced-degree holders, with intensive training, senior mentorship, and rotational assignments across agencies.

How to Apply for Pathways Positions

  1. Search USAJOBS with the student filter: Go to USAJOBS.gov and filter by hiring path — "Students" or "Recent graduates" — to see only Pathways-eligible announcements.
  2. Join the Early Career Talent Network: The government runs an Early Career Talent Network at earlycareers.gov where you can share your profile and interests to get matched with opportunities across agencies.
  3. Watch agency pages: Many agencies maintain dedicated Pathways pages listing intern and recent-graduate openings as they post.
  4. Prepare a federal resume: Even for internships, use the USAJOBS Resume Builder and include exact dates and hours for every experience.
  5. Track your hours: If you land an internship, track your 480 hours carefully — hitting the threshold is what unlocks conversion eligibility.
Timing matters: Pathways internship postings often cluster around academic calendars, with many agencies posting in fall and spring. Set a USAJOBS saved search for the student hiring path now so you see announcements the day they appear.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the federal Pathways Programs?

Pathways is a framework of three programs — Internship, Recent Graduates, and Presidential Management Fellows — that give students and recent graduates access to federal jobs through Schedule D excepted-service appointments.

Are Pathways internships paid?

Yes. The Internship Program offers paid opportunities for enrolled students from high school through graduate school, with a path to permanent employment.

How many hours do I need to convert from a Pathways internship?

Interns must complete a minimum of 480 internship hours (320 hours with a waiver for outstanding performers) to be eligible for conversion to the competitive service.

How long after graduation can I apply for the Recent Graduates Program?

Within two years of completing your degree — or up to six years for veterans whose military service delayed their application.

What is the Presidential Management Fellows Program?

A two-year premier leadership development fellowship for people who earned an advanced degree (master's, professional, or doctorate) within the preceding two years, including mentorship, 160+ hours of training, and rotational assignments.
