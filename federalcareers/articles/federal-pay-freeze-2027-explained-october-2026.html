2027 Federal Pay Freeze: Who Gets 0% and Who Gets 3.8%

The August 26, 2026 alternative pay plan would freeze 2027 pay for most federal civilians while giving law enforcement a 3.8% special-rate increase. Here is who is affected, what is still undecided, and what Congress could still change.

On August 26, 2026, the administration issued an alternative pay plan under 5 U.S.C. 5303(b) and 5304a announcing the January 2027 pay decision: base and locality rates frozen at 2026 levels for most General Schedule employees, with the Office of Personnel Management (OPM) directed to use special pay authorities to provide a 3.8 percent increase for law enforcement personnel. The plan is a proposal, not a final paycheck — Congress can still change it, and OPM has not yet published final coverage for the law-enforcement increase.

Who Gets What in 2027

The plan splits the workforce three ways. Most civilian General Schedule employees — at least 1.27 million GS and GL workers by one workforce count — would see no increase to base or locality pay in January 2027. Law enforcement personnel covered by OPM's special-rate action would receive 3.8 percent, tentatively effective January 10, 2027, after OPM consults affected agencies. Active-duty military would receive 5–7 percent and other uniformed personnel 3.6 percent, as reported.

Special salary rates are not a bonus: they are basic pay for most purposes, including retirement calculations, overtime, and premium pay computed as a percentage of basic pay. OPM establishes them to address significant recruitment or retention problems — higher non-federal pay, remote locations, undesirable working conditions, or other circumstances. That is why the law-enforcement carve-out exists while the broader freeze applies.

How We Got Here: The 2026 Pattern

The 2027 proposal mirrors 2026: most employees received a 1 percent base increase effective January 11, 2026, locality pay stayed at 2025 levels, and qualifying law-enforcement officers received 3.8 percent. The stated rationale for the 2027 freeze is fiscal responsibility and economic conditions. Because FERS and CSRS pensions rest on a High-3 average salary, a frozen raise slightly slows that average's growth for employees retiring in the affected years.

What Congress Could Still Change

Congress has overridden freezes before: a 1.9 percent raise in 2019 after a freeze, and a lift from 3.0 to 3.5 percent in 2008. The current vehicle is the FAIR Act (H.R. 7480 / S. 3823), which would raise basic and prevailing-rate pay 3.1 percent and locality pay 1 percent — an average of 4.1 percent, per sponsors. Both bills remain in committee, and one analysis notes Congress has until the December spending-bill deadline to attach a raise. Until then, plan for a flat paycheck, review health choices before the December 14 FEHB deadline, and watch for the finalizing executive order.

What is still undecided: OPM has not published final coverage for the 3.8 percent law-enforcement increase. Do not assume your position is covered until the final list appears.

What Federal Employees Should Do Now

  1. Plan for a flat paycheck. Budget on 2026 pay levels unless Congress acts or your role qualifies for the law-enforcement increase.
  2. Check law-enforcement coverage. If in a law-enforcement role, watch OPM's final special-rate list.
  3. Review FEHB before December 14. Premiums are rising even as pay stays flat — compare plans in open season.
  4. Track the FAIR Act. Follow H.R. 7480 and S. 3823 on Congress.gov for movement on the 4.1 percent alternative.
  5. Watch for the executive order. Final 2027 numbers typically arrive in mid-to-late December.
