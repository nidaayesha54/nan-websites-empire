Federal Pay Raise 2027 News

The White House's August 26 alternative pay plan proposes a 0% pay raise for most civilian federal employees in 2027 — with bigger raises for law enforcement and the military. Here is the latest.

The biggest federal pay story of the year is not a raise — it is the proposed absence of one. On August 26, 2026, the President transmitted an alternative pay plan to Congress proposing a 0% pay increase for most civilian federal employees in 2027, freezing both base and locality pay at 2026 levels. Eligible federal law enforcement personnel would get 3.8%, and military members would see tiered raises of 3.6% to 7.0%. With 110 lawmakers now fighting the freeze, here is everything federal employees and job seekers need to know.

The Numbers: What the Alternative Pay Plan Proposes

Under the Federal Employees Pay Comparability Act of 1990, the President must submit an alternative pay plan by September 1 each year or allow the statutory formula to take effect. The August 26 letter states that without an alternative plan, the formula would have produced a 3.1% across-the-board base pay increase plus an average 20.6% locality pay increase — a combination the administration estimates would cost $26 billion in its first year. Citing fiscal responsibility, the plan set both adjustments to zero for civilian employees.

The Pushback: 110 Lawmakers vs. the Freeze

How GS Pay Is Actually Calculated

Confusion about the freeze usually starts with how federal pay works. OPM calculates General Schedule pay from three factors: your GS grade (GS-1 through GS-15, set by the position's responsibility level), your step within the grade (1–10, progressing with satisfactory service), and your locality pay area — there are currently 53 locality pay areas adjusting pay for regional labor costs. The proposed freeze holds all three components flat: no base increase, no locality increase.

How to Stay Ahead of the 2027 Pay Decision

  1. Know your pay plan. Confirm whether you are GS, a law enforcement pay system, or another schedule — the 3.8% exception only covers qualifying LE positions.
  2. Watch OPM in December. Final 2027 pay tables arrive with the year-end executive order; check OPM's salaries and wages pages.
  3. Track the FAIR Act and appropriations. Either could still change the outcome before the December funding deadline.
  4. Check your January LES. Verify your first 2027 Leave and Earnings Statement reflects the correct rate.
  5. Plan your career moves. In a freeze year, promotions and grade-increasing moves matter more than ever — keep applying.
Proposal, not final law: The 0% freeze is the administration's alternative pay plan proposal. Congress retains authority to override it through appropriations, and final rates are set by executive order in December. Monitor OPM announcements for confirmation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will federal employees get a raise in 2027?

As proposed, most civilian federal employees are on track for a 0% raise — base and locality pay frozen at 2026 levels. Eligible law enforcement personnel are slated for 3.8%, and military members for tiered raises of 3.6% to 7.0%. Congress could still change the outcome.

What is an alternative pay plan?

Under the Federal Employees Pay Comparability Act, the President must submit an alternative pay plan to Congress by September 1 each year or let the statutory formula take effect. The August 26, 2026 plan set 2027 civilian adjustments to zero.

What is the FAIR Act?

The Federal Adjustment of Income Rates Act, reintroduced by some lawmakers, proposes a 4.1% average raise for civilian federal employees instead of the freeze. It has not been enacted as of October 2026.

Has Congress ever overridden a pay freeze?

Yes, but rarely — only twice in recent history, in 2008 and 2019. A similar 2027 raise already failed on a party-line vote in a House Appropriations Committee markup.

When will 2027 federal pay tables be final?

Final 2027 pay tables are normally established through a presidential executive order in December. There are currently 53 locality pay areas, all of which would stay at 2026 levels under the proposal.
