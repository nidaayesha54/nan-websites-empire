White House Fellows 2026–27: Applications Open — Eligibility, What Fellows Do & How to Apply

The Office of Personnel Management has opened applications for the 2026–27 class of White House Fellows — a one-year, full-time paid placement at the highest levels of the federal government. Check the eligibility rules and how to apply.

The Office of Personnel Management (OPM) has announced that the President's Commission on White House Fellowships is now accepting applications for the 2026–27 class of White House Fellows. Founded in 1964, the program is described as the nation's premier program for leadership and public service — placing exceptional Americans in full-time, paid government roles for one year alongside cabinet secretaries, senior White House staff, and other top-ranking officials.

The 2026–27 program year runs from September 2026 through August 2027 in Washington, DC. If you are an accomplished professional or emerging leader with a record of achievement and a commitment to public service, this is one of the most prestigious one-year opportunities in American public life.

What White House Fellows Actually Do

Fellows work as full-time, paid federal employees embedded with senior leadership — aiding cabinet secretaries, senior White House staff, and other top officials on real policy and operational work.

Eligibility: Who Can Apply

The bar is clear and non-negotiable on three points, per OPM's announcement:

  • U.S. citizenship — all applicants must be United States citizens.
  • Completed undergraduate education — you must have finished your bachelor's degree before the program begins.
  • Federal employees are not eligible — with one exception: active-duty military personnel may apply.

Beyond those gates, the Commission looks for a demonstrated record of professional achievement, leadership, and commitment to service — across business, government, academia, nonprofits, medicine, law, the military, and other fields. There is no single "right" background; classes deliberately mix sectors and disciplines.

The Legacy: Where Fellows Go

The program's alumni network is extraordinary: 894 alumni across six decades, with graduates rising to top positions in the military, business, education, media, and philanthropy. Current public officials who are alumni include the Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, a former Undersecretary of Defense for Research and Engineering, U.S. Senators, and senior White House policy officials, per OPM's announcement.

That network is part of the fellowship's value.

How to Apply

Applications run through the President's Commission on White House Fellowships via the official program site linked from OPM's announcement. Expect a demanding application: detailed professional history, essays, and recommendations — followed by a multi-stage selection process including regional and national interviews for finalists.

How to Apply for the White House Fellowship

  1. Confirm eligibility. U.S. citizen, undergraduate degree completed, and not a current federal employee (unless active-duty military).
  2. Open the official application via the President's Commission on White House Fellowships portal.
  3. Line up recommenders early. Choose people who can speak to your leadership and impact with specifics.
  4. Write essays that show judgment. Selection panels reward evidence of leadership under pressure and commitment to service.
  5. Prepare for multi-stage interviews. Finalists face regional and national selection panels — practice articulating your record concisely.
Verify before you apply: application deadlines and requirements are set by the Commission and can change. Always confirm the current cycle's deadline and materials on the official White House Fellows site.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is eligible for the White House Fellows program?

U.S. citizens who have completed their undergraduate education. Federal employees are not eligible, with the exception of active-duty military personnel.

When does the 2026–27 White House Fellowship run?

The program year begins in September 2026 and concludes in August 2027, based in Washington, DC.

Is the White House Fellowship paid?

Yes. Fellows serve as full-time, paid government employees for the one-year program.

What do White House Fellows do day to day?

They work directly with cabinet secretaries, senior White House staff, and other top-ranking officials on real policy and operational work, alongside a structured leadership-development program.

How competitive is the White House Fellows program?

Extremely. Founded in 1964, it has produced 894 alumni over six decades, including Joint Chiefs chairmen, senators, and senior officials — and selection involves a demanding application plus regional and national interviews.
