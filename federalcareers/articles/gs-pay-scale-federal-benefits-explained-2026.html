GS Pay Scale and Federal Benefits Explained 2026

The General Schedule (GS) pay scale sets salaries for most white-collar federal jobs - but the real compensation story includes locality pay, FEHB health insurance, FERS retirement, and the Thrift Savings Plan. Here is the complete 2026 breakdown.

When a USAJOBS announcement lists "GS-12" and a salary range, it is speaking the language of the General Schedule (GS) - the pay system covering most white-collar federal civilian positions. Understanding GS grades, steps, and locality pay is essential to evaluating any federal offer, and the benefits package behind the salary is a major part of total compensation. This guide explains the GS pay scale and federal benefits for 2026.

How the GS Pay Scale Works

The General Schedule has 15 grades (GS-1 through GS-15), each with 10 steps. Your grade reflects the complexity and responsibility of the position; your step reflects longevity and performance within the grade. Employees typically advance one step every 1-3 years (depending on the step) through waiting periods, plus quality step increases for outstanding performance.

  • GS-1 to GS-4: Entry-level, trainee, and support roles.
  • GS-5 to GS-8: Entry-level professional roles - the typical starting point for bachelor's degree holders (often GS-5 or GS-7).
  • GS-9 to GS-12: Mid-level professional and specialist roles - master's holders often enter around GS-9.
  • GS-13 to GS-15: Senior experts, supervisors, and program managers.

Above GS-15 sits the Senior Executive Service (SES), a separate system for top agency leadership.

Sample 2026 Salary Ranges by Grade

Base GS pay is adjusted by locality pay - an addition reflecting the cost of labor in your duty station's metro area. Washington, D.C., San Francisco, and New York carry the highest locality adjustments; "Rest of U.S." the lowest. Locality pay matters enormously: The same GS-12 Step 1 can pay roughly $25,000+ more in San Francisco than in a "Rest of U.S." location. When comparing offers, compare the locality-adjusted figure for the actual duty station - not the base table.

Federal Benefits: The Other Half of Compensation

Health Insurance (FEHB)

The Federal Employees Health Benefits program offers a wide choice of nationwide and regional plans, with the government paying roughly 70% of premiums. Coverage continues into retirement for eligible annuitants - one of the most valuable long-term benefits in American employment.

Retirement (FERS)

Most employees hired since 1984 are under the Federal Employees Retirement System (FERS) - a three-part package: a basic pension based on years of service and high-3 average salary, Social Security, and the Thrift Savings Plan. Law enforcement officers, firefighters, and air traffic controllers have enhanced FERS provisions with earlier retirement eligibility.

Thrift Savings Plan (TSP)

The federal 401(k)-equivalent: the government automatically contributes 1% of pay and matches up to 5% of employee contributions. The match vests quickly and compounds over a career - contributing at least 5% from day one is the single highest-return move a new fed can make.

Leave and Holidays

Full-time employees earn 13 to 26 days of annual leave per year depending on service length (13 days under 3 years, 20 days for 3-15 years, 26 days after 15 years), plus 13 sick days annually and 11 paid federal holidays. Unused annual leave can be carried over up to 240 hours in most cases.

Total Compensation: Putting It Together

A GS-12 offer of $99,200-$128,956 is only the starting point. Add locality pay, the government's ~70% health-premium share, 5% TSP matching, pension accrual, and 4-5+ weeks of paid time off, and total compensation runs substantially above the salary line. When comparing a federal offer to private-sector pay, price the benefits - many feds find the total package beats a higher private salary once health costs and retirement are counted.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the GS pay scale?

The General Schedule is the federal white-collar pay system: 15 grades (GS-1 to GS-15), each with 10 steps. Salary depends on grade, step, and locality pay for the duty station.

What is locality pay?

An additional percentage added to base GS pay reflecting labor costs in your metro area. High-cost areas like San Francisco, New York, and Washington, D.C. have the largest adjustments.

What benefits do federal employees get?

FEHB health insurance (government pays ~70% of premiums), FERS pension plus Social Security, Thrift Savings Plan with up to 5% matching, 13-26 days annual leave, 13 sick days, and 11 paid holidays.

How do I move up GS grades?

Through time-in-grade progression (typically 52 weeks at the next-lower grade for promotion eligibility), step increases within a grade, and competitive promotions to higher-graded positions via merit promotion announcements.

Is the SES part of the GS scale?

No. The Senior Executive Service is a separate system above GS-15 for top agency executives, with its own pay bands and performance-based compensation.
