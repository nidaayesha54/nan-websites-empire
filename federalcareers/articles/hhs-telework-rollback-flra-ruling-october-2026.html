On October 21, 2026, the Federal Labor Relations Authority (FLRA) upheld a union challenge to the HHS telework rollback — and on October 1, Rep. Suhas Subramanyam (D-VA) introduced the Federal Telework Protection Act (H.R. 10713) to restore federal telework rights.

What the FLRA Ruling Actually Says

The case turned on the 1978 Federal Services Labor-Management Relations Statute, which makes it an unfair labor practice to enforce a rule conflicting with a pre-existing collective bargaining agreement. Per FLRA precedent, a presidential memorandum "is not a governmentwide rule or regulation that the employer is obligated by law to implement immediately upon issuance."

The ruling noted HHS seemingly used generative AI tools to draft its legal brief, with "fictitious, erroneous, or irrelevant" citations. HHS says it still allows situational telework, military-spouse flexibilities and disability accommodations.

What this means practically: negotiated telework terms still carry legal weight — leadership cannot nullify them by pointing to a presidential memorandum.

The Federal Telework Protection Act (H.R. 10713)

Introduced October 1, 2026 by Rep. Suhas Subramanyam (D-VA), the bill's key provisions:

  • Formal requests. Supervisors must weigh medical, caregiving, disability, commute and job-requirement factors.
  • Interim relief. Urgent medical cases get 10–15 business days of telework while the request is pending.
  • Written decisions. Every decision needs a written explanation; silence after 90 days means approval.
  • Real appeals. Denied requests can be appealed to agency telework offices, then the MSPB. Retaliation is prohibited.
  • Funding ban. No federal funds may be used to implement the "Return to In Person Work" memorandum of January 20, 2025 or associated directives.
  • Restored bargaining. Previously negotiated telework clauses return; agencies can bargain over telework terms again.
Reality check: the bill faces long odds while the GOP controls Congress, and a veto threat remains. Its near-term value is political — a template for post-election action.

Where Telework Policy Stands Right Now

The status quo still governs: the January 20, 2025 memorandum ordered agencies to end remote work, and OPM's January 2026 guidance says telework may not "shorten" the workday — with exceptions only for military spouses, disabilities, certain medical conditions and "compelling" reasons.

About 317,000 federal workers left their jobs in 2025, per OPM data — a figure now central to every debate about federal retention and recruitment.

What This Means for You as a Federal Worker

  1. Know your CBA. Negotiated telework terms still have legal weight — check with your union first.
  2. Document accommodation requests. Telework can be a reasonable accommodation under the Rehabilitation Act — put requests in writing.
  3. Track H.R. 10713 — but don't bank on it. The bill is a signal, not a lifeline, until the legislative math changes.
  4. Ask about telework before accepting a job. Availability varies widely — confirm it in writing.

Frequently Asked Questions

What did the FLRA rule about the HHS telework rollback?

The FLRA upheld the union challenge to HHS's telework rollback on October 21, 2026: a presidential memorandum is not a governmentwide rule an agency must implement immediately if it conflicts with a pre-existing collective bargaining agreement.

What is the Federal Telework Protection Act (H.R. 10713)?

A House bill (Rep. Suhas Subramanyam, D-VA, October 1, 2026) creating a formal telework request and appeal process, barring funds for the January 2025 return-to-office memo, and restoring negotiated telework terms.

What telework rights would H.R. 10713 give federal employees?

Supervisors must weigh medical, caregiving, disability and commute factors; urgent medical cases get 10–15 days of interim telework; silence after 90 days means approval; denials can be appealed up to the MSPB.

What is the current federal telework policy under the Trump administration?

The January 20, 2025 memorandum ordered agencies to end remote work; January 2026 OPM guidance narrowed exceptions to military spouses, disabilities, certain medical conditions and compelling reasons.
