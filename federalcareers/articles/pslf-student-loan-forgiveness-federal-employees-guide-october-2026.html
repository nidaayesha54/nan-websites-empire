PSLF for Federal Employees: Student Loan Forgiveness Guide 2026

Every federal job is a qualifying employer for Public Service Loan Forgiveness. The 120-payment rule, qualifying plans, 2026 changes, and how to certify — explained.

Here is a benefit worth potentially tens of thousands of dollars that comes free with your federal job: every federal agency is a qualifying employer for Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF). After 120 qualifying monthly payments on federal Direct Loans while working full-time in government, your remaining balance is forgiven — federally tax-free. This guide breaks down the four requirements, the 2026 repayment-plan changes, and the certification steps federal employees should take now. Program rules evolve; confirm your loan and repayment-plan status with your servicer and StudentAid.gov.

The Four Requirements (All in the Same Month)

For a month to count toward your 120, four things must be true simultaneously:

  1. Qualifying employment — full-time work for a government or nonprofit employer at some point that month. Federal civilian service and military service both count.
  2. Qualifying loans — federal Direct Loans. Private loans never qualify, and refinancing federal debt privately ends PSLF eligibility permanently.
  3. Qualifying repayment plan — an income-driven plan (or the 10-year Standard plan, though you would pay the loan off before forgiveness kicks in).
  4. Qualifying payment — the full scheduled amount, on time. The 120 payments do not need to be consecutive — leave government, come back, and your count resumes.

2026 Changes Federal Borrowers Must Know

Two developments matter right now. First, borrowers on the SAVE plan are being moved off it: starting July 1, 2026, affected borrowers get at least 90 days' notice to switch to another qualifying IDR plan (including the new Repayment Assistance Plan) or risk losing PSLF-qualifying months. Second, PSLF processing has slowed — expect roughly 3–6 months for application reviews. Proposed eligibility restrictions floated for mid-2026 were blocked by federal judges, so the core program remains intact — but stay proactive.

Why Federal Employment Is the Ideal PSLF Path

Federal workers have structural advantages: guaranteed qualifying employer status (no EIN lookup anxiety), stable full-time schedules that easily clear the 30-hour bar, and annual certification built into HR routines. A typical graduate-degree borrower can see six figures forgiven. The single highest-leverage habit: submit the PSLF Employment Certification Form annually (and whenever you change agencies) through StudentAid.gov so your payment count stays accurate.

Common mistakes that cost years: never consolidating FFEL loans into Direct Loans; sitting on the wrong repayment plan; assuming an employer qualifies without checking the EIN tool; and skipping annual certification until year nine.

Your PSLF Action Plan as a Federal Employee

  1. Confirm Direct Loans. Log in to StudentAid.gov; consolidate any FFEL or Perkins loans into a Direct Consolidation Loan if needed.
  2. Pick a qualifying IDR plan. If you are on SAVE, watch for your servicer's 90-day switch notice and move to a qualifying plan.
  3. Certify employment annually. Submit the PSLF form each year — your agency HR signs the employment section.
  4. Track your count. Review qualifying-payment counts on StudentAid.gov after each certification.
  5. Apply at 120. Submit the final PSLF application; continue working full-time until forgiveness is granted.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do federal jobs qualify for PSLF?

Yes. All U.S. federal government employment — civilian and military — counts as qualifying employment for Public Service Loan Forgiveness.

How many payments does PSLF require?

120 qualifying monthly payments — roughly 10 years. They do not have to be consecutive; your count pauses and resumes if you leave qualifying employment.

What loans qualify for PSLF?

Federal Direct Loans only. FFEL and Perkins loans must first be consolidated into a Direct Consolidation Loan. Private student loans never qualify.

What counts as full-time for PSLF?

An average of at least 30 hours per week. If you work for two qualifying employers, you can combine hours to meet the threshold.

Is forgiven PSLF debt taxed?

No. Amounts forgiven under PSLF are not taxed at the federal level under current law.

What is happening to SAVE plan borrowers in 2026?

Borrowers on the SAVE plan are being transitioned off it; starting July 1, 2026, affected borrowers receive at least 90 days' notice to switch to another qualifying repayment plan to keep earning PSLF credit.
