AAAS Science & Technology Policy Fellowships 2027–28: Deadline Nov 1 — Apply Now

The AAAS Science & Technology Policy Fellowships put PhD scientists and engineers inside Congress and federal agencies as policy advisers. Applications for the 2027–28 class close November 1 — here is how to apply.

Scientists and engineers who want to shape public policy have a premier on-ramp: the AAAS Science & Technology Policy Fellowships (STPF), placing doctoral-level STEM professionals as advisers across Congress, federal agencies, and the courts. Applications for the 2027–2028 class opened August 1, 2026 — deadline November 1, 2026, 11:59 PM Eastern, just two weeks away.

Fellows do substantive work: advising lawmakers and officials on technical issues, analyzing and drafting legislation, translating cutting-edge science into policy — across AI, cybersecurity, biotech, semiconductors, energy, quantum, and space.

New for 2027–28: a dedicated Legislative Branch cohort in AI, embedding fellows in congressional offices for September 1, 2027–August 31, 2028.

What Fellows Do and Where They Serve

STPF spans legislative, executive, and judicial placements plus diplomacy assignments: a fellow might advise a congressional office, embed in an agency policy shop, or support international science diplomacy — always as the technical expert in the room.

The work is consequential — fellows' analyses shape bill provisions, regulations, and negotiating positions — and the 4,000+ STPF alumni network across government, industry, and academia compounds as a career asset for decades.

Eligibility: The Doctoral Bar

Applicants need a PhD or equivalent doctoral degree at application, or a master's in engineering with three-plus years of post-degree experience. U.S. citizenship is required for most placements, including State Department slots.

Selection favors communication skill and policy motivation: the application includes a candidate statement, biography, CV, and references, with semifinalist interviews in spring. Foreground any science communication, outreach, or policy engagement.

Timeline and the AI Cohort

AAAS is recruiting an AI-focused Legislative Branch cohort — scientists and engineers across AI, machine learning, data science, robotics, and related fields to serve as embedded congressional staff during the September 2027–August 2028 term.

Timeline: November 1, 2026 (11:59 PM ET) application deadline; spring semifinalist interviews; placement offers by early May; fellowship year September 1–August 31, renewable up to 12 months at most placements.

How to Apply for the AAAS S&T Policy Fellowships

  1. Confirm the credential bar. Doctoral degree in hand (or engineering master's + 3 years experience) plus U.S. citizenship for most placements.
  2. Open the AAAS portal immediately. With the November 1 deadline two weeks out, review every required component now.
  3. Craft the candidate statement and biography. These carry your policy motivation and communication ability — the key differentiators.
  4. Line up references early. Choose people who vouch for technical depth and communication with non-technical audiences.
  5. Flag AI-cohort interest if relevant. AI, ML, data science, or robotics backgrounds fit the dedicated congressional AI cohort.
  6. Submit by November 1, 11:59 PM ET. Late or incomplete applications — including missing references — are not considered.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the deadline for the 2027–28 AAAS fellowships?

Applications for the 2027–2028 class opened August 1, 2026; the deadline is November 1, 2026, at 11:59 PM Eastern Time.

Who is eligible for the AAAS S&T Policy Fellowships?

A PhD or equivalent doctoral degree at application — or a master's in engineering plus three years of post-degree experience. U.S. citizenship is required for most placements.

Where are AAAS fellows placed?

Across Congress, federal agencies, and the judiciary — including legislative, executive, and diplomacy tracks, plus a dedicated Legislative Branch AI cohort for 2027–28.

How long is the fellowship?

A year-long term, September 1 to August 31, renewable for up to 12 additional months at most placements.

How does this differ from the IEEE-USA fellowship?

The IEEE-USA fellowship is run by the engineering society with Congressional and Diplomacy tracks. AAAS STPF is the larger program placing doctoral-level scientists across all three branches of government.
