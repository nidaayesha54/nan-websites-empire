2027 Federal Pay Freeze Update (Oct 20): 0% for Most GS, 3.8% Law Enforcement, 5–7% Military

As of October 20, 2026, the 2027 federal pay freeze remains the plan: 0% for most civilian GS employees, 3.8% for federal law enforcement, and 5–7% for military members. But Congress could still change the outcome before December — here is the latest status and what to watch.

P27 Pay Freeze Update

The 2027 federal pay picture remains exactly where the August alternative pay plan left it: most civilian federal employees are scheduled to receive no 2027 pay increase, with base and locality pay frozen at 2026 rates. But the outcome is not yet final — and the next two months contain the last real opportunities to change it. Here is the status as of October 20, 2026.

The Current Plan: Who Gets What

On August 26, 2026, the President transmitted the annual alternative pay plan required under the Federal Employees Pay Comparability Act (FEPCA), setting the 2027 adjustment at zero for most civilian GS employees — no base-pay increase and no locality-pay increase. Without that alternative plan, the statutory formula would have produced roughly a 3.1% across-the-board base increase plus locality adjustments averaging over 20%.

The carve-outs: federal law enforcement personnel on special rate tables are slated for a 3.8% raise through OPM's special salary-rate authority, and military members are set for tiered raises of 7% (junior enlisted E1–E5), 6% (E6–O3), and 5% (senior officers O4+) — one of the widest civilian-military pay gaps in the modern General Schedule era.

What Could Still Change: Congress and December

The freeze is a proposal, not a signed order. Pay rates are not locked in until the President signs the annual executive order, typically in mid-to-late December. Before that, Congress can legislate a different number — and there is an active push to do so.

A bipartisan group of 110 lawmakers asked congressional leadership in a September 15 letter to reject the freeze. Separately, the Federal Workforce Caucus and union leaders are pressing for a 4.1% raise under the FAIR Act. The key vehicle: the stopgap funding law expires December 11, 2026, and the spending bill that replaces it is the last scheduled legislative opportunity to override the freeze before the December pay order.

Why This Freeze Hits Harder Than Past Ones

Three factors compound the impact. First, the freeze follows only a 1% base-pay raise in 2026 (with no locality increase), so two consecutive years of below-inflation adjustments are eroding real purchasing power. Second, for employees near retirement, a frozen year inside the High-3 window permanently lowers the FERS pension calculation — the freeze is not just a one-year pay story.

Third, the civilian-military gap: with service members receiving 5–7% while civilians get 0%, the divergence is the widest in decades — a point federal employee groups are using heavily in their lobbying for the FAIR Act alternative.

What Federal Employees Should Do Before December

You cannot control the outcome, but you can control your positioning. If you are due for a within-grade increase (WGI), the freeze does not block it — WGIs, promotions, and other individual pay actions continue normally. Make sure your performance documentation supports your next WGI date.

If you are in a law enforcement or shortage occupation, verify whether your position falls under the 3.8% special salary rates — OPM determines eligibility, and the designation matters enormously to your January paycheck. And if retirement is on your horizon, model your High-3 with and without the freeze year before making timing decisions; a consultation with a federal benefits specialist is worthwhile.

Timeline to watch: the December 11 funding deadline is the last real legislative window, and the December executive order finalizes the rates. Expect the definitive answer in the second half of December 2026.

What Federal Employees Should Do Before December

  1. Confirm your WGI date. Within-grade increases are not blocked by the freeze — make sure your performance record supports your next step increase.
  2. Check special-rate eligibility. If you are in law enforcement, verify with your HR whether your position qualifies for the 3.8% special salary rates.
  3. Model your High-3. If retirement is near, calculate your pension with a frozen year in the window before deciding on timing.
  4. Watch December 11. The spending bill replacing the stopgap is the last legislative chance to override the freeze.
  5. Wait for the December executive order. Rates are not final until the President signs the annual pay order — treat everything before that as the plan, not the outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the 2027 federal pay freeze final?

No. As of October 20, 2026, the freeze is the administration's plan under the August 26 alternative pay plan, but it is not final until the President signs the December executive order — and Congress could still legislate a different raise.

Who gets a raise in 2027 under the current plan?

Most civilian GS employees: 0%. Federal law enforcement on special rate tables: 3.8%. Military members: 7% for junior enlisted (E1–E5), 6% for E6–O3, 5% for senior officers (O4+).

What is the FAIR Act and what raise does it propose?

The FAIR Act is legislation backed by the Federal Workforce Caucus and federal employee unions proposing a 4.1% federal pay raise for 2027 as an alternative to the freeze.

When will the 2027 federal pay rates be finalized?

Typically by a presidential executive order in mid-to-late December 2026, after which OPM publishes the official pay tables. The December 11 funding deadline is the last legislative window to change the outcome.

Does the pay freeze block within-grade increases or promotions?

No. The freeze covers across-the-board base and locality adjustments only. Within-grade increases, promotions, and other individual pay actions continue normally.
