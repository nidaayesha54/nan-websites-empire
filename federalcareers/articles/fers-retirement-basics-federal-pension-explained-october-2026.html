FERS Retirement Basics: How the Federal Pension Works (2026)

FERS is the three-part retirement system for federal employees: a pension, Social Security, and TSP. Eligibility ages, the benefit formula, and the supplement — explained.

The Federal Employees Retirement System (FERS) covers most federal civilian employees — and unlike most private-sector jobs, it still includes a traditional pension. FERS is a three-part system: a basic pension annuity, Social Security, and the Thrift Savings Plan (TSP). Understanding how the three pieces fit together is the foundation of every federal retirement plan. This guide covers eligibility ages, the benefit formula, and the special supplement that bridges early retirees to Social Security. Figures below reflect OPM rules; run your personal estimate with OPM's calculators and your agency HR.

Pillar 1: The Basic Benefit (Your Pension)

The FERS pension is calculated from your "high-3" — the average of your highest three consecutive years of basic pay — multiplied by your years of creditable service and a percentage factor. The standard factor is 1% per year of service; it rises to 1.1% if you retire at age 62 or older with at least 20 years of service. Example: 30 years at a $100,000 high-3 yields roughly $30,000/year (more at the 1.1% tier). Special groups — federal law enforcement, firefighters, and air traffic controllers — earn 1.7% for the first 20 years.

Pillar 2 & 3: Social Security and TSP

FERS employees pay into and earn Social Security like private-sector workers. The third pillar, the Thrift Savings Plan, is the government's 401(k)-style account with matching contributions — financial planners routinely call capturing the full agency match (by contributing at least 5%) the single most important FERS move. Together, the three pillars are designed to replace a far larger share of working income than any one piece alone.

When Can You Retire?

Eligibility depends on age and service. The main immediate (unreduced) combinations:

  • Minimum Retirement Age (MRA) with 30 years — the earliest unreduced path for most feds.
  • Age 60 with 20 years of service.
  • Age 62 with 5 years of service.
  • MRA with 10 years (MRA+10) — allowed, but the annuity is reduced 5% for each year under 62.

MRA itself slides with birth year: 55 (born before 1948) up to 57 (born 1970 or later). Early-out options exist too — age 50 with 20 years, or any age with 25 years — when your agency offers voluntary early retirement (VERA), and disability retirement requires only 18 months of service.

The Special Retirement Supplement

Retire before 62 and you face a gap before Social Security starts. FERS fills it with the Special Retirement Supplement — roughly your years of service divided by 40, times your age-62 Social Security benefit — paid until you turn 62. Note the fine print: regular FERS retirees get no cost-of-living adjustments (COLA) until 62, and keeping FEHB health coverage into retirement requires retiring on an immediate annuity plus five continuous years of FEHB enrollment before retiring.

Your FERS Planning Checklist

  1. Contribute at least 5% to TSP. Capture the full agency match from day one — it compounds for decades.
  2. Know your MRA and service date. Your earliest unreduced retirement date drives every other decision.
  3. Protect FEHB continuity. Stay continuously enrolled for the five years before any planned retirement.
  4. Model the supplement. If retiring before 62, factor the Special Retirement Supplement into your income bridge.
  5. Start formal planning ~5 years out. Work with your agency HR and OPM's tools to verify service credit and estimates.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is FERS?

The Federal Employees Retirement System — the retirement system for most federal civilian employees, with three parts: a basic pension annuity, Social Security, and the Thrift Savings Plan.

How is the FERS pension calculated?

High-3 average salary × years of creditable service × 1% (or 1.1% if you retire at 62+ with 20+ years). Special categories like law enforcement earn 1.7% for the first 20 years.

What is the minimum retirement age under FERS?

MRA ranges from 55 to 57 depending on birth year — 57 for anyone born in 1970 or later.

When can I retire with an unreduced FERS annuity?

At MRA with 30 years, age 60 with 20 years, or age 62 with 5 years of service. MRA+10 is allowed with a 5%-per-year reduction under age 62.

What is the FERS Special Retirement Supplement?

A bridge payment for eligible retirees under 62, approximately (years of service ÷ 40) × your age-62 Social Security benefit, paid until you turn 62.

Can I keep FEHB health coverage in retirement?

Yes, if you retire on an immediate annuity and were continuously enrolled in FEHB for the five years immediately before retiring.
