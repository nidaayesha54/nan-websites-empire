IEEE-USA Government Fellowship 2027–28: Deadline Jan 8, 2027 — How to Apply

The IEEE-USA Government Fellowship puts engineers and technologists inside Congress and federal agencies as high-level policy advisors. Applications for the 2027–28 cycle close January 8, 2027.

Engineers who want to shape public policy have a proven on-ramp: the IEEE-USA Government Fellowship Program, which places science and technology professionals as high-level advisors in Congress, federal agencies, and diplomatic posts. Applications for the 2027–2028 cycle are open now, with a deadline of January 8, 2027, at 12:00 PM Eastern Time.

An important distinction: fellows are not employees of Congress or of IEEE. Instead, they serve as expert advisors — the technical translators who help policymakers understand everything from semiconductor supply chains to AI safety, spectrum policy, and energy systems.

For STEM professionals eyeing a pivot into policy — or a prestigious credential that opens doors across government, think tanks, and industry — this fellowship is one of the most direct routes available.

What Fellows Do

Fellows embed with congressional offices, committees, or federal agencies, where their duties include assessing and drafting proposed legislation; advising members of Congress or federal officials on complex technical issues; translating intricate engineering concepts into actionable policy recommendations; conducting research and preparing technical policy briefs for committees and offices; and — on the Diplomacy track — supporting international diplomatic efforts related to science and technology.

It is substantive, visible work: a fellow's analysis can directly shape the technical provisions of a bill or an agency's regulatory approach.

Tracks and Who Should Apply

The program operates across Congressional and Diplomacy tracks, letting fellows match their interests to legislative work, agency advising, or international science diplomacy. IEEE-USA looks for candidates with a strong science, engineering, or technology background, excellent verbal and written communication skills, and a demonstrated interest in public policy.

You do not need prior Hill experience — but you do need evidence that you can communicate technical ideas to non-technical audiences, whether through writing, teaching, outreach, or prior policy engagement.

Application Timeline and Process

The deadline for the 2027–2028 cycle is January 8, 2027, at 12:00 PM ET. IEEE-USA advises candidates to monitor the official IEEE-USA Government Fellowship website for application materials and to review the Program Policies and Procedures carefully before applying to confirm eligibility.

As with any competitive fellowship: start early, secure strong recommenders who can speak to both your technical depth and your communication ability, and tailor your materials to the track you are targeting.

How to Apply for the IEEE-USA Government Fellowship

  1. Confirm you meet the eligibility bar. Strong STEM credentials, excellent written and spoken communication, and a demonstrable interest in public policy — review IEEE-USA's Program Policies and Procedures first.
  2. Choose your track. Decide between the Congressional and Diplomacy tracks based on where your expertise and interests fit best.
  3. Monitor the official IEEE-USA fellowship page for the 2027–2028 application materials and any information sessions.
  4. Build a policy-facing application. Highlight writing, briefing, teaching, or outreach that proves you can translate technical concepts for policymakers.
  5. Line up recommenders early. Choose people who can vouch for both technical depth and communication skill — ideally with policy relevance.
  6. Submit by January 8, 2027, 12:00 PM ET. Fellowship deadlines are absolute; plan to submit at least several days ahead.
