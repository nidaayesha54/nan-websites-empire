SES Candidate Development Program 2026: New Rules & How to Apply

An SES Candidate Development Program (SESCDP) is one of two routes into the Senior Executive Service. OPM's final rule, effective July 27, 2026, rewrote the program requirements — here is what changed, what the new ECQs demand, and how GS-14/15 feds find open cohorts.

The SES Candidate Development Program (SESCDP) is the structured training route into the Senior Executive Service — and 2026 changed it. OPM's final rule (effective July 27, 2026) imposed stricter requirements on agency cohorts: minimum 12-month programs, 180-day developmental assignments, and two ECQ-tied assessments. If you are a GS-14 or GS-15 with executive ambitions, here is what the program is now and how to find an open cohort.

What an SESCDP Is — and Why It Matters

There are two ways into the SES: apply directly for a specific vacancy, or graduate from an SESCDP and earn certification from an OPM-administered Qualifications Review Board (QRB). QRB-certified graduates become eligible for career SES appointment without further competition — not guaranteed a job, but cleared of the hardest hurdle: proving your Executive Core Qualifications (ECQs) to an independent board.

The program combines interagency training, rotational assignments, and senior mentoring. It is not required for the SES — many executives entered through direct application — but for GS-14/15 candidates without deep executive experience, it is the most deliberate credential-building path available.

What the 2026 Final Rule Changed

The final rule (effective July 27, 2026), following a January 2025 presidential memorandum and a December 2025 proposal, set the framework now in force:

  • Program length: minimum 12 months, maximum 24 months.
  • Developmental assignments: at least 180 continuous days.
  • Two validated assessments: one at selection, a second during the program, both tied to the ECQs.
  • Succession planning: SESCDPs are positioned inside agency succession planning.

For applicants: today's cohorts are shorter, more assessment-driven, and more closely overseen by OPM.

The current ECQs (per USAJOBS): commitment to the rule of law and the principles of the American founding; driving efficiency; merit and competence; leading people; achieving results.

How to Apply: Where Cohorts Appear

There is no single SESCDP application window — each agency announces its own cohorts on USAJOBS, identified by the ES pay scale and ECQ requirements in the announcement.

The differentiator is recent, executive-level accomplishments with measurable outcomes. The ECQs are evidence categories, not job duties — describe results attributable to your leadership.

How to Apply for an SES Candidate Development Program

  1. Track USAJOBS senior executive postings. Use the senior executive hiring path and saved searches ("candidate development program") so new cohorts reach you first.
  2. Verify OPM approval. Agencies needed OPM approval before launching new cohorts — post-July-2026 announcements reflect the new rules.
  3. Build ECQ evidence now. You face validated assessments at selection and during the program. Document executive-level results — budget scope, outcomes, cross-agency work — under each ECQ.
  4. Expect the 180-day assignment. The rule requires a long, continuous rotation — plan accordingly before accepting a slot.
  5. Finish the QRB package promptly. Submit the certification package within about 90 days of graduation — certification unlocks non-competitive SES appointment eligibility.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to complete an SES Candidate Development Program to join the SES?

No. Either apply directly for an SES vacancy, or graduate from an SESCDP and earn QRB certification for eligibility without further competition.

Who is eligible for an SES Candidate Development Program?

Typically GS-14/15 or equivalent; each agency's USAJOBS announcement sets exact requirements.

How long is an SESCDP under the 2026 rules?

12–24 months, with 180-day developmental assignments and two ECQ assessments. Effective July 27, 2026.

What are the current Executive Core Qualifications (ECQs)?

Commitment to the rule of law and the principles of the American founding; driving efficiency; merit and competence; leading people; and achieving results.

Does QRB certification guarantee me an SES job?

No. Certification gives eligibility, not a guarantee — placement depends on vacancies and agency selection.
