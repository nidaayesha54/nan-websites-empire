GTA 6 Busted: What Getting Arrested Really Costs You

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and every player in Leonida will see the same two screens sooner or later: WASTED and BUSTED. Most guides cover the first one in detail — dying costs you the cash in your pocket — but getting arrested is a different event with its own rules, its own escape, and its own bill. The wanted system in GTA 6 is more lethal than ever, and when the police finally catch Jason or Lucia, the consequences are not the same as dying.

That matters because arrests are not rare. The police in GTA 6 can catch you without killing you: you can surrender, or officers can take you down without lethal force, and the game has a capture sequence of its own. Knowing what happens when the BUSTED screen appears — and what you can do before it does — is the difference between losing an afternoon's earnings and losing everything you were carrying.

How Arrests Happen: Surrender or Capture

Getting arrested starts with the wanted system, and the police act on what they actually witness or what a witness reports — there is no magic GPS that knows where you are. When officers close in, you have two ways to end up in cuffs. You can surrender, which is the game's way of letting you give up a chase you cannot win. Or the police can catch you: officers can tackle you to the ground and restrain you, and the game gives you a chance to struggle free — if the struggle fails, you are taken in.

That struggle is the one moment where an arrest is different from a death. Dying is instant; an arrest is a sequence, and the game shows the officer bringing you down before the screen fades. The reports from launch week are consistent on what follows: the character who is caught is taken to Leonida Prison, and the game strips away the weapons and the money they were carrying. The exact destination is reported rather than confirmed by Rockstar, but every launch-era account describes the same outcome — you lose what you had on you.

What You Lose — and What Survives

The bill for getting arrested looks a lot like the bill for dying, and that is the point. Any money in your pocket that was not deposited in the bank is gone. That is the same rule as dying: cash only becomes safe once it reaches an ATM and goes into your BuckMe account, and until then one bad moment wipes it. A stash bag filled with cash and contraband is also lost when you are taken in, just as it is when you die — the robbery is not over until the money is banked.

Weapons work the same way too, with one mercy. Any weapon you picked up from another character and never added to your loadout is gone. But the weapons you actually own are not lost for good: you can get them back from your personal vehicle's trunk or from a weapon locker at Ammu-Nation, where you re-equip your sidearms and longarms. Vehicles work the same way — a car you were driving without saving it is gone, while the cars you own stay yours. The practical rule is the same as for dying: before anything dangerous, deposit your money at an ATM or stash it at your safehouse, because the cash in your pocket is the money you can lose.

After the Busted Screen: Where You Wake Up

Here is where GTA 6 breaks with the old games. In GTA 5, getting busted sent you to the entrance of the nearest police station, and getting wasted sent you to the nearest hospital. In GTA 6, both screens work the same new way: you respawn at a location close to where you were taken down, the same system Red Dead Redemption 2 used. You do not have to fight your way back across the map to get revenge or pick up where you left off, and you do not have to walk out of a hospital or a police station to do it.

That change is a mercy in a map as large as Leonida, but it does not make arrests free. The hospital fee that GTA 5 charged is gone — GTA 6 has no treatment fee at all — but the money you were carrying is still gone, and the weapons you had not secured are still gone. If a story mission ends because you were arrested, you retry from a checkpoint, just as you would after dying. The arrest costs you the same as a death, plus the time it took the police to catch you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What happens when you get Busted in GTA 6?

You are taken to Leonida Prison and stripped of the weapons and money you were carrying. You then respawn near where you were arrested, and you can recover owned weapons from your vehicle's trunk or an Ammu-Nation locker.

Can you escape an arrest in GTA 6?

When officers tackle you, the game gives you a chance to struggle free. If the struggle fails, you are taken in. Surrendering is also an option when a chase is unwinnable.

Is getting Busted worse than getting Wasted?

The losses are nearly the same: pocket cash, unowned weapons and stash bags are gone either way. The difference is the route — an arrest is a capture sequence with a struggle, while death is instant — and neither one charges a hospital or police fee.

The Bottom Line

Getting arrested in GTA 6 costs you what you were carrying: your pocket cash, your unsecured weapons and your stash. The only defence is the same one that works against dying — bank your money at an ATM before you do anything dangerous, keep your owned weapons in your trunk or a locker, and remember that in Leonida, the arrest is not the punishment. Losing the cash is.
