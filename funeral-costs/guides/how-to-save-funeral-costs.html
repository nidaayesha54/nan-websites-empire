10 Ways to Reduce Funeral Costs — With Full Dignity

Spending less on a funeral is not about loving someone less. The most meaningful farewells are rarely the most expensive ones. Here are ten honest, respectful ways families save thousands.

1. Consider cremation instead of burial

The single biggest saving. Direct cremation ($2,550–$3,300) versus traditional burial ($15,250–$17,200) — a difference of roughly $12,000. Many families follow cremation with a personal celebration of life that guests remember far longer than a formal service.

2. Buy the casket elsewhere

Funeral home caskets carry markups of 300–500%. The identical style online can cost half as much, and federal law forbids the funeral home from charging a handling fee. This one decision commonly saves $1,500–$3,000.

3. Skip embalming when it's not needed

Embalming ($500–$1,200) is almost never legally required — especially for cremation or prompt burial. If there's no public viewing with an open casket, you can simply decline it.

4. Decline the package; choose items individually

Packages bundle things you may not want: limousines, premium prayer cards, video tributes at $400+. Selecting only what matters to your family routinely cuts 15–25% off a packaged quote.

5. Hold the service somewhere meaningful — not necessarily the funeral home chapel

Facility fees run $500–$3,000. A service at a family home, a park, a place of worship your loved one attended (often free or low-cost for members), or a favorite restaurant's private room can be more personal and far cheaper.

6. Ask about a graveside-only or memorial-only service

Skipping the funeral home viewing and holding a short graveside service — or a memorial after cremation — eliminates viewing fees, embalming, and facility charges in one move.

7. Compare at least three funeral homes

The same service has been found $7,355 apart between two homes in one state. Call, ask for the General Price List, and compare. An hour of calls can save thousands.

8. Donate to science or consider whole-body donation

Accredited body donation programs typically cremate at no cost to the family and return ashes afterward. It's a choice that helps medical education and removes nearly all funeral expense. Research programs carefully and discuss with family.

9. Plan the reception simply

Post-service gatherings at $50–$100 per head add up fast. A home gathering with food from family and friends is warmer, more personal, and costs a fraction of catered funeral-home receptions.

10. Preplan — for yourself, one day

The kindest financial gift: writing down your own wishes and setting aside funds. Preplanning locks in today's clarity and spares your family every decision on this list during their hardest week. Funeral prices rose 17% in three years — planning early protects against that.

Frequently asked questions

Is it disrespectful to choose the cheapest option? +
No. Respect lives in how you honor someone's memory — the stories shared, the people gathered — not in the price of a casket. Most families who choose simpler options report feeling more at peace with the decision.
What's the cheapest dignified funeral option? +
Direct cremation ($2,550–$3,300) followed by a family gathering or celebration of life. It's simple, legal everywhere, and leaves room for a truly personal farewell.
Does the funeral home have to honor my choices? +
Yes. Under the FTC Funeral Rule, they must let you select individual items, accept an outside casket, and provide itemized pricing. Read our guide to your rights.
