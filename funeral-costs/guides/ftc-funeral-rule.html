The FTC Funeral Rule: Your Rights, in Plain English

Most families have never heard of the FTC Funeral Rule — and funeral homes don't always volunteer it. This federal law, in place since 1984, is the single strongest protection grieving families have. Here's what it guarantees you.

  • Your right to choose only what you want
  • Your right to buy a casket anywhere
  • Your right to prices by phone

1. Your right to see prices — in writing

Before discussing any arrangements, the funeral home must give you a General Price List (GPL): an itemized list of every good and service they offer, with prices. Not a package brochure — an item-by-item list. If they start talking arrangements without handing you the GPL first, that's a violation.

They must also provide a Casket Price List before showing you caskets, and an Outer Burial Container Price List before discussing vaults or liners.

2. Your right to buy only what you want

Funeral homes love packages. The Rule says you don't have to buy one. You may select individual items — for example, direct cremation without embalming, viewing, or a ceremony — and the funeral home cannot refuse or penalize you for it.

The only fee they may require is the basic services fee, which covers overhead like planning and permits. Everything else is optional.

3. Your right to buy a casket anywhere

This is the right that saves families the most money. Funeral home caskets are routinely marked up 300–500%. The Rule explicitly allows you to buy a casket from any seller — including online retailers, where the same style can cost half as much — and the funeral home may not charge a handling fee or refuse it.

The same applies to urns and, in most cases, outer burial containers.

4. Your right to prices by phone

You do not have to walk into a funeral home to learn prices. The Rule requires funeral homes to give price information over the phone when asked. This is how smart families compare: call two or three homes, ask for the GPL totals for the service you want, and compare before visiting anyone.

Reality check FTC spot-checks have found dozens of funeral homes failing to give proper prices by phone. If a home is evasive on the phone, consider that a warning sign about how they'll treat you in person.

5. Your right to an itemized statement

After you've made your selections — and before you pay — you must receive a Statement of Funeral Goods and Services Selected: an itemized bill showing exactly what you chose and what each item costs. Review it. Question anything you don't recognize. Nothing is final until you agree.

6. What to do if a funeral home breaks the rules

Violations carry civil penalties of over $50,000 per violation — the FTC takes this seriously. If a funeral home refuses an itemized list, pressures you into a package, or charges a casket handling fee:

  1. Document it. Note names, dates, and exactly what was said or refused.
  2. File a complaint with the FTC at consumer.ftc.gov (search "funeral complaint").
  3. Contact your state attorney general or funeral licensing board — many states have additional protections.
  4. Consider walking away. A home that breaks the rules before you've paid will not treat you better afterward.
Your three-sentence script Walk in (or call) and say: "Please give me your General Price List. I'd like to see itemized prices before we discuss anything. I'm comparing a few funeral homes." Those three sentences put the law on your side from the first minute.

Frequently asked questions

Does the Funeral Rule require funeral homes to post prices online? +
Not currently. The Rule predates the internet and requires in-person and phone disclosure. Fewer than 1 in 5 funeral home websites voluntarily post a price list — which is why calling and asking for the GPL matters so much.
Is embalming required by law? +
Almost never. No federal law requires it, and most states don't either. Funeral homes must disclose that embalming is not legally required in most cases — and you can decline it.
Can a funeral home refuse my outside casket? +
No. They must accept it without a handling fee. If they claim otherwise, that's a Funeral Rule violation — document it and report it.
