UAE Overtime Pay 2026: Rates, Night Hours & How to Claim

Unpaid overtime costs UAE employees thousands of dirhams a year — mostly because nobody runs the numbers. Here's the exact legal rates for 2026 (including the night-hour rule most guides get wrong), the formula, and how to claim what's yours.

The legal rates: 125%, 150%, and when each applies

Overtime in the UAE is governed by Articles 19–21 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021. Normal working hours are 8 hours a day / 48 hours a week. Anything beyond that is overtime, paid on your basic hourly wage (basic salary only — allowances excluded):

Normal overtime (daytime, beyond 8 hours)125% of basic hourly wage
Night overtime (10pm – 4am)150% of basic hourly wage
Weekly rest day (if no day off given)150% of basic hourly wage
Public holiday (if no substitute day off)Day's wage + 50% extra on top of salary

The 10pm rule most guides get wrong

Many HR blogs and calculators claim the night rate starts at 9pm. The actual statute — Article 19(3) — sets the enhanced night rate for hours worked between 10pm and 4am, not 9pm. If your payroll tool uses 9pm, your night-overtime figures are wrong. One more nuance: employees hired for permanent night shifts don't get the 150% night premium on top of their normal night pay unless their contract says so — the premium is for overtime at night.

The overtime formula, with examples

Basic hourly wage = (Basic monthly salary × 12) ÷ (52 × 48)
Overtime pay = Hourly wage × rate (1.25 or 1.50) × overtime hours

Worked examples (basic salary AED 6,000)

Basic hourly wage6,000 × 12 ÷ 52 ÷ 48 = AED 28.85/hour
10 daytime overtime hours28.85 × 1.25 × 10 = AED 360.58
8 night overtime hours (10pm–4am)28.85 × 1.50 × 8 = AED 346.20
Worked Friday rest day, 8 hours, no day off28.85 × 1.50 × 8 = AED 346.20

Notice how quickly it adds up: 12 night-overtime hours a month at this salary is over AED 5,000 a year — money most workers never claim. Check your own hours on our UAE overtime calculator.

Ramadan hours

During Ramadan, normal working hours are reduced by 2 hours per day for all employees — so the overtime threshold drops from 8 hours to 6 hours. Hours 7 and 8 in Ramadan are overtime at 125%, even though they'd be normal hours in other months. Many employers "forget" this.

Rest days and public holidays

Every employee gets a paid weekly rest day (often Friday, but the employer can designate the day). If you're asked to work it, the employer must give you another day off or pay 150% for the hours worked. For public holidays, your salary already covers the holiday itself — working it earns you the day's wage plus at least 50% extra, or a substitute day off instead.

Limits: 2 hours a day, 144 hours a year

The law caps overtime at 2 extra hours per day except where genuinely necessary to prevent loss or damage, and sets an annual ceiling of 144 overtime hours (extendable with MOHRE approval for specific projects). Overtime also requires employer–employee agreement — your boss can't force routine overtime indefinitely, though refusing in genuine emergencies can have consequences.

Who is exempt

Not everyone is covered: managerial/supervisory roles (depending on contract terms), domestic workers (separate law), and DIFC/ADGM employees follow different regimes. If your contract labels you "manager" but you do ordinary shift work, the label alone may not exempt you — the actual duties matter.

How to claim unpaid overtime

  1. Keep your own time log. Dates, start/end times, and whether hours fell in the 10pm–4am window. Employers must keep records too — but yours is your backup.
  2. Calculate the exact amount with our overtime calculator, using your basic salary, not gross. If you're nearing the end of your contract, check your gratuity figure at the same time.
  3. Raise it with HR in writing, citing Articles 19–21 and showing the calculation. Many underpayments are "payroll errors" that get fixed fast once quantified.
  4. File a MOHRE complaint if ignored. Your time log plus salary slips is the core of the dossier — complaints with precise dates and calculations succeed far more often than vague ones.
  5. Act within the year. Don't let unpaid overtime accumulate for years; claim periods and evidence both decay.
Disclaimer: This guide explains Articles 19–21 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021 for general information. Exemptions and free-zone rules vary — consult MOHRE or a UAE labour lawyer for your specific case.

How overtime should appear on your payslip

A compliant UAE payslip separates basic salary, allowances, and overtime into distinct lines — with overtime ideally split into standard (125%) and night/rest-day (150%) components. If your payslip shows a single mysterious "allowance" line that never changes regardless of your extra hours, that's a red flag: real overtime varies month to month. Ask payroll for the overtime breakdown by rate; employers are required to maintain detailed records of each overtime instance (date, hours, rate, amount), so the data exists even if your slip hides it.

Shift workers vs overtime: the crucial distinction

If you were hired to work nights (permanent night-shift role), your night hours are your normal hours — paid at your contracted rate, not at the 150% overtime premium. The 150% night rate applies when a day worker works extra hours that fall between 10pm and 4am. Similarly, rostered weekend shifts in industries like hospitality or healthcare are normal scheduled hours, not "rest-day overtime" — the 150% rest-day rate kicks in when you're called in on your designated weekly rest day beyond your roster.

Real-world case: the month-end close

Consider a logistics coordinator in Dubai on AED 7,200 basic (AED 4,800 basic + allowances is a common split — overtime runs on the AED 4,800). Every month-end she stays until midnight clearing shipments: roughly 12 overtime hours a month, mostly 10pm–4am. Her basic hourly wage is about AED 23.08; at the 150% night rate that's AED 34.62/hour → AED 415/month, nearly AED 5,000 a year. Her manager called it "part of the job." It wasn't — it was AED 5,000 of unpaid wages, every year. Cases like this are routine across the private sector, which is why running your own numbers matters more than any policy document.

Overtime during probation

Probation doesn't suspend the law. If you're on a 6-month probation and working extra hours, Articles 19–21 apply exactly the same. Some employers imply probationers "shouldn't claim" — legally meaningless. Log the hours from day one; probation is actually when underpayment is most common because new joiners are least likely to question it.
