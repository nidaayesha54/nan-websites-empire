UAE Overtime Rules at a Glance

  • Normal working hours: 8 hours per day / 48 hours per week (reduced to 6 hours daily during Ramadan for all employees).
  • Hourly rate: basic monthly salary ÷ 30 ÷ 8.
  • Normal overtime: basic hourly rate + 25% (125%) for each extra hour.
  • Night overtime (10 PM – 4 AM): basic hourly rate + 50% (150%).
  • Rest day / public holiday work: 150% of the basic rate, or a compensatory day off instead.
  • Daily limit: maximum 2 hours of overtime per day, except to prevent serious loss, damage or accidents.
  • 3-week cap: total working hours including overtime must not exceed 144 hours over any 3-week period.
  • Meal breaks, prayer breaks, rest periods and commuting time are not counted as working hours.
  • Some senior managerial and supervisory roles may be exempt — check your contract.
Example: basic salary AED 6,000 → hourly rate = 6,000 ÷ 30 ÷ 8 = AED 25.
10 hours of normal overtime: 25 × 1.25 × 10 = AED 312.50
8 hours of night overtime: 25 × 1.50 × 8 = AED 300.00

FAQs

Is overtime based on basic salary or total salary?

Basic salary only. Your hourly overtime rate is your basic monthly salary divided by 30 days, divided by 8 hours.

How many overtime hours are allowed per day?

A maximum of 2 hours per day, unless the extra work is needed to prevent serious loss, damage or an accident.

What are the overtime rates during Ramadan?

The rates are the same (125% normal, 150% night/holiday), but the standard workday drops to 6 hours — so hours beyond 6 count as overtime during Ramadan.

Can I choose a day off instead of holiday pay?

For work on a rest day or public holiday, the employer may give you a compensatory day off instead of the 150% payment.

Are night-shift workers paid differently?

The night overtime rate (10 PM–4 AM at 150%) applies to overtime hours; employees on regular night shifts may have specific exemptions depending on contract and sector.

Can my employer refuse to pay overtime?

No — overtime worked under the legal limits must be paid at the statutory rates unless your role is specifically exempt by contract or law.
