AI Voice Pricing Models Explained: Characters vs Minutes vs Words

October 12, 2026 · 5-minute read

Shopping for an AI voice generator feels like comparing apples to bicycles: ElevenLabs charges per character, Murf charges per minute, and Play.ht charges per word. Here's how to translate between them so you can actually compare prices.

The conversion math

For natural-paced English narration, these are the rules of thumb the industry uses:

  • ≈850 characters = 1 minute of audio (including spaces and punctuation)
  • ≈150 words = 1 minute of audio
  • So: 1 word ≈ 5.7 characters of quota

These shift with pace — a slow, dramatic audiobook read can drop to 130 words/minute, while a punchy ad read hits 170. But for budgeting, 850/150 is the standard.

Worked example: 2 hours of audio per month

Say you publish a weekly 30-minute podcast — 120 minutes monthly. What does each model cost?

  • ElevenLabs (characters): 120 min × 850 = ~102,000 characters. The $22/mo Creator plan (100k chars) just barely misses — you'd land on the $99 Pro tier, or trim scripts. Effective cost: ~$0.83–$0.97/minute.
  • Murf (minutes): the $19/mo Creator plan includes exactly 120 minutes. Effective cost: ~$0.16/minute. Clean and predictable.
  • Play.ht (words): 120 min × 150 = ~18,000 words. The $19/mo Creator plan includes 60,000 words — you'd use less than a third of it. Effective cost: ~$0.16/minute, with huge headroom.

Same 120 minutes, three answers: ~$99, $19, or $19. The model matters as much as the sticker price — which is exactly why we built the cost calculator: enter your minutes and it does this math for every platform instantly.

Which model favors whom?

  • Characters favor concise writers. Short sentences, tight scripts — every trimmed word is money saved. Punishes long-winded drafts and languages with longer words.
  • Minutes favor planners. You know exactly what an episode costs before you generate it. But regenerating a flubbed paragraph burns the same minutes again.
  • Words favor slow narrators. Deliberate, pause-heavy delivery stretches each word into more audio time — you get more listening minutes per dollar.

Hidden costs to watch

Regeneration: most platforms deduct quota every time you hit generate, including retries. Draft your script carefully first. Overage rates: exceeding a plan can trigger steep per-unit charges — set usage alerts. Voice cloning add-ons: professional cloning sometimes costs extra beyond the base plan. Commercial licenses: free tiers may forbid monetized use; factor the cheapest paid tier into any business plan.

The takeaway

Don't compare "$22 vs $19" — compare cost per finished minute at your volume. For most creators under 2 hours/month, minutes- or word-based plans (Murf, Play.ht) are the cheapest predictable route; for premium realism at moderate volume, ElevenLabs' character plans are worth it.
