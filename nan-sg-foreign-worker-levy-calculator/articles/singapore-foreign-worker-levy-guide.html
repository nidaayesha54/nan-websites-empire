Singapore Foreign Worker Levy: Rates, Tiers and S Pass Rules Explained

Hiring foreign workers in Singapore means paying a monthly levy on top of salary — and the rate depends on sector, tier and skill level. This guide maps every rate.

Key takeaways:
  • Monthly levy per worker depends on sector, quota tier, and skill level.
  • S Pass levy is a flat S$650/month across all sectors since 1 September 2025.
  • The levy is the employer’s cost — deducting it from wages is prohibited.

Hiring a foreign worker in Singapore costs more than the salary on the offer letter. Every Work Permit and S Pass holder attracts a monthly foreign worker levy — paid by the employer, never deducted from the worker's wages — and the rate swings widely by sector, quota tier, and skill level. Get the tier wrong in your budgeting and a hire that's profitable on paper quietly isn't.

This guide maps the levy system: how rates are set, every current rate table, the S Pass flat rate, the daily-rate formula for part months, and the dependency ceilings that cap how many foreign workers you can hire at all.

What the Levy Is For

The levy is a deliberate pricing mechanism in Singapore's labour policy. By attaching a monthly cost to each foreign hire, the Ministry of Manpower (MOM) creates a financial incentive to hire locally first and reserve foreign hiring for roles where local talent genuinely isn't available. It is not a tax on the worker — deducting it from wages is prohibited.

Three Factors Set Your Rate

1. Sector (services, manufacturing, construction, process, marine shipyard) · 2. Quota tier — the more of your workforce on Work Permits, the higher the tier · 3. Skill level — higher-skilled (R1) workers cost less than basic-skilled (R2) in the same tier.

Tier 3: 50–60% of workforceR1: S$550/moR2: S$650/mo

Construction, Process and Marine Shipyard

These sectors price by worker source rather than workforce-percentage tiers:

  • Construction: Malaysia / North Asia / PRC — R1 S$300, R2 S$500; Non-Traditional Sources — R1 S$700, R2 S$900.
  • Process: Malaysia / NAS / PRC — R1 S$200, R2 S$450; NTS — R1 S$300, R2 S$650. (Basic-skilled process rates are scheduled to rise in 2028.)
  • Marine shipyard: R1 S$350, R2 S$500.

S Pass: One Flat Rate

The simple case: since 1 September 2025, the S Pass levy has been harmonised at S$650 a month across all sectors and tiers (previously S$550 at the basic tier). S Pass holders are sub-quota capped at 10% of the workforce in services and 15% in construction, manufacturing, marine shipyard and process — and they count toward the overall dependency ceiling. Employment Pass holders pay no levy at all.

Foreign Domestic Workers

FDWs have their own rates: S$300/month for the first helper, S$450/month for additional helpers, with a concessionary rate available for eligible households (e.g. families with young children or elderly members).

The Daily Rate: Part Months

When a worker doesn't complete a full calendar month, you pay a daily rate instead:

Daily rate = (monthly levy × 12) ÷ 365, rounded up to the nearest cent

Example: a construction worker from Malaysia, higher-skilled (S$300/month) → daily rate S$9.87. Permit issued on the 20th of a 30-day month → 11 days × S$9.87 = S$108.57. An S Pass holder's daily rate is S$21.37.

Dependency Ratio Ceilings: The Hard Caps

Rates only matter within quota. The dependency ratio ceiling — the maximum share of your workforce that may be foreign — is 35% in services, 60% in manufacturing, 83.3% in construction and process, and 75% in marine shipyard. MOM computes quota from your local employees' CPF records as a three-month average, so late or inaccurate declarations shrink your headroom.

Worked Example: Budgeting a Team

A services company hires 5 basic-skilled Work Permit holders at Tier 2:

  • Levy: 5 × S$600 = S$3,000/month — S$36,000 a year on top of salaries.
  • Upskilling two of them to higher-skilled (R1): 2 × S$400 + 3 × S$600 = S$2,600/month — a S$4,800 annual saving that illustrates why the skills ladder is priced in.

How Quota Is Actually Computed

Your quota isn't a number you declare — MOM computes it from your local workforce: citizens and PRs on a contract of service, counted from CPF contribution records as a three-month average. Locals earning at least the Local Qualifying Salary (S$1,800) count as full units; those earning S$900–S$1,800 count as half. Practical consequences: a recent local hire doesn't give immediate quota, late CPF declarations shrink your headroom, and EP holders don't count as locals. Check your live headroom in Work Permit Online before every application — estimates from calculators don't override the live figure.

The Employer's Budgeting Checklist

  • Levy + salary + insurance: budget the full stack per worker — levy alone is S$3,600–S$9,600 a year at common tiers.
  • Tier creep: each hire raises your dependency ratio, so the marginal levy per worker rises as you approach the ceiling. Model the 5th hire at the tier you'll actually be in, not the tier you're in today.
  • Skill upgrades: moving a worker from basic-skilled to higher-skilled cuts the levy every month — training spend can pay for itself.
  • Pass mix: where the role genuinely needs mid-level skills, compare an S Pass (S$7,800/year levy, quota-capped) against an EP (no levy, no quota, higher salary floor).

Staying Audit-Ready

MOM audits focus on three things: correct tier classification (is your dependency ratio computed right?), skill-level claims (does the worker actually hold the certification for higher-skilled rates?), and wage deductions (is any levy cost being passed to the worker?). Keep tier calculations, skills certificates, and clean payslips filed monthly — the levy is straightforward to get right and expensive to get wrong.

Levy at a Glance

What: a monthly employer-paid levy on every Work Permit and S Pass holder — never deductible from wages. Rate drivers: sector, quota tier (your dependency ratio), and skill level (higher-skilled R1 is cheaper than basic-skilled R2). S Pass: flat S$650/month across all sectors since 1 September 2025. Part months: daily rate = monthly × 12 ÷ 365, rounded up. Caps: dependency ceilings of 35% (services), 60% (manufacturing), 83.3% (construction/process), 75% (marine). Check: live quota headroom in Work Permit Online before hiring.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I deduct the levy from my worker's salary?

No — the levy is the employer's cost by law. Deducting it from wages is prohibited.

We're at 30% foreign workforce in services. Which tier applies to a new hire?

Tier 3 (25–35%) — the tier is set by your current dependency level, so each additional hire near the ceiling costs more per worker.

Does the levy apply to Employment Pass holders?

No. EP holders are exempt from both levy and quota — one reason the EP suits higher-paid professional roles.

What happens if we exceed our quota temporarily?

In services, employers that temporarily exceed the S Pass sub-quota through attrition are charged the higher Tier 2 levy on the excess — another reason to watch headcount monthly.

Are levy rates changing soon?

Process-sector basic-skilled rates are scheduled to increase in 2028, and MOM has signalled services Tier 1 and Tier 2 will merge from 2028. Budget with the current tables and re-check annually.
