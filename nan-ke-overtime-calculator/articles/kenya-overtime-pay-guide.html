Kenya Overtime Pay: How the 1.5x and 2x Rates Work (Employment Act 2007)

Worked extra hours in Kenya? The Employment Act sets clear premium rates for overtime. This guide shows how your overtime pay is calculated — and how to check your payslip.

Stayed late to finish the month-end close? Worked a Sunday to cover a colleague? In Kenya, those extra hours aren't a favour to your employer — they're paid time at premium rates set by law. The Employment Act, 2007 fixes exactly how much overtime is worth: 1.5 times your normal hourly rate on weekdays, and double on your rest day or a public holiday.

This guide explains the overtime rules end to end: the legal rates, how your hourly rate is derived from your monthly salary, the limits on how much overtime you can be asked to work, and what to do when overtime pay doesn't show up on your payslip.

The Legal Foundation

Overtime in Kenya is governed by the Employment Act, 2007, supported by the Regulation of Wages orders that set sector-specific minimums. The Act's position is straightforward: work beyond your normal hours must be compensated at premium rates, overtime should generally be voluntary, and an employer must give 24 hours' notice when requesting it. You cannot be forced to work overtime — and if you simply stay late on your own initiative to finish spillover work, that isn't automatically claimable overtime either. The cleanest position is documented, requested overtime.

Standard Working Hours

Before overtime starts, you need to know where "normal" ends. Kenya's framework:

  • Maximum 52 hours per week, typically spread over six days — though most office contracts set 40–45 hours over five days.
  • At least one rest day per week (24 consecutive hours), usually Sunday but any agreed day qualifies.
  • A one-hour break for every six continuous hours worked.

Overtime is any work beyond your normal contractual hours — the hours in your contract, not just the legal maximum.

The Overtime Rates

The minimum rates are fixed by law. Your contract or collective bargaining agreement can set higher rates, never lower:

Weekday overtime (beyond normal daily/weekly hours)1.5× normal hourly rate
Rest-day work (e.g. Sunday)2× normal hourly rate
Public-holiday work2× normal hourly rate

How Your Hourly Rate Is Calculated

If you're paid monthly rather than by the hour, the Act deems your basic hourly rate to be 1/225 of your monthly wage:

Hourly rate = monthly basic wage ÷ 225

A worker on KES 45,000 a month therefore has an hourly rate of KES 200. That single divisor is the key to checking any overtime line on your payslip.

Worked Example

Monthly basic wage KES 60,000 → hourly rate KES 266.67. In one month the worker logs 10 weekday overtime hours and 8 rest-day hours:

  • Weekday: 10 hrs × 1.5 × 266.67 = KES 4,000
  • Rest day: 8 hrs × 2 × 266.67 = KES 4,266.67
  • Total overtime pay: KES 8,266.67

Limits: How Much Overtime Is Too Much?

The law caps total working time to prevent exhaustion: a worker cannot be asked to work more than 116 hours in any consecutive two-week period (144 hours for night workers). Overtime beyond that isn't just unpaid — it's unlawful to require. If your schedule regularly pushes past this, that's a compliance issue, not a badge of honour.

Time Off Instead of Overtime Pay

An employer may offer time off in lieu of overtime pay — but only if you agree in writing. Without your written agreement, the default is cash at the premium rates. Never let "we'll give you a day off later" stay verbal; the Act's protection only works when the agreement is documented.

Who the Rates Don't Cover

The statutory minimums don't apply to everyone. Senior management and genuinely managerial staff are commonly excluded from overtime provisions (check your contract's classification), and some sector wage orders set their own overtime tables that override the general rule. When in doubt, your contract plus the applicable wage order — not office folklore — decides.

Keep Your Own Records

Overtime disputes are won on paper. Keep a simple log: date, start and end times, what the work was, and who requested it. Screenshot or save any written request. When the payslip arrives, recompute with the 1/225 divisor and the 1.5×/2× multipliers. Most underpayments are arithmetic errors, not malice — but you can only spot them if you check. And remember, overtime is only one part of your final dues — unused annual leave must also be paid out when you leave.

What to Do If Overtime Isn't Paid

1

Raise it with HR

Present your log and calculation in writing. Ask for the specific month to be corrected.

2

Escalate formally

If HR stalls, put the complaint in writing to management and keep copies.

3

Go to the labour office

Unresolved claims can go to the county labour office and then the Employment and Labour Relations Court.

Sector Wage Orders Can Change the Details

The Employment Act sets the floor, but Kenya's Regulation of Wages orders — issued per sector and updated periodically — can specify their own overtime tables, normal hours, and minimum rates. If you work in agriculture, hospitality, private security, or domestic service, check the wage order for your sector: it may define normal hours differently (and therefore change where overtime begins) or set higher multipliers.

The hierarchy is simple: whichever is most favorable to the worker applies — the Act, the wage order, your contract, or the CBA. They stack upward, never downward. Working outside Kenya? Overtime premiums differ by country — see, for example, the UAE overtime calculator.

Night Shifts and Rotating Rosters

Night workers get a higher two-week hour cap (144 hours instead of 116), recognizing the health toll of night work — but the overtime multipliers are the same: 1.5× on weekdays, 2× on rest days and public holidays. What changes on a rotating roster is which day counts as your rest day: it's the 24-hour rest period in your schedule, not necessarily Sunday. Overtime on your rostered rest day is 2× even if it falls on a Wednesday.

How Overtime Should Appear on Your Payslip

A compliant payslip separates basic pay from overtime pay as distinct lines, ideally showing hours and rate. If your payslip shows a single lump sum, ask payroll for the breakdown — you can't verify the 1.5×/2× multipliers without it. Also check that overtime isn't being quietly folded into a fixed "allowance" that never changes month to month; genuine overtime varies with hours worked.

Frequently Asked Questions

My contract says 45 hours a week. When does overtime start?

After 45 hours — overtime is measured against your contractual normal hours, not the 52-hour legal maximum. Hours 46+ are overtime at 1.5× on weekdays.

Can my employer refuse to pay and give time off instead?

Only with your written agreement. Without it, you're entitled to cash at the statutory premium rates.

I'm on a monthly salary of KES 90,000. What's my overtime hourly rate?

KES 90,000 ÷ 225 = KES 400 per hour. Weekday overtime pays KES 600/hr; rest-day or holiday work pays KES 800/hr.

Does the 1.5× rate apply to Saturday if Saturday is my rest day?

If Saturday is your designated rest day, work on it is paid at 2×. The 1.5× rate covers overtime on normal working days.

My boss asks for overtime with 2 hours' notice. Is that allowed?

The Act requires 24 hours' notice for overtime requests, and overtime should be voluntary. Habitual short-notice demands are worth raising formally.
