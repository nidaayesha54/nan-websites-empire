How overtime pay is calculated in Kenya

Under the Employment Act 2007 and the Regulation of Wages (General) Order 1982, overtime in Kenya follows a fixed formula:

  • Hourly rate: your monthly basic salary divided by 225 (one two-hundred-and-twenty-fifth of the basic monthly wage) for workers not paid by the hour.
  • Ordinary weekdays: overtime is paid at 1.5× the basic hourly rate.
  • Weekly rest day and public holidays: work is paid at 2× the basic hourly rate.
  • Normal hours: the General Wages Order sets normal working hours at up to 52 hours per week; anything beyond your agreed schedule counts as overtime.

Example: if your basic salary is KES 45,000/month, your hourly rate is 45,000 ÷ 225 = KES 200. Ten weekday overtime hours earn 10 × 200 × 1.5 = KES 3,000, and four hours on a rest day earn 4 × 200 × 2 = KES 1,600 — a total of KES 4,600.

Frequently asked questions

Who is entitled to overtime pay in Kenya?

Non-managerial employees who work beyond their agreed normal hours are generally entitled. Your employment contract or collective bargaining agreement may set better rates — the statutory rates are the minimum.

Is overtime pay taxed?

Yes. Overtime pay is part of your taxable employment income and is subject to PAYE like your regular salary.

What is the maximum overtime allowed?

Wage orders and common CBAs limit overtime to about 4 hours per day and roughly 12 hours per week. Any regular excess should be agreed in writing.

What if I work on a public holiday?

Work on a public holiday is paid at double (2×) your basic hourly rate — the same rate as the weekly rest day.

Does my employer have to pay more than the statutory rate?

The law sets the floor, not the ceiling. Many contracts and CBAs pay higher overtime rates, and those contract terms apply if they are better for you.
