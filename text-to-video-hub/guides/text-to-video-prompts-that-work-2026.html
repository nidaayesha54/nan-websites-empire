Text-to-Video Prompts That Work in 2026 (25 Proven Examples)

Updated October 2026 · 9 min read

The difference between a breathtaking AI video and a mushy mess is almost never the model — it's the prompt. After testing hundreds of prompts across Sora, Runway Gen-4, Kling, Google Veo 3, and Luma, the pattern is clear: prompts that specify subject, action, camera, lighting, and style win every time. Here are 25 examples that work, organized by use case, with the formula behind each.

The 5-part formula every winning prompt follows

1. Subject — who or what is in the shot
2. Action — what is happening (motion is the whole point of video)
3. Camera — shot type and movement
4. Lighting & mood — the feeling
5. Style — the visual finish

Don't want to assemble these by hand? Our free prompt generator builds all five parts automatically.

Animal prompts

Animal clips dominate short-form feeds. The trick: one clear action, one clear subject, warm light.

  • "Close-up of a baby elephant splashing water at a riverbank, slow-motion, warm golden-hour light, joyful mood, ultra photorealistic, 8k."
  • "Wide shot of a tiny corgi puppy running through tall green grass toward camera, ears bouncing, bright morning light, playful mood, cinematic."
  • "Slow push-in on a cat opening a tiny treasure chest with its paw, sparkling light, whimsical mood, fantasy style."

Product & ad prompts

Product videos need a locked camera and controlled motion — jitter kills the "premium" feel.

  • "Slow rotating shot of a matte-black perfume bottle on a marble pedestal, studio lighting with soft reflections, luxurious mood, photorealistic product video."
  • "Macro shot of coffee pouring into a glass cup, steam rising, warm kitchen backlight, cozy morning mood, cinematic."

Cinematic scenes

  • "Aerial drone shot sweeping over a medieval castle on a misty cliff at dawn, dramatic clouds, epic orchestral mood, cinematic film still."
  • "Tracking shot following a lone astronaut walking across a neon-lit rainy street, reflections on wet pavement, moody cyberpunk style."

Why prompts fail (and the 10-second fix)

The three most common failures: no camera direction (the model picks randomly), no motion verb (you get a static-looking clip), and contradictory details ("bright dark moody"). Fix them by running your idea through our prompt improver — it detects exactly what's missing and rebuilds the prompt.

Always pair with a negative prompt

Even great prompts generate extra fingers and flickering backgrounds. Append a negative prompt — "blurry, extra limbs, flickering, watermark, distorted faces" — or build a custom one with our negative prompt builder.
