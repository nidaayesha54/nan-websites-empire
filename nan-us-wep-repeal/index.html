What Did the Social Security Fairness Act Do?

Signed into law on January 5, 2025, the Social Security Fairness Act repealed two long-standing provisions — the Windfall Elimination Provision (WEP) and the Government Pension Offset (GPO) — that reduced Social Security benefits for public workers whose government jobs did not pay into Social Security. The repeal is retroactive to January 2024, and the Social Security Administration recalculated benefits automatically — no application was required. By spring 2025, SSA had issued over $14.8 billion in retroactive payments to more than 2 million people.

How WEP Reduced Benefits (the Old Formula)

Social Security computes benefits from your average indexed monthly earnings using "bend points": normally 90% of the first $1,286 (2026 bend point), 32% of the next bracket, and 15% above that. WEP cut that first-bracket factor as low as 40%, depending on years of substantial covered earnings:

Years of substantial earningsFirst-bracket factor
30 or more90% (no reduction)
2565%
2145%
20 or fewer40% (maximum reduction)

One safeguard limited the damage: the WEP guarantee — the reduction could never exceed half of your monthly non-covered pension. The maximum WEP cut was about $587/month (2024). Our estimator above applies exactly this math in reverse.

How GPO Reduced Benefits (the Old Formula)

The Government Pension Offset reduced spousal and survivor Social Security benefits by two-thirds of the recipient's non-covered government pension — often wiping the benefit out entirely. Repeal restores the full spousal or survivor benefit.

Average Increases (Congressional Budget Office)

  • WEP repeal: about +$360/month on average for ~2.1 million workers.
  • GPO repeal (spouses): about +$700/month on average for ~380,000 spouses.
  • GPO repeal (survivors): about +$1,190/month on average for ~390,000 surviving spouses.

Who Qualifies?

  • Retired teachers, firefighters, police officers, and other state/local workers with pensions from jobs not covered by Social Security.
  • Spouses and surviving spouses whose spousal/survivor benefits were reduced or eliminated by GPO.
  • Some federal workers hired before 1984 under the old Civil Service Retirement System.

What Should You Do Now?

  1. Nothing to apply for — SSA recalculated eligible records automatically.
  2. Make sure SSA has your current mailing address and direct-deposit information (update at ssa.gov or call 1-800-772-1213).
  3. Create or check your my Social Security account to confirm your new benefit amount.
  4. Retroactive payments covering January 2024 onward were issued as one-time lump sums during 2025 — if you believe you were affected and never received one, contact SSA.

FAQs

Do I need to apply for the Fairness Act increase?

No. The Social Security Administration identified affected records and recalculated benefits automatically. If you think you were missed, contact SSA at 1-800-772-1213.

When did the higher payments start?

Adjusted monthly payments began in 2025, and one-time retroactive payments covered every month back to January 2024.

Will my retroactive lump sum be taxed?

Possibly — the lump sum counts as Social Security income in the year received, and up to 85% of benefits can be taxable depending on your combined income. Consider estimated tax payments.

I have 30+ years of covered earnings. Does this affect me?

No — WEP never applied with 30+ years of substantial earnings, so the repeal changes nothing for you.

Does the repeal affect my government pension?

No. Your state/local pension is unchanged; only the Social Security reduction was removed.

What were "substantial earnings"?

An annual threshold set by SSA (about $31,275 in 2024, rising yearly). Only years above the threshold counted toward the 30-year WEP exemption.
