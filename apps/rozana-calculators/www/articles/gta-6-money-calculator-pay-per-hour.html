GTA 6 Money Calculator: What Every Job Pays Per Hour

20 November 2026 · 5 min read

Every calculator on this site exists for the same reason: before you spend your time or your money, work out the rate. Grand Theft Auto 6 deserves the same treatment. Leonida is full of ways to earn — tills, cars, races, dives, vaults — and they pay wildly different amounts for the same hour of your life. This guide is a money calculator in words: the payout of every job players have reported, the time each one takes, and the rate that comes out when you divide one by the other. All figures are reported from launch-week guides, not measured by Rockstar, and every rate below says so where it matters.

The maths is simple. Rate equals payout divided by minutes, times sixty. A job that pays $2,700 for ten minutes of work pays $16,200 an hour. A job that pays $400 for half a day pays almost nothing an hour — but it also costs you nothing in risk. The rate is only half the story. The other half is what the job demands: the chapter it unlocks in, the wanted level it brings, and the chance you lose the money before you bank it.

The big payouts, worked out

Large bank vaults are the biggest reported payout in the game: up to $205,000 for about five minutes of work, according to PowerPyx's launch guide. The arithmetic says that is more than $2 million an hour, which is exactly why arithmetic alone misleads — the vaults only open at the end of Chapter 4, they need an optional mission first, and they bring an instant four-star wanted level with SWAT heat. Supercars sold to Randy pay $30,000 or more per car in perfect condition, from about halfway through Chapter 3, once you have the Key Cloner — roughly eight minutes a car if nothing goes wrong. Small banks pay $5,000 to $45,000 each, and there are 14 of them. Jewelry stores pay $10,000 to $25,000. Pilot Pete pays $20,000 per delivery mission, seven packages against a clock, Jason only, from Chapter 4. Payday loan stores pay $2,000 to $25,000 depending on the store. Smash-and-grab jobs across 35 shops pay about $1,000 to $25,000. Stolen cars to Moses typically pay $1,000 or more, and a good car at a Pay 'n' Spray brings around $4,000.

The hourly rates, for the patient

Not every job pays in one lump sum. Some pay by the hour, and the hourly figures are where the calculator earns its keep. Playing pool in bars pays around $758 an hour. Monster truck mudding at the Thrillbilly Mud Club north of Ambrosia pays around $3,000 an hour. Street, track and dirt races pay around $5,000 an hour when you win. Treasure diving in the Keys pays around $6,000 an hour, and gym training improves your lung capacity for longer dives. These are player-reported figures from launch week, not measured rates — but they are consistent enough to plan around. The pattern is clear: the louder the job, the higher the rate and the higher the risk. The quiet jobs pay less per hour and cost you nothing in heat.

Two worked examples

Example one: the story pays $120,000 at the end of Chapter 3. At the pool-table rate of $758 an hour, that is about 158 hours of pool. At the treasure-diving rate of $6,000 an hour, it is 20 hours. At one large vault job, it is five minutes — once you have reached the end of Chapter 4. Example two: maxing out the Family Savings quest costs $2,000,000. At the mudding rate, that is about 667 hours. At the vault rate, it is ten vaults — if every one pays the maximum, which it will not. The lesson of both examples is the same: the best rate in the game is useless until it unlocks, and the job you can do right now is worth more than the job you cannot reach yet.

The catch the calculator cannot see

Three things break every rate in this guide. First, unlocks: the best-paying jobs are gated behind story chapters, and no amount of grinding opens them early. Second, death: cash in your pocket is lost when you die or get arrested, and banked cash is safe — a $205,000 vault payout is worth nothing if you are carrying it when a chase goes wrong, so bank it at one of the 454 ATMs before you do anything else. Third, heat: a four-star wanted level costs time, repairs, and sometimes the whole payout. A rate that ignores the escape is not a rate. It is a fantasy.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best-paying job in GTA 6? Large bank vaults, at up to $205,000 per job — but they only open at the end of Chapter 4 and bring instant four-star heat.

What is the best job for a new player? Stealing cars for Moses: $350 to $2,700 a car, repeatable from Chapter 2, low risk.

How much does treasure diving pay per hour? Around $6,000 an hour, according to launch-week player reports — the best hourly rate among the quiet jobs.

Are these rates confirmed by Rockstar? No. Every figure here is reported from launch-week guides, mainly PowerPyx, and labelled as reported rather than measured.

Does dying lose your money? Pocket cash is lost on death or arrest. Banked cash is safe — deposit at an ATM after every job.

Bottom line: do the maths before you grind. The vault pays best and unlocks last; the quiet jobs pay less and cost nothing. Work out your rate, bank your cash, and let the numbers choose your next job.
