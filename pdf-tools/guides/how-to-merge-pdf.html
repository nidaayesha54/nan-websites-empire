How to Merge PDF Files: 4 Easy Methods (2026 Guide)

By PDF Tools Hub · October 2026 · 6 min read

Need to combine several PDFs into one file? Maybe you're assembling a job application (resume + cover letter + certificates), merging scanned pages, or joining chapters of a report. Whatever the reason, merging PDFs is a two-minute job once you know the right method. This guide covers four easy ways — starting with the fastest and most private one.

The quickest way to merge PDFs is a browser-based tool like our free Merge PDF tool. Here's how it works:

  1. Open the merge tool and drop in two or more PDF files.

Why this method wins: no account, no watermark, no upload queues. And because our tool processes everything inside your browser, your documents never travel to a server — a big deal for contracts, medical records, or financial paperwork.

Method 2: Preview on Mac (built-in, no downloads)

Mac users can merge PDFs without installing anything:

  1. Open the first PDF in Preview.
  2. Go to View → Thumbnails to show the sidebar.
  3. Drag the other PDF files into the thumbnail sidebar, in order.
  4. Choose File → Export as PDF to save the combined file.

This works well for a handful of files, though large merges can make Preview sluggish.

Method 3: Print to PDF on Windows (workaround)

Windows has no built-in PDF merger, but you can combine files through the print dialog in some apps, or use the free PDFsam Basic desktop app. The print-to-PDF trick flattens documents though, so links and form fields may break — an online merger preserves them better.

Method 4: Adobe Acrobat (paid, most features)

Adobe Acrobat Pro can merge PDFs via Tools → Combine files. It's powerful — you can also reorder, delete, and rotate pages — but it requires a paid subscription, which is overkill if you just need to join a few files.

Most "free" PDF sites upload your documents to their servers for processing. For sensitive files — tax returns, legal contracts, medical forms — prefer tools that work locally. Our merge tool runs 100% in your browser: close the tab and nothing remains anywhere.

Frequently asked questions

Will merging change my PDF quality?

No. Merging copies pages exactly as they are — text, images, and formatting stay identical.

Can I merge scanned PDFs?

Yes. Scanned pages are just images inside a PDF, and they merge like any other pages.

Is there a page limit?

Our tool has no hard limit, but extremely large files are easier to handle on a desktop browser with plenty of memory.

Can I reorder pages before merging?

Yes — arrange your files in the exact order you want them merged before clicking the merge button.
