7 Tax Mistakes Freelancers Make

Freelancing means freedom — and full responsibility for your taxes. Here are the errors that cost gig workers the most.

1. Skipping quarterly estimated payments

No employer withholds for you. Owe $1,000+ and skip quarterlies, and the IRS adds underpayment penalties. See quarterly taxes explained.

2. Forgetting self-employment tax exists

On top of income tax, you owe 15.3% SE tax on 92.35% of net earnings (2026). Many new freelancers budget for income tax and get blindsided. Run the SE tax calculator.

3. Missing the deductible half of SE tax

You can deduct half your SE tax from income — an above-the-line deduction that lowers AGI. Don't leave it on the table.

4. Mixing business and personal money

One account for everything makes deductions a nightmare and raises audit risk. Open a separate business account.

5. Not tracking expenses all year

Home office, mileage, software, phone, health insurance premiums — every untracked dollar is tax you overpay. Log as you go.

6. Ignoring retirement contributions

SEP-IRA or Solo 401(k) contributions cut your taxable income now. A freelancer earning $80k can shelter up to ~$20k+ with a Solo 401(k).

7. Filing late because "it's complicated"

Complexity isn't an excuse the IRS accepts. Start early, use the safe-harbor rules, and get help if needed.
