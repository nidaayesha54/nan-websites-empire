Standard Deduction vs Itemizing in 2026

Every filer chooses: take the flat standard deduction, or itemize individual deductions on Schedule A. You pick whichever is larger — about 9 in 10 filers take the standard deduction.

2026 standard deduction amounts

Filing status2026 amount
Single / Married Filing Separately$16,100
Married Filing Jointly$32,200
Head of Household$24,150

Filers 65 or older (or blind) get an additional amount: $2,050 if single/head of household, $1,650 per qualifying spouse if married.

What counts toward itemizing?

  • State & local taxes (SALT): capped at $40,000 for 2026 under current law
  • Mortgage interest: on up to $750,000 of acquisition debt
  • Charitable donations: cash and property gifts to qualified organizations
  • Medical expenses: only the portion above 7.5% of AGI

The simple decision rule

Add up your itemizable deductions. If the total beats your standard deduction, itemize. Example: a married couple with $18,000 in mortgage interest, $12,000 in SALT, and $5,000 in charity = $35,000 itemized — that beats the $32,200 standard deduction, saving them tax on an extra $2,800. If their total were $25,000, the standard deduction wins and itemizing would be pointless paperwork.

When itemizing usually wins

  • Homeowners with large mortgages in high-tax states
  • Very generous charitable givers (consider "bunching" donations into one year)
  • People with major medical bills in a single year
