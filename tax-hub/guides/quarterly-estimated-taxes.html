Quarterly Estimated Taxes Explained (2026)

Employees pay tax through paycheck withholding. But freelancers, contractors, landlords, and investors get no withholding — so the IRS requires them to pay in four quarterly installments via Form 1040-ES.

Who must pay quarterly?

Generally, if you expect to owe $1,000 or more in tax for 2026 after withholding and credits, you should make estimated payments. This covers the self-employed, gig workers, landlords, and anyone with significant untaxed income.

How much to pay: the safe harbors

To avoid underpayment penalties, pay at least one of:

  1. 90% of your actual 2026 tax, or
  2. 100% of your 2025 total tax (110% if your 2025 AGI exceeded $150,000).

Most people use option 2 — it's a known number. Divide it by 4 and pay that each quarter.

Quick calculation

Estimate your total 2026 tax with our self-employment tax calculator (SE tax) plus the income tax estimator. Add them, subtract any withholding, divide by 4. That's your quarterly payment.

Penalties

Miss a payment and the IRS charges an underpayment penalty (interest-based, set quarterly — recently around 8% annualized). The penalty applies per quarter, so catching up late only stops part of it.

⚠️ Educational only. Estimated tax rules have exceptions (farmers, fiscal-year filers). Consult a qualified tax professional.
