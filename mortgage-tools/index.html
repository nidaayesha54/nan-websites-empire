Frequently asked questions

How is a mortgage payment calculated?

With the standard formula M = P·r(1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1), where P is the loan amount, r the monthly rate, and n the number of payments. Taxes, insurance and PMI are added on top to get PITI.

What is PITI?

PITI stands for Principal, Interest, Taxes, and Insurance — the four parts of a typical mortgage payment. Lenders use PITI (plus PMI if your down payment is under 20%) to judge affordability.

When should I refinance?

Refinance when the monthly savings recover your closing costs well before you plan to sell or refinance again. A common shortcut: divide closing costs by monthly savings — that is your break-even in months.

Is a 15-year mortgage better than a 30-year?

A 15-year loan charges far less total interest and usually a lower rate, but the payment is much higher. Choose 15 years if the payment fits comfortably; choose 30 if you need flexibility.

How much house can I afford?

Lenders generally cap housing costs at 28% of gross income and total debts at 36–43%. Our mortgage calculator shows the real PITI payment so you can test price points against your budget.
