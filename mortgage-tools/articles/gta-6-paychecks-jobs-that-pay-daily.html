GTA 6 Paychecks: The Jobs That Pay Every Day

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and it has no salary. There is no weekly paycheck, no business that pays you while you sleep, and no job that deposits money on a schedule. What it does have is repeatable work — jobs you can do again and again, each with a known payout, that add up to a steady income if you treat them like a shift. This guide lists every repeatable paycheck in the game, what it pays, when it unlocks, and which ones are worth your time.

The most reliable paycheck belongs to a pilot. Pilot Pete is a pilot you meet in Chapter 3 who starts offering regular work in Chapter 4: timed cocaine drops to buyers across Leonida. The jobs pay $10,000 to $20,000 per run, and one outlet puts a single delivery mission at $20,000 if all seven packages arrive in time. The work is Jason only, the runs are timed, and you only get the missions as the quest line unfolds — so practice flying first, because the runs punish a bad route. It is the closest thing GTA 6 has to a salary: the same man, the same plane, the same pay, again and again.

Hunting, racing and the fence

The second paycheck is quieter. Bret, Jason's old army buddy, runs hunting jobs in Lummox County from Chapter 2, for Jason only. He pays more as the jobs go on, and he buys animals you find yourself too: one reviewer's large boar earned $2,500, and smaller kills sell straight out of your trunk. The regular hunting rate is reported at $500 per job paid through BuckMe — it is a 100% checklist item as much as an income, but it never runs out. The Demolition Derby at Ambrosia Raceway is the loudest paycheck: $3,800 to $6,300 per win, with the oval paying just over $3,000 and the figure-eight paying about double, and you win by default when the other cars are wrecked. Rent a car at the track for a few hundred and the maths works. Then there is the fence. Once you meet Moses in Chapter 2, he buys older, low-profile cars for roughly $1,000 to $20,000 depending on the car, with a cooldown between sales — and from Chapter 3, the fence Randy pays more for flashy cars, around $4,000 or more for a good tracker.

The mid-tier repeatables

Between the small paychecks and the big scores sits a middle tier that repeats too. Jewelry stores pay $10,000 to $25,000 per robbery from Chapter 3, once you have Yasmani's jobs and a way to turn off the security system and rob the back-room safe before the police arrive. Payday loan stores pay $5,000 to $15,000 each, with Little Cuba paying $15,000 to $25,000 and Cash Flow in Kelly County paying $2,000 to $10,000 — and robbing two of them is what unlocks Yasmani's tips on security vans, which he sells for $10,000. These are robberies, not shifts, so they carry wanted levels and preparation, but they repeat, and they are the bridge between the early game and the vaults. Below them sits the small stuff: mugging gets you a couple of hundred dollars at most, car trunks and rooftop lockboxes usually hold a little cash or drugs, treasure diving is fun but slow, and towing ATMs with a winch-equipped truck is loud and brings the cops. Gang operations pay a ton, usually in narcotics, but expect a firefight — and pawn shop safes pay well, but the clerks shoot back.

One warning from the launch guides matters more than any payout: the money you build up is reported to reset at the end of Chapter 1. In Chapter 1, spend what you make, because the chapter ending wipes the slate. The routine starts in Chapter 2, when Moses, Bret and the derby open up, and the smartest play is often to keep moving through the story — money opens up chapter by chapter, and the best earner in each chapter is usually the one the story just unlocked. A paycheck is not the biggest number in the game, but it is the number that is still there tomorrow, and the players who treat Leonida like a shift, not a lottery, are the ones who can afford the garages, the gear and the next mission's cash goal.

The big scores are not paychecks

It is worth separating the paychecks from the scores, because the guides mix them up. Jewelry stores pay $10,000 to $25,000 per robbery from Chapter 3, payday loan stores pay $5,000 to $15,000, and the large bank vaults at the end of Chapter 4 pay up to $205,000 for about five minutes of work — but those are one-off robberies with wanted levels, preparation and risk, not work you clock into. The same goes for robbing other crews mid-job ($1,000 to $15,000 a crew) and mugging (a couple of hundred dollars at most). The paychecks are the jobs that are still there tomorrow: Pete's plane, Bret's hunts, the derby, the fence. One warning from the launch guides: the money you build up is reported to reset at the end of Chapter 1, so spend what you make early and start your routine in Chapter 2, when the repeatable work opens up. A paycheck is not the biggest number in the game. It is the number you can count on.
