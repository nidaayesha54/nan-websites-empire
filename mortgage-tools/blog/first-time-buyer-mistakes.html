7 First-Time Home Buyer Mistakes to Avoid

Updated 2026-10-12

The 7 costliest first-time home buyer mistakes — from skipping pre-approval to draining savings for the down payment.

1. House-hunting before pre-approval

Falling for a home you cannot finance wastes everyone's time — and in hot markets, sellers ignore offers without a pre-approval letter. Get pre-approved before the first showing.

2. Draining every dollar for the down payment

Putting 20% down then having $2,000 left is how one roof leak becomes a credit-card crisis. Keep a 3–6 month emergency fund after closing, even if it means 10% down plus PMI.

3. Ignoring the true monthly payment

Principal and interest is maybe 70% of the bill. Taxes, insurance, PMI, and HOA make up the rest — always budget the full PITI payment.

4. Big purchases before closing

Financing furniture or a car between pre-approval and closing changes your debt-to-income ratio and can kill the loan. Freeze new credit until keys are in hand.

5. Skipping the inspection to "win" the bid

Waiving inspection on a 40-year-old house is gambling five figures to save $500. At minimum, get an informational inspection even if you do not make it a contingency.

6. Shopping lenders once

Rates and fees vary wildly. Three Loan Estimates take an afternoon and routinely save $3,000–$10,000 over the loan's life.

7. Forgetting closing costs

Budget 2–5% of the price on top of the down payment. On a $400,000 home, that is $8,000–$20,000 due at the table.

Buy smart

Avoid these seven and you are ahead of most buyers. Start with how much house you can afford, then run the payment math before every offer.
