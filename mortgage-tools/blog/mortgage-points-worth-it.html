Are Mortgage Points Worth It?

Updated 2026-10-12

Are mortgage points worth it? How discount points work, the break-even math, and when paying points pays off.

What points are

One discount point costs 1% of the loan amount upfront and typically cuts your rate by about 0.25%. On a $350,000 loan, one point = $3,500 for roughly 0.25% off your rate.

The break-even math

Divide the point cost by the monthly savings. If $3,500 in points saves $55/month, break-even is 64 months — over 5 years. Sell or refinance before then and you lost money; stay longer and you profit every month after.

When points make sense

  • You will stay well past break-even (7+ years is the sweet spot).
  • You have extra cash after down payment and emergency fund.
  • Rates are high and you expect to hold the loan long-term.

When points are a bad deal

  • You might move or refinance within 5 years.
  • Paying points drains your emergency fund.
  • The lender's "points" are mostly origination markup — compare the APR, not just the rate.

Negative points (lender credits)

The mirror image: accept a slightly higher rate in exchange for the lender covering closing costs. Smart if cash is tight or your horizon is short.

Bottom line

Points are a bet on how long you keep the loan. Do the break-even division honestly — our refinance calculator uses the same logic and works for purchase points too.
