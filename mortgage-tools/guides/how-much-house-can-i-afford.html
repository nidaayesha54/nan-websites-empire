How Much House Can I Afford in 2026?

Updated 2026-10-12

How much house can you afford in 2026? Use the 28/36 rule, DTI limits, and real payment math to find your safe price range.

The 28/36 rule lenders use

Most lenders cap your housing costs at 28% of gross monthly income and your total debts at 36% (up to 43–45% for qualified mortgages with compensating factors). On a $100,000 salary ($8,333/month), that means roughly $2,333/month for PITI — principal, interest, taxes, and insurance.

Work backward from the payment

Forget price-first shopping: start from the monthly payment you can afford, then see what price that buys at today's rates. Our mortgage calculator includes taxes, insurance, and PMI so you test the real payment, not just principal and interest. At 6.5% with 20% down, a $2,333 PITI payment buys roughly a $330,000–$350,000 home depending on local taxes.

The costs buyers forget

  • Closing costs: 2–5% of the price, due at signing.
  • Maintenance: budget ~1% of home value per year.
  • PMI: under 20% down adds $30–$70/month per $100k borrowed.
  • HOA dues — and special assessments.

Down payment: how much is enough?

20% avoids PMI, but waiting years to save it while prices and rents rise can cost more than PMI itself. Many buyers do fine with 10% down; 3–5% works with FHA or conventional low-down programs if the payment fits the 28% rule.

Stress-test before you offer

Run three scenarios in the calculator: today's rate, rate +1%, and one income drop. If the payment still fits your budget in all three, you can buy with confidence. Also generate an amortization schedule to see total interest before you commit.

Bottom line

Affordability = payment, not price. Stay near 28% of gross income for housing, keep total debts under 36–43%, and keep a 3–6 month emergency fund after closing.
