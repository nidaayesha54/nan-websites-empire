15 vs 30 Year Mortgage: Which One Wins?

Updated 2026-10-12

15 vs 30 year mortgage compared: monthly payments, total interest, and who should pick which term in 2026.

The payment gap

On a $350,000 loan at typical 2026 rates (say 6.0% for 15-yr vs 6.5% for 30-yr): the 15-year payment is about $2,950/month vs $2,210/month for 30 years — roughly 33% higher. That gap is the whole decision.

Total interest: no contest

The 15-year loan costs about $181,000 in total interest; the 30-year costs about $446,000 — a $265,000 difference. Shorter term + usually lower rate = massive savings, if you can afford the payment.

When 15 years wins

  • The higher payment still leaves 3–6 months of emergency savings intact.
  • You are buying well below your max approval (payment < 25% of income).
  • You value being mortgage-free before retirement over flexibility.

When 30 years wins

  • The 15-year payment would strain your budget — a stretched buyer should never take it.
  • You invest the difference: 30-year + investing the $740/month gap often beats 15-year at historical market returns (with more risk).
  • You want flexibility for career changes, kids, or variable income.

The hybrid: 30-year loan, 15-year behavior

Take the 30-year for safety, then pay extra principal to mimic a 15-year payoff. You keep the lower required payment as a safety valve. Model it with an amortization schedule.

Bottom line

15 years builds equity furiously; 30 years buys flexibility. The wrong choice is the one whose payment keeps you up at night — run both in the mortgage calculator first.
