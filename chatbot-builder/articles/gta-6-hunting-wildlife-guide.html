GTA 6 Hunting: 170 Animals, One Paying Job

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Most GTA 6 guides talk about the city. This one talks about the wild — because Leonida has more than 170 animal species living across its land, air and sea, and hunting them is one of the ways to make money in the game. GTA 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and Rockstar's own description of Mount Kalaga National Park names hunting, fishing and off-road trails outright. This guide covers where to hunt, what lives in Leonida, what pays and what does not, and the parts of the system Rockstar has not explained yet — because the honest hunting guide is shorter than the invented ones, and far more useful.

It matters because the invented hunting guides are already everywhere: full species lists, pelt prices, legendary animal locations, all published before the game had been out a week. Rockstar has not released a full species list. The animals in this guide are the ones seen in Rockstar's trailers, screenshots and the Extended Look, or named in Game Informer's cover story. Everything else is labelled for what it is.

Where to Hunt: Mount Kalaga and the Grassrivers

There are two hunting grounds in Leonida, and they are very different places. Mount Kalaga National Park sits against the state's northern border — forests, rivers and a watering hole where official screenshots show deer and a big cat — and Rockstar describes it as offering prime hunting, fishing and off-road trails. The Grassrivers swamp, Rockstar's version of the Everglades, is the other ground — shallow water, tall grass and mud, where the hunting happens from airboats. Rockstar's own screenshot of the region shows three mud-caked hunters on an airboat, rifles in hand, with a dead alligator across the deck.

The two grounds suit two different hunts. Mount Kalaga is for tracking animals on land — deer at the watering hole, the big cat in the forest, the birds overhead. The Grassrivers is for the water: alligators in the swamp, wading birds in the shallows, and the airboat as the only way to cross the marsh properly. Learn both, and you cover the whole wild half of the map.

What Lives in Leonida: 170 Species

Leonida has more than 170 animal species, across land, air and sea — more than 60 kinds of birds and more than 30 land mammals, plus fish and reptiles. Several have never appeared in a Rockstar game before. The Florida natives Rockstar names are flamingos, manatees, pelicans and alligators, alongside the invasive species Florida is known for: green iguanas, cane toads, feral cats, armadillos and rhesus macaques. Sea life includes dolphins, sea turtles and sharks off the Keys, and the trailers show an alligator walking into a convenience store — a reminder that the wildlife is not confined to the wild.

Some animals can be tracked, and they fall into three tiers: Rare, Discoverable and Legendary. Rockstar is keeping secret which animals are in each tier, and has not said what finding one earns you. The Progress menu has an Animals section, and the stats count animals killed — so the game is tracking your hunting whether or not you are tracking the animals. And there is an in-game agency watching too: POACH, short for Leonida Protection of Animals and Controlled Hunting, is a real agency in the game, which suggests the hunting has rules as well as rewards.

What Pays — and What Does Not

Here is the split that matters most. Hunting pays; fishing does not. Rob Nelson, Rockstar North's co-head of development, told YouTuber TGG that hunting is one of the ways to make money in GTA 6, and that it is unlocked through a character who knows Jason. Fishing, by contrast, is for fun and some narrative and collectible purposes — and it is catch and release, as deep as in Red Dead Redemption 2, because keeping the fish did not fit this game. You cannot keep, eat or sell the fish you catch. Do not plan your money around fishing: it is a pastime, not a payday.

The early numbers for hunting come from early-copy gameplay, so treat them as reported rather than confirmed. In third-party footage of an early copy, Bret Vogel paid Jason $500 for the first hunt. Studying a Whitetail Doe through the scope showed a value of $220 to $380, and the animal loaded into the truck was tagged Pristine — which suggests condition matters to the price, as it did in Red Dead Redemption 2. Those are the only payout numbers available so far. If a guide quotes a full price list for every animal, it is guessing.

The Dangers: Alligators Are Not Decoration

The wildlife is not scenery, and the Grassrivers proves it. Rockstar describes the swamp's alligators as hostile, and the region's own copy warns that gators may be the famous attraction but the swamp hides deadlier predators — which could mean armed poachers as much as animals. In one third-party stream, an alligator attack in the water ended with the WASTED screen. Getting close to an alligator brings up a STUDY prompt, and studying it is the safe way to learn what you are dealing with before you decide whether to hunt it or leave it alone.

The practical advice is the same as in any hunting game: study first, shoot second, and respect the water. The Grassrivers is shallow, the alligators are fast, and the airboat is your only quick way out. Mount Kalaga is safer ground, but the big cat in the forest is a reminder that the land has teeth too. Hunt with a plan, keep your distance from the water's edge, and do not wade into the swamp on foot.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you hunt in GTA 6?

Yes. Rockstar's Mount Kalaga description names hunting directly, and Rob Nelson has said hunting is a way to make money, unlocked through a character who knows Jason. How the hunt itself works — tracking, the shot, the sale — has not been shown by Rockstar.

Can you fish in GTA 6, and does it pay?

Yes, you can fish — and no, it does not pay. Fishing is catch and release, as deep as in Red Dead Redemption 2, but you cannot keep, eat or sell the fish. Hunting is the money; fishing is for fun, narrative and collectibles.

How many animals are in GTA 6?

More than 170 species, including more than 60 birds and more than 30 land mammals. Some can be tracked as Rare, Discoverable or Legendary — but Rockstar has not said which animals are in each tier.

The Bottom Line

Hunting in GTA 6 is real, it pays, and it lives in two places: Mount Kalaga for the land hunt and the Grassrivers for the alligators. Fishing is catch and release and pays nothing. The species list is 170 strong and mostly unmapped, the legendary animals are still secret, and the only prices anyone has are the first hunt's $500 and a deer's $220 to $380. Hunt carefully, study first, and let the wild half of Leonida reveal itself as you play.
