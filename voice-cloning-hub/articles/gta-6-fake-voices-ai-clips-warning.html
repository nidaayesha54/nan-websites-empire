GTA 6 Fake Voices: The AI Clips Fooling Millions

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Some of the most-watched "GTA 6" clips of the year are not GTA 6 at all. They are AI-generated videos with AI-generated voices — characters who sound like Jason and Lucia, in scenes that never happened, presented as if they were filmed inside the game. One hoax clip of Lucia on a rain-soaked beach pulled about 34,000 likes and 8.1 million views in days. The creator later admitted everything was AI-generated, and said about half the viewers had believed it was real. This guide explains how the fake voices work, the clips that fooled millions, and how to spot one before you share it.

It matters because the fakes are getting harder to spot. AI video tools can now mimic camera shake, weather effects, character animation and interface overlays that feel close to Rockstar's style. For a casual viewer scrolling on a phone, a fake clip looks good enough to pass. The voice is usually what gives it away — if you know what to listen for.

The hoax that proved how easy it is

The clearest case is the account that called itself Zap Actu GTA6. After GTA 6 was pushed from May 26 to November 19, 2026, every hint of fresh footage started to feel priceless to fans, and the account began posting short clips of characters walking through Leonida, claiming the footage came from anonymous accounts filming from inside Rockstar's studios, and branding one clip an "EXTREMELY SERIOUS SITUATION". The Lucia beach clip surged to about 34,000 likes and 8.1 million views in days, and community notes flagged the videos as AI-generated — yet huge numbers of viewers still treated them as real.

Within a week, the backlash caught up. The poster wiped the old clips, rebranded the account, and published a statement admitting there was no genuine footage behind the viral clips, only generative video stitched together to look believable: "Alright, enough, I trolled you. I have NO real information about GTA 6. Everything you saw was entirely generated by AI." The poster called it a social experiment, saying the goal was to prove how easy it is to fool people. Their own rough stats suggested about half of the viewers believed the videos.

The AI "Lucia" earning thousands

The same technology has a commercial side. An AI-generated Instagram model called Adriana Vega became known as the "AI Lucia" of GTA 6, after fans noticed her resemblance to Lucia Caminos. Her creator, a 26-year-old from south London identified only as James, says fans who spotted the resemblance have paid him more than £30,000 in a single month on Fanvue, the subscription platform that openly allows AI creators. Reports add two more AI accounts riding the same wave and a combined figure of £52,000 a month. None of these numbers has been verified independently — there is no dashboard, no statement from Fanvue and no comment from Rockstar — but the pattern is real: a face that looks like Lucia, a voice that sounds like her, and an audience willing to pay.

The creator says he built Adriana from scratch and never designed her to look like Lucia. That is the problem with AI lookalikes: intent does not matter once the resemblance is there. A voice clone only needs to be close enough, for three seconds, on a phone screen.

The fake relationship clips

The most viral fakes are not action scenes — they are domestic ones. One clip posted on launch day shows Jason in bed while Lucia, in a black robe, sits upset and accuses him of cheating on her "last night" — which turns out to have been in her dream. To appease her, Jason walks to a florist in his tank top and boxers, demands a large bouquet of red roses at gunpoint, and prepares to apologise at a front door. The clip drew 46,000 likes and hundreds of comments, and the commenters were split: many immediately identified it as AI-generated, pointing to unnatural faces and voices, while others treated it as real footage and reacted strongly to the premise.

Another satirical clip in the same style shows Jason taking care of Lucia on a pink bed, then robbing a convenience store to skip the checkout queue on his way home with roses and chocolate. These clips are jokes, and they are funny — but they are not the game. The game does have a relationship system, and it does track how Jason and Lucia treat each other, but no scene in it plays out like these clips. When a clip shows a relationship moment that feels like a sitcom, that is the first sign it was generated, not captured.

How to spot an AI voice

Listen for flat emotion first. AI voices handle short lines well and long emotional ones badly — the anger sounds the same as the apology, and the emphasis lands on the wrong word. Then listen for names: AI voices mispronounce names and places, and they stumble on words a real actor would say without thinking. Then watch the mouth: AI lips lag the audio by a fraction, and the face stays oddly still while the voice gets loud.

Then check the source. A real clip comes from a player with a history, a channel, and other footage. A fake clip comes from an account with no history and a caption that dares you to believe it.

What to do when you spot one

Do not share it as real. Do not re-upload it with a caption that presents it as gameplay. If you want to share it, label it clearly as AI-generated fan content — in the title or description, in the video itself, and in the platform's AI disclosure setting. The creators who label their clips build trust. The ones who present fakes as real lose it the moment the truth comes out, and the truth about an AI clip always comes out.

And if a clip promises something Rockstar has never announced — a new voice, a hidden scene, a secret mechanic — treat it as fabricated until a named, checkable source confirms it. The game is out now, and real footage is everywhere.

Flat emotion, mispronounced names, lips that lag the audio, and a face that stays still while the voice gets loud. Then check the account: no history and no other footage is a red flag.

No — Adriana Vega is an AI-generated model whose resemblance to Lucia is coincidental, according to her creator. The £30,000-a-month earnings figure is self-reported and unverified.

The bottom line: if a GTA 6 voice sounds almost right, check the source before you share it. Flat emotion, wrong names, lagging lips and an account with no history are the tells — and a clip with no source is a fabrication until proven otherwise.
