AEC Service Support Jobs 2026 — Apply by Oct 29

The Australian Electoral Commission is hiring Service Support Officers across Queensland and New South Wales for electoral roll maintenance and election delivery — part-time roles closing 29 October 2026.

✅ Updated October 24, 2026
Home » Australia Government Jobs » Articles

The Australian Electoral Commission (AEC) is hiring Service Support Officers for its Queensland and New South Wales state operations, with positions based in Hervey Bay/Maryborough, Toowoomba and Robina (QLD). The roles were posted on APS Jobs on 16 October 2026 and applications close on Thursday 29 October 2026 at 11:59pm AEDT.

Service Support Officers sit at the heart of Australia's electoral machinery: they maintain the integrity of the electoral roll, deliver frontline client services, and support the delivery of electoral events — the federal elections, by-elections and referendums that define the AEC's mission. In small divisional teams of about three staff, you will assist in supervising APS 1 employees, work with Project Officers and Service Officers, and report to an APS 6 Team Leader.

The roles are part-time, but the listing is upfront that full-time work will likely be required during peak workload periods — electoral events bring tight timeframes, increased public scrutiny and a rapidly expanding temporary workforce that these officers help manage.

What Service Support Officers Actually Do

  • Electoral roll maintenance — enrolment administration that keeps the roll accurate between and during electoral events;
  • Frontline client services — answering public enquiries on enrolment and voting;
  • Project delivery — election-specific projects with tight timeframes and multiple interdependent priorities;
  • Supervision — assisting APS 1 staff day-to-day, and supervising small teams plus temporary election workers during events;
  • Risk and issues management — identifying problems that could affect delivery, escalating concerns, and contributing improvement ideas.

Eligibility — The Non-Negotiables

  • Citizenship: you must be an Australian citizen — this is a condition of engagement, not a preference;
  • Political neutrality: anyone who is, or is seen to be, active in political affairs or referendum issues cannot be considered — an inherent requirement of AEC employment;
  • Clearances: you must consent to, undergo, obtain and maintain both a character clearance and the role's security clearance;
  • Capabilities: resilience in changing environments, clear communication, collaboration, accountability for accurate work, and general computer proficiency including Microsoft Office.

Why These Roles Suit Career-Starters

The capability list reads like a generalist APS entry profile — no degree specialisation is demanded, and the emphasis is on behavioural strengths (adaptability, judgement, teamwork) rather than technical credentials. For Queensland and northern NSW residents, the regional office locations mean no relocation to Canberra, and electoral-event peaks offer a fast track to supervisory experience that is hard to get elsewhere in the APS at this level. The part-time structure also suits students or carers — with the honest caveat that election periods will demand full-time commitment.

Frequently Asked Questions

When do AEC Service Support Officer applications close?

Thursday 29 October 2026 at 11:59pm AEDT. The roles were posted on APS Jobs on 16 October 2026.

Where are the AEC Service Support Officer roles based?

Hervey Bay/Maryborough, Toowoomba and Robina in Queensland, plus New South Wales state operations offices.

Do I need to be an Australian citizen to work at the AEC?

Yes — Australian citizenship is a condition of engagement for all AEC positions. Political neutrality is also mandatory: active political campaigners cannot be considered.

Are the AEC Service Support Officer roles full-time?

The roles are part-time, but full-time hours are likely during peak workload periods such as electoral events.

What does an AEC Service Support Officer do?

Electoral roll maintenance, frontline client services, enrolment administration and project work across the electoral cycle — including supervising APS 1 staff and temporary election workers during events.

What clearances are required?

Applicants must consent to, obtain and maintain a character clearance and the security clearance required for the role.
