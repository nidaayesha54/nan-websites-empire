Australia Government Jobs 2026

The Australian Public Service Commission's 13 October 2026 pay proposal — plus a $630 million investment in care-sector wages — is reshaping what government work pays. Here's what job seekers need to know.

✅ Updated October 17, 2026
Home » Australia Government Jobs » Articles

Pay in the Australian Public Service is moving. On 13 October 2026, the Australian Public Service Commission (APSC) announced proposed pay increases for the next APS enterprise bargaining round — the mechanism setting wages for 170,000+ federal public servants. The Government also committed $630 million to fund wage increases for 460,000+ health, disability and community-services workers.

For job seekers, these are not abstract policy moves — they change the math of choosing public-sector work. The Australian Federal Police Association (AFPA) has already welcomed the care-sector investment while calling for a similar commitment to fix AFP recruitment and retention pressures. When unions and agencies talk pay this loudly, hiring conditions are shifting in applicants' favour.

What Enterprise Bargaining Means for Your Pay

APS pay is not set by individual negotiation — it is set by enterprise agreements negotiated between agencies and unions. Each bargaining round typically runs for three years and determines:

  • Base pay increases — the percentage applied to salary bands (APS1 through EL2 and SES);
  • Allowances and loadings — overtime, shift penalties, higher-duties and remote-location allowances;
  • Conditions — flexible work, leave and redundancy provisions;
  • Superannuation — the APS already pays 15.4%, well above the national guarantee.

A strong bargaining outcome lifts the floor for every advertised APS role — which is why timing your entry around a new agreement can pay off for years.

The $630 Million Care-Sector Signal

The Government's $630 million commitment funds wage increases flowing from Fair Work Commission decisions that found care work — health, disability, aged care, community services — has been historically undervalued because it is female-dominated. For job seekers, the signal is direct: care-sector public roles are entering a period of structurally rising pay, not one-off bonuses.

The AFP Pressure Point — and What It Tells Applicants

AFPA President Alex Caruana welcomed the care-sector investment but argued the same logic applies to the Australian Federal Police, which competes with state and territory police for talent under Commonwealth bargaining constraints. Translation for applicants: agencies under hiring pressure lobby for better pay to attract you. Sectors where unions are publicly demanding parity are sectors where applicants currently hold leverage.

How to Use This as a Job Seeker

1
Target sectors with funded pay growth — care, health, disability and community-services APS roles are riding a funded uplift.
2
Check the enterprise agreement behind any advertised role — its pay tables are the real numbers, not the ad's headline range.
3
Factor in 15.4% super when comparing APS offers to private-sector salaries.
4
Ask about allowances at interview — higher-duties, overtime and location allowances add materially to base pay.
5
Time bulk-recruitment entries around new agreements — intakes hired under fresh agreements lock in the new rates.

The Bottom Line

October 2026 is a negotiation-heavy moment for Australian public-sector pay: a fresh APSC bargaining proposal, $630 million flowing into care-sector wages, and the AFP publicly demanding its share. None of this guarantees any individual offer — but the direction is unmistakable, and it is upward. Job seekers who understand which sectors carry funded pay growth, and who read the enterprise agreement behind the advertisement, will negotiate and choose better.

Frequently Asked Questions

What did the APSC announce on 13 October 2026?

The APSC outlined the Government's proposed pay increases for the next APS enterprise bargaining round, covering 170,000+ federal public servants.

What is the $630 million government investment for?

It funds wage increases for more than 460,000 workers across health, disability and community services, following Fair Work Commission decisions on the historic undervaluation of female-dominated care work.

Will AFP officers get a pay rise too?

The AFPA has called for an equivalent investment for the Australian Federal Police — but any AFP increase depends on its own bargaining outcome.

How does enterprise bargaining affect advertised APS salaries?

Advertised ranges come from the agency's current enterprise agreement pay tables. Roles hired under fresh agreements lock in the new rates.

Is APS superannuation really 15.4%?

Yes — the APS employer superannuation contribution is 15.4%, significantly above the national super guarantee, and it applies on top of the advertised base salary.
