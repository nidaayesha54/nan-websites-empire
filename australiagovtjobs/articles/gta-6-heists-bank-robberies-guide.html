GTA 6 Heists: Bank Robberies and the $205,000 Vault

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

The biggest paydays in Grand Theft Auto 6 do not come from side jobs or treasure dives. They come from heists — bank robberies and vault jobs that pay up to $205,000, but only at the end of Chapter 4. GTA 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and its story is about escape rather than empire-building — which means the heists are not about getting rich for its own sake. They are about getting out. This guide explains how heists and bank robberies work in GTA 6, what the verified money methods are along the way, and how to protect the cash once you have it.

The Mid-Tier Money Methods

The verified mid-tier methods are the foundation of the whole economy. Jewelry store robberies pay around $20,000 each — Victoria Lee Jeweler in Capri, and Blanc and Giroux in the Decor District, are the named targets, and a snap gun from Chapter 3 breaks in silently. That last detail matters: a silent break-in means fewer witnesses, and in a game where the wanted system only chases what it sees, fewer witnesses means a cleaner getaway. Do the job quietly, and the police never know you were there.

Stealing cars and selling them right next to a Pay 'N' Spray is the low-effort staple — no planning, no setup, just a car and a buyer. Kenton's treasure dives pay around $1,400 per wreck with almost no risk, once they unlock after the mission "Island Dreams". Each of these is a verified method, and each of them feeds the same goal: enough money to reach the vaults, and enough money to enjoy Vice City on the way. The treasure dives are the safest of the three; the jewelry robberies are the most lucrative; the car sales are the most convenient. Use all three, and the money adds up fast.

The Bank Vaults: $205,000 at the End of Chapter 4

The bank vaults are the biggest single paydays in the game — up to $205,000 — and they come at the end of Chapter 4. That timing is deliberate: the vaults are the climax of the money story, the job everything else has been building toward. By the time you reach them, you will have the skills, the equipment, and the crew to pull them off — and the money to make the risk worthwhile. The vaults are not a shortcut to wealth. They are the reward for playing the whole game.

That is also why the vaults matter to the story. GTA 6 is a tale about escape, not empire-building, and the vault money is the escape fund: the money that gets Jason and Lucia out. The story treats the biggest heist as a means to an end, and the end is freedom — which is a different emotional shape from GTA 5, where the heists were about building an empire. Play the vaults as the story asks you to, and they feel like the climax they are meant to be.

Protecting Your Money: Bank It or Lose It

Earning the money is only half the job; keeping it is the other half. Pocket cash is lost when you die or get arrested, and it does not carry into Chapter 2 — so walking around with a fortune in your pocket is a risk, not a flex. The verified ways to protect it are simple: stash spare money under the floorboards in Jason's bedroom, and open the BuckMe bank account once the mission "Buck Me" is available, then deposit your cash at ATMs. Banked money is safe. Pocket money is a donation to the next mugger, cop, or bad decision.

This matters most around the big paydays. A jewelry robbery pays around $20,000 — deposit it before you lose it. A vault pays up to $205,000 — deposit it before you celebrate. The habit is the same every time: earn, bank, then play. And remember that missions cannot be replayed, so the money you lose to a death or an arrest is gone for good. The bank is the only safety net the game gives you, and it is free to use.

Heists and the Wanted System

When the police do come, the escape is about breaking line of sight. Get out of view and stay out of view — the search is local, not omniscient. Duck down a side street, change vehicles, and put distance between you and the scene. The police are looking for what they saw: the car, the clothes, the direction you were heading. A different car, a different street, and a few blocks of distance can turn a hot pursuit into a cold search. And save manually after every big payday — Pause, Game, Save Game — because the autosave only fires after a mission ends, and a crash after a vault job is the worst time to discover it.

Money Gates: When the Game Is Waiting for Cash

One more thing to know about the money system: some missions are money-gated. The next mission only opens once Jason and Lucia have earned enough money, a detail confirmed by Rockstar North's Rob Nelson speaking via Famitsu. If no mission marker appears and your cash is thin, the game is not broken — it is waiting for you to earn. Jewelry robberies, car sales, and treasure dives are the verified routes to the cash you need, and the marker will appear once you have it.

This is also why the mid-tier methods matter even after the vaults. The vaults are the climax, but the money gates are the rhythm of the whole game: earn, unlock, earn, unlock. A player who banks every payday and works the mid-tier methods steadily will never hit a gate they cannot open. A player who spends everything on the first neon car they see will spend hours wondering why the story has stopped.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the fastest way to make money early in GTA 6?

Jewelry store robberies pay around $20,000 each — Victoria Lee Jeweler in Capri and Blanc and Giroux in the Decor District, with a snap gun from Chapter 3 for a silent entry. Car sales next to a Pay 'N' Spray and Kenton's treasure dives at around $1,400 per wreck are the low-risk staples.

How do I keep my money safe?

Deposit it. Pocket cash is lost when you die or get arrested and does not carry into Chapter 2. Stash spare money under the floorboards in Jason's bedroom, and open the BuckMe bank account after the mission "Buck Me", then deposit at ATMs.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6's heists pay up to $205,000 from bank vaults at the end of Chapter 4 — but the vaults are the reward, not the shortcut. Work the mid-tier methods — jewelry at around $20,000 a job, car sales, treasure dives at around $1,400 a wreck — bank every dollar at ATMs, and keep your jobs quiet, because the police only chase what they see. Earn steadily, save manually after every payday, and the vaults will be there when you are ready for them.
