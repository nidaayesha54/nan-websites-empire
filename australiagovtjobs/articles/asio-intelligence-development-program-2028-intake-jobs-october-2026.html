Australia Government Jobs 2026

The Australian Security Intelligence Organisation (ASIO) is recruiting Intelligence Officers and Analysts for its 2028 Intelligence Development Program intake in Canberra — paying $100,425–$118,220, closing 16 November 2026.

✅ Updated October 23, 2026

The Australian Security Intelligence Organisation (ASIO), Australia's domestic security intelligence agency, is recruiting for the Intelligence Development Program 2028 intakes — the flagship entry pathway into intelligence analysis and operations. The official vacancy listing places the role at $100,425–$118,220 per annum in Canberra, with applications closing 16 November 2026.

This is a genuine once-a-year entry point into Australia's national security establishment. Successful candidates undertake a 12-month development program with comprehensive training in intelligence assessment, analysis and advice — and at the end of it, they earn an Intelligence Analyst licence, the credential that opens the door to a long ASIO career.

The published recruitment timeline is tight: the program advertisement closes 16 November 2026, online testing runs through November 2026, and assessment centres are scheduled from March 2027.

What the 12-Month Program Covers

The Intelligence Development Program is ASIO's structured training ground. Over twelve months, participants build the fundamental skills and knowledge required to gain an Intelligence Analyst licence — the qualification that certifies them to work across ASIO's analytical functions:

  • Discovery — finding the crucial piece of intelligence inside large datasets, uncovering the "unknown unknowns";
  • Investigations — taking one piece of security intelligence and fitting it into the big picture to uncover individuals seeking to harm Australia;
  • Intelligence assessment and advice — producing high-quality assessments that inform government, national-security partners and industry;
  • Rotations — licenced analysts regularly move positions, so one career can contain many different jobs.

Critically, the program is also the entry point to the Intelligence Officer stream — the internally selected pathway into human intelligence (HUMINT) collection, where officers build confidential relationships within communities to mitigate security threats.

Who ASIO Is Looking For

Notably, ASIO's program materials emphasise attributes over qualifications. The agency says it wants proactive team players who are curious and creative, exercise good judgement, think critically and enjoy working in a dynamic team.

There are hard gates, however. Australian citizenship is a standard requirement for all ASIO employment, and candidates must be prepared to undergo a rigorous and extensive selection process — application assessment, eligibility checks, online testing, skills-based assessment and interview — plus high-level security vetting. Applications are handled in strict confidence, and ASIO requires applicants to maintain the same confidentiality about their own application.

Two Rules That Could End Your Application

ASIO's recruitment materials flag two non-obvious traps that candidates routinely miss:

  • AI policy: ASIO does not use any form of AI in its recruitment process, and every application is reviewed by a selection panel. If you use AI tools to draft, keep it to initial drafts — the final submission must be personalised and accurately reflect your own skills. The use of AI tools or recording devices during interview is strictly prohibited, and detection can discontinue your application and any future ones;
  • Discretion: do not discuss your application with others. The confidentiality expectation is itself part of the assessment.
