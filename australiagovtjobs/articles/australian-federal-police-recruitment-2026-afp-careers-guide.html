Australian Federal Police Recruitment 2026: AFP Careers, $82,499 Salary & How to Apply

The Australian Federal Police is recruiting in 2026 with a $82,499 starting salary, 15.4% super and six weeks of paid leave. Here is who can apply and how the process works.

AFP Police Jobs 2026 Recruitment

The AFP: Federal Policing With National Reach

The Australian Federal Police (AFP) is Australia’s national policing agency — investigating cybercrime and serious organised crime, countering terrorism, running international operations and protecting Commonwealth interests. October 2026 recruitment advertising shows the AFP is actively looking for new recruits, and the package is competitive by any measure.

The headline numbers: a starting salary of $82,499 plus 15.4% superannuation, six weeks of paid leave, overtime and relocation support — with recruits paid to train and accommodation and meals covered during training.

Why AFP Recruitment Appeals in 2026

  • $82,499 starting salary with 15.4% super on top
  • Six weeks paid leave — well above the national standard
  • Paid training, with accommodation and meals paid for
  • Relocation support for postings across the country
  • A career with national scope: cyber, counter-terrorism, international deployments

The Selection Process

AFP recruitment is multi-stage and demanding — expect a process along these lines (confirm exact stages on the AFP’s current recruitment page):

  1. Online application. Lodge through the AFP’s official recruitment portal with your details, history and motivation.
  2. Aptitude and entry assessments. Cognitive and reasoning tests mapped to policing duties.
  3. Interview. Behavioural questions about integrity, judgment under pressure and community interaction.
  4. Fitness assessment. Policing is physical — the AFP tests baseline fitness before training.
  5. Psychological and medical assessment. Suitability and fitness for operational duties.
  6. Security clearance. A thorough background check — be prepared for it to take time.

How to Apply

  1. Check the AFP’s official recruitment page for current intakes — the AFP recruits in scheduled intakes, not continuously.
  2. Confirm eligibility early. Citizenship, age and integrity requirements are assessed first; don’t start the long process without clearing these.
  3. Train for fitness now. The fitness assessment surprises many applicants — build it into your routine from day one.
  4. Prepare your background record. Gather identification, employment and address history — the clearance stage moves faster with complete documents.
Federal policing selection can take many months from application to offer. Treat the clearance and medical stages as timelines you cannot rush — apply early and keep alternatives warm.

AFP Career Paths and Progression

An AFP career is not one job but a ladder of specialisations. Recruits typically start in general duties or protective service roles and can later move into specialist streams: cybercrime investigation, counter-terrorism, serious and organised crime, forensics, intelligence, and international operations — the AFP deploys liaison officers and missions across the Pacific and beyond. Protective service officers guard Commonwealth interests, parliamentarians and critical infrastructure.

Progression is structured and merit-based, with rank advancement bringing both responsibility and pay rises. The six weeks of annual leave, overtime provisions and relocation support continue throughout the career — and the AFP’s national footprint means postings from Canberra to regional hubs to overseas, a variety few Australian employers can match.
