Germany Government Jobs 2026

Every German public-sector job notice cites a pay grade — E9b, E13, A12 — that decides your salary. This guide explains the TVöD system for 2026: what E1–E15 grades mean, how experience steps work, which variants apply where, and the verified salary thresholds foreign applicants must clear.

German public-sector salaries are set by collective agreements, not individual negotiation. The key one is the TVöD (Tarifvertrag für den öffentlichen Dienst), whose pay tables current for 2026 are valid from 1 May 2026. Knowing the grades lets you read any notice and gauge pay. This guide explains the structure and says plainly where to check official tables for exact euro figures — we do not publish per-grade euro amounts we cannot verify from an official source.

What the TVöD Is and Who It Covers

The TVöD covers most public-sector employees — Beamte (civil servants) use a separate system. It applies across the federal administration and municipalities; the federal states run the parallel TV-L.

Entgeltgruppen E1–E15: What the Grades Mean

Each role gets an Entgeltgruppe (pay grade) E1–E15, reflecting complexity and qualification level:

  • E1–E4: auxiliary roles — no specialist training required;
  • E5–E8: trained skilled staff — completed vocational training (Ausbildung);
  • E9–E12: graduate professionals — Bachelor's/Master's-level work, including specialist posts;
  • E13–E15: highly qualified specialists — roughly doctorate-level expertise, e.g. research roles.

Some grades have sub-divisions (e.g. E9a, E9b, E9c) that fine-tune the band. An E13 posting signals Master's/PhD-level work — useful for judging whether you are over- or under-qualified.

Stufen 1–6: How Experience Steps Work

Within each grade, pay rises through Stufen (steps) 1 to 6 with recognised experience. New hires start at Stufe 1; documented comparable experience can place you higher. The same E11 posting pays differently at Stufe 1 than at Stufe 4.

TVöD-VKA, TVöD Bund and TV-L: Know Which Applies

"TVöD" has variants depending on the employer:

  • TVöD Bund — federal administration (ministries, agencies);
  • TVöD-VKA — municipal employers (cities, districts);
  • TV-L — the federal states (Länder): state ministries, police and universities.

Tip: a posting saying "E10 TVöD-VKA" needs the VKA table specifically — the variants differ slightly. Tables are published by the BMI and employer associations.

Beamte vs Employees: An Honest Note on Civil-Servant Status

Many foreign applicants assume public jobs can lead to Beamte (civil servant) status with lifetime tenure. The honest position: Beamte status generally requires German or EU citizenship. Non-EU applicants should plan on the TVöD employee track — strong protection, regulated hours, transparent pay — rather than a path the law normally closes to them.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the TVöD?

The Tarifvertrag für den öffentlichen Dienst — the collective agreement setting pay for most public-sector employees, with Bund, VKA and TV-L variants.

What do the E1–E15 grades mean?

Grades from E1 (auxiliary) to E15 (doctorate-level). Graduates typically sit in E9–E12; skilled staff in E5–E8.

Are the TVöD tables current in 2026?

Yes — valid from 1 May 2026. Check BMI and municipal employer associations for official euro figures.

What EU Blue Card salary thresholds apply in 2026?

€50,700 standard; €45,934.20 for shortage occupations. The Chancenkarte needs €1,091/month funds proof.

Can foreigners become Beamte in Germany?

Generally no — Beamte status normally requires German or EU citizenship. Foreign applicants are hired as TVöD employees.
