Germany Public Sector Jobs 2026: Bund.de, TVöD Pay & English-Speaking Roles

Your complete 2026 guide to Germany public sector jobs — how to search Bund.de and Interamt.de, how the TVöD and TV-L pay scales work, and where English-speaking roles exist.

Germany's public sector is one of Europe's most stable employers — transparent pay scales, strong job security, generous benefits, and, in some areas, roles open to English speakers. This guide explains how Germany public sector jobs in 2026 work, walks you through the two official portals — Bund.de and Interamt.de — breaks down TVöD and TV-L pay groups, and shows where English-only candidates can compete.

The German Public Sector in 2026

Germany's public service (öffentlicher Dienst) operates on three levels: the federal government (Bund), the 16 federal states (Länder), and municipalities (Kommunen). Federal ministries and agencies employ two staff categories: Angestellte (public employees paid under collective agreements) and Beamte (appointed civil servants with lifetime tenure and separate pension rights). In 2026, digitisation and demographic change keep demand steady in IT, administration, and technical fields.

Bund.de and Interamt.de: A Portal Walkthrough

Bund.de is the central portal of the federal administration, with a jobs section aggregating vacancies from federal ministries and agencies, filterable by location, job family, and pay group. Interamt.de is the administration's dedicated job portal and online application system — where most federal vacancies are published and applications are actually submitted.

  • Search weekly New vacancies post continuously, with deadlines typically four to eight weeks out.
  • Filter by pay group Use the Entgeltgruppe filters (E9–E13) to match your qualifications.
  • Read the Anforderungsprofil Check each posting's requirements profile line by line — German ads are precise about credentials.
  • Apply in the portal Most federal vacancies accept applications only via the portal's online form.

TVöD and TV-L Pay Scales Explained

Public sector pay follows collective agreements, not individual negotiation. Federal employees are paid under TVöD, most state employees under TV-L. Both use numbered pay groups (Entgeltgruppen E1–E15) — higher numbers mean higher qualifications and pay — with experience steps (Stufen) raising pay over time. Beamte use a separate A-scale.

Where English-Speaking Roles Exist

Many federal roles need German language, but English-speaking roles exist in research, IT, and international organisations. Federal research centres advertise English-language positions for scientists and engineers; IT and cybersecurity roles increasingly accept English applications for specialists; and international organisations based in Germany — such as the UN bodies in Bonn — recruit in English as standard. How to Apply for Germany Public Sector Jobs

  1. Search Bund.de and Interamt.de for roles matching your qualifications and pay group.
  2. Prepare a tabular CV (Lebenslauf), cover letter (Anschreiben), and degree certificates — German hiring is formal.
  3. Check each posting's language requirement: most need German at B2 or above; English-only roles say so explicitly.
  4. Submit via the portal's online form before the deadline — late or email applications are routinely rejected.
  5. Expect a structured process: written tests or panel interviews are common, and decisions take 6–12 weeks.
  6. Non-EU applicants should line up residence and work-permit options (e.g. the EU Blue Card) alongside applications.

Eligibility and Language Notes

EU/EEA citizens apply on equal terms with German nationals. Non-EU applicants need a valid residence and work permit; many skilled roles qualify for the EU Blue Card. Applications are generally expected in German, and most federal roles require German at B2 level or above — exceptions are research, IT, and international-organisation postings. Regulated professions such as teaching or healthcare may need credential recognition (Anerkennung) before hiring.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between TVöD and TV-L?

TVöD covers federal and municipal employees; TV-L covers employees of the 16 federal states. Both use E1–E15 pay groups.

Can I apply for German public sector jobs without speaking German?

Some research, IT, and international-organisation roles accept English. Most federal administrative roles require German at B2 or above.

Do non-EU citizens get hired in the German public sector?

Yes, with a valid residence and work permit — many skilled roles qualify for the EU Blue Card.

What is Interamt.de?

Interamt.de is the federal administration's official job portal and application system, where most federal vacancies are published.

How long does German public-sector hiring take?

Typically 6–12 weeks from deadline to offer, including selection tests or panel interviews.
