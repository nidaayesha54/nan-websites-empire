The Federal Ministry for Education, Family, Seniors, Women and Youth (BMBFSFJ) is hiring a fixed-term Referent in Berlin — reference BMBFSFJ-2026-017, applications close 1 November 2026.

The Bundesministerium für Bildung, Familie, Senioren, Frauen und Jugend (BMBFSFJ) — Germany's Federal Ministry for Education, Family, Seniors, Women and Youth — is hiring a Referentin / Referent (m/w/d) on a fixed-term (befristet) basis, reference BMBFSFJ-2026-017. The vacancy was published on service.bund.de on 22 October 2026 and applications close on 1 November 2026 — just eight days later, so interested candidates must move fast.

A Referent at a federal ministry is a senior policy officer role: drafting ministerial briefings, preparing legislation and parliamentary answers, coordinating with other ministries and Länder, and representing the ministry's position in working groups. The BMBFSFJ covers an unusually broad portfolio — from early-childhood education and family policy to senior citizens' affairs and women's equality — so the role touches some of the most publicly visible policy areas in German domestic politics.

Honesty first: this is a German-language role — ministry policy work is done entirely in German, and the listing gives no English-language option. It is also fixed-term, not a permanent appointment. The vacancy is single-sourced from the service.bund.de listing, so verify the contract length, pay grade and exact qualification requirements on the live listing before applying.

What a Ministry Referent Does

  • Policy drafting: preparing ministerial briefings, cabinet papers, draft legislation and parliamentary answers;
  • Coordination: aligning positions with other federal ministries, the Länder and EU institutions;
  • Stakeholder work: liaising with associations, expert bodies and civil-society organisations in the ministry's portfolio areas;
  • Representation: speaking for the ministry in inter-ministerial working groups and committees;
  • Project ownership: running defined policy projects from concept to decision, often under tight political deadlines.

Who Should Apply

  • A completed university degree at master's level (or equivalent) in a field relevant to the ministry's portfolio — law, political science, education, social sciences or public administration;
  • Professional-level German in speech and writing — the working language of the ministry;
  • Experience in policy, administration or public affairs — prior work in a ministry, parliament, association or think tank is a strong asset;
  • Analytical rigour, discretion and the ability to work under political time pressure;
  • Willingness to accept a fixed-term appointment — this is not a permanent post.
