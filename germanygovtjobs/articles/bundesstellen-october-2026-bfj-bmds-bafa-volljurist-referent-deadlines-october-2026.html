Germany Government Jobs 2026

A cluster of federal vacancies closes October 25–26, 2026: fully-qualified lawyers at the Federal Office of Justice and BAFA, plus a Referent post at the new Ministry for Digitalisation. Here is the complete roundup with honest eligibility notes.

✅ Updated October 20, 2026
Home » Germany Government Jobs » Articles

A wave of federal-government vacancies closes on 25–26 October 2026, and several are senior professional roles. Current listings include Volljuristen (fully-qualified lawyers) at the Bundesamt für Justiz (BfJ) in Bonn, a Volljurist for energy law at BAFA in Eschborn, and a Referent for change management and personnel development at the Bundesministerium für Digitales und Staatsmodernisierung (BMDS) — Germany's ministry for digitalisation and state modernisation.

These are classic höherer Dienst (higher service) openings: the legal posts require the second state examination (Volljurist), and the BMDS Referent role sits in the ministry's central staff division. State-level listings close in the same window too — including a housing-policy Referent in Berlin (26 October) and a department-head post in Mecklenburg-Vorpommern (26 October).

Deadlines this tight mean the application window is days, not weeks. If you match a profile, prepare documents now.

What the Key Roles Involve

  • BfJ Volljurist (Bonn): legal work in Department III of the Federal Office of Justice — federal legal administration, registers and legal-services functions; requires both state examinations (Volljurist);
  • BMDS Referent (S 3): change management and personnel development in the ministry steering digital transformation and state modernisation — strategy, HR development and organisational projects;
  • BAFA Volljurist (Energy, Eschborn): energy-law work at the Federal Office for Economic Affairs and Export Control — a growth field as Germany's energy transition accelerates;

Eligibility Requirements

  • Legal posts: Volljurist status — first and second state examinations (zweites Staatsexamen);
  • Referent posts: relevant university degree (Master's level) plus professional experience in the field;
  • Citizenship: German or EU citizenship for standard federal roles (check each notice);
  • German language: near-native written and spoken German is effectively mandatory for federal legal and policy roles;
  • Willingness to undergo security screening where the role requires it.

How to Apply

1
Find each posting on the federal jobs portals — bund.de and the recruiting authority's own careers pages — and note the exact reference number and deadline.
2
Prepare the standard federal application set: tabular CV, degree and examination certificates, proof of citizenship, and work references.
3
Write a targeted cover letter addressing the specific tasks in the notice — federal selections score criterion-by-criterion.
4
Submit through the online portal named in the posting before 25 or 26 October 2026 — late applications are not considered.
5
Expect structured interviews and possibly written assessments in November/December.

Why This Cluster Matters

The BMDS Referent post is a signal of where federal hiring is heading: digitalisation and change management inside the ministry created to modernise the German state. Lawyers at BfJ and BAFA get the stability of the higher service with genuinely substantive legal work. With all three closing within 48 hours of each other, this is the last week to act — shortlist the one that fits your profile and submit a complete, criterion-mapped application.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which federal jobs close October 25–26, 2026?

Volljurist posts at the Federal Office of Justice (Bonn) and BAFA (Eschborn), a Referent post at the digital ministry BMDS, plus state-level roles in Berlin and Mecklenburg-Vorpommern.

What does Volljurist mean?

A fully-qualified lawyer who has passed both German state examinations.

What is the salary?

Higher-service roles pay A 13+ or E 13+ TVöD Bund — roughly €60,000–€90,000+ gross per year.

Can non-EU citizens apply?

Standard federal roles require German or EU citizenship; check the Staatsangehörigkeit clause in each notice.

How do I apply for federal jobs?

Through the online portal named in each posting (bund.de and agency career pages), with CV, certificates and references before the deadline.
