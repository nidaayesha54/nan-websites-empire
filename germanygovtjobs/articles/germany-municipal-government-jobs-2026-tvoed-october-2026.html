Germany Government Jobs 2026

Germany's cities and municipalities are among the country's biggest public employers — and they hire on the transparent TVöD-VKA tariff. Here are the roles, the pay groups and where to find 2026 vacancies.

✅ Updated October 18, 2026
Home » Germany Government Jobs » Articles

When people think of German public-sector jobs, they picture federal ministries — but the real hiring volume sits with the Kommunen: Germany's cities, towns and districts. Municipal administrations run the Bürgeramt (citizens' office), Ordnungsamt (public order office), Bauamt (building authority), Sozialamt (social services), the Standesamt (registry office) and thousands of Kitas (municipal childcare centres) — and they recruit continuously.

Municipal employees are typically Tarifbeschäftigte paid on the TVöD-VKA scale (the municipal variant of the public-sector tariff), with published pay groups that make salary expectations unusually transparent.

Role Families in Municipal Administration

  • Bürgeramt / Bürgerbüro — resident services: IDs, registrations, certificates (classic E6–E8 roles);
  • Ordnungsamt — public-order enforcement, permits and local regulations;
  • Bauamt & Stadtplanung — building permits, urban planning and engineering (E9–E13);
  • Sozialamt & Jugendamt — social work and youth welfare (often TVöD-SuE);
  • Kitas — Erzieher/innen in municipal childcare, one of the largest shortage occupations in the country;
  • Standesamt, Kämmerei, IT & Digitalisierung — registry, finance, and the growing municipal IT and digitalisation teams.

Entry Routes

  • Ausbildung: Verwaltungsfachangestellte/r — the classic 3-year municipal administration apprenticeship, the single most common door into city government;
  • Direct entry: experienced hires into advertised E-group positions via Interamt;
  • Quereinstieg: career changers, especially in Kitas, IT and the skilled trades within municipal utilities (Stadtwerke);
  • Dual study: Public Management and Verwaltungsinformatik bachelor's programmes run in partnership with municipalities.

Language Reality Check

Unlike federal research or IT roles, municipal administration is German-language work: you serve residents, write official notices (Bescheide) and apply German administrative law. Realistically, B2–C1 German is the minimum for most roles — but for German speakers, municipal jobs offer exceptional stability close to home.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is TVöD-VKA?

The municipal variant of the TVöD public-sector tariff scale, used by German cities, towns and districts for their tariff employees (Tarifbeschäftigte).

What do German municipal jobs pay?

It depends on the Entgeltgruppe: E8 Stufe 3 is about €3,840/month gross (~€2,450–2,550 net for a single person), while E13 specialist roles sit around €4,900–€7,100 gross. Kita educators use the TVöD-SuE table.

Do I need German for municipal jobs?

Yes, realistically B2–C1 — municipal administration serves residents in German and applies German administrative law.

What is the main training route into city government?

The 3-year Verwaltungsfachangestellte/r apprenticeship (Ausbildung) — the classic entry path into municipal administration.

Where are municipal jobs advertised?

On Interamt.de (filter to municipal employers) and on each city's own career page.
