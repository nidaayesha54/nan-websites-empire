Germany Government Jobs 2026 for Foreigners: Eligibility, Visa Rules & EU Citizenship Requirements

Thousands of public-sector vacancies are advertised in Germany in 2026 — but citizenship rules decide who can apply. Here is the full eligibility picture for Germans, EU citizens and third-country nationals.

Germany Jobs Expats Eligibility 2026

Germany’s Public Sector Is Hiring — With Rules

Germany’s public service (öffentlicher Dienst) is one of Europe’s largest employers, and in 2026 thousands of vacancies are advertised at any given time — from federal ministries to city administrations. Popular fields for international applicants include IT and digitalisation, engineering and technical services, nursing and healthcare administration, education, social work, finance and tax administration, and skilled trades within public utilities.

But German public employment has a two-track system — and your nationality decides which track is open to you. This guide lays out the rules clearly.

Beamte vs Angestellte: The Two Tracks

Germany distinguishes between Beamte (civil servants with lifetime tenure and special status) and Angestellte (public-sector employees on normal contracts, usually under the TVöD collective agreement). For internationals, the practical difference is this:

  • Angestellte roles: broadly open to non-EU citizens with the right qualifications and residence/work rights — this is where most foreign hires land.
  • Beamte status: under the Federal Civil Servants Act (Bundesbeamtengesetz), normally limited to German citizens, EU citizens, EEA nationals (Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway) and Swiss citizens. Third-country nationals can only be appointed in exceptional cases with Interior Ministry approval — rare in practice.

Sovereign Authority: The Hard Limit

German law gives preference to German and EU citizens for posts involving sovereign authority (hoheitliche Aufgaben) — police, the judiciary, tax inspection with enforcement powers, and certain security-related roles. If a job involves exercising state power, assume citizenship requirements apply and check the ad carefully.

How to Apply as a Foreigner

  1. Confirm your work rights. EU/EEA/Swiss citizens can work freely; third-country nationals need a qualifying residence permit (e.g. EU Blue Card, skilled-worker visa) before a public employer can hire them.
  2. Target Angestellte postings. Filter for employee-status roles rather than Beamtenstellen — and avoid hoheitliche Aufgaben posts unless you hold EU citizenship.
  3. Prepare German-language documents. A German-style CV (tabellarischer Lebenslauf), certified translations of qualifications, and proof of German proficiency where required.
  4. Get qualifications recognised. Regulated professions (teaching, nursing, some engineering) require formal recognition (Anerkennung) before appointment.
  5. Apply through the official portal. Most postings run through karriere.bund.de or Interamt.de with strict deadlines — late applications are rejected.
Beware of unofficial sites charging “application fees” for German public jobs — real öffentlicher Dienst recruitment is free, and vacancies appear on the official portals above.

FAQs

Can non-EU citizens work for the German government?

Yes, as salaried employees (Angestellte) in many fields — IT, engineering, healthcare and administration hire internationally. Full civil-servant status (Beamte) is heavily restricted for non-EU citizens.

Who can become a Beamte in Germany?

Normally German citizens, EU citizens, EEA nationals (Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway) and Swiss citizens. Third-country nationals need exceptional approval from the Interior Ministry.

Which German public-sector jobs are off-limits to foreigners?

Posts involving sovereign authority (hoheitliche Aufgaben) — police, judiciary, tax inspection with enforcement powers and certain security roles — generally require German or EU citizenship.

Where are German government jobs advertised?

karriere.bund.de (federal), Interamt.de (federal, state and municipal — the largest portal), service.bund.de, and city/state agency career pages.

Do I need German language skills?

For most public-sector roles, yes — administrative German is essential. IT and international-organisation roles are the main exceptions where English can suffice.
