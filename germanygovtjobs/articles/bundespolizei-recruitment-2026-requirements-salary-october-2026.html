Germany Government Jobs 2026

The Bundespolizei — Germany's federal police — recruits year-round for the mittlerer and gehobener Dienst. Here are the 2026 requirements, the A6–A11 pay bands, the training pipeline and how to apply.

✅ Updated October 18, 2026
Home » Germany Government Jobs » Articles

The Bundespolizei (German Federal Police) is one of the country's largest uniformed employers, responsible for border protection, railway policing, aviation security and the protection of federal institutions. Recruitment runs year-round through the official portal — with two main entry routes: the mittlerer Dienst (middle service) and the gehobener Dienst (upper service).

As federal civil servants (Beamte), Bundespolizei officers are paid on the Besoldungsordnung A scale — transparent, published pay bands that rise with seniority, plus allowances for shift work and special duties.

Federal police median-pay data for 2026 places the Polizeivollzugsdienst “Fachkraft” level near €3,887/month gross. Shift and hardship allowances (Zulagen) add more on top.

Requirements

  • Citizenship: German or EU citizenship is the standard route into the Beamtenverhältnis, since these are sovereign-authority roles;
  • Education: mittlerer Dienst — mittlere Reife (intermediate school-leaving certificate); gehobener Dienst — Abitur or Fachhochschulreife (university-entrance qualification) or equivalent;
  • Age & fitness: minimum age 16–18 depending on the route, with a sport and physical test, medical examination and eyesight standards;
  • Character: a clean criminal record and the personal suitability for a position of public trust;
  • German language: strong German is essential — it is the working and training language throughout.

The Selection Process

Applicants face the Eignungsauswahlverfahren (aptitude selection procedure): a written or computer-based test of reasoning and language ability, the sport test, a medical examination and a structured interview or assessment stage. The physical standards are published in advance, so candidates can train to them deliberately — preparation is the decisive factor for most applicants.

Training Pipeline

1
Mittlerer Dienst: 2.5-year Vorbereitungsdienst at a Bundespolizei academy — law, tactics, firearms handling, first aid and practical placements.
2
Gehobener Dienst: 3-year dual study programme (bachelor-equivalent) combining academic study with operational practice.
3
Graduation: appointment as Polizeimeister or Polizeikommissar with full Beamter status.
4
Career: promotion through the A-scale with seniority, plus specialist units — investigations, aviation security and federal protection duties.

How to Apply

1
Open bundespolizei.de (careers section) or karriere.bund.de and choose your entry route.
2
Check the current application deadlines for your desired start date — intakes run on fixed cycles.
3
Submit the online application with certificates and a tabular CV.
4
Prepare for the Eignungsauswahlverfahren: train for the sport test and practise the written modules in advance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What salary does the Bundespolizei pay in 2026?

Officers are paid on the Besoldungsordnung A scale: roughly €2,900–€3,400/month gross at A6–A8 entry (mittlerer Dienst) and €3,600–€4,400 at A9–A11 entry (gehobener Dienst), plus shift and hardship allowances.

What are the requirements to join the Bundespolizei?

German or EU citizenship, mittlere Reife for the mittlerer Dienst or Abitur/Fachhochschulreife for the gehobener Dienst, physical fitness (sport test), a medical exam, a clean record and strong German.

How long is Bundespolizei training?

2.5 years of Vorbereitungsdienst for the mittlerer Dienst; a 3-year dual study programme for the gehobener Dienst.

Can EU citizens join the Bundespolizei?

Yes — EU citizens can generally be appointed as Beamte; third-country nationals only in exceptional cases with special approval.

Where do I apply?

Through the official careers section of bundespolizei.de or the federal portal karriere.bund.de — never through intermediaries.
