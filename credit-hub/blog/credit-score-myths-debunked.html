7 Credit Score Myths, Debunked

The bad advice that refuses to die — and what actually works.

Myth 1: "Checking my score hurts it"

False. Checking your own score is a soft inquiry — it never affects your score. Only hard inquiries (when you apply for credit) have a small, temporary effect. Check freely.

Myth 2: "Carrying a balance builds credit"

False — and expensive. Paying in full every month builds the exact same payment history without the interest. Carrying a balance only helps your card issuer's profits.

Myth 3: "Closing old cards helps my score"

Usually the opposite. Closing a card cuts your total available credit (raising utilization) and eventually shortens your credit history. Keep old no-fee cards open.

Myth 4: "My income affects my score"

False. Credit scores measure how you handle credit — payment history, balances, history length. Income isn't on your credit report at all (lenders consider it separately when you apply).

Myth 5: "All debt is equally bad"

Nuanced. Revolving balances (credit cards) hurt utilization immediately; installment loans (mortgage, student loans) weigh less per dollar once established. Maxed-out cards are the real score-killer.

Myth 6: "I only have one credit score"

False. You have dozens — three bureaus (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion), multiple FICO versions, plus VantageScore. They usually move together, but small differences are normal.

Myth 7: "Paying off collections removes them"

Not automatically. Paid collections can still report for up to 7 years (newer FICO versions ignore paid collections, but many lenders use older versions). Always negotiate pay-for-delete in writing before paying.
