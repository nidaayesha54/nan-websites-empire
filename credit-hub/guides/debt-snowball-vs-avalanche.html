Debt Snowball vs Avalanche: Which Wins?

Math vs psychology — the honest comparison, with real numbers.

The two methods

  • ⛄ Snowball: pay minimums on everything, throw extra cash at the smallest balance first. Quick wins, one fewer bill sooner.
  • 🏔️ Avalanche: pay minimums on everything, throw extra cash at the highest APR first. Mathematically optimal — least total interest.

How big is the actual difference?

Smaller than most people think. Take $12,000 across three debts ($4,500 at 24.99%, $1,800 at 19.99%, $6,000 at 11.99%) with $200/month extra:

MethodMonthsTotal interest
Avalanche~28~$2,100
Snowball~29~$2,350

Avalanche saves roughly $250 and one month here. With bigger rate spreads, the gap widens — run your own numbers to see.

The psychology argument for snowball

Behavioral research consistently finds that quick wins build momentum: people who eliminate a whole debt early are more likely to stick with the plan. A mathematically perfect plan you abandon in month three loses to an imperfect plan you finish.

The hybrid: snowball the tiny ones, avalanche the rest

A popular compromise: kill any balance under ~$500 first for the dopamine hit, then switch to avalanche for the heavy lifting. You get momentum and near-optimal math.

Rules that matter more than the method

  1. Stop adding new debt while paying off — otherwise you're bailing a leaking boat.
  2. Automate the extra payment so willpower isn't required monthly.
  3. Keep a small emergency buffer ($500–1,000) so one surprise doesn't become new credit-card debt.
  4. Negotiate APRs — a 5-minute hardship call can lower rates, which beats any method choice.
