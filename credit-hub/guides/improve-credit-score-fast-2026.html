How to Improve Your Credit Score Fast in 2026

Ten moves ranked by impact — starting with things that can work within a single billing cycle.

1. Pay down revolving balances (fastest lever)

Credit utilization — your balances divided by your limits — is ~30% of a FICO score and has no memory. Only the latest reported ratio counts. Dropping from 60% to under 10% can add 20–50+ points as soon as the new statement reports. Use our utilization calculator to find your exact pay-down targets.

2. Pay before the statement closing date

Most issuers report your statement balance to the bureaus. Paying your balance down a few days before the statement closes means a lower number gets reported — even if you pay in full every month anyway.

3. Set up autopay for at least the minimum

Payment history is ~35% of your score — the single biggest factor. One 30-day late payment can cost 60–110 points. Autopay on minimums guarantees you never miss; you can always pay extra manually.

4. Ask for credit limit increases

A higher limit with identical spending instantly lowers utilization. Many issuers grant increases with only a soft inquiry (no score hit) — ask first whether it will be a hard pull.

5. Become an authorized user (strategically)

Being added to a family member's old, well-managed card can import their positive history to your file. Only do this with someone whose payment record is spotless.

6. Dispute errors on your reports

Pull your free reports at AnnualCreditReport.com and dispute anything wrong — wrong balances, accounts that aren't yours, late marks you paid on time. The bureau must investigate, usually within 30 days.

7. Keep old cards open

Closing your oldest card shortens your average account age and cuts total available credit — both hurt. If there's an annual fee, ask to downgrade to a no-fee version instead of closing.

8. Diversify with a credit-builder product

Credit mix is ~10% of your score. A small credit-builder loan or secured card adds an installment account, which helps thin files. Just make sure payments are reported to all three bureaus.

9. Space out applications

Each hard inquiry costs a few points. They're minor individually, but several in a short window signal risk. Rate-shopping for a mortgage or auto loan within 14–45 days counts as one inquiry — use that window.

10. Be patient with the long game

There's no legitimate overnight fix. But 6–12 months of on-time payments plus low utilization reliably moves scores from fair to good. Track progress with our score simulator.

What NOT to do

  • Don't pay a "credit repair" company upfront — everything they can legally do, you can do free.
  • Don't close cards to "simplify" — see #7.
  • Don't max out a new card right after approval — high utilization on a fresh account is a double hit.
