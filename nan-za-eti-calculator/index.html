How the ETI Is Calculated (SARS Tables from 1 April 2025)

The incentive amount depends on the employee's monthly remuneration and whether they are in their first or second 12-month qualifying period:

Monthly remunerationFirst 12 monthsNext 12 months
R0 – R2,49960% of remuneration30% of remuneration
R2,500 – R5,499R1,500R750
R5,500 – R7,499R1,500 − [0.75 × (rem − R5,500)]R750 − [0.375 × (rem − R5,500)]
R7,500 and aboveNo ETI

Worked Example

An employee earns R6,000 a month in their first year: ETI = R1,500 − [0.75 × (6,000 − 5,500)] = R1,500 − R375 = R1,125 per month. In year two the same employee gives R750 − [0.375 × 500] = R562.50 per month.

Who Qualifies? The ETI Eligibility Checklist

Employee must: be 18–29 years old (age rule falls away inside designated special economic zones), hold a valid South African ID, asylum seeker permit or refugee ID, not be a domestic worker, not be a person connected to the employer, and have been employed on or after 1 October 2013. The wage must meet the applicable minimum wage — if no minimum wage applies, the employee must earn at least R2,000 (for 160 hours worked) and less than R7,500 a month.

Employer must: be registered for PAYE, be tax compliant, and not be a national/provincial/local government body or public entity. The incentive may not be used to displace existing employees.

Part-Time Workers (Under 160 Hours)

If the qualifying employee is employed and paid for fewer than 160 hours in a month, the ETI is reduced in the same ratio — hours worked ÷ 160. A worker paid for 120 hours attracts 75% of the full amount.

How the Claim Works

ETI is claimed on your monthly EMP201 return to SARS and set off against the PAYE you owe for that month. If the ETI is bigger than your PAYE liability, the balance is carried forward to the next month (or can be reimbursed). The incentive runs for a maximum of 24 qualifying months per employee — only months in which the employee actually qualified count toward the two 12-month periods.

FAQs

What is the Employment Tax Incentive?

ETI is a SARS incentive that lets eligible employers reduce their monthly PAYE bill when they hire qualifying young employees (18–29). It was introduced in 2014 to encourage youth employment and the current calculation tables took effect on 1 April 2025.

What is the maximum ETI per employee?

R1,500 per month in the first 12 qualifying months (for pay of R2,500–R5,499), dropping to R750 per month in the second 12 months. Low earners get a percentage instead: 60% then 30% of their wage.

Can I claim ETI for a domestic worker?

No. Domestic workers are expressly excluded from ETI, as are persons connected to the employer.

Do public-sector employers qualify?

No. National, provincial and local government authorities and public entities cannot claim ETI.

What happens to unused ETI?

If your ETI claim exceeds the PAYE payable in a month, the excess is carried forward to the following month, and SARS can reimburse the balance — the incentive is never wasted.

Is ETI still available in 2026?

Yes. The incentive continues, with the April 2025 remuneration thresholds (R2,500 / R5,500 / R7,500) still in force. ETI is claimed via your normal monthly EMP201 return.
