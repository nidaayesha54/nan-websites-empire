South Africa ETI: The Complete Guide to the Employment Tax Incentive

The Employment Tax Incentive pays employers up to R1,500 a month for each qualifying young worker — claimed straight against PAYE. This guide covers the rules, the tables and the common mistakes.

Key takeaways:
  • Up to R1,500/month per qualifying worker in year one, R750/month in year two.
  • Claimed by paying less PAYE on the monthly EMP201 — no application needed.
  • Workers must be 18–29 earning under R7,500/month; claims cap at 24 months.

South Africa has a standing offer for employers: hire young people, and SARS will reduce your PAYE bill every month. The Employment Tax Incentive (ETI) has been running since 2014, was extended to February 2029, and in April 2025 its tables were updated — keeping the headline value at R1,500 a month per qualifying worker for the first year.

This guide explains how the ETI works: who qualifies, the exact SARS tables, how part-time workers are handled, how the claim is made on the EMP201, and the mistakes that cost employers their claims.

What the ETI Is — and Isn't

The ETI is a tax incentive, not a grant. There is no application form and no payout to the business. Instead, a qualifying employer simply pays over less PAYE to SARS each month — the incentive amount is deducted from the PAYE liability on the monthly EMP201 return. Any unused portion rolls over to the next month, and persistent excesses can be reimbursed by SARS.

Crucially, the ETI never reduces the employee's pay. It's a rebate on the employer's tax bill, invisible on the payslip.

Who Qualifies? The Checklist

The employer must: be registered for PAYE, be tax compliant, and not be a government department (or certain public entities). The employee must:

  • Be 18 to 29 years old (with a valid SA ID, asylum seeker permit, or refugee ID).
  • Earn less than R7,500 per month.
  • Meet the minimum wage test: at least R2,500 a month for 160+ hours (apportioned down for fewer hours).
  • Not be a domestic worker, a connected person, or someone the employer is already claiming ETI for beyond the allowed period.

The SARS Tables (from 1 April 2025)

The incentive runs for a maximum of 24 months per employee — 12 months at the full rate, then 12 months at half:

Monthly remunerationFirst 12 monthsSecond 12 months
R0 – R2,49960% of remuneration30% of remuneration
R2,500 – R5,499R1,500R750
R5,500 – R7,499R1,500 − 0.75 × (pay − R5,500)R750 − 0.375 × (pay − R5,500)
R7,500+No ETI

The April 2025 changes widened the bands (previously the R1,500 band topped out at R4,500 and the ceiling was R6,500) to keep pace with minimum-wage increases — but the R1,500/R750 maximums were deliberately retained.

Worked Examples

  • R3,000/month, first year: falls in the R2,500–R5,499 band → R1,500/month; second year → R750/month.
  • R2,000/month, first year: 60% × 2,000 = R1,200/month.
  • R6,000/month, first year: 1,500 − 0.75 × (6,000 − 5,500) = 1,500 − 375 = R1,125/month.
  • Maximum over two years for one worker: (12 × 1,500) + (12 × 750) = R27,000.

Part-Time Workers: The 160-Hour Rule

Where an employee works fewer than 160 hours in a month, the incentive is scaled down: multiply the table amount by hours worked ÷ 160. The R2,500 minimum wage is apportioned the same way — so a worker on 80 hours must earn at least R1,250 to qualify, and the claim is halved. Overtime hours count toward the 160; the test is hours employed and paid, not just ordinary hours.

How the Claim Works on the EMP201

1

Identify qualifiers

Each month, list employees meeting every eligibility test for that month.

2

Compute per employee

Apply the SARS table to that month's remuneration, with the part-time adjustment.

3

Offset PAYE

Deduct the total from the PAYE due on the EMP201. Unused amounts roll over.

Common Mistakes That Kill Claims

  • Claiming for workers earning R7,500+. The ceiling is hard — one rand over and the month's claim is zero.
  • Claiming beyond 24 months. Track each employee's qualifying months; month 25 gets nothing.
  • Ignoring tax compliance. A non-compliant employer loses the incentive for the non-compliant months.
  • Double-claiming. Only one employer can claim ETI for an employee in a given month.
  • Forgetting the minimum wage test. Below R2,500 (for 160 hours) means no ETI at all.

What SARS Checks: Record-Keeping

Claiming ETI creates a paper trail SARS can audit. For each qualifying employee and each month, keep: proof of age (ID), the employment contract showing hours, monthly remuneration records, and your tax-compliance status. The two things auditors probe most are age eligibility (is the employee really 18–29 for the whole claim period?) and remuneration accuracy (does the claimed band match actual pay?). A worker who turns 30 mid-claim stops qualifying from that month — the 24-month clock doesn't override the age test.

ETI Alongside Other Incentives

The ETI can sit alongside other employment-related tax benefits, but each has its own rules — for example, learnership allowances under section 12H of the Income Tax Act reward registered learnerships with separate deductions. The incentives address different policy goals (youth hiring vs skills development) and are computed independently. Don't assume claiming one blocks the other, but do confirm with your tax practitioner that the same employee-month isn't being double-counted under two provisions that forbid it. Employers operating in Malaysia face a different kind of payroll levy — the HRDF training levy.

Should Small Businesses Bother?

Absolutely — the ETI is arguably most valuable for small employers. A business with three qualifying workers at R4,000 a month claims R4,500 monthly — R54,000 a year — with no application, just accurate EMP201 completion. The main cost is discipline: monthly eligibility checks and clean payroll records. For many SMEs, that single incentive covers a meaningful share of a junior hire's cost, which is exactly the policy intent. And don't forget the other employer-side obligations — estimate your annual COIDA assessment while you're reviewing payroll compliance.

ETI at a Glance

What: a monthly reduction in PAYE payable for each qualifying young worker. How much: up to R1,500/month (months 1–12), up to R750/month (months 13–24). Who: tax-compliant PAYE-registered employers; employees aged 18–29 earning under R7,500/month. How: complete the ETI fields on the monthly EMP201 — no separate application. Until: the incentive is legislated through February 2029. Watch out for: the 24-month per-employee cap, the 160-hour part-time adjustment, and the minimum-wage test.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the ETI reduce what my employee takes home?

No. It's a reduction in the employer's PAYE payable to SARS. The employee's gross pay, PAYE withheld on their certificate, and net pay are unaffected.

Can I claim ETI for a 30-year-old?

No — the employee must be 18 to 29. The incentive is specifically a youth-employment measure.

What if my PAYE bill is smaller than my ETI claim?

The excess rolls over to following months. Persistent unusable excesses can be claimed back from SARS as a reimbursement.

Do I need to apply to SARS first?

No application is needed. If you're a tax-compliant employer registered for PAYE with qualifying employees, you claim by completing the ETI fields on the monthly EMP201.

Until when does the ETI run?

The incentive is currently legislated through the end of February 2029, following extensions endorsed after Nedlac reviews.
