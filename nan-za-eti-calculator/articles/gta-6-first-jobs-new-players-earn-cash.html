GTA 6 First Jobs: 7 Ways New Players Earn Their First Cash

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

South Africa's Employment Tax Incentive exists to get young people into their first job: a break for employers who hire someone starting out, so that a first paycheque is easier to earn. Leonida works the same way, minus the paperwork. When you start Grand Theft Auto 6 you are broke, unknown, and a long way from the big scores — and the game expects you to earn your way up through small, repeatable jobs before the vaults and the supercars open up. This guide lists the first jobs that actually pay in GTA 6, in the order a new player can reach them, with the payouts players have reported from launch week.

Rob Nelson, co-studio head at Rockstar North, named the two earners himself: robbing stores, and stealing and selling cars. Those are the foundation. Everything else on this list builds on them, and none of it needs a big investment, a high wanted level, or a story chapter you have not reached yet.

1. Store robberies — the first paycheque

Walk into any store you can enter, aim at the clerk, take the register cash, and leave. Launch footage puts a till at $260 to $435 — a night gas station, the Key Lento Mini-Mart, a liquor store. It is not much, but it is immediate, it needs no tools, and it teaches the core loop of the whole game: take the money, bank it, and do not get caught carrying it. The mistake new players make is robbing the same till twice in one run. Take it once, bank it, move on.

2. Stealing cars for Moses — the steady wage

In Chapter 2 you meet Moses, a mechanic at Watkins Auto Parts in Port Gellhorn, and he buys cars without trackers. A rough '86 Rancher fetched $350 in launch play; a Vapid Speedo brought $2,700. The range depends on the car, and Moses needs a couple of minutes between purchases. Scan a car first — hold the button and the game shows its sell value — and skip anything with a tracker. This is the closest thing Leonida has to a salary: repeatable, low-risk, and it pays every single time.

3. Dive tours — $400 for half a day in the water

The Leonida Keys are built on water, and the dive tour is the friendliest first job in the game. On a launch stream, a dive guide paid Jason $400 at the dock for half a dive tour. It needs no weapon, no wanted level, and no risk at all — just the time to get out to the Keys and back. It will not make you rich. It will keep you fed while you learn the map.

4. Street races — $3,000 to $6,000 a win

Talk to the organiser in Salton to browse the races. Buy-ins start at $300, a rental car costs $600 to $900 if yours does not qualify, and payouts run up to $6,000. Lucia won a point-to-point race for $3,000 in launch footage. Racing is the first job that feels like a job: an entry fee, a start line, and a result that depends on how well you drive rather than how loud you are.

5. Hunting with Bret — a boar pays $2,500

From Chapter 2, Jason can take hunting requests from Bret, an old military friend in Lummox County. Bret pays more as the requested animals get larger, and one documented boar sale brought in $2,500, with Bret collecting the carcass. Hunting needs patience and a rifle, not a crew, and it pays in the kind of quiet money that never draws police attention.

6. Side hustles by the hour

Once you know the map, the small stuff adds up. Playing pool in bars pays around $758 an hour. Monster truck mudding at the Thrillbilly Mud Club north of Ambrosia pays around $3,000 an hour. Street, track and dirt races pay around $5,000 an hour when you win. Treasure diving in the Keys pays around $6,000 an hour, and gym training improves your lung capacity for longer dives. None of these figures is measured by Rockstar — they come from launch-week player reports — but the pattern is consistent: the quieter the hustle, the steadier the pay.

7. What unlocks next — and why it matters

First jobs are the ladder, not the ceiling. Chapter 3 opens jewelry stores at $10,000 to $25,000 a job and small banks at $5,000 to $45,000. The end of Chapter 4 opens the large bank vaults, which pay up to $205,000 for about five minutes of work — with an instant four-star wanted level as the price. None of that is reachable on day one, and that is the point. The game wants you to learn the small jobs first, bank the cash, and arrive at the big ones with owned weapons, a full garage, and money in the bank instead of your pocket.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best first job in GTA 6? Stealing cars for Moses. It pays $350 to $2,700 a car, needs no tools beyond a slim jim, and repeats all day.

How much does a store robbery pay? A till pays $260 to $435 in launch footage. It is quick cash, not a career.

Can you earn money without a wanted level? Yes — dive tours, hunting, pool, mudding and racing can all pay without police attention.

When do the big payouts unlock? Jewelry and small banks open in Chapter 3. Large vaults open at the end of Chapter 4.

Should you bank your cash after every job? Yes. Cash in your pocket is lost when you die or get arrested. Banked cash is safe.

Bottom line: start with the till, graduate to Moses, and let the quiet hustles pay your rent while the story opens the big doors. In Leonida, as in real life, the first job is never the best-paid one — it is the one that teaches you how the money works.
