GTA 6 Time: How Long Is a Day in Leonida?

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Time in GTA 6 is not decoration. It decides when shops open, when the streets are crowded, when the neon comes on, and how long you have to wait for anything that depends on the clock. And it moves faster than you think: one full day in Leonida lasts 144 real minutes, which means every in-game hour passes in six real minutes. This guide explains how the clock works, what changes between day and night, and how to skip time when you need to.

The number to remember is 144. Two hours and twenty-four minutes of your real time is one complete day and night in the game. That is three times slower than the clock in GTA 5, where a day passed in 48 real minutes — Leonida's days are long, and that length is deliberate. You will spend real time in each part of the day, and the game wants you to notice the difference between them.

How the clock works

The clock runs constantly while you play. Six real minutes make one in-game hour, so a drive that takes you ten real minutes has moved the game forward by nearly two in-game hours. Missions, side activities and the world's routines all run on this clock, and the sun, the traffic and the crowds move with it. Morning brings commuters and open shops; the afternoon brings heat and crowds; the evening brings the neon and the nightlife.

Because the day is long, you cannot simply wait out the clock by standing still. If a mission or an activity needs a particular time of day, standing around for an hour of real time is a poor way to get there. The game gives you a better tool, and it is the same one every GTA has had: sleep.

Sleeping to skip time

The fastest way to skip time in GTA 6 is to sleep in a safehouse bed. Walk up to the bed, use the Rest prompt, and lie down — then choose the time of day you want to wake up at. The clock jumps straight there, so you can go from noon to midnight in seconds instead of sitting through more than an hour of real time. Sleeping costs nothing, and a full night also restores your health and stamina and gives a buff to focus.

Jason's stilt house in Key Lento is the first safehouse with a bed, available from the opening hours of the story. Jason and Lucia move to a new safehouse at the end of each chapter, so the bed you use changes as the story moves on. The routine is the same everywhere: go home, sleep, wake up when you need to be awake.

Day and night in Vice City

Night is when Vice City becomes itself. The neon comes on, the clubs fill, the streets change character, and the game's own guides point to night as the best time for the kind of work Jason and Lucia do. That is not a rule written in the game — it is the shape of the city. Crowds thin out in some places and gather in others, patrols move differently, and the cover of darkness is real in a game where the police act on what they witness.

Day has its own uses. Shops are open, the streets are busy enough to blend into, and the countryside — the Leonida Keys, the Grassrivers, the farms around Ambrosia — is a different place in daylight than it is at night. The smart approach is to match the time to the job: daylight for errands and exploration, darkness for the work that should not be seen.

Why the long day matters

A 144-minute day changes how you plan. In GTA 5, a day passed so quickly that time of day barely mattered — you could blink and it was night. In GTA 6, the time you choose is a real decision, because you will live in it for a while. If you start a job at noon, you will still be in daylight when it ends. If you sleep to midnight, the whole session takes on a different mood.

It also makes the health app's sleep tracking matter. The game tracks whether Jason and Lucia are well rested, and going without sleep slows the regeneration of some attributes. The clock and the body are linked: time passes, tiredness builds, and the bed is the reset. Players who sleep regularly will find their characters sharper than players who run on empty for days at a time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is one day in GTA 6?

One full in-game day lasts 144 real minutes — two hours and twenty-four minutes. Each in-game hour takes six real minutes. That is three times longer than a day in GTA 5.

Can you skip time in GTA 6?

Yes. Sleep in a safehouse bed and choose your wake-up time. The clock jumps straight to it, and a full night also restores health and stamina and buffs focus.

Does time pass while you are in a mission?

The clock runs during normal play. Sleeping is the only way to jump it forward quickly, which is why planning your time around the bed is the most useful time skill in the game.

The bottom line: Leonida's clock is slow enough to live in and fast enough to respect. Learn the 144-minute day, use the bed to choose your hours, and match the time to the job — daylight for errands, darkness for the work that should not be seen.
