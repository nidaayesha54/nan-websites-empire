GTA 6 Map: Vice City Spots to Visit First

November 28, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and its map is the biggest the series has ever built: Vice City and the whole Leonida state — neon boulevards, beaches, swamps, countryside, and small towns, all connected. Vice Beach: Start Where the Game Starts Feeling Big

Vice Beach is the first place to visit, because it is where the map shows off everything at once: the ocean, the palm trees, the art-deco hotels along the boulevard, and the crowds. It is also home to the two wildest viral moments since launch — the mutant crab attack on the sand, and the dog on a jet ski. Nobody has confirmed what triggers the crab, so treat any guide that promises to make it appear with caution, and the dog's rescue of a stranded swimmer is just as unexplained. Neither can be scheduled, so enjoy them when they happen. While you are there, take a jet ski out, and remember Kenton's treasure dives pay around $1,400 per wreck once they unlock after the mission "Island Dreams".

The Neon Boulevards at Night

Vice City changes after dark, and the neon boulevards are the second stop. Pink and teal neon, wet asphalt, headlights piercing the dark, busy traffic — the night lighting is the part of the game reviewers praised most, and it is best seen on foot or in a slow drive rather than at full speed. The boulevards also show the new wanted system clearly: a crowded street at noon has dozens of potential witnesses, and the police act only on what they witness. Learn the city at night before you try anything risky in it by day.

Grassrivers and the Swamps

North of the city, the map turns into Grassrivers: swamps, airboats, and alligators. This is the wild side of Leonida. Hunting lives here — around 170 animals, and the first hunt pays about $500, a real early income source. Fishing, by contrast, does not pay. Move slowly, watch the water, and remember the alligators are part of the world, not scenery. Learn the airboats here too — they handle nothing like a car and are the only sensible way through shallow water.

The Keys and the Open Water

South of the city, the Keys are the map's other world: small islands, bridges, boats, and open water — quieter roads, water on both sides, and the best boating in the game. Airboats, jet skis, and kayaks all have a home here, and kayaking appears in the series for the first time. The water is also where the money is calmest: treasure dives pay around $1,400 a wreck with almost no risk, and scuba gear opens up wrecks below the surface. Take a boat out and watch the city shrink behind you — the map feels biggest from the water.

Capri and the Decor District

Two neighbourhoods matter for money, and both are worth visiting early: Capri and the Decor District. Capri is home to Victoria Lee Jeweler, and the Decor District is home to Blanc and Giroux — the two named jewelry stores, each paying around $20,000 a job. A snap gun from Chapter 3 breaks in silently, which matters: the police act only on what they witness, so a quiet entry means a smaller response. Visit these streets first to learn the approach, the exits, and the patrol routes. And remember: you can only carry $20,000, pocket cash is lost on death or arrest and does not carry into Chapter 2, so bank every job at one of the 454 ATMs.

The Countryside and Small Towns

Beyond the city and the swamps, Leonida's countryside and small towns are where the map breathes. The driving changes here — wider roads, real traffic, longer distances — and the side jobs change with it: towing and deliveries pay steadily, and Pilot Pete pays around $20,000. The countryside is also where the map's scale becomes real. Players who timed it report crossing the map takes around three hours and twenty-one minutes on foot and around eleven minutes by car — player reports, not measured figures, but they show the size: this is a state, not a city with scenery attached. If you get arrested out here, you will see Leonida Prison — and it costs you your pocket cash, so bank it before you roam.

How to Explore Without Wasting the Trip

The map rewards a plan, not a wander. Visit the beach first, then the boulevards at night, then the swamps, the Keys, and the money streets — and bank your cash between stops, because exploration without banking ends with an empty pocket. Save by hand after every mission: the autosave only fires after a mission ends and the Prologue never autosaves. Learn the wanted rules before you test them: the police only chase what they witness, and breaking line of sight is the whole escape. Take the map slowly — Leonida is built to be lived in, not checked off a list.
