Puppy's First Week Home: Survival Guide

📅 Updated October 2026 · ⏱️ 9 min read

The first week with a new puppy is magical and exhausting in equal measure. The crying at night, the puddles on the rug, the tiny teeth on everything — it's all normal, and it all gets better. This guide gives you a realistic day-by-day plan, the famous 3-3-3 rule, and the fixes for the three problems that stress new owners out most.

📏 The 3-3-3 Rule: What's Normal

Rescues and trainers use the 3-3-3 rule to set expectations: 3 days to feel overwhelmed, 3 weeks to settle into routine, 3 months to truly feel at home. In the first 3 days, don't be surprised if your puppy barely eats, hides, whines, or tests every boundary. They're not "bad" — they're processing the biggest change of their life.

"Almost every new puppy owner has a moment in week one where they think: what have I done? That feeling is so common it has a name — the puppy blues — and it passes."

Your only job in week one: keep the puppy safe, fed, and on a predictable routine. Training can wait; trust comes first.

🗓️ Day-by-Day Plan

Days 1–2: Decompression

  • Keep the world small: one puppy-proofed room plus the potty spot outside.
  • Let the puppy explore at their own pace. No parade of visitors yet.
  • Start the routine immediately — same wake time, meal times, potty trips. Predictability is calming.
  • Feed the same food the breeder/shelter used.

Days 3–4: Routine locks in

  • Begin short crate sessions with stuffed Kongs — 10 minutes, door open, then closed.
  • Start name recognition: say the name, treat when they look. Five minutes, twice a day.
  • Introduce one new room or a short car ride. Keep outings under 15 minutes.

Days 5–7: Building confidence

  • Add one 5-minute training session daily (sit is the perfect first skill).
  • Invite one calm visitor — no grabbing, let the puppy approach.
  • Begin gentle handling practice: paws, ears, mouth — treat for each touch. This pays off at every future vet visit.
  • By day 7 you should see the first real personality emerging. That's your puppy settling in.

🌙 Night Crying: The Fix

Night one is the hardest. Your puppy has slept in a warm pile of siblings their whole life, and now they're alone in a box. Crying is not defiance — it's loneliness.

  1. Crate next to your bed for the first week (move it later gradually). Hearing you breathe is enormously calming.
  2. Tire them out properly — play, potty, then bed. A wired puppy cannot settle.
  3. Warmth + heartbeat: a warm water bottle wrapped in a towel and a ticking clock near the crate mimics the litter pile.
  4. Respond to genuine need: if the crying is urgent after 2–3 hours, it's probably a potty need. Carry them out silently, no play, straight back to bed.
  5. Don't start a habit you'll hate: comforting is fine; turning 2 AM into playtime is not.

Most puppies sleep through the night within 1–3 weeks. The first three nights are the peak — it gets better fast.

🚽 Potty Training in Week One

Forget perfection — week one is about building the habit, not achieving reliability. Take your puppy out:

  • First thing in the morning (carry them — don't let little legs wander and squat on the rug)
  • Every 2 hours during the day (for an 8–12 week old)
  • 15–20 minutes after every meal
  • Right after waking from any nap
  • Right after play sessions
  • Last thing before bed

Same door, same spot, same boring routine. When they go outside: party! Treats, praise, genuine excitement. When accidents happen inside (they will): clean with enzyme cleaner and say nothing. Punishment after the fact only teaches puppies to hide their accidents from you.

⚠️ Reality check: full potty reliability typically takes 4–6 months. Week one success = fewer accidents each day and a puppy starting to head for the door. That's a win.

🦷 Biting and Chaos

Puppy biting peaks between 8–16 weeks and it's completely normal — it's how puppies explore the world. What works:

  • Yelp and withdraw: a short, high-pitched "ouch!" then turn away for 10 seconds. You're speaking puppy.
  • Redirect, don't scold: teeth on skin → toy in mouth, every time.
  • Enforce naps: an overtired puppy bites 10x more. If biting is escalating, it's nap time, not training time. Puppies need 18–20 hours of sleep.
  • Freeze Kongs and chew toys for teething relief.

🏥 When to Call the Vet

Most week-one weirdness is normal adjustment. Call your vet promptly if you see:

  • Not eating for more than 24 hours (or more than 12 hours in a very young puppy)
  • Repeated vomiting or diarrhea, especially with lethargy
  • Extreme lethargy — hard to wake, won't play at all
  • Coughing, sneezing with discharge, or labored breathing
  • Anything you suspect they swallowed

When in doubt, call. Vets would far rather reassure you about something normal than see you too late about something serious — and most offer a free "is this normal?" triage call.

You've Got This 💛

By the end of week one you'll be tired, your house will smell faintly of enzyme cleaner, and you'll already be a little bit obsessed with this tiny creature. The routine you're building now — the schedule, the crate, the calm consistency — is the foundation for the next 12–15 years. Keep it boring, keep it kind, and keep going.
