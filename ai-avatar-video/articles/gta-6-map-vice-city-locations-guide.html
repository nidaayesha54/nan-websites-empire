GTA 6 Map: Vice City Locations You Must See

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

GTA 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S — thirteen years after GTA 5 — and the first thing most players want to know is where they are going. Searches for "GTA 6 map" run to about 879,000 a month, and the answer is bigger than one city. GTA 6 returns to Vice City, but the map is the whole of Leonida: the neon beachfront boulevards of the city itself, the beaches, the swamps, and the countryside beyond. This guide covers the regions as they are known from verified launch reporting, the named locations confirmed so far, and how to explore them without wasting your first hours.

One honest note up front: this is a text guide based on verified launch reporting, not a full interactive map. Only a handful of locations have been confirmed by name, and everything below sticks to what is verified.

Vice City: Neon Beachfront Boulevards

Vice City is the heart of the map: neon beachfront boulevards, bright lights, and the dense urban core where Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos begin their story. The beachfront is the most practical starting point for exploring, because the city's main districts, shops, and services cluster around it. Start here: drive the beachfront slowly, learn the road layout, and get a feel for how the city connects before you push outward.

The city is also where the deep car culture lives. Custom neons, engine swaps, and named boats are all part of Vice City's world. Cars parked next to a Pay 'N' Spray can be stolen and sold, which makes the city's streets both a playground and an early income source.

Beyond the City: Beaches, Swamps, and Countryside

Leonida does not end at the city limits. The wider state stretches out into beaches, swamps, and countryside, and each region plays differently. The beaches are the open, relaxed edge of the map — good driving, good views, and the natural home of the treasure dives. The swamps are slower, wilder, and more isolated. The countryside fills in the space between, with the small roads and open stretches that make driving itself enjoyable.

Exploring these regions matters for a practical reason, not just a scenic one: some of the game's money and activities live outside the city. Kenton's treasure dives take you to wrecks off the coast, and the side jobs, fishing spots, and pool halls scattered across Leonida are the low-key ways to earn between story missions. A player who never leaves Vice City is missing a large part of the map — and a large part of the income.

Verified Spots Worth Visiting

Only a few locations have been confirmed by name so far. These are the ones worth putting on your list first:

Victoria Lee Jeweler — Capri

The Victoria Lee Jeweler sits in Capri, and it is one of the jewelry stores you can rob. Jewelry store robberies pay around $20,000 each, which makes this one of the most valuable single stops on the map in the early game. Visit it with a plan — and remember the wanted system is nuanced: the police act only on what they witness, so who is watching matters as much as what you take.

Blanc and Giroux — the Decor District

Blanc and Giroux is the second confirmed jewelry store, located in the Decor District, and it pays the same way: around $20,000 per robbery. Two jewelry stores, two districts, two paydays — and together they are the most reliable early-game money route on the map. If you are saving toward a money-gated mission, these two shops are the fastest way to get there.

Kenton's Treasure Dive Wrecks

Kenton's treasure dives take you underwater to wreck sites, paying around $1,400 per wreck. They unlock after the mission "Island Dreams", so they are not available from the start — but once they are, they turn the coastline into a steady earner.

How to Explore the Map

Leonida rewards slow exploration. Start with the beachfront boulevards to learn the city, then push out along the coast to the beaches, and only then head into the swamps and countryside where the roads thin out. Use the activities as your guide: fishing spots, pool halls, and side jobs are scattered across the regions, and following them is the most natural way to see the whole state.

Money on the Map: Plan Around the Big Payday

The map's economy has a shape. The jewelry stores pay around $20,000 each, the treasure dives around $1,400 per wreck, and stolen cars sold near a Pay 'N' Spray add a steady trickle. But the big bank vaults — worth up to $205,000 — only open at the end of Chapter 4. Some missions are money-gated, opening only once Jason and Lucia have earned enough, so the cash you earn exploring is the key to the next mission. Bank it in your BuckMe account, stash the rest under the floorboards in Jason's bedroom, and never carry your whole fortune in your pocket: pocket cash is lost on death or arrest, and it does not carry into Chapter 2.

Exploring Safely: Saves, Storage, and Frame Rate

Three practical facts will save you grief while you explore. First, save manually: autosave only fires after a mission ends, the Prologue never autosaves, and you have 15 manual save slots — use Pause, then Game, then Save Game, rotating two or three slots. Second, the install is 212 GB and the game needs PS5 system software 26.07-14.23.00, so make sure updates are done before a long session. Third, expect 30 fps everywhere — Rockstar confirmed to Kotaku on November 18 that there is no 60 fps performance mode, and the base PS5 dips below 30 in heavy combat while the Xbox Series S can fall to around 18–20 fps in shootouts.

The Bottom Line

Start on the neon beachfront boulevards of Vice City, learn the city's roads and its car culture, then push out to the beaches, swamps, and countryside of Leonida. Visit the Victoria Lee Jeweler in Capri and Blanc and Giroux in the Decor District for around $20,000 a robbery, dive Kenton's wrecks for around $1,400 each after "Island Dreams", and remember that the $205,000 bank vaults wait until the end of Chapter 4. Save manually, bank your cash, and explore slowly.
