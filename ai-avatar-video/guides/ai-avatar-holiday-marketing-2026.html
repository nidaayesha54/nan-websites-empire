It's early November, and the holiday campaign clock is now in its decisive phase: Black Friday is November 27, 2026, and ad sets should be live around November 10 to clear the learning phase. Black Friday, Cyber Monday, and Christmas creative usually means one brutal tradeoff: pay for a studio shoot with a spokesperson, or run static graphics while competitors show faces. AI avatar videos break that tradeoff — a digital presenter can announce your 40%-off flash sale this morning and your "last chance before Christmas" offer next week, with zero reshoots.

This guide is a practical playbook: the campaign formats that work, three scripts you can paste straight into our free script writer, and a week-by-week calendar from now to December.

November 2 refresh: you are in Week 3 — what to prioritise now

If you are starting from this guide today, skip straight past Weeks 1–2 of the calendar below: the offer-locking and first-batch production those weeks describe should already be done, or compressed into the next 48 hours. Your Week 3 job (November 2–8) is to launch, not to perfect. Get early-access promos live, start the daily deal-of-the-day clips, and run your retargeting test at a small budget so you know your winning hook before the big-spend week — not during it. A test running at $10–$20 per variant per day for three to four days gives you a readable hook rate and CTR signal; waiting for a flawless hero video while competitors learn what works is the most expensive mistake of the season.

Three November-specific fixes to apply to the playbook above. First, shipping deadlines now outrank discount depth in your scripts: "order by" dates are the urgency that converts in mid-to-late November, so swap generic "lowest price of the year" lines for a concrete last-order date wherever you know it, and update the script the day a carrier deadline passes. Second, freeze your avatar and voice for the rest of the season — switching presenters mid-campaign resets audience recognition and breaks the retargeting variants that are already learning, so any new format should reuse the same face, voice, and background. Third, plan the post–Black Friday pivot now: the Cyber Monday finale and the "last chance before Christmas" gift-guide angle need scripts written before Black Friday week consumes all your production time, or December becomes a scramble. Write those two scripts this week, even if you do not generate the videos until November 30.

One caution for the final stretch: as volume rises, so does scrutiny. Keep the AI disclosure toggle on for every upload, keep avatar scripts to product facts and offers (never fake testimonials from an AI face), and never let a stock avatar present as a real named founder. Disclosed, honest avatar content converts fine through the holidays — deceptive content gets reported exactly when you can least afford a complaint.

Why holiday season is the perfect avatar use case

Most video formats are worth doing year-round. Holiday promos are different because of how fast they decay — and that's exactly where avatars win:

  • Offers change constantly. A flash deal goes live at noon and dies at midnight. Re-editing a script takes five minutes; re-shooting a human spokesperson takes a week and a budget you don't have.
  • Volume is the game. One hero video doesn't cut it in November. You need variants per product, per audience, per placement (Reels vs. feed vs. email embed). Avatars turn one script template into 20 videos.
  • Personalization at scale is finally real. Tools like Tavus can generate one video per customer — the avatar says their first name, mentions their abandoned cart. Personalized holiday greetings used to cost agencies five figures. Now they cost a monthly plan and a CSV.
  • Multilingual reach without voice actors. Selling to Spanish- and French-speaking shoppers? The same avatar delivers the same message in 100+ languages with synced lip movement.
  • No talent scheduling. Spokespersons get sick, cameras need crews, studios need booking. An avatar pipeline runs at 11 p.m. on the Sunday before Black Friday — exactly when you need it most.

5 campaign formats, with ready-to-use scripts

All scripts below are written for a ~30-second read (roughly 75 words). Paste them into the script writer, then run them through the script timer before generating.

1. Countdown promos

The simplest format and the workhorse of the season: a daily or weekly "X days left" announcement. Film one template; swap the number and the offer. Works for Black Friday week and the December shipping-deadline panic.

Sample script: "Quick heads-up — our Black Friday sale ends in three days. Forty percent off everything in the store, no code needed. But once the countdown hits zero, prices go back up. Tap the link below and don't leave your favorites sitting in the cart — this is the lowest we'll go all year."

2. Personalized greeting videos

One video per customer: the avatar says their name, references their last purchase or cart, and delivers a holiday code. Tavus-style personalization platforms do this via API from a customer list. Keep it warm, not creepy — first-name greetings on your own email list, never cold outreach.

Sample script: "Hey {{first_name}}, happy holidays from all of us! You've got twenty dollars off waiting in your account — just for being with us this year. Use code THANKS20 before December twentieth. And if you left something in your cart last month... it might just be on sale right now."

3. "Deal of the day" talking-heads

A daily 20-second vertical clip announcing one deal: product on screen, avatar delivering the hook. Generate 7–10 in one sitting each Sunday and schedule them out — consistency beats production value here.

Sample script: "Today's deal is kind of ridiculous. The bestselling candle set — the one that sold out twice last year — is thirty percent off, today only. When it's gone, it's gone until next Christmas. Link's in the comments."

4. Founder-style holiday messages

A calmer, warmer video: the "founder" (a custom avatar twin, or a stock avatar playing the role) thanks customers and tells a short story about the year. Post it on social and send it in the December newsletter — this is the format that makes January retention emails actually work.

One caution: if the "founder" is a real person whose face you're cloning, get written consent and disclose it. If it's a stock avatar playing a character, never let the script imply it's a real named individual.

5. Retargeting ad variants

The highest-ROI use of avatars in the whole playbook. Take your best-performing holiday ad, then generate variants: different hook in the first 3 seconds, different offer, different avatar or background per audience segment. Run 10 variants where you used to run 2, then put spend behind the winner.

The AI disclosure rules you can't skip

This section isn't optional. Holiday ads get extra scrutiny, and a complaint during your biggest revenue weeks is the last thing you need.

  • YouTube, TikTok, and Meta all require or strongly encourage labeling AI-generated content that looks realistic. Use the platform's built-in AI disclosure toggle on every upload — it takes seconds.
  • Don't mislead about who is speaking. A stock avatar presenting as "our founder Sarah" is deceptive. US and EU ad guidance requires ads not to mislead — endorsements must come from real people. Keep avatar scripts to product facts and offers.
  • Testimonials need real customers. "Customers love us" from an AI face is a fabrication risk. Use real review screenshots or real UGC for social proof; use avatars for the presenter, not the customer.
  • Personalization consent. Name-based personalization should only go to people who gave you their data (your email list, your customers) — never cold outreach, and respect opt-outs instantly.

If a viewer would feel tricked learning it's AI, you're doing it wrong. Disclosed, honest avatar content converts fine — sneaky content gets reported.

Metrics to watch and a realistic testing budget

For short-form avatar promos, three numbers matter before anything else:

  • Hook rate — what percentage watch the first 3 seconds. Under ~30% means your opening line or visual is weak. This is the metric your retargeting variants should be optimizing.
  • Hold rate — average watch time relative to video length. If viewers drop at the 5-second mark, your offer lands too late.
  • CTR (click-through rate) — the bridge between views and revenue. A great hook with no clicks usually means the CTA was vague — say exactly what to do ("tap the link," not "check it out").

Testing budget: a realistic minimum is $300–$500 in ad spend plus one avatar tool plan (~$25–$50/month) for 2–3 weeks. Run variants at $10–$20/day each, give each 3–4 days, then scale the winner and kill the rest.

Ignore vanity metrics (raw views, likes) during testing. A video with 2,000 views and a strong CTR beats a viral clip that sells nothing.
