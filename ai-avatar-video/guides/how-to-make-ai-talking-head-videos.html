A talking-head video is just a person speaking to camera — and AI avatar tools now generate one from text alone. The full workflow has five steps, and the script matters more than the software. Follow this guide once and you'll be producing publish-ready avatar videos in under an hour.

Step 1 — Write an avatar-friendly script

Avatars can't improvise, emphasize with eyebrows, or recover from a clunky sentence. Your script has to do that work:

  • Write the way you speak. Contractions, short sentences, zero corporate jargon.
  • Hook in the first 5 seconds. State the payoff immediately — avatars can't earn attention with charisma alone.
  • One idea per sentence, ~18 words max. Long clauses sound robotic when synthesized.
  • Punctuate for pauses. Commas, em dashes, and ellipses become breath marks.
  • Spell out numbers and symbols. "Twenty percent", not "20%". "Dollars", not "$".
  • Target 150 words per minute. That's ~75 words for 30 seconds, ~150 for a minute.

Step 3 — Pick avatar & voice

This is where most videos are won or lost:

  • Match avatar to audience. A friendly presenter for consumer content; a polished professional for B2B. Age, attire, and background should mirror your viewers' expectations.
  • Voice matters more than face. Preview 3–5 voices reading YOUR script, not the demo line. Listen for natural emphasis and correct pronunciation of your key terms.
  • Keep one avatar per series. Consistency builds recognition — the same presenter across episodes feels like a real host.
  • Check the language. If you need non-English, test lip-sync in that language on the free trial before committing.
  • Consider a custom twin. Most platforms can clone your own appearance from a short recording — powerful for personal brands, but get familiar with consent and disclosure rules first.

Step 4 — Generate & review

Paste your script, generate, and then review like an editor — not a fan:

  1. Watch the whole thing once at normal speed. Note every moment that sounds "off".
  2. Check lip-sync on plosives — B, P, and M sounds expose bad sync fastest.
  3. Listen for mispronounced names and terms. Most tools let you add phonetic spellings ("NIGH-duh" style) — use them.
  4. Verify pacing. If a section drags, cut words rather than speeding up the voice.
  5. Regenerate the weak sections only. Don't re-render a perfect 4 minutes because 10 seconds are off — most tools support per-scene regeneration.

Step 5 — Edit, caption, disclose

  • Add captions. 80%+ of social video is watched muted at first — burned-in captions are non-negotiable.
  • Add light B-roll or graphics over sections where the avatar would feel static (lists, stats, steps).
  • Disclose AI use. Turn on the platform's AI-generated label (YouTube, TikTok, and Meta all offer one) and mention it in the caption or description. Disclosure builds trust — hiding it destroys it when discovered.
  • Export correctly. 1080p minimum; 9:16 for Shorts/Reels/TikTok, 16:9 for YouTube and training portals.
Realistic AI-generated content without disclosure can violate platform policies and, in some jurisdictions, consumer-protection rules. When in doubt, disclose — it costs nothing and protects everything.

7 mistakes beginners make

  1. Copy-pasting blog text as a script. Written prose sounds stiff spoken. Rewrite for the ear.
  2. Skipping the hook. "Hi guys, welcome back to my channel" loses to a payoff-first opener every time.
  3. One 10-minute take. Break scripts into scenes — regeneration and editing are 10x easier.
  4. Ignoring the voice preview. The default voice is rarely the best voice for your script.
  5. No captions. Instant reach killer on every social platform.
  6. Forgetting disclosure. The fastest way to lose an account's trust.
  7. Publishing the first render. Every pro regenerates at least the weak lines. Budget time for it.
