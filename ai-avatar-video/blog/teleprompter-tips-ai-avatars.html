7 Teleprompter Tips That Make AI Avatars Sound Human

October 2026 · 6-minute read

The difference between an avatar video that sounds professional and one that sounds robotic is rarely the software — it's the script formatting. AI voices read exactly what you give them, including your punctuation. These seven tweaks take five minutes and transform the delivery.

1. Punctuate for pauses, not grammar class

Commas, em dashes, and ellipses become breath marks. Read your script aloud and put a comma everywhere you naturally pause — even if a grammar teacher would object. "Here's the thing — most people skip this step... and it costs them." reads dramatically better than the same words in one flat sentence.

2. Use contractions everywhere

"You will see results" sounds like a robot. "You'll see results" sounds like a person. Search your script for "will not", "cannot", "it is", "you are" and contract them all. Formal writing is the #1 source of robotic reads.

3. Write numbers as words

Voices handle "twenty percent" gracefully and "20%" unpredictably. Same for "$50" ("fifty dollars"), "3rd" ("third"), and years ("twenty twenty-six"). Spell out anything you'd stumble reading cold.

4. One idea per sentence

Long, clause-stacked sentences flatten into monotone because the voice can't find the emphasis. Break them up. "Our platform helps teams create videos faster, which saves money and improves consistency across departments" becomes three punchy sentences — and the avatar suddenly sounds confident.

5. Add emphasis with structure, not caps

ALL-CAPS words get spelled out letter-by-letter by some voices ("S-A-L-E" instead of "sale"). For emphasis, use short standalone sentences instead: "This is the part most people miss. Read that again. Most people miss this." Repetition and isolation create emphasis that synthesis respects.

6. Front-load names and terms phonetically

Unusual names, brand terms, and acronyms are where voices embarrass you. Most platforms accept phonetic hints — write "NIGH-duh" in a pronunciation dictionary or respell inline on first use, then switch to the normal spelling. Always generate a 10-second test of tricky words before the full render.

7. End sentences on the point

Voices naturally drop pitch at periods — that's what makes speech sound "finished" and confident. Avoid trailing filler ("...you know?", "...right?", "...basically") which makes the avatar sound uncertain. Land the sentence, full stop.
