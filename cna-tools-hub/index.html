CNA career FAQs

Quick answers to the questions future CNAs ask most.

How long does it take to become a CNA?

Most programs take 4–12 weeks: 75+ federally required training hours (at least 16 clinical), then your state's certification exam. Full-time students can finish in about a month.

How much does a CNA make in 2026?

The national median is roughly $19–$20/hour (~$40,000/year). Top states (WA, OR, CA, AK) average $23–$25/hour; the lowest (MS, LA, AR) average $15–$16/hour. Calculate your pay →

Is CNA training worth the cost?

Usually yes — training costs $300–$2,000 (often free via employers or grants) and most CNAs break even within 2–6 weeks of full-time work. Run your ROI →

Should I become a CNA or an LPN?

CNA = fastest entry (weeks). LPN = higher pay (~$61k median) but 12–18 months of school. Many start as CNAs and bridge to LPN. Full comparison →

Can I do CNA training online?

Theory can be online/hybrid, but at least 16 hours of supervised in-person clinical practice is federally required — fully online certification isn't possible.
