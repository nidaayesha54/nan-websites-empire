1. Save manually after every mission

The autosave cannot be trusted: it only fires after a mission ends, the prologue never autosaves at all, and crashes have already cost players whole evenings — streamer JasonTheWeen lost around ten hours to a single crash. The fix is simple and already in your hands: fifteen manual save slots. Pause the game, go to Game, then Save Game, and pick a slot. Rotate two or three slots and take turns, so a bad save never strands you, and always keep one slot from before any tricky mission. Save after every mission, save before a big purchase, and save before any mission that looks risky. It takes five seconds, and it is the only safety net the game gives you.

2. Bank your cash before you lose it

You can only carry $20,000, and pocket cash is lost when you die or get arrested — and it does not carry into Chapter 2. Walking around with a fortune is a risk, not a flex. The verified ways to protect it are simple: stash spare money under the floorboards in Jason's bedroom, and open the BuckMe bank account once the mission "Buck Me" is available, then deposit your cash at any of the 454 ATMs across Leonida. Banked money is safe. Get into the rhythm early: earn, bank, spend — never carry more than you can afford to lose.

3. Learn the wanted system before you need it

The wanted system in GTA 6 works differently from the older games: the police act only on what they actually witness. There is no magic GPS, no instant knowledge of where you are. If a cop sees you commit a crime, expect a response right away; if a civilian sees you, expect one shortly after, because witnesses report what they see. If nobody sees anything, the crime may pass unnoticed entirely. That is why the same action can have completely different consequences depending on where and when you do it. When the police are after you, the core of the escape is breaking line of sight: get out of view, stay out of view, change vehicles, and put distance between you and the scene. The police search where they saw you, not where you are — so stop being seen, and the chase goes cold.

4. Earn before the story gates you

Some missions only open once Jason and Lucia have earned enough money — a detail that comes from Rockstar North's Rob Nelson speaking via Famitsu. If no mission marker appears and your cash is thin, the game is not broken; it is waiting for you to earn. Jewelry store robberies pay around $20,000 each, treasure dives pay about $1,400 per wreck with almost no risk, and the big bank vaults pay up to $205,000 — but only at the end of Chapter 4. The practical lesson: rob a store, dive a wreck, sell a car, and check your cash before you assume a mission is missing. A player who earns steadily never meets the gate.

5. Know when character switching unlocks

Free switching between Jason and Lucia unlocks only after the "Breakfast Run" mission in Chapter 2. Then you hold Right on the D-pad to open the Partner Menu and press R1 on PS5 or RB on Xbox. Before that point the story picks the character, and some missions lock one character entirely — so if the switch does not work in your first hours, nothing is wrong. A greyed-out portrait means your partner is busy, for example riding in a RydeMe — wait and retry. Once switching opens up, use it: the two leads have different strengths and different ways into the same job.

6. Try the side jobs early

The story is only half the game. Fishing, pool, towing, deliveries, and treasure dives are all paying work, and they pay from the start — Kenton's treasure dives, which unlock after the mission "Island Dreams", pay about $1,400 per wreck with almost no risk, and towing and delivery jobs pay steadily without the heat of a robbery. Side jobs also teach you the map: the Leonida countryside, the Grassrivers, the Keys, and the back roads of Vice City are all places the story takes its time reaching, and a player who works the side jobs knows them before the story needs them.

7. Play the launch reality, not the rumours

Three launch facts catch new players out, and all three are easy to get wrong. First, there is no online mode at launch — GTA 6 is a single-player experience for now, so the story needs no subscription and no PS Plus. Second, there is no PC version at launch, and no PC date has been announced — any "GTA 6 PC download" link is a scam. Third, the game runs at 30 frames per second on every console, and there is no 60 fps mode; the frame rate is a design choice, not a setting you can change. Knowing these three facts saves you the three most common launch-night mistakes: paying for a subscription you do not need, downloading a fake installer, and hunting for a mode that does not exist.
