GTA 6 Day-One Patch: How Big Is It Really?

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and like every big game it arrived with a day-one patch — or, more precisely, two of them. The good news is that they are small: roughly 1 GB on PS5 and 2 GB on Xbox for the first patch, and even smaller for the second. The bad news is that they are mandatory, and they sit on top of a 212 GB download and a console system update you also need. This guide gives you the real sizes, explains what the patches do, and tells you what to download first so you are playing as soon as possible.

The sizes matter because the internet spent the week before launch arguing about them. One widely shared claim put the day-one patch at 18.4 GB — a number that was repeated everywhere and is contradicted by every other source. The real figures are much smaller, and this guide uses them.

Patch 1.002.000: November 13

The first patch, version 1.002.000, arrived on November 13 — six days before launch, so that anyone who preloaded the game could download it in advance. On PS5 it is about 1.07 GB; on Xbox Series X|S it is about 2.18 GB. The Xbox version is roughly double the size, which is normal: the two consoles package their updates differently, and the same fixes take up different amounts of space on each.

Rockstar published no patch notes and no sizes of its own, so these figures come from players and from the console stores themselves, which report the download size when the patch is fetched. What the patch actually changes is not documented in detail — day-one patches are usually stability fixes, last-minute bug fixes, and small balance changes made after the game went to manufacturing. The important fact is that it is mandatory: the game will not start without it.

Patch 1.002.001: November 16

A second patch, version 1.002.001, followed on November 16 — three days before launch. This one is smaller: about 602.5 MB on PS5 and about 1.59 GB on Xbox. Again, the Xbox version is larger, for the same packaging reasons. And again, Rockstar published no notes, so the contents are not documented beyond the usual day-one fixes.

Two patches in six days tells you something about how launch week works at Rockstar's scale. The game that went to manufacturing was finished weeks earlier, and the team kept fixing things right up to launch — then kept fixing things after that. If you are reading this after launch day, there may already be a third patch. Check your console's update screen before you sit down to play.

The PS5 System Update You Also Need

On PS5 there is one more download before you can play, and it is bigger than both game patches put together: the PS5 system update 26.07-14.23.00, which is about 1.2 to 1.3 GB. It is mandatory before playing GTA 6, and it is a console update, not a game update — so it will not appear in the game's own download queue. If you turn your PS5 on, start the game download, and wonder why nothing is happening, check the system software first.

Add it all up and the total extra download, on top of the 212 GB game itself, is about 1.7 GB on PS5 and about 3.8 GB on Xbox. That is the real number — not 18.4 GB, and not zero. On a fast connection it is a few minutes. On a slow one, it is worth starting early.

What to Download First

The order matters if you want to play as soon as possible. First, download the 212 GB game itself — that is the long pole, and everything else is small by comparison. Second, let the two game patches download; on PS5, do the system update at the same time if you can, since it is a separate download. Third, check for a newer patch before you start, because Rockstar has already shipped two in a week and a third is likely.

One more thing: the game is 212 GB, and the patches add to that. Make sure you have the space. A PS5 with a nearly full drive will not start the download at all, and the time to discover that is before launch day, not on it.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6's day-one patches are small and mandatory: 1.002.000 (about 1 GB on PS5, 2 GB on Xbox) and 1.002.001 (about 600 MB on PS5, 1.6 GB on Xbox), plus a 1.2 GB PS5 system update.
