Travel Nurse Tax Tips: Stipends, Tax Homes & Deductions

What every traveler must know about tax-free stipends — and the mistakes that can make them taxable.

Home › Blog › Travel Nurse Tax Tips

October 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Tax-free stipends are the engine of travel nurse pay — often half your package arrives untouched by federal and state taxes. But "tax-free" is conditional, not automatic. Get the rules wrong and the IRS can reclassify years of stipends as taxable wages, with penalties and interest. These are the essentials every traveler should know. (This is general information, not tax advice — see the disclaimer below.)

1. Why stipends are tax-free (and when they aren't)

Housing and meals/incidentals stipends are tax-free because they reimburse the duplicated living expenses of working away from home. Two conditions must both hold:

  • You maintain a legitimate tax home (more below), and
  • The assignment is temporary — generally expected to last one year or less in a single location.

Fail either test and the stipends become taxable wages. This is the single most expensive mistake in travel nursing.

2. Maintain a real tax home

Your tax home is generally where you live and work regularly — not just an address on your license. To defend it, you should:

  • Maintain and pay for a residence there (rent or mortgage, utilities) — duplicated expenses are the key phrase.
  • Return there regularly between contracts.
  • Keep ties: voter registration, driver's license, vehicle registration, and ideally some local income or business activity.
  • Keep receipts and records proving you actually incur costs at home while paying for lodging on assignment.

Travelers who sell everything, have no home base, and hop contracts indefinitely are classified as itinerant workers — no tax home, no tax-free stipends, and every dollar is taxable wherever earned.

3. Watch the 12-month rule

Staying in one metropolitan area beyond 12 months generally makes the assignment "indefinite" in the IRS's eyes — stipends received after that point become taxable, and some argue the whole area's stipends are tainted. If you love a location, take a break elsewhere or restructure before month twelve. Track your time per metro area carefully, especially with extensions.

4. Keep stipends realistic

Stipends must reasonably reflect actual local costs. A package with a $25/hour taxable rate and $2,000/week in "housing" stipends in a low-cost market looks engineered — and engineered stipends attract scrutiny for both you and the agency. If an offer's stipend split looks too good to be true, ask how the numbers were set, and model the after-tax result with our take-home pay calculator.

5. Track deductible expenses

Even with tax-free stipends, travelers often have deductible or reimbursable costs: licensing and endorsement fees, required certifications (BLS, ACLS), uniforms and scrubs, professional liability insurance, and unreimbursed travel to assignments. Keep a simple spreadsheet with receipts — your future self at tax time will thank you.

6. Multi-state filing is normal — plan for it

Working contracts in three states in a year usually means filing in multiple states. Keep every pay stub and W-2, note the state of each assignment, and consider a tax professional experienced with travelers rather than generic tax software. The cost is deductible against the far larger cost of getting multi-state filing wrong.

7. Get professional help early

A CPA or enrolled agent who specializes in travel healthcare workers pays for themselves: proper tax-home setup, per-diem substantiation, state filing strategy, and audit defense. Interview them the way you interview recruiters — ask how many travelers they serve and how they document tax homes.

Quick checklist before tax season

  • ☐ Tax home documented: lease/mortgage, utility bills, return trips logged
  • ☐ No single metro area exceeded 12 months
  • ☐ All W-2s and final pay stubs collected per state
  • ☐ Licensing, certification, and uniform receipts filed
  • ☐ Traveler-specialist tax pro engaged before April
