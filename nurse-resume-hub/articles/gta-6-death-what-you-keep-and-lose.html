GTA 6 Death: What You Keep and Lose When You Die

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Dying in GTA 6 is more expensive than dying in GTA 5. The old games charged you a hospital fee and sent you on your way. GTA 6 takes the cash in your pockets, the stash bag on your back and the weapons you picked up along the way — and the only things it cannot take are the things you already protected. This guide lists exactly what you keep and what you lose when the Wasted screen appears, so you can walk into danger with your eyes open.

The rule behind the whole system is simple: GTA 6 punishes what you are carrying in the moment, not what you have built up. Your bank balance, your owned vehicles and your owned weapons survive. Your pockets do not. Once you understand that split, every decision before a dangerous job becomes obvious.

What you lose: pocket cash

The biggest loss is the cash you are carrying. If Jason or Lucia dies with money in their pockets, that money is gone — permanently. Rockstar North's Rob Nelson put it plainly to previewers: if you get killed and have not deposited it, that money disappears. Getting arrested costs you the same. There is no fee, no percentage, no cap to negotiate with — the cash is simply gone.

That is why the deposit habit is the most important habit in the game. Bank your cash at an ATM after every job, and hide it under the floorboards in Jason's bedroom before the Buck Me mission opens your account. The walk from the store to the ATM is where you are most likely to lose the money, so make that walk first, before anything else. A robbery is not finished when you lose the cops — it is finished when the cash is in the bank.

What you lose: stash bags and looted gear

Stash bags go the same way as pocket cash. If you are carrying a bag filled with cash and contraband when you die, the bag is lost. Unowned weapons are lost too — any gun you picked up from an enemy or off the ground, without adding it to your loadout, disappears when you respawn. The same goes for vehicles: any car you were driving without saving it is gone, and so is anything you were trying to hijack.

The pattern is consistent. Anything you grabbed in the moment and have not yet secured is temporary. The game is telling you to secure things quickly: fence the loot, bank the cash, save the car, store the weapon. Every minute something sits unsecured is a minute it can be taken from you.

What you keep: bank, owned weapons, owned vehicles

Money in your BuckMe bank account is safe from dying and from the police. It is the one pool nothing can touch, which is why everything in this guide comes back to the ATM. Owned weapons survive too — the guns you bought and added to your loadout come back with you, and you can re-equip them from your personal vehicle's trunk or a weapon locker at Ammu-Nation. Owned vehicles survive as well.

Dying also does not damage everything else you have built. Launch reporting is clear that death does not affect your relationship with Lucia or your Criminal Profile — the penalty is material, not social. You lose things, not progress. And if you die during a mission, you restart from a checkpoint rather than losing the mission entirely, so the story itself is never at risk.

Death vs arrest: the two penalties

Death and arrest are similar but not identical. After a death, you respawn — where exactly is still disputed between preview accounts, with one describing a nearby respawn and another a classic hospital respawn — and you have lost your pocket cash, your stash bag and your unowned gear. After an arrest, you reappear outside the police station with less money than before, and the weapons you picked up from enemies are confiscated while the weapons you already owned stay with you.

The practical difference is small: both cost you what you were carrying. So the advice is the same for both. Before anything dangerous, deposit your money, stash your loot at the safehouse, and make sure the weapons that matter are owned and stored, not looted and carried. The players who do that lose almost nothing when they die. The players who do not can lose everything they earned that day.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you lose money when you die in GTA 6?

You lose the cash you are carrying. Money in your BuckMe bank account is safe, which is why depositing at an ATM after every job is the single most important habit in the game.

Do you lose your weapons when you die?

You lose weapons you picked up but did not own. Weapons you bought and added to your loadout survive, and you can re-equip them from your vehicle's trunk or an Ammu-Nation locker.

Does dying affect your story progress?

No. Dying in a mission restarts you from a checkpoint, and death does not affect your relationship with Lucia or your Criminal Profile. The penalty is the things you were carrying, not the story itself.

The bottom line: death in GTA 6 is a tax on the unprepared. Bank your cash, fence your loot, own your weapons and save your vehicles — and the Wasted screen becomes an inconvenience instead of a disaster.
