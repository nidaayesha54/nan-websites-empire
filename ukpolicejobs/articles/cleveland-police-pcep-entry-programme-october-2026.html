Two Pathways: Uniformed and Detective

Two directions. The uniformed pathway leads into response and neighbourhood policing — emergency calls, patrols and community work. The detective pathway starts with initial training and a tutor phase in response and neighbourhood policing, then moves into an investigative role — an early route into investigation that builds evidence-gathering and casework skills sooner than the traditional uniform-first route.

Entry Requirements

  • Qualifications: a Level 3 qualification — two A-levels or equivalent — or significant and equivalent work experience under Police Regulation 10, so career changers can qualify on experience.
  • Age: you must be at least 17 to apply and 18 by the time of appointment.
  • Nationality: you must be a British Citizen, an EC/EEA national, a Commonwealth citizen, or a foreign national with no restrictions on your stay in the UK.
  • No degree required: unlike the police constable degree apprenticeship, the PCEP does not require a university qualification during probation — the key difference between the two routes.
  • Vetting and checks: as with all police roles, applicants must pass vetting, fitness and medical assessments.

How the PCEP Works

The PCEP is a structured, paid route into the office of constable: classroom learning combined with operational policing, earning a full officer salary from day one. The programme normally takes two years, after which probation completes and you are confirmed as a police constable.

Unlike the degree apprenticeship, the programme needs no university study during probation, while still offering professional development, tutor support and mentoring. The £32,256 starting salary comes with benefits, staff associations and a generous pension — competitive with many graduate jobs, without the degree.
