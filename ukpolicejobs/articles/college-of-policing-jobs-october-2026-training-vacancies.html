College of Policing Jobs October 2026: PPST, Firearms & Training Vacancies

Civilian careers at the College of Policing — the latest training vacancies, salaries, and how to apply for PPST, firearms and professional-development roles.

Published 13 October 2026 · NAN Jobs Portal editorial team

You do not have to be a serving police officer to build a career in policing. The College of Policing — the professional body for policing in England and Wales — employs hundreds of civilians: trainers, subject-matter experts, curriculum designers, digital learning specialists and corporate professionals. Its vacancies are advertised on the Civil Service Jobs portal, and October 2026 brought a notable training recruitment round.

October 2026: Featured Vacancy

RoleLocationSalary
Public and Personal Safety Training (PPST) SME SupervisorRemote£36,520 – £40,120

The PPST SME Supervisor role sits within the College's national training operation. PPST — Public and Personal Safety Training — is the programme that keeps officers safe: officer safety tactics, first aid, conflict management and personal protective skills. As a subject-matter expert supervisor, you would oversee the quality and consistency of PPST delivery, support trainers nationally, and help evolve the curriculum. The remote working pattern makes it accessible to experienced trainers anywhere in the country.

Check live status: this vacancy was advertised in the October 2026 cycle and may have closed. The College recruits training roles on a rolling basis — search "College of Policing" on civilservicejobs.service.gov.uk for the current list.

Training Roles the College Recruits Regularly

  • PPST trainers and supervisors — delivering and assuring public and personal safety training to forces.
  • Firearms training staff — supporting authorised firearms officer training, tactics and licensing standards.
  • Investigative training specialists — detective training, interview skills and major-crime investigation programmes.
  • Curriculum and digital learning designers — building e-learning, assessments and blended programmes.
  • Professional development leads — shaping national guidance, codes of practice and the policing education qualifications framework.

Who These Roles Suit

College training posts typically suit three profiles: former officers and police staff with training or operational expertise; professional trainers from the military, fire service, NHS or private sector with transferable instructional skills; and learning professionals (instructional designers, assessors, quality managers) who can build and assure national programmes. Firearms and tactical roles usually require specific operational backgrounds, while curriculum and digital roles are open to wider experience.

How to Apply for College of Policing Jobs

Search Civil Service Jobs. Filter by "College of Policing" as the organisation. Also check the College's own careers pages for specialist campaigns.
Map your experience to the advert. Training adverts list essential operational or instructional criteria — gather evidence for each before writing.
Write behaviour examples. Applications use Civil Service Success Profiles: 250-word STAR examples (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for each assessed behaviour.
Prepare for practical assessment. Training roles often include a delivery exercise or micro-teach at interview — prepare a short session on a familiar topic.
Complete checks. Expect references, right-to-work verification and police vetting appropriate to the role's access level.

Eligibility Notes

Standard Civil Service nationality rules apply — UK, Irish, Commonwealth or EEA nationality for most roles, with right-to-work checks at offer stage. Police vetting (rather than full security clearance) is typical for College posts. Operational training roles may require specific licences or qualifications — firearms posts, for example, demand relevant instructional accreditation — all stated in the advert.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the College of Policing?

The College of Policing is the professional body for policing in England and Wales. It sets standards, develops national training and guidance, and employs trainers, subject-matter experts and corporate staff.

What is PPST training?

PPST stands for Public and Personal Safety Training — the national programme covering officer safety, first aid, conflict management and related skills. The College employs PPST subject-matter experts and supervisors.

Where are College of Policing jobs advertised?

College of Policing vacancies are advertised on Civil Service Jobs (civilservicejobs.service.gov.uk) and on the College's own careers pages.

Do College trainers need to be ex-police?

Many training roles value operational policing experience, but the College also recruits training designers, digital learning specialists and assessors from wider professional backgrounds.

Are College of Policing roles remote?

Many are — recent training and subject-matter expert roles have been advertised as remote or hybrid, with travel to training sites as required.
