Met Police Recruitment Drive October 2026: 'We've Accepted the Challenge' Campaign

The Metropolitan Police has launched a major recruitment drive — 'We've accepted the challenge, will you?' — with applications open for constable roles paying £30,000 from day one. Here are the entry routes, the eligibility rules and how to apply.

Published 14 October 2026 · NAN Jobs Portal editorial team

On 12 October 2026 the Metropolitan Police launched a major new recruitment campaign — "We've accepted the challenge – will you?" — calling on Londoners of all backgrounds to join the UK's largest police force. The campaign, backed by the Mayor of London and Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley, is built around a simple message: you do not need to have all the answers; what matters is integrity, compassion, resilience and a willingness to serve.

Applications are currently open across the Met's two main constable entry routes, with a starting salary of £30,000 — and you can join without already holding a degree.

Apply early: the Met warns of expected high application volumes and encourages early applications. Candidates who pass all stages during this campaign are likely to be offered a start date after March 2027.

The Two Entry Routes Open Now

  • Police Constable Entry Programme (PCEP): a two-year route combining training at a Met training centre with on-the-job learning. It does not lead to a degree — ideal if you want to focus purely on policing.
  • Police Constable Degree Apprenticeship (PCDA): a three-year, fully funded route combining university study with operational training, leading to a degree. Met recruits study at partner universities including Brunel, the University of West London, Anglia Ruskin University and the University of East London — and you earn while you learn.

If your preferred route becomes fully subscribed, the Met may consider you for an alternative based on your qualifications and experience.

Why the Met Is Recruiting Hard

Writing in the national press on 12 October 2026, Commissioner Sir Mark Rowley insisted the force will keep recruiting inclusively despite "culture wars" rhetoric. The numbers explain the urgency: 46% of Londoners are from a minority ethnic background, compared with just 18% of Met officers. Rowley's argument is practical, not ideological — effective policing by consent depends on the trust of every community, and a force that reflects London attracts the widest, strongest pool of talent.

It is working: of 143 new constables who joined in January, over 33% were from Black, Asian and minority ethnic backgrounds and 50% were women.

Eligibility: Can You Join the Met?

Check your age and nationality. You can apply at 17 but cannot start training until 18. Non-UK applicants need indefinite leave to enter or remain in the UK.
Pass the fitness and eyesight standards. You must pass the police fitness test and meet the eyesight standards agreed by the College of Policing — regular exercise is enough for most applicants.
Declare tattoos and convictions. Some tattoos (depending on depiction and placement) will disqualify you; criminal convictions or cautions do not automatically bar you — eligibility depends on age and nature of the offence.
Apply online early. Submit through the Met careers portal, then monitor your email (including junk) and the recruitment portal for updates and monthly newsletters.
Complete the stages. Expect online assessment, interview, fitness test, medical and vetting before a final offer — with likely start dates after March 2027 for this campaign.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Met Police 'We've accepted the challenge' campaign?

The Metropolitan Police's major recruitment drive, launched 12 October 2026, calling on Londoners of all backgrounds to apply as constables.

What is the Met Police starting salary in 2026?

New constables start on £30,000 a year, with both the PCEP and PCDA entry routes open in the October 2026 campaign.

What is the difference between PCEP and PCDA?

PCEP is a two-year route without a degree; PCDA is a three-year fully funded route combining university study with operational training, leading to a degree.

Do I need a degree to join the Met Police?

No — neither the PCEP nor the PCDA requires you to already hold a degree. The PCDA route actually earns you a degree while you train.

When would I start if I apply in the October 2026 campaign?

Candidates passing all stages in this campaign are likely to be offered a start date after March 2027.
