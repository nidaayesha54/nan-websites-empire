Met Police Special Constable 2026 — Apply by 31 Dec

The Met is recruiting Special Constables (volunteer police officers) — same powers and uniform, free TfL travel, minimum 16 hours a month, closing 31 December 2026.

✅ Updated October 23, 2026
Home » UK Police Jobs » Articles

The Metropolitan Police Service is recruiting for its Special Constable intake (campaign 08-2026) — volunteer police officers with the same powers and the same uniform as paid colleagues. Specials commit a minimum of 16 hours a month at times and days that suit them, and applications for this intake close on 31 December 2026 at 23:55 GMT.

The Met describes this as a volunteering opportunity like no other: specials respond to 999 and 101 calls, carry out foot and vehicle patrols, tackle anti-social behaviour, support neighbourhood policing, apprehend and arrest suspects, gather evidence, support victims and witnesses, and help police major public-order events across the capital. In return, the Met provides full training and a benefits package including free travel on TfL services within London, a council tax discount within Greater London and other Met-friendly benefits and discounts.

What Special Constables Do

Special constables are warranted officers: when on duty you hold the same powers of arrest and the same uniform as regular officers. The Met’s advert lists a genuinely broad remit — responding to emergency calls, neighbourhood policing, patrols, anti-social behaviour, public-order policing at national and international events in the capital, arresting suspects, gathering and presenting evidence in court, and engaging with children and young people. You will complete extensive training first, designed to give you the skills and confidence of a Metropolitan Police Constable.

Eligibility

  • Age: 17+ at application; you cannot start training until you are 18
  • Nationality: if you are from outside the UK, you must have indefinite leave to enter or remain
  • Qualifications: Level 2 English (GCSE or equivalent) at grade A–C (4–9), evidenced at the start of the process
  • Fitness and eyesight: you must pass the Met fitness test and meet College of Policing eyesight standards
  • Tattoos: some tattoos will bar you depending on what they depict and where they are
  • Convictions: having a record does not automatically disqualify you — eligibility depends on the age and nature of the offence
  • Groups/organisations: membership of any group contradicting Met values or inclusion commitments bars appointment

Training and Progression

The Met promises extensive training to prepare you for policing London — and the role doubles as a development opportunity: conflict resolution, leadership and communication skills that transfer to your day job. Longer term, specials can move into policing specialisms or take rank to supervise future officers, and many use the special constabulary as a route into regular paid policing.

How to Apply

1

2
Be realistic about the commitment. A minimum of 16 hours a month, on top of work and life — make sure the pattern is sustainable for you.
3
Apply on the Met careers portal. Use the Apply Link above to reach the official vacancy page and start the application.
4
Submit before 31 December 2026. The intake closes at 23:55 GMT — but popular intakes can close early, so apply sooner rather than later.

The Bottom Line

Same powers, same uniform, free TfL travel and a council tax discount — the Met’s Special Constable intake is one of the most substantial volunteering opportunities in UK policing, and a proven route into a paid policing career. It is unpaid and demands a real monthly commitment; if that works for you, apply before 31 December 2026.

Deadline reminder: The Met Special Constable intake (08-2026) closes on 31 December 2026 at 23:55 GMT. Verify the live closing date on the Met careers portal — intakes can close early.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Special Constable role paid?

No — it is unpaid volunteering (minimum 16 hours a month). Benefits include free TfL travel within London, a Greater London council tax discount and Met discounts.

What is the closing date?

31 December 2026 at 23:55 GMT for intake 08-2026 — though intakes can close early if volumes are high.

What qualifications do I need?

Level 2 English (GCSE A–C / 4–9), evidenced at the start of the process — plus passing the fitness test and meeting eyesight standards.

Do Specials have the same powers as regular officers?

Yes — when on duty, special constables hold the same powers and wear the same uniform as paid colleagues.

Can it lead to a paid policing career?

Yes — many specials progress into regular policing, specialisms or supervisory rank, and the training builds transferable leadership and communication skills.
