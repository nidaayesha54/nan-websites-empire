Devon & Cornwall and Dorset Police are hiring a Safer Schools & Communities Officer (Scale E) for Bournemouth and Christchurch — closing 27 October 2026.

Devon & Cornwall Police and Dorset Police are recruiting a Safer Schools & Communities Officer (vacancy ID 7804, Scale E) for Bournemouth Police Station and Christchurch Police Station. Applications close at 12:00 noon on Monday 27 October 2026, with interviews scheduled for the week commencing 9 November 2026.

Safer Schools & Communities Officers work at the join between policing, schools and local communities: building trusted relationships with young people, supporting schools on safety and prevention, and helping communities tackle the issues that matter to them. It is a frontline community-facing role for people who can earn trust, communicate clearly and work in partnership.

The Role: Safer Schools & Communities Officer

Officers in this role are the police’s face in schools and neighbourhoods: engaging with children and young people, delivering prevention and safety messages, supporting vulnerable pupils, and working with schools, councils and community partners to resolve local problems early — before they become crimes. You will need to be comfortable with young people, credible with professionals, and able to switch between a classroom, a community meeting and a partnership case conference in the same week.

The role sits within the wider neighbourhood policing and prevention model. It is a staff (non-warranted) post, not a police constable role — but the community impact is direct and visible.

Selection Process

  • Application stage: register, complete the eligibility form, then evidence your suitability in the application form
  • Shortlisting: applications are anonymised and shortlisted by a panel — you will receive the decision by email with feedback
  • Interview: week commencing 9 November 2026, assessed against the Competency and Values Framework (CVF) at levels 1, 2 or 3 as detailed in the role profile and CVF document
  • Assessment: some roles may also require an assessment — the force will confirm if applicable
  • Pre-requisites: vetting, reference, medical, qualification and right-to-work checks before a formal offer

Who Should Apply

This suits people with experience working with young people or communities — youth work, education, social care, community safety or policing support backgrounds all fit. The key qualities are communication, empathy, resilience and the ability to build trust across very different audiences, from teenagers to headteachers to partner agencies.

Your application will be anonymised at sift, so evidence your suitability against the role profile with concrete examples — not job titles alone.
