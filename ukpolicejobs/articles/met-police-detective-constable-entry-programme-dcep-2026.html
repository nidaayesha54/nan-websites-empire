Met Police Detective Constable Entry Programme (DCEP) 2026: How to Join as a Detective

Want to investigate crime rather than patrol the beat? The Met's 2026 Detective Constable Entry Programme intake is now open — here is what detectives do, what the Met looks for, and how to apply.

Published 14 October 2026 · NAN Jobs Portal editorial team

The Metropolitan Police is accepting applications for its 2026 Detective Constable intake through the Detective Constable Entry Programme (DCEP) — the direct route into one of policing's most sought-after roles. If you thrive on problem-solving, have good communication skills and love teamwork, the Met wants to hear from you.

Unlike the uniformed constable routes, DCEP trains you from day one as an investigator: supporting victims when they need help most, analysing evidence to uncover the truth, executing search warrants, making arrests and seeing cases through to court.

Now open: the Met states it is "now accepting applications for our 2026 Detective Constable intake" — apply now through the official Met police careers portal. As with all Met campaigns, early application is encouraged because of high volumes.

What Met Detectives Actually Do

Met detectives are part of a team delivering high-quality service to the public — investigating and analysing evidence, supporting victims of crime, and bringing offenders to justice. The work is deliberately varied: one week you might be executing search warrants and making arrests, the next building a case file for court. The Met's own description captures the mission — reducing crime and building trust with Londoners, ultimately making London safer.

Detective work suits people who are determined, hard-working and committed to protecting vulnerable people. The Met is explicit about the values it recruits for: integrity, courage, accountability, respect and empathy — and your application and interview will be assessed against them.

DCEP vs Other Entry Routes

  • DCEP (Detective Constable Entry Programme): direct entry as a detective — investigations from day one. The 2026 intake is open now.
  • PCDA (Police Constable Degree Apprenticeship): three-year fully funded route to a degree plus operational training as a uniformed constable.
  • PCEP (Police Constable Entry Programme): two-year uniformed route, no degree.

Choose based on where you want to end up: if investigations, evidence and casework excite you more than response policing, DCEP is the direct line. (All three routes share the same core eligibility standards — age, nationality, fitness, eyesight and vetting.)

How to Apply for DCEP

Check core eligibility. You must meet the Met's standard requirements: 18+ at start of training, right to remain (indefinite leave for non-UK nationals), fitness and eyesight standards, and vetting clearance.
Apply on the Met careers portal. Select the DCEP route and submit your application — monitor your email (including junk folders) and the recruitment portal for updates.
Evidence the five values. Prepare genuine examples showing integrity, courage, accountability, respect and empathy — problem-solving and teamwork examples score well for detective roles.
Complete assessment stages. Expect online assessment, interview, fitness test, medical and vetting — the same rigour as constable entry, tuned for investigative potential.
Start your detective career. Successful candidates begin structured detective training alongside operational investigative work.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Met Police DCEP?

The Detective Constable Entry Programme is the Metropolitan Police's direct-entry route into detective work — training recruits as investigators from day one rather than starting in uniformed response policing.

Is the Met DCEP 2026 intake open?

Yes — the Met states it is now accepting applications for its 2026 Detective Constable intake through the official police careers portal.

What values does the Met assess for detectives?

Integrity, courage, accountability, respect and empathy — alongside problem-solving ability, communication skills and teamwork.

Do I need a degree for DCEP?

DCEP is a direct-entry programme; check the current advert on the Met careers portal for the exact qualification requirements, which can vary by campaign.

What does a Met detective do day to day?

Detectives investigate evidence, support victims, execute search warrants, make arrests and take cases to court.
