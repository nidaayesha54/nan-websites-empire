What PCSOs Do

The primary role of a PCSO is to provide a regular and effective presence in their local community and to prioritise community demands so neighbourhood policing is delivered effectively and proportionately. That means preventing, reducing and detecting low-level problems, managing the intelligence-led identification of community concerns, and prompting targeted action with partners — from councils to housing associations — to improve the local environment and quality of life.

You will work as part of a dedicated Neighbourhood Policing Team: visible, accessible, skilled, knowledgeable and familiar to the community you serve. The work is varied — patrols, school visits, community meetings, problem-solving with partners — and it rewards people who genuinely enjoy being out in the community.

Who Should Apply

PCSO roles suit people who love community work: strong communicators, problem-solvers, and people with local knowledge and empathy. No degree is required — the force looks for personal qualities, judgement and community commitment. You will need to pass vetting and the force’s selection process, and be comfortable working shifts and weekends as part of a neighbourhood team.

For many officers, the PCSO role is a proven route into warranted policing: the experience, community knowledge and force relationships you build are exactly what constable recruitment panels look for.
