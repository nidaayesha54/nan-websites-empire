GTA 6 Wanted Levels: The Six-Star System Explained

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 brings back the six-star wanted level, and it rebuilds what the stars mean. GTA 5 topped out at five stars; GTA 6 goes to six, and the system around the stars is far more detailed than the old one. The police no longer just chase you — they remember you. They track what you look like, what you are wearing, what car you are driving, whether you were seen on camera, and whether you were with your partner. This guide explains the star states, the heat icons, and the hot areas that follow you after the chase ends.

The change is easy to miss if you play the way you played GTA 5, because the old habits no longer work. In the old games, losing the cops wiped the slate clean. In GTA 6, losing the cops is only the first half of the escape — the second half is dealing with what the police learned about you while they were chasing you.

Six stars are back

The six-star wanted level was missing in GTA 5, and its return is one of the most talked-about changes in GTA 6. The stars are visible on screen in the Extended Look showcase, and the escalation is real: at higher levels the response grows from patrol cars to helicopters, armoured vehicles and roadblocks, with the whole police force of the state of Leonida thrown at you. Whether the military gets involved at six stars, as it did in older games, has not been confirmed — treat any guide that promises it as speculation, not fact.

Reaching six stars is deliberately hard. Causing havoc for a short time will only get you to four or five stars, and guide reporting says it takes around ten minutes of continuous chase to reach five stars, and another ten to fifteen minutes of evading after that to earn six. It is a long, punishing process by design, and it is meant to feel like the whole state is hunting you — because at that point, it is.

What the stars mean

The stars change colour and pattern to show what the police know right now, and there are four states to learn. Hollow stars mean a crime has been reported — by a witness, an alarm or a vehicle tracker — and the police are heading to the scene, but they have no details to identify who did it. If you leave the area before they arrive, they investigate the scene without targeting you.

Solid white stars mean the police have identified you and are actively pursuing you with a direct line of sight. Flashing white stars appear once you break that line of sight: the police are searching your last known position, and if you leave their search radius without being spotted, the stars keep flashing until you lose them. Red stars mean you have escaped the immediate pursuit, but the crime scene remains an active hot area on the map, and the police are still stationed there with your details.

Heat icons: what the police remember

Underneath the stars sit small red icons, and they are the most important new idea in the system. Each icon is a piece of information the police hold about you, and each one can be cleared — which makes it much easier to escape an area that still has heat. A CCTV camera icon means you were spotted on camera, and you need to destroy the evidence to get rid of it. A two-person icon means the police saw Jason and Lucia together, so splitting up helps you avoid being recognised.

A clothing icon means the police recognise what you are wearing, so you should change clothes. A face icon means they recognise your facial features — wear a mask, or, if you are Jason, change your facial hair. A weapon icon means they saw you carrying a specific weapon, so ditch it or switch to another one. A vehicle icon means they recognise your car, so switch to a different one. Clearing these icons is the difference between slipping away and being recognised the moment you step back into the area.

Heat and hot areas

After you lose the cops, you still have heat in the area around your last known location, marked on the map. The police keep looking for you there, using everything they know about you, and if you are spotted or commit another crime in that zone, you get your wanted level back. At six stars, the hot area locks down the entire map, which is why a six-star escape is less about hiding and more about surviving long enough for the heat to fade.

Police in GTA 6 respond when a crime is reported: a witness calls them, an alarm goes off during a robbery, or a stolen vehicle with an advanced tracker reports itself. The more serious your offences, the higher your wanted level and the harder the response. The old system searched a small area for a short time; the new one casts a much wider net, and wherever you go in Leonida, the police are on alert and looking for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many wanted stars are in GTA 6?

Six. GTA 5 had five; GTA 6 brings back the sixth star, with a harder escalation to reach it.

How do I lose the cops in GTA 6?

Break the line of sight, leave the search radius without being spotted, and then clear the heat icons — change clothes, switch cars, split from your partner — before you re-enter the hot area. A full escape guide covers the routes and hiding spots in detail.

What happens when I am wasted or busted?

Being wasted sends you to the nearest hospital, and being busted sends you to the police station, with the losses that follow — pocket cash, unowned weapons and unowned vehicles are at risk, while banked money and owned gear are safe.

The bottom line: GTA 6's wanted system is not just stars any more — it is a memory. The police remember your face, your clothes, your car and your camera footage, and the chase only truly ends when you have cleared what they know about you.
