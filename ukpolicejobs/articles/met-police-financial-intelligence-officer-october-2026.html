Met Police Financial Intelligence Officer — Apply by 31 Dec

The Met’s Economic Crime Command is hiring a Financial Intelligence Officer for its Payback Unit — £36,228, 2-year fixed term, closing 31 December 2026.

✅ Updated October 23, 2026
Home » UK Police Jobs » Articles

The Metropolitan Police Service is recruiting a Financial Intelligence Officer for its Payback Unit, within the Economic Crime Command. The post pays a starting salary of £36,228 including allowances (£33,219 basic rising to £35,541, plus a £2,009 location allowance and a £1,000 non-pensionable allowance), is a 2-year fixed-term contract at 36 hours a week (Monday to Friday), and closes on 31 December 2026 at 23:55 GMT.

Payback Units sit inside frontline investigation teams with one core objective: stop offenders benefiting financially from crime. The unit strengthens asset recovery under the Proceeds of Crime Act (POCA) — cash seizures, account freezing and confiscation proceedings — and promotes POCA as a tool for disrupting serious and organised criminality across London.

Honesty note: This is a 2-year fixed-term contract, not a permanent post. The role does not attract Shift Disturbance Allowance — if you are currently in a shift-working role, SDA stops on appointment. Posting location preferences are accommodated where possible but cannot be guaranteed.

The Role: Financial Intelligence in the Payback Unit

You will gather, develop and analyse financial intelligence to support ongoing investigations and identify new investigative opportunities — using intelligence systems, databases and financial information sources to build the money trail behind serious and high-harm offending. The role also means identifying where POCA powers apply, helping develop asset-recovery strategies, and maintaining accurate, auditable records to legislative and organisational standards.

As you progress, the responsibilities grow: after accreditation, you will use Part 5 POCA powers, attend Magistrates’ and Crown Courts to obtain POCA orders, present evidence in financial investigations, and lead the financial aspects of designated operations — working closely with investigating officers and shaping investigative strategy.

Training and Career Pathway

The Met provides comprehensive specialist training: roughly six months at Lewisham, Putney, Barking or Hendon, with support to achieve accreditation as a Financial Intelligence Officer and then progression into Financial Investigation and POCA Civil Recovery qualifications. Those accreditations attract enhanced banding — so the £36,228 starting figure is a floor, not a ceiling, for a successful candidate who progresses.

Essential and Desirable Criteria

  • Essential: proven experience leading or supporting criminal or financial investigations; strong understanding of investigative processes including evidence handling and intelligence development; proven ability to use intelligence systems and databases; strong analytical skills with complex financial and intelligence data; working knowledge of the criminal justice system, evidential standards and case-file preparation; experience with large datasets, financial systems and intelligence databases; clear structured communication and presentation skills
  • Desirable: previous financial-investigation experience; evidential material preparation and court attendance; working knowledge of POCA; asset-recovery experience; policing, law-enforcement, financial-services or regulatory background; relevant professional qualifications in finance, criminology or intelligence

The Bottom Line

£36,228 to start, full specialist training with a structured accreditation pathway, and frontline work stripping criminals of their profits under POCA — the Met’s Financial Intelligence Officer post is a strong target for investigators and financial-crime specialists. Note the 2-year fixed term; if that suits you, apply before 31 December 2026.
