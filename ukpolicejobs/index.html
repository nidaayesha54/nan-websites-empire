How to Apply

Five steps to joining the UK police.

Choose your force and route
Each of the 43 forces in England and Wales (plus Police Scotland and PSNI) recruits independently. Check force websites and the College of Policing for open windows and entry routes (PCDA, DHEP or pre-join degree).
Check eligibility
You must be 18+, have indefinite right to work in the UK, and meet residency requirements. Review the force's vetting policy on convictions, tattoos and finances before applying.
Pass online assessment
Most forces use the College of Policing's national online assessment — situational judgement, written exercises and a video interview testing the Competency and Values Framework.
Complete fitness and medical
Pass the 15-metre shuttle run (level 5.4) fitness test and a medical examination, including eyesight and BMI checks.
Clear vetting and start training
Final right-to-work checks and police vetting precede a conditional offer. Training then begins — typically 2–3 years including probation, earning a degree-level qualification.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I become a police constable in the UK?

Apply directly to a police force when recruitment is open. Entry routes include the Police Constable Degree Apprenticeship (PCDA), the Degree Holder Entry Programme (DHEP) for graduates, and pre-join degrees. All routes include the fitness test, medical and vetting.

What are the basic eligibility requirements for UK police?

You must be 18 or over, have the right to work in the UK with indefinite leave to remain, pass a fitness test and medical, and clear police vetting. Some convictions may bar entry — each force publishes its vetting policy.

What is the difference between a PCSO and a police constable?

Police Community Support Officers (PCSOs) are uniformed staff with limited powers focused on community engagement and reassurance, while police constables hold full warranted powers of arrest and investigation.

How long is police training in the UK?

Initial training varies by route: the Police Constable Degree Apprenticeship takes 3 years, the Degree Holder Entry Programme takes 2 years, and traditional initial police learning and development is around 2 years including probation.

Do police officers need a degree?

Under the Policing Education Qualifications Framework, new constables qualify through degree-level routes — either the PCDA apprenticeship (no prior degree needed) or the DHEP for graduates — so a degree is earned during training.

What is police vetting?

Vetting checks your background, finances, associations and social media to ensure integrity. Most constable roles require Recruitment Vetting (RV), while specialist and counter-terrorism roles require higher Management Vetting or Developed Vetting.
