GTA 6 Real Miami: Every Landmark You Can Visit

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and its city is not imaginary. Vice City is Miami, rebuilt block by block — the same hotels, bridges, towers and swamps, renamed and remixed, but recognizable enough that you can walk them in real life. This guide lists every real landmark behind Leonida, what it is called in the game, and where to find it when you visit Florida yourself.

The first thing GTA 6 shows you is the spot every Miami visitor already knows. Rockstar's site says Ocean Beach mirrors South Beach and Ocean Drive, and Trailer 1 opens on the beach and the Art Deco skyline around Lummus Park. The strip is a row of 1930s Art Deco hotels, neon signs and palm-lined beach paths, and the trailers rebuilt the famous ones: the Colony Hotel becomes the Boardwalk Hotel, the Clevelander becomes the Ocean View Hotel, and the Breakwater becomes the Hotel Dixon. Some 800 Art Deco buildings line the drive. Trailer 1 drives Ocean Drive at night and Trailer 2 shows it by day, and in Trailer 2 Jason lifts weights at a beachfront outdoor gym that Miami press identified as Lummus Park's. Stand in Lummus Park — free and open to the public — and you are standing where the game opens.

Bridges, murals and the arena

From the beach, the trailer cuts to a narrow road that crosses Biscayne Bay one small island at a time: the Venetian Causeway, with the address 1000 Venetian Way recreated in Vice City, and the MacArthur Causeway beside it, the multi-island bridges that link downtown Miami's skyline to Miami Beach. Downtown, the skyline carries exact counterparts of real towers — Miami's 500 Brickell twin towers with their distinctive circular white roof arch, and One Thousand Museum, designed by Zaha Hadid — alongside buildings that are not in real locations at all, such as the Blue and Green Diamond buildings. Vice City's skyline is a remix, not a copy. Wynwood is a warehouse district covered in large-scale graffiti and street murals, and the trailer's murals resemble real tags by artists from Miami's street art scene, with one match pinned to 2516 NW Second Avenue. The arena on the bay is the Kaseya Center, renamed the Sahara Arena in the game, and the port beside it is PortMiami, renamed Vice City Port.

The arena, the port and the bridge

Three more landmarks complete the downtown picture. The arena on the bay is the Kaseya Center, home of the Miami Heat, renamed the Sahara Arena in the game — a big, glowing bowl on the waterfront that anchors the skyline in every wide shot. Beside it sits the port: PortMiami, the cruise capital of the world, renamed Vice City Port, with container ships and lit skyscrapers behind it in Rockstar's website art. And the road out of the city is the Seven Mile Bridge, the Florida Keys' iconic span, which becomes the road through the Leonida Keys in the game. Rockstar even paid Miami-Dade County $975,000 for one of these locations, according to Forbes — the studio spent nearly a million dollars to turn Miami into Vice City, and the bridge, the arena and the port are where that money shows.

Visiting them is the other half of the fun. Lummus Park, across from the Ocean Drive hotels, is free and open to the public — stand there at dusk and you are standing in the trailer's opening shot. The Venetian Causeway is a toll road, so bring a few dollars if you drive it. Wynwood's murals change constantly, so the walls you see will not be the ones in the trailer, but the district is the same warehouse grid at 2516 NW Second Avenue. Little Havana runs on Calle Ocho, Southwest Eighth Street, with the domino park and the cigar shops the game borrows. And the Everglades — the Grassrivers in the game — is a national park you can airboat through, which is the closest you will get to driving an airboat in real life. The game is a caricature, but the places are real, and a single day in Miami covers most of them.

Beyond the city: the Keys, the swamp and Little Havana

Leonida stretches far beyond Miami. The Florida Keys' iconic Seven Mile Bridge becomes the road through the Leonida Keys. The Florida Everglades becomes the Grassrivers, the swamp at the heart of the map. Little Havana, organized around Calle Ocho — Southwest Eighth Street in real life — becomes Little Cuba in the game, and it was in Vice City before Vice City had a trailer. The headline matches are simple: Ocean Beach is Ocean Drive and South Beach; Little Cuba is Little Havana; Stockyard is Wynwood; the Sahara Arena is the Kaseya Center; Vice City Port is PortMiami; the Leonida Keys are the Florida Keys; and the Grassrivers are the Everglades. Miami New Times mapped 20 real Miami landmarks from the first trailer alone. One honest caveat: Rockstar has never published a real-world key to the GTA 6 map, and never says "this is that place" — the matches in this guide are the widely reported, trailer-verified ones, and Leonida is a caricature of Florida, not a copy. But the hotels, towers, causeways, clubs and swamps behind it are real, and you can drive to most of them in a single day.
