Florida State Government Jobs 2026: How to Apply via the People First Portal

From corrections officers to forest rangers, Florida's state agencies list 1,000+ openings on the People First portal. Here is how the State Personnel System works and how to apply in 2026.

The State of Florida is one of the largest public employers in the Southeast, with executive agencies spanning healthcare, corrections, law enforcement, environmental protection, transportation, and administration. Nearly all openings are advertised in one place: the People First portal at jobs.myflorida.com.

Recent listings show the breadth of opportunity — correctional sentence specialists, property specialists, compliance officers, law enforcement dispatchers, magistrates, juvenile probation officers, government operations consultants, and forest rangers have all appeared on the portal, with postings concentrated in Tallahassee but spread across the state from the Panhandle to South Florida.

Florida hires through the State Personnel System, which has its own pay plans and job classifications. Understanding that system — Career Service vs. Selected Exempt Service vs. OPS — is the key to reading postings correctly.

The State Personnel System, Explained

Most Florida state jobs fall into the Career Service pay plan, which provides uniform pay, job classification, benefits, and recruitment rules for the majority of nonmanagerial positions across agencies. If you see “Career Service” on a posting, it means standardized salary bands and full state benefits.

The Selected Exempt Service (SES) covers middle management — bureau chiefs — plus professional roles such as physicians and attorneys, and supervisory positions. The Senior Management Service (SMS) is upper management and policymaking jobs. OPS (Other Personal Services) positions are temporary, funded by OPS appropriations: hourly wages with limited benefits, useful as a foot in the door.

Where the Jobs Are

The People First portal lets you search by agency, job family, location, and pay plan. Big hiring agencies include the Department of Corrections, the Agency for Health Care Administration (AHCA), the Department of Juvenile Justice, and the Department of Environmental Protection. Tallahassee hosts the densest concentration of headquarters roles, while field positions like probation officers, rangers, and inspectors are posted statewide.

Tip: many postings list a requisition number and working title (e.g., “Government Operations Consultant I”) rather than a plain-English title, so search keyword variations and browse by agency when a keyword search comes up thin.

Pay and Benefits

Career Service postings show biweekly or annual salary ranges upfront. Beyond base pay, Florida offers state employee health insurance, Florida Retirement System (FRS) pension or investment-plan options, paid holidays, and annual and sick leave accrual.

How to Apply for Florida State Government Jobs

  1. Create a People First profile at jobs.myflorida.com. This single profile powers applications to every State Personnel System agency.
  2. Search by agency, keyword, and location. Browse broadly first — working titles like “Government Operations Consultant” cover many different actual jobs.
  3. Read the pay plan and requirements. Confirm whether the posting is Career Service, SES, or OPS, and check for Florida-specific licenses or certifications.
  4. Complete the state application fully. Florida's application asks for detailed employment history — incomplete applications are routinely screened out before a human sees them.
  5. Apply before the posting closing date. People First postings show explicit closing dates; the portal stops accepting applications at the deadline.
  6. Track your status in the portal. People First lets you follow each application's progress and receive notifications about next steps.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do I apply for Florida state government jobs?

All State of Florida agency openings are posted on the People First portal at jobs.myflorida.com. You create one profile and can apply to multiple agencies from the same account.

What is the Florida State Personnel System?

It is the statewide HR framework covering most agencies. The Career Service holds the majority of nonmanagerial jobs with uniform pay and benefits; the Selected Exempt Service covers professional and supervisory roles; and OPS covers temporary hourly positions.

How many people work for the State of Florida?

Florida's executive agencies employ well over 100,000 people, and the People First portal routinely lists 1,000+ open positions spanning corrections, healthcare, law enforcement, administration, and natural resources.

What benefits do Florida state employees get?

Career Service employees typically receive state health insurance, Florida Retirement System pension or investment-plan options, paid holidays, and annual/sick leave — with exact benefits varying by position and appointment type.

Can out-of-state applicants apply for Florida state jobs?

Yes. Most postings are open beyond Florida residents, though some positions require or prefer Florida residency, licenses, or certifications — always check the individual posting's requirements.
