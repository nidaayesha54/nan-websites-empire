Washington state runs its hiring through a single official portal — careers.wa.gov — and right now several agency postings there share a tight window of closing dates from October 26 through October 28, 2026. The headline opening is the Legislative Operations Lead (Management Analyst 5) at the Washington Utilities and Transportation Commission (UTC) in the Policy and Legislative Affairs division: salary range 64, Step A ($78,504) to Step L ($102,996) annually, hybrid out of Lacey, and open until October 28, 2026, at 11:59 PM.

Also closing this week: Washington State Parks is hiring an IT Security Analyst – Journey in Lacey, closing October 26 at 11:55 PM PDT — a telework-eligible cybersecurity role inside the Parks Information Security Office supporting vulnerability management and incident response. And the Department of Fish & Wildlife (WDFW) is recruiting an Otolith Laboratory Biologist (Fish & Wildlife Biologist 3) in Olympia, with the first review of applications on October 26. Meanwhile the Department of Ecology has Environmental Specialist 3 and 4 openings in its Water Quality Program (industrial permits, Puget Sound nutrients) expiring November 7–8.

Two of these windows close within days. The Parks IT Security Analyst posting closes October 26 and the UTC posting closes October 28 — Washington agencies can close or extend recruitments at any time, so submit before the listed date rather than on it.

What the Washington Postings Tell You About Each Role

The UTC Legislative Operations Lead is an expert-level lead for agency-wide legislative operations — legislative intake, fiscal-note coordination, analysis, and implementation tracking. The posting notes the role owns the institutional systems behind the commission's legislative work, and the incumbent must reside in Washington and be available to report to the Lacey headquarters when needed. Applicants needing accommodations can contact UTC HR at (360) 664-1130 or humanresources@utc.wa.gov.

The WDFW Otolith Laboratory Biologist is one of the more distinctive state postings of the season: preparing and analyzing fish otoliths (ear bones) to provide age and origin data for salmon management, stock assessment, and Endangered Species Act recovery programs. The posting runs until November 3, but application review is ongoing and the first review is October 26 — early applicants get looked at first. The Ecology Environmental Specialist roles sit in the Water Quality Program at the Lacey headquarters office, with telework and flexible schedules (minimum two days per week in office), and one is a project position funded through December 31, 2029.

Washington State Benefits and Work Arrangements

Washington state postings consistently highlight flexible and hybrid schedules where the work allows it — the state's own job bulletins list roles tagged as Flexible/Hybrid, Remote optional, and on-site, so each posting's arrangement is stated up front. Current openings span the full spectrum: Health Care Authority analyst roles marked remote-optional, Ecology and UTC roles on hybrid schedules, and field roles (Natural Resources, Agriculture) listed as on-site. State employment also comes with the standard public-sector package: health coverage, paid leave, and retirement through state plans.

How to Apply on careers.wa.gov

Washington's application system is profile-based: you build one account and use it across every agency. The state's own job-seeker guidance recommends filling out every field, using advanced search filters by job category and agency, and saving searches to create automated job alerts. Supplemental questionnaires on postings are common — answer them fully, because they feed the screening process.

How to Apply for Washington State Jobs

  1. Open careers.wa.gov and create an account. Build a complete profile — a complete profile ranks better in agency searches.
  2. Search by agency. Filter by Utilities and Transportation Commission, State Parks, Fish & Wildlife, or Ecology to find these exact postings.
  3. Read the duties and supplemental information sections. Each Washington posting has distinct required questionnaires — tailor your responses to the listed duties.
  4. Submit before the closing date. UTC closes October 28, Parks closes October 26, and WDFW reviews from October 26. Agencies may close early once they have enough candidates.
  5. Set job alerts. Save your searches so new postings at your target agencies land in your inbox.
