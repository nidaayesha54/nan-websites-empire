Texas Federal Career Fair 2026 Recap: FBI, DEA, CBP and Secret Service Hiring

On October 8, 2026, federal agencies including the FBI, DEA, CBP, and Secret Service met job seekers face-to-face at the Texas Federal Career and Recruitment Fair in Pasadena. Here is what happened, what the agencies were hiring for, and how veterans and military spouses can prepare for the next one.

On Thursday, October 8, 2026, from 10:30 AM to 2:30 PM, the BakerRipley Pasadena Campus at 720 Fairmont Parkway, Pasadena, TX 77504 hosted the Texas Federal Career and Recruitment Fair 2026. Organized by the Texas Veterans Commission, the event was open to veterans, military spouses, and the public - and it brought an unusually strong lineup of federal law enforcement and oversight agencies under one roof.

Which Agencies Attended

Agencies represented at the fair included:

  • Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI)
  • Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF)
  • Railroad Retirement Board
  • U.S. Secret Service
  • Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA)
  • Department of Labor, Office of Inspector General (DOL-OIG)
  • U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP)

The mix was notable: alongside the big-name law enforcement agencies, oversight bodies like the DOL Inspector General and the Railroad Retirement Board reminded attendees that federal careers extend far beyond badges and uniforms - into auditing, investigations, benefits administration, and program analysis.

Career fairs do not replace USAJOBS applications - agencies cannot hire on the spot for competitive positions. What they do offer is something the portal cannot: recruiters who explain which announcements fit your background, what the questionnaire is really screening for, and what timelines to expect.

Veterans Guide: Making the Next Fair Count

The Texas Veterans Commission organizes these events specifically with veterans and military spouses in mind. If you missed the October 8 fair, here is how to prepare for the next one:

  1. Bring a federal-format resume - with exact dates, hours per week, and detailed duties. Recruiters will tell you on the spot whether it meets the standard.
  2. Know your hiring authorities - veterans' preference, VEOA, and military-spouse preference change which announcements you can apply to. Bring your DD-214.
  3. Prepare a 30-second introduction - your service background, clearance level, and the career field you are targeting.
  4. Ask about direct-hire authorities - some agencies can hire veterans faster under special authorities; recruiters know which ones are active.
  5. Follow up on USAJOBS the same week - fair conversations expire fast. Apply to the announcements recruiters pointed you toward within days.
Missed it? Watch the Texas Veterans Commission website and your nearest workforce center for the next federal career fair. Similar events run throughout the year across Texas and other states - and the preparation steps above apply to all of them.

Why In-Person Federal Recruiting Still Matters

In an era of USAJOBS questionnaires and automated screening, face-to-face recruiting fills gaps the portal cannot. Recruiters demystify opaque announcements, flag upcoming direct-hire openings, and - critically for veterans - explain how military experience translates to GS grades. For military spouses navigating a new duty station, a single fair can map an entire local federal job landscape in an afternoon. If a federal career fair comes within driving distance, go.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the next Texas federal career fair?

The October 8, 2026 fair has passed. The Texas Veterans Commission organizes federal recruiting events periodically - check their website and local workforce centers for the next scheduled date.

Can federal agencies hire me on the spot at a career fair?

Generally no for competitive positions - formal applications still go through USAJOBS. Fairs are for guidance, networking, and learning which announcements to target. Some direct-hire authorities move faster, which recruiters can explain.

Are these fairs only for veterans?

The Texas Federal Career and Recruitment Fair was organized by the Texas Veterans Commission for veterans, military spouses, and the public - civilians were welcome too.

What should I bring to a federal career fair?

Copies of a federal-format resume, your DD-214 (for veterans), transcripts if you are targeting degree-required roles, and a list of target agencies and job series.

Do I still need to apply on USAJOBS after attending?

Yes - always. Conversations with recruiters do not constitute applications. Submit a complete USAJOBS application before each announcement's 11:59 PM ET closing time.
