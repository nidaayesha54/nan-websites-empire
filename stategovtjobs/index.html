How to Apply

Five steps to land your government job.

Pick Your State Portal

Each state runs its own job board — CalCareers (CA), WorkInTexas (TX), statejobsny (NY), People First (FL). Create an account on the portal for your state.

Check Civil Service Requirements

Many states require a civil service exam or an eligibility list ranking before you can apply. Take the exam first, then apply to postings that match your list.

Tailor Your State Application

State applications ask for detailed employment history and supplemental questionnaires. Answer every question — incomplete applications are screened out automatically.

Understand State Pay Grades

States use their own pay ranges and grades (not the federal GS scale). Compare the posted range with the state's salary schedule and factor in pension benefits.

Prepare for Background Checks

Public safety, education and healthcare roles require fingerprinting, background investigations and sometimes drug screening. Keep your records clean and documents ready.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about government hiring.

Do state jobs require civil service exams?

Many do. States like New York and California use exams or eligibility lists to rank candidates, while Texas and Florida mostly use standard applications. Check the posting for exam requirements.

What benefits do state employees get?

State jobs typically offer pensions or 401(k)-style plans, generous health insurance, paid vacation and sick leave, and strong job security compared to private-sector roles.

Can I apply for a state job in another state?

Yes — most states accept out-of-state applicants, though you may need to relocate quickly. Residency requirements, where they exist, are usually listed in the announcement.

How is state hiring different from federal?

State hiring runs through each state's own portal and pay system, not USAJOBS.gov or the GS scale. Processes are often faster, typically 30 to 60 days.

Do state jobs offer remote work?

It varies widely by state and role. Administrative and IT positions increasingly offer hybrid or remote options, while field, healthcare and public-safety roles are on-site.

What is a pension vs. 401(k) for state workers?

Many states still offer defined-benefit pensions that pay a guaranteed monthly amount in retirement, while others use 401(k)-style defined-contribution plans — or a hybrid of both.
