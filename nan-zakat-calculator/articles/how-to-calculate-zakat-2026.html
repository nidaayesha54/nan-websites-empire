How to Calculate Zakat 2026: Cash, Gold, Silver & Investments

Zakat is one of the five pillars of Islam — an annual obligation on every sane, adult Muslim whose wealth reaches a minimum threshold called the nisab. Yet every year, millions of people ask the same question: "how do I calculate my zakat?" This 2026 guide answers it step by step: the nisab thresholds for gold and silver, exactly which assets count, how to handle debts, and a worked example you can follow with your own numbers using the free NAN Zakat Calculator.

1. The Zakat Formula in One Line

Zakat is 2.5% (one-fortieth) of your net zakatable wealth, payable once every lunar year — provided that wealth is at or above the nisab. In formula form:

Zakat = (Total Zakatable Assets − Deductible Debts) × 2.5%

Three conditions must all be true before zakat becomes obligatory: you are a sane, adult Muslim; your net wealth reaches the nisab; and you have held wealth above the nisab for one full lunar year (hawl), roughly 354 days.

2. The Nisab: Gold or Silver?

The nisab is defined in metal, not currency, so its cash value moves with market prices. The classical standard (used by the Hanafi school and most zakat institutions) is:

  • Gold nisab: 87.48 grams of gold
  • Silver nisab: 612.36 grams of silver

Because silver is far cheaper than gold, the silver nisab is much lower in cash terms. Most contemporary scholars recommend using the silver nisab when your wealth is a mix of assets, because it is the more cautious option — it means more people qualify to give, and more eligible recipients benefit. The calculator lets you choose either basis and converts it with the metal price per gram you enter, so always use your local market price (check a jeweller or bullion site on your zakat date).

Why prices matter: if gold is $182 per gram, the gold nisab is about $15,900 — but the silver nisab at $1.60 per gram is only about $980. That gap is exactly why scholars debate which to use, and why entering a current price matters more than any fixed number you read online.

3. Which Assets Count? (Step-by-Step Inventory)

On your chosen annual zakat date, list everything below at its current value. Only wealth you actually own and that has growth potential (or is itself money) counts:

AssetHow to value it
Cash & bank savingsFull balance on your zakat date, including cash at home
Gold & silverCurrent market value of all gold/silver — coins, bars and (per most Hanafi scholars) jewellery too
Stocks, mutual funds, ETFsMarket value on your zakat date
CryptocurrencyMarket value, like any tradable asset
Business inventoryWholesale value of stock held for sale
Money owed to youLoans/receivables you realistically expect to recover
Rental propertyNot the property's value — but rental income saved/held is zakatable cash
Pensions (401k, EPF, etc.)Many scholars count only the portion accessible now; ask a scholar for your scheme

What does not count: your home, your car, furniture, clothes and personal items you use — these are necessities, not growing wealth.

4. Subtract Your Debts

From the asset total, subtract debts and liabilities due now — loans you owe, credit-card balances, bills payable and any immediate outgoings. (Long-term debts like a 20-year mortgage are treated differently by different scholars; most deduct only the instalments due within the year. When in doubt, ask a scholar you trust.) The result is your net zakatable wealth.

5. A Worked Example

Meet Sarah. On her zakat date she holds:

  • Bank savings: $12,000
  • Gold jewellery (market value): $4,500
  • Stocks: $6,000
  • Money a friend owes her: $1,000
  • Credit-card debt due: $2,000

Step 1 — total assets: 12,000 + 4,500 + 6,000 + 1,000 = $23,500
Step 2 — subtract debts: 23,500 − 2,000 = $21,500 net wealth
Step 3 — check nisab: silver at $1.60/g × 612.36 g ≈ $980. Her $21,500 is well above it — and she has held wealth above nisab for a full lunar year.
Step 4 — apply 2.5%: 21,500 × 0.025 = $537.50 zakat due

6. When and How to Pay

Pick one annual zakat date — the day your wealth first reached the nisab — and pay on it every lunar year. Many people align it with Ramadan for the multiplied reward, but any date is valid. Pay in cash or kind to eligible recipients directly, or through a trustworthy zakat organisation. Keep a simple record: your zakat date, the calculation, and who received it.

7. Who Can Receive Zakat? (The 8 Categories)

The Quran (9:60) names eight eligible categories: (1) the poor (fuqara), (2) the needy (masakin), (3) zakat administrators, (4) those whose hearts are to be reconciled, (5) freeing captives, (6) people burdened by debt, (7) in the cause of Allah, and (8) stranded travellers. Zakat cannot be given to your direct ascendants/descendants (parents, children) or your spouse — support for them is your separate duty.

8. Zakat al-Mal vs. Zakat al-Fitr

Do not confuse the two. Zakat al-mal (this guide) is the annual 2.5% on wealth above nisab. Zakat al-Fitr (Fitrana) is a separate, small per-person payment — roughly the cost of one meal — due before the Eid al-Fitr prayer, paid by the head of household for every family member including children.

9. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using last year's nisab: metal prices move — always use a current price per gram.
  • Forgetting receivables and inventory: business stock and money owed to you are zakatable.
  • Paying on income, not wealth: zakat is on what you still hold on your zakat date, not what you earned.
  • Skipping the hawl: wealth must stay above nisab for a full lunar year — mark your zakat date.
  • Guessing on complex assets: stocks in mixed companies, pensions and business partnerships have scholarly nuances — ask a qualified scholar rather than guessing.
