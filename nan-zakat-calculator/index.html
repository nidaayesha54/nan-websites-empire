FAQs

What is the nisab for zakat in 2026?

The nisab is 87.48 grams of gold or 612.36 grams of silver (classical standard). Its cash value changes with metal prices — enter your local gold/silver price per gram above and the calculator converts it automatically. Most contemporary scholars recommend using the silver nisab, since its lower threshold means more people qualify to give.

Do I pay zakat on my salary?

Not on income as it is earned. Zakat is due on wealth you still hold on your zakat date — so the part of your salary sitting in savings (or converted into gold or investments) counts as cash/assets. Money already spent during the year is not zakatable.

Is zakat due on gold jewellery I wear?

Scholars differ: many (notably the Hanafi school) say zakat is due on gold jewellery above the nisab whether worn or stored, while others exempt personal-use jewellery. If you are unsure, ask a scholar you trust — and you can include its market value in the gold field above.

What about stocks, crypto and pensions?

Stocks and mutual funds count at current market value, as do crypto holdings. For pensions and retirement accounts, many scholars count only the portion you can access now. Business inventory counts at wholesale value.

When should I pay my zakat?

Zakat becomes due once a full lunar year (hawl, ~354 days) has passed since your wealth first reached the nisab. Pick one annual "zakat date" and pay on it every year. Many people choose Ramadan for the extra reward, but any date is valid.

Who can receive my zakat?

The Quran (9:60) names eight eligible categories: the poor, the needy, zakat administrators, those whose hearts are to be reconciled, freeing captives, people in debt, in the cause of Allah, and stranded travellers.
