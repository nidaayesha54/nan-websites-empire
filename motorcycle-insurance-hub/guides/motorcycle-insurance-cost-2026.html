The short answer: average cost

In 2026, the average motorcycle insurance policy in the United States costs roughly $500 to $1,500 per year. That's the middle of the road — a full-coverage policy on a mid-size cruiser for a 40-year-old rider with a clean record. But averages hide enormous variation: the same legal coverage can cost $200/year for one rider and $3,000/year for another.

Motorcycle insurance is generally cheaper than car insurance because bikes cause less third-party damage and insurers price for smaller average claims. However, riders face higher injury risk, so medical and liability coverage matter more per dollar of premium than they do for cars.

Average cost by bike type

Your motorcycle's class is the single biggest rating factor. Insurers price by claims history, and sport bikes crash more — and more expensively — than anything else on two wheels.

  • Sport / supersport: $1,000–$2,500+/yr. A 1000cc superbike ridden by a 22-year-old is the most expensive combination in motorcycling.
  • Cruiser: $400–$900/yr. Mature rider demographics and moderate speeds keep claims low.
  • Standard / naked: $350–$800/yr. Often the cheapest full-size category to insure.
  • Touring: $600–$1,200/yr. Higher bike values and long highway miles push premiums up.
  • Adventure / dual-sport: $500–$1,000/yr. Off-road exposure adds some cost, but lower speeds help.
  • Scooter / moped: $150–$400/yr. Small engines, low speeds, tiny claims.

Engine size multiplies the effect: moving from a 300cc standard to a 1000cc sport bike can easily double or triple your premium even with the same rider and address.

Average cost by state

Where you garage your bike matters almost as much as what you ride. States with dense traffic, high theft rates, generous jury awards and no-fault quirks charge more.

  • Most expensive: Florida, Michigan, Louisiana, California, Texas and New Jersey — expect 20–35% above the national average.
  • Cheapest: Iowa, North Dakota, South Dakota, Vermont, Idaho and Wyoming — often 15–20% below average thanks to low traffic density and theft.
  • Middle: most of the Midwest and Mid-Atlantic sit within 10% of the national average.

Moving across state lines? Your premium follows your garaging address, so a move from Miami to Des Moines can cut your rate nearly in half — report the change promptly to stay compliant.

7 factors that raise or lower your rate

  1. Age: riders under 25 pay roughly 60–80% more than riders 35–54. Rates typically drop sharply at 25 and again around 30.
  2. Riding record: a clean record earns the best rates. One speeding ticket adds ~10–15%; an at-fault accident ~30–40%; a DUI can add 60%+ for years.
  3. Coverage limits: state-minimum liability is cheapest, but raising limits from 25/50 to 100/300 usually adds only $50–$150/year — excellent value.
  4. Deductible: moving from a $250 to a $1,000 deductible on collision/comprehensive can cut that portion of the premium by 25–40%.
  5. Credit-based insurance score: in most states, better credit means lower premiums — sometimes by hundreds of dollars.
  6. Mileage and use: daily commuters pay more than weekend riders. Low-mileage and pleasure-use ratings save real money.
  7. Safety course: completing an MSF Basic RiderCourse typically earns a 5–15% discount for three years, and it makes you a safer rider too.

What does minimum-liability coverage cost?

A liability-only policy — the legal minimum in nearly every state — typically runs $100 to $400 per year for an experienced rider on a modest bike. It's the cheapest way to ride legally, but it pays nothing toward your bike or your injuries.

Liability-only makes sense when your bike is paid off, worth under ~$3,000, and you have health insurance plus an emergency fund. For anything financed or valuable, take our coverage quiz before deciding — the quiz weighs your assets, passengers and gear.

⚠️ Reality check: state minimums (often 25/50/25) haven't kept up with medical costs. One serious crash can produce $100k+ in bills — minimum limits leave your savings and future wages exposed.

How to pay less in 2026

The fastest wins: complete a safety course, bundle with your auto or renters policy, raise your deductible, and shop at least three insurers — quotes for identical coverage routinely differ by 40% or more between carriers.
