How to Get Cheap Motorcycle Insurance: 12 Pro Tips

Updated October 2026 · 7-minute read · By MotorcycleInsuranceHub

Two riders with identical records can pay wildly different premiums — sometimes 40% apart — for the exact same coverage. The difference isn't luck; it's strategy. Here are 12 proven tactics that cut motorcycle insurance costs by 20–50% without leaving you dangerously underinsured.

Shop and compare smarter

1. Get at least 3–5 quotes — every single year

Insurers re-price risk constantly, and loyalty is rarely rewarded. Quotes for identical coverage routinely differ by 30–50% between carriers. Set a calendar reminder 30 days before renewal and shop again. Start with our quote estimator to know your ballpark before you call.

2. Talk to an independent agent

Captive agents sell one company; independent agents quote dozens. A 20-minute call with an independent agent often surfaces carriers you'd never find on your own — especially regional insurers that specialize in motorcycles.

3. Compare identical coverage, not just price

A $400 quote with state-minimum limits isn't cheaper than a $550 quote with 100/300 liability and a gear endorsement — it's just less insurance. Line up limits, deductibles and add-ons side by side before deciding.

Discounts most riders miss

4. Complete a safety course (5–15% off)

The MSF Basic RiderCourse — or your state's equivalent — earns a discount with most major insurers for about three years. It also genuinely makes you safer, which is the whole point. Some carriers accept advanced courses for even bigger credits.

5. Bundle everything (10–20% off)

Adding your bike to the same company that insures your car, home or renters policy is the single easiest discount. Multi-policy savings of 10–20% are common, and one renewal date is simpler to manage.

6. Ask about the obscure ones

Insurers offer discounts many riders never claim: garage or locked-storage parking, anti-theft devices, ABS-equipped bikes, paid-in-full (vs monthly billing), paperless billing, autopay, loyalty, and membership in riding associations like the AMA. Ask for the full list — agents won't always volunteer it. We cover 15 of them in our discounts roundup.

7. Pay annually, not monthly

Monthly billing often carries installment fees of $5–$10 per payment — $60–$120/year for nothing. If cash flow allows, pay the full premium upfront and pocket the difference.

Bike and coverage choices

8. Choose an insurable bike

A standard or cruiser under 600cc can cost half as much to insure as a liter-class sport bike. If you're shopping for a bike, get insurance quotes before you buy — the premium difference over five years can exceed the price gap between the bikes.

9. Right-size your coverage, then raise the deductible

On a paid-off bike worth under ~$3,000, dropping collision and keeping comprehensive + liability often saves hundreds. Whatever you keep, raising your deductible from $250 to $1,000 typically cuts the collision/comprehensive portion by 25–40%. Just keep the deductible in an emergency fund. Not sure what you need? Habits that lower your rate

10. Keep your record clean — it compounds

Violations surcharge your premium for 3–5 years. One avoided ticket can be worth $500+ in cumulative savings. Defensive riding courses can also remove points in many states.

11. Ride fewer miles / declare pleasure use

If the bike is a weekend toy rather than a commuter, tell your insurer — pleasure-use ratings are cheaper. Some carriers offer verified low-mileage discounts.

12. Consider a seasonal policy

If you park the bike all winter, a lay-up policy that suspends liability/collision during storage months can save 20–40% of your annual premium. Run the numbers in our seasonal calculator — but check lender and state rules first.

💡 The 80/20: if you do only three things — shop 3+ carriers, bundle with your auto policy, and complete a safety course — most riders save 25–35% with about two hours of effort.

What NOT to cut when chasing cheap insurance

Cheap is good; naked is not. These are the cuts riders regret most:

  • Don't drop uninsured motorist coverage. It costs relatively little and protects you from the roughly 1 in 8 US drivers with no insurance — the exact drivers most likely to hit a motorcyclist and flee.
  • Don't default to state minimums blindly. Raising liability from 25/50 to 100/300 often costs less than $100/year and multiplies your protection fourfold. Minimums are a legal floor, not a recommendation.
  • Don't skip MedPay if your health insurance is thin. A $5,000 MedPay add-on is cheap and pays immediately after a crash, before fault is even determined.
  • Don't lie about garaging or mileage. Misrepresentation can void a claim entirely — the most expensive "discount" there is.

The goal is the lowest price for adequate protection, not the lowest price, period. Run the coverage quiz to find your personal floor before you start trimming.
