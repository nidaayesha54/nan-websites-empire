15 Motorcycle Insurance Discounts You're Probably Missing

October 12, 2026 · 6-minute read · By MotorcycleInsuranceHub

Most riders claim two or three discounts and leave the rest on the table. Insurers rarely volunteer the full list — you have to ask. Stack enough of these and a 25–35% total reduction is realistic. Here's the complete checklist for 2026.

The big three (claim these first)

1. Multi-policy bundling — 10–20%

Insuring your bike with the same company as your car, home or renters policy is the largest single discount available to most riders. If your carriers are split, get a bundled quote — it beats two separate policies more often than not.

2. Safety course completion — 5–15%

An MSF Basic RiderCourse (or state equivalent) earns a discount with most major insurers, typically lasting three years. Advanced courses can earn more. It pays for itself in the first year.

3. Paid-in-full — 5–10%

Paying the annual premium upfront instead of monthly avoids installment fees ($5–$10 per payment) and often earns an explicit paid-in-full discount on top.

Rider-based discounts

4. Clean / accident-free record — 10–20%

Three to five years without tickets or claims unlocks the best tier with many carriers. Some offer formal accident-forgiveness programs — ask.

5. Experienced rider — 5–10%

Several insurers discount riders with 3+ (sometimes 5+) years of licensed motorcycle experience, separate from age.

6. Mature rider — 5–10%

Riders over 50–55 often qualify for mature-rider credits, especially with carriers that court the touring demographic.

7. Riding association membership — 5–10%

AMA (American Motorcyclist Association) membership earns discounts with multiple insurers — and the membership itself costs less than the typical savings.

8. Homeowner — 5–10%

Owning (not renting) your home signals stability to insurers. Some carriers apply it even without bundling the home policy.

Bike-based discounts

9. Garage / locked storage — 5–10%

A bike parked in a locked garage is far less likely to be stolen. Tell your insurer — "street parked" vs "garaged" is a real rating factor.

10. Anti-theft device — 5–15%

Alarms, immobilizers, GPS trackers and disc locks can each earn credits. Keep receipts; some insurers want proof of professional installation.

11. ABS-equipped bike — 5–10%

Anti-lock brakes measurably reduce crash rates, and several carriers price that in. It's automatic if your bike has ABS — but confirm it's applied.

12. Low mileage / pleasure use — 5–15%

Weekend-only bikes crash less than daily commuters. Declare pleasure use and, where offered, verify low annual mileage for extra credit.

Policy and payment discounts

13. Paperless + autopay — 3–7%

Small individually, but they stack with everything else and take two minutes to enable.

14. Loyalty / renewal — 5–10%

Staying with one carrier for 3–5 years can earn loyalty credits — though you should still shop annually, because a competitor's new-business price often beats your loyalty discount.

15. Defensive / advanced riding course — 5–10%

Beyond the basic MSF course, advanced and track-based training programs earn additional credits with some insurers — and make you genuinely faster and safer.

How to stack them

Discounts multiply against your base rate, so the order doesn't matter — claiming all of them does. The practical playbook:

  1. Run our quote estimator for your baseline.
  2. Call your insurer (or an independent agent) with this list and ask which apply — don't assume the quote included them.
  3. Complete the safety course and switch to paid-in-full + autopay this week — the fastest wins.
  4. Re-shop 3+ carriers at renewal with all discounts applied; see the full strategy in our cheap insurance guide.
💡 Realistic math: bundling (15%) + safety course (10%) + paid-in-full (7%) + garaged (5%) + paperless/autopay (5%) compounds to roughly 35% off a base premium — often $250–$500/year back in your pocket.

Discount myths and traps to avoid

Not every "discount" you hear about at bike night is real. A few cautions:

  • There's no "loyalty discount" worth staying for blindly. Some carriers advertise loyalty credits of 5–10%, but their renewal price creep often exceeds it. Always re-shop — a competitor's new-business rate frequently beats your loyalty-discounted renewal.
  • Usage-based / telematics programs exist but are rare for motorcycles. A few insurers pilot phone-based riding monitors; savings are modest and the privacy tradeoff is real. Don't count on this as a major lever.
  • "Military discount" varies wildly. A couple of carriers (notably USAA-affiliated and Armed Forces Insurance partners) offer genuine military pricing; most others' "military discounts" are marketing. Verify the actual number.
  • Never misrepresent facts to earn a discount. Claiming garaged parking for a street-parked bike, or pleasure-use for a daily commuter, is material misrepresentation — it can void your coverage exactly when you need it. The savings are never worth that risk.

Stick to the legitimate 15 above, claimed honestly, and you'll land among the cheapest-insured riders in your zip code — with coverage that actually pays when it matters.
