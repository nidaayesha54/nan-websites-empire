Canada Government Jobs 2026

The Correctional Service of Canada is running a national CX-01 Correctional Officer inventory across 24+ institutions — paying $77,510 to $97,266 with a 14-week paid training program, closing December 31, 2026.

✅ Updated October 22, 2026
Home » Canada Government Jobs » Articles

The Correctional Service of Canada (CSC) is running an ongoing national inventory for Correctional Officer I (CX-01) positions — a pool of partially and fully qualified candidates that the service draws on as vacancies arise across 24+ institutions in eight provinces. The reported salary range is $77,510 to $97,266 per year, with the inventory closing December 31, 2026.

Correctional officers maintain the safety and security of federal institutions while actively supporting offender rehabilitation — a dual role that blends dynamic security work with case management, program delivery and crisis intervention. It is physically and mentally demanding, but it is also one of the federal government's most consistently hiring uniformed streams.

The entry bar is unusually accessible: a secondary school diploma or approved equivalency, then a 14-week in-residence Correctional Training Program with a $400-per-week allowance (up to $5,600) for recruits who pass selection.

What a Correctional Officer Does

  • Dynamic security — conducting patrols, searches and counts to maintain institutional safety;
  • Supervision and casework — monitoring offender behaviour and contributing to correctional plans;
  • Crisis response — intervening in disturbances, medical emergencies and use-of-force situations under strict policy;
  • Program support — helping deliver rehabilitation, education and work programs;
  • Documentation — writing accurate, legally significant reports on incidents and observations.

Education and Eligibility Requirements

CSC keeps the paper qualifications simple but the screening rigorous:

  • Education: a secondary school diploma or an approved equivalency — no post-secondary degree is required;
  • Security: candidates must obtain Enhanced Reliability Status, involving a deep background check;
  • Training: selection leads to the 14-week Correctional Training Program, delivered in residence, with a $400-per-week allowance to a maximum of $5,600;
  • Fitness and assessment: expect physical ability testing, psychological screening and scenario-based interviews;
  • Citizenship: check the poster — federal uniformed competitions typically restrict to Canadian citizens, with permanent residents considered in some cases;
  • Employment equity: CSC maintains dedicated inventories for Indigenous peoples and women; members of these groups should look for the stream-specific poster.
