Canada Government Jobs 2026

Employment and Social Development Canada is filling 400 Service Canada positions across the country — PM-01 roles paying $61,786–$69,106 that accept a secondary school diploma as the minimum education.

✅ Updated October 17, 2026
Home » Canada Government Jobs » Articles

The Government of Canada has opened one of the largest federal frontline hiring processes of the year: Employment and Social Development Canada (ESDC) is staffing 400 positions in Service Canada across the country. These PM-01 client-service roles pay $61,786 to $69,106 (under review) under selection process 2026-CSD-EA-NHQ-0040582.

What makes this intake stand out is accessibility: the minimum education is a secondary school diploma, positions exist in urban, suburban, rural and remote locations, and appointments will primarily be made on a term or acting basis — the classic entry route into indeterminate federal careers.

What Service Canada Officers Do

Service Canada is the federal government's front door for programs Canadians rely on — Employment Insurance, the Canada Pension Plan, Old Age Security and passports. PM-01 officers in this stream typically:

  • Serve clients in person, by phone and through digital channels, helping them access federal programs and benefits;
  • Assess basic eligibility, gather documents and explain next steps in plain language;
  • Handle sensitive situations professionally — many clients arrive stressed or in financial difficulty.

Eligibility & Requirements

The official posting sets a deliberately reachable bar. Your application must clearly show:

  • Education: a secondary school diploma — or an acceptable combination of education, training and/or experience (evaluated on your highest education plus 12 months of customer-service experience);
  • Competencies: oral and written communication, teamwork, attention to detail, client focus and digital skills;
  • Language: various — English or French Essential; bilingual imperative positions need PSC Second Language Evaluation results;
  • Security: ESDC assists qualified candidates through the security clearance process;
  • Operational needs: some positions may require overtime, shift work (days, evenings, nights, weekends) and travel.

Preference is given first to veterans, then to Canadian citizens and permanent residents (with special provisions for Nunavut and Yukon positions).

Selection Process — What Happens After You Apply

Unlike a single vacancy, this is an inventory-style national process: ESDC builds a pool of qualified candidates and draws from it as positions open. Applicants are invited to a written examination by email shortly after the closing date — missing it can remove you from the process. Use an email address that accepts external mail and keep your contact details current.

Why This Intake Matters

Four hundred positions in one national process is a rare volume for client-service roles, and the secondary-school entry bar makes it one of the most democratic routes into the federal public service. Term and acting appointments frequently convert to indeterminate (permanent) careers with the federal pension plan. Check the live posting for the current closing date and apply early: national inventories can close once enough qualified candidates are found.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the salary for Service Canada PM-01 positions in 2026?

The posting reports $61,786 to $69,106, marked as under review. Confirm the current rate on the live GC Jobs listing.

What is the minimum education for Service Canada jobs?

A secondary school diploma — or an acceptable combination of education, training and/or experience, including about 12 months of customer-service experience.

Who can apply for the 400 Service Canada positions?

Persons residing in Canada, plus Canadian citizens and permanent residents abroad. Veterans receive preference first.

Do I need to be bilingual to work at Service Canada?

Not always. Many roles are English or French Essential; bilingual imperative positions require PSC Second Language Evaluation results.

Is this a permanent job?

Appointments will primarily be made on a term or acting basis, though permanent (indeterminate) possibilities are also listed. Term roles are a common stepping stone to permanent federal careers.
