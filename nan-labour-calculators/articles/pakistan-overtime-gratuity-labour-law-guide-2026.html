Overtime & Gratuity in Pakistan 2026: Complete Labour-Law Guide

Pakistan's labour laws give workers two powerful money rights: overtime at double the hourly rate, and gratuity of 30 days' wages for every year of service. Here's how both work in 2026, with the exact legal formulas and worked examples.

Overtime: what the law actually says

Pakistan's standard working time is 9 hours a day and 48 hours a week (usually spread over six days). Anything beyond that is overtime — and the law is unambiguous about the price. Under Section 47 of the Factories Act, 1934 (and the provincial factories laws that succeeded it, plus Section 9 of the Shops and Establishments Ordinance, 1969 for office staff), overtime must be paid at twice the ordinary rate of pay — 200% of your normal hourly wage.

"Ordinary rate of pay" means your gross monthly salary expressed hourly — not just basic pay. The legally correct hourly rate is: (monthly salary × 12) ÷ (52 × normal weekly hours). Many employers quietly calculate overtime on basic pay only, which is one of the most common wage violations in Pakistan.

Working on a public holiday is even more strongly protected: you get 300% of your normal wage for the day worked, plus a paid substitute holiday and one day's paid compensatory leave. Work on your weekly rest day earns compensatory holidays.

The overtime formula, with examples

Overtime pay per hour = (Monthly salary × 12) ÷ (52 × 48) × 2
For a 48-hour week, this simplifies to roughly: monthly salary ÷ 208 × 2.

Salary Rs 80,000/month, 20 overtime hoursHourly: Rs 385 → OT rate Rs 769 → Rs 15,385

Compare these numbers with what your employer actually pays. If your "overtime" is paid at the normal hourly rate — or on basic pay only — you are likely being underpaid by half or more. Run your own figures on our overtime calculator.

Overtime limits and common violations

  • Daily/weekly caps: total working hours including overtime must generally not exceed 60 hours per week, and the Factories Act limits overtime to 150 hours per quarter per worker.
  • You can't be forced into excessive overtime that harms your health — and employers must keep accurate overtime registers.
  • Common violation #1: paying overtime at the single rate instead of double.
  • Common violation #2: computing the hourly rate on basic pay instead of gross salary.
  • Common violation #3: "off-the-books" overtime with no record at all — which is why you should keep your own log of extra hours with dates.
  • Ramadan: working hours are reduced to roughly 6 hours a day during the fasting month.

Gratuity: the 30-day rule explained

Gratuity is Pakistan's statutory end-of-service benefit for workmen. Under Standing Order 12(6) of the Industrial and Commercial Employment (Standing Orders) Ordinance, 1968 (and the provincial equivalents), when a workman resigns or is terminated for any reason other than misconduct, the employer must pay gratuity equal to 30 days' wages for every completed year of service — and any part of a year over six months counts as a full year.

The wages base is your last month's wages (for fixed-rated workers) or your highest pay in the last 12 months (for piece-rated workers). Note the provincial variations: in Sindh the statutory measure is commonly expressed as one month's wages per year, and Balochistan's 2021 law goes further — but the 30-day federal rule is the baseline most workers fall under.

Who is covered? The Standing Orders laws apply to industrial and commercial establishments with 20 or more workers (10 or more in some provinces). Government departments with their own service rules are excluded.

The gratuity formula, with examples

Gratuity = Last month's wages × number of qualifying years
(a "year" = 12 months; any leftover period over 6 months rounds up to a full year)

Worked examples

Last salary Rs 60,000, 5 years of serviceRs 300,000
Last salary Rs 45,000, 8 years 7 months9 qualifying years → Rs 405,000
Last salary Rs 100,000, 12 years 4 months12 qualifying years → Rs 1,200,000

Dependants of a worker who dies in service are also entitled to gratuity, paid through the Commissioner. And gratuity is in addition to anything in your contract — if your company scheme is more generous (some banks and multinationals pay more), the better terms apply.

Gratuity vs provident fund: which applies?

This confuses almost everyone. The rule: if your employer runs a provident fund to which it contributes at least as much as you do, no statutory gratuity is payable for the period that fund existed. The provident fund effectively replaces gratuity — and the law requires the fund payout to be no less than the gratuity would have been. (Balochistan is the exception: its 2021 Act pays the provident fund in addition to gratuity.)

Similarly, if the employer contributes to an approved pension fund through collective bargaining, gratuity doesn't apply for that period. Check which of the three retirement benefits — gratuity, provident fund, or pension fund — your employer actually operates, because you can't always claim all of them.

Under the newer Punjab Labour Code, gratuity starts only after one year of continuous service; the older Standing Orders laws of other provinces count any part-year over six months without stating a separate one-year minimum. When in doubt, the interpretation most favorable to the worker is the one labour courts tend to apply.

How to claim what you're owed

  1. Document everything. Keep your own overtime log (dates and hours), salary slips, appointment letter, and any HR emails about extra work.
  2. Calculate the legal amount using our overtime and gratuity calculators — going to HR with exact numbers changes the conversation.
  3. Raise it in writing with HR, citing the Factories Act (overtime) or Standing Orders Ordinance (gratuity). Written complaints create a paper trail.
  4. Escalate if ignored. Complaints can be filed with the provincial Labour Department or the Labour Court. For gratuity after termination, don't let the employer stall — the entitlement arises on your last working day.
  5. Watch the clock on gratuity. The longer you wait after leaving, the harder recovery gets. Act within months, not years.

Punjab Labour Code 2026: what changed

Punjab has been consolidating its labour statutes into a single Punjab Labour Code, and workers should know the headline changes. The Code keeps the double-rate overtime principle intact and standardizes the three-month maximum probation (after which you're deemed permanent). On gratuity, the Code introduces a clearer one-year minimum service threshold for Punjab workers — stricter than the older Standing Orders reading in other provinces, where any part-year over six months could count. If you work in Punjab, that first anniversary genuinely matters: leave at 11 months and you may get nothing; leave at 12 months and a full year's gratuity vests.

The Code also strengthens the paper trail: employers face clearer duties to maintain wage and overtime registers, which helps workers enormously in disputes. When the law requires your employer to keep records, missing records start to look like the employer's problem, not yours.

Women workers, night shifts and weekly rest

A few protections workers often don't know: women may only be required to work between 6am and 7pm in factories (some summaries say 9am–7pm with safe transport required for later hours) — night work for women needs specific safeguards. Every worker gets one weekly rest day (24 consecutive hours); you can't be made to work more than 10 days in a row without a holiday. During Ramadan, the working day drops to about 6 hours. And annual leave (14 days), casual leave (10 days), and sick leave (8 days, with carry-forward) are statutory rights — an employer who "doesn't believe in" annual leave is breaking the law, and refused leave can be encashed.
