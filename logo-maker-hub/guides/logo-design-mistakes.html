10 Logo Design Mistakes to Avoid (With Fixes)

Most amateur-looking logos fail for the same ten reasons. The good news: every one of them is fixable in minutes, for free. Work through this checklist before your logo goes public.

1. Using a low-resolution file everywhere

A stretched, blurry logo on your website screams amateur. Fix: always keep a vector SVG master and export PNGs at 2–3x the display size.

2. Too many colors

Four-plus colors look cluttered and cost more to print. Fix: one primary color plus one neutral. Test the logo in pure black and white — it should still work.

3. Unreadable typography

Overly decorative fonts vanish at small sizes. Fix: check legibility at 16px with our size checker; prefer clean letterforms.

4. Copying a competitor

"Inspired by" too closely is a legal and branding risk. Fix: list three things that make your brand different, and let one of them shape the mark.

5. No small-size version

A detailed logo that works on a sign becomes mush as a favicon. Fix: design an icon-only variant for avatars and favicons — make one in our icon logo maker.

6. Relying on gradients and effects

Bevels, shadows, and rainbow gradients date fast and fail in single-color uses (embroidery, stamps, fax). Fix: design flat first; add effects only as a secondary variant.

7. Ignoring clear space

Cramming the logo against text or edges looks chaotic. Fix: keep padding around the logo equal to at least the height of one letter.

8. Trendy for trend's sake

Chasing this year's aesthetic guarantees a redesign next year. Fix: choose timeless shapes; express trends in campaigns, not the logo.

9. No monochrome version

If your logo only works in full color, you will struggle with receipts, stamps, and single-color merch. Fix: export a one-color version on day one.

10. Skipping the trademark check

Falling in love with a name or mark someone else owns is expensive. Fix: search your country's trademark database before printing anything.

Frequently asked questions

What is the biggest logo design mistake?

Using a low-resolution raster file everywhere. One blurry stretched logo on a website or sign undoes all your branding work — always keep a vector SVG master.

How many colors should a logo have?

One to three. Logos with more than three colors look cluttered, cost more to print, and fail at small sizes.
