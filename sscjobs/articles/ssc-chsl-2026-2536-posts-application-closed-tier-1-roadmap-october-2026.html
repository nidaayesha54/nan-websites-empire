SSC CHSL Exam Scheme — The Two Tiers

Tier-1: Computer Based Objective Test

Tier-1 is the screening stage: an objective CBT of 100 questions for 200 marks in 60 minutes, split equally across four sections — General Intelligence & Reasoning (25), General Awareness (25), Quantitative Aptitude (25) and English Comprehension (25) — with 0.50 negative marking per wrong answer. Only Tier-1 qualifiers move to Tier-2, and the Tier-1 score feeds the final merit.

Tier-2: Descriptive + Skill Test

Tier-2 has historically combined a descriptive paper with a skill test — DEST (data-entry speed test) for DEO posts and a typing test for LDC/JSA posts. Confirm the exact Tier-2 scheme for the 2026 cycle in the official notification, since SSC revises skill-test norms periodically.

Pattern note: the section split and marking above follow the CHSL examination scheme. Always cross-check the 2026 notification on ssc.gov.in for any cycle-specific change before finalising your strategy.

Tier-1 Preparation Roadmap

1
Master the 25-mark blocks equally — CHSL rewards balance — a 25/25 in Reasoning plus 10/25 in Quant loses to steady 18s across all four sections. Diagnose your weakest section first.
2
Treat English as a scoring engine — error spotting, idioms, one-word substitution and comprehension are high-yield and the least time-consuming to improve.
3
Drill Quant with the clock — simplification, percentages, ratio, time-work and DI sets under timed conditions — Tier-1 Quant is speed, not depth.
4
Build a current-affairs engine — the last 6–8 months of national news, awards, sports, schemes and static GK linked to current events cover most GA questions.
5
Attempt full mocks from week one — 60-minute, 100-question mocks with 0.50 negative marking train the attempt-vs-accuracy judgment the real paper demands.
6
Start typing practice early — if you are targeting DEO/LDC, 8,000 key depressions per hour (DEO) and the LDC typing standard take months to build — do not leave them for Tier-2 notice.

What Happens Next — Expected Timeline
