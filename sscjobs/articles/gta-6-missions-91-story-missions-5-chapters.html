GTA 6 Missions: 91 Story Missions Across 5 Chapters

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and the first question every player asks after the prologue is the same one: how long is this? The answer is bigger than any GTA before it. The story runs across five chapters and 91 main missions by the count most guides use, and the road from the prologue to the finale takes most players somewhere between 50 and 65 hours — longer if you stop to explore Leonida along the way, which the game keeps inviting you to do.

This guide lays out the whole campaign: how the missions split across the five chapters, how long each part takes, and the two things you need to know before you start — that there is no mission replay, and that the chapter finales are points of no return.

Five chapters, 91 missions

The campaign is built in five chapters, and the mission count most guides agree on is 91 story missions, prologue included. A few guides count 93, because they count two extra mandatory story activities separately — the difference is bookkeeping, not extra content, so 91 is the number to plan around. The chapters are not equal in size, and that matters for pacing: Chapter 1, Just Like Paradise, has 22 missions including the prologue; Chapter 2, Paradise Lost, has 11; Chapter 3 has 26 and is the longest part of the game; Chapter 4, Vice City, Baby, has 25; and Chapter 5, Top of the World, Ma, closes the story in just 7.

Rockstar's Scott Mitchell put it plainly in an interview: Chapters 1 and 2 are slightly shorter if you want them to be, the longest chapters are 3 and 4, and the game tightens up again to close in Chapter 5. Chapters 3 and 4 together hold 51 missions — more than half the campaign — so the middle of the story is where most of your hours will go. Chapter 3 ends with The Cartel Heist, the robbery of Sinfrontera National Bank, and Chapter 4 opens in Ocean Beach with the story's pace at its highest.

How long the story takes

The main story takes most players around 50 hours if they push through the missions, according to the consensus Insider Gaming gathered from reviewers — and GameSpot's estimate puts it closer to 65 hours. The spread is wide because the game keeps pulling you sideways: IGN's playtime roundup recorded finishes between 42 hours for a mostly story-focused run and 115 hours for a player who took every detour, with a 70-hour finish in the middle. One Rockstar studio head said his own playthrough took 80 hours, including optional goals and a lot of wandering.

For the full picture, the 100 percent checklist and the Platinum trophy are estimated at roughly 200 hours by PowerPyx — a number that comes from a guide author's own 70-hour story run with optional missions included, not from a measured full completion, so treat it as an estimate. The practical point is simple: the story alone is a 50-to-65-hour commitment, and the world around it is easily three times that. Do not plan a weekend for this game. Plan a month.

The chapter finales: save before each one

Each chapter ends with a mission that closes a door behind you. Chapter 1 ends with Discharge Time, the hospital escape; Chapter 2 ends with Checking Out, which needs at least $15,000 saved before it appears and costs you the motel as a home; Chapter 3 ends with The Cartel Heist; and Chapter 4 opens the road to the finale itself. There is no mission replay in GTA 6, so a finale you play once is a finale you cannot play again — unless you saved first.

The game gives you 15 manual save slots, and the smart move is to use a fresh slot before every chapter finale. That way, if you want to replay a finale, try a different approach, or just watch a scene again, you can load the save instead of starting the whole game over. It costs ten seconds and saves you fifty hours. The same rule applies to the finale itself: the game asks before it, and a manual save there is the only way to keep both the ending and the open world on your terms.

What counts as a main mission

If you are ever unsure whether a marker on the map is a main mission, the story tab is the answer: missions listed there that are not marked optional are main missions. Optional missions, Strangers and Freaks activities, side jobs and random encounters all sit outside the 91 — they are the reason the game lasts 200 hours, not 50, and they are also the reason the mission count can look different from guide to guide. A guide that counts 93 is counting two mandatory activities that other guides file elsewhere. The story is the same either way.

That split is worth understanding because the game gates progress behind the main missions. New areas, new contacts and new money methods all open as the chapters turn, and the side content that pays best — the deliveries, the vault jobs, the fence network — only appears once the story has reached it. Rushing the missions unlocks the world faster; taking your time fills it in. Either way, the chapters are the spine, and everything else hangs off them.

How to plan your run

First, play Chapter 1 and 2 at your own pace — they are the shortest, and they teach you the systems that the long middle chapters will test. Second, save in a fresh slot before every chapter finale, and keep at least one save from the start of Chapter 3, because that is where the game's longest stretch begins.

FAQ

How many missions are in GTA 6? 91 story missions across five chapters by the most common count — 22 in Chapter 1, 11 in Chapter 2, 26 in Chapter 3, 25 in Chapter 4 and 7 in Chapter 5. Some guides count 93 by counting two extra mandatory activities separately.

How long is the GTA 6 story? Around 50 hours on a focused run, closer to 65 hours with side content, and up to 100 hours or more if you explore heavily. The 100 percent checklist is estimated at roughly 200 hours.

Can you replay missions in GTA 6? No — there is no mission replay feature. Save in a fresh slot before each chapter finale if you want to play it again.

The bottom line: GTA 6 is five chapters and 91 missions, with the long middle — Chapters 3 and 4 — holding more than half the story. Save before every finale, take the middle chapters slowly, and let the 200-hour checklist wait until the credits roll.
