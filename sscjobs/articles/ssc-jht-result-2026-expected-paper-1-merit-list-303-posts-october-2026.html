The Staff Selection Commission (SSC) is set to declare the SSC JHT Paper-1 result 2026 in October–November 2026 for the Combined Hindi Translators (CHT) Examination 2026 — covering Junior Hindi Translator, Junior Translator, Senior Hindi Translator and related posts. As of 24 October the result is not yet declared; this is a result-watch article, not a declared-result notice.

Paper-1 (the computer-based test) was held on 8 and 13 September 2026, and the provisional answer key followed on 21 September (objections closed 24 September at ₹50 per question). Candidates who clear Paper-1 move to the descriptive Paper-2 — translation and essay — which is where this exam is actually won.

Posts and Vacancy Note (303)

The initial notification (23 April 2026) listed 84 posts, but the revised notice of 9 July 2026 raised the tentative total to 303 — use 303 as the working figure, with the caveat that it remains tentative until the final result notice. The posts sit in central government ministries, departments and attached offices that need Hindi/English translators.

Eligibility and Paper-1 Pattern

  • Qualification: Master's degree in Hindi with English as a compulsory/elective subject at degree level (or vice versa), or a Master's in any subject with a recognised translation diploma, or equivalent — per the detailed notice.
  • Age: 18–30 years (relaxations as per rules).
  • Paper-1: 200 questions / 200 marks / 2 hours — General Hindi + General English, negative marking 0.25 per wrong answer.
  • Qualifying marks: UR 30%, OBC/EWS 25%, others 20%.
  • Paper-2: descriptive — translation + essay, 200 marks; date announced only after the Paper-1 result.

How to Check the SSC JHT Result (When It Drops)

2
Download the JHT Paper-1 result PDF and search your roll number (Ctrl+F).
3
Note your Paper-2 window — the result PDF itself carries the Paper-2 date, venue instructions and admit-card release details.
4
Start Paper-2 practice now — translation (Hindi↔English) and essay writing cannot be crammed in the gap between result and exam.

Paper-2 Prep — The Decisive Stage

Paper-2 is descriptive: translation passages in both directions plus an essay. The standard advice from previous toppers: translate one passage daily in each direction, build a glossary of official/administrative terms (rajbhasha vocabulary), and practise essay structure — introduction, argument, conclusion — under a timer. Accuracy of register (formal vs colloquial) matters more than literary flair.
