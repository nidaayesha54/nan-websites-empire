SSC MTS 2026 Notification Expected by End of October: Eligibility, Posts &amp; How to Apply

The SSC MTS 2026 notification is expected to be released by the end of October 2026, according to the Staff Selection Commission's indicative calendar — and it is the biggest 10th-pass central government opportunity of the year. The Multi-Tasking Staff (Non-Technical) examination, along with the Havaldar (CBIC & CBN) posts recruited through the same cycle, opens central government careers to candidates with just a matriculation qualification. If you missed the CGL or CHSL cycles, MTS is your entry point.

MTS posts are Group C non-gazetted, non-ministerial roles in various central government ministries, departments and offices — peon, daftary, jamadar, junior gestetner operator, chowkidar, safaiwala and similar cadres — while Havaldar posts in the Central Board of Indirect Taxes & Customs and the Central Bureau of Narcotics add a uniformed, enforcement-flavoured option to the same examination. The selection is a computer-based test followed by document verification, with a physical test for Havaldar candidates. Here is everything to know before the notification drops.

Eligibility Criteria (As Per Previous Cycles)

  • Educational qualification: Matriculation (10th pass) or equivalent from a recognized board. Candidates appearing in the qualifying exam may also apply as per the notification's cutoff date.
  • Age limit: Typically 18–27 years for most MTS posts and 18–25 years for Havaldar, with the standard relaxations — 5 years for SC/ST, 3 years for OBC, 10 years for PwBD — as per government rules. The exact age reckoning date will be fixed in the 2026 notification.
  • Havaldar physical standards: Havaldar candidates must additionally clear the Physical Efficiency Test and Physical Standard Test — including the prescribed walking/running benchmarks — so start basic fitness work now.

Exam Pattern: What the MTS CBT Looks Like

The MTS computer-based test is split into two sessions. Session 1 — Numerical & Mathematical Ability and Reasoning Ability & Problem Solving — carries no negative marking. Session 2 — General Awareness and English Language & Comprehension — carries negative marking of one mark per wrong answer. Session 2 marks decide the merit; Session 1 is qualifying in nature. That structure rewards accuracy in Session 2 above all else — blind guessing there is punished, so attempt only what you can defend.

5
Choose Havaldar only if you are PET-ready — the physical test is non-negotiable for that cadre.
Tip: Because Session 1 has no negative marking, attempt every question there — but in Session 2, skip anything you are unsure about. That single discipline routinely separates selected candidates from the rest.

Frequently Asked Questions

When will the SSC MTS 2026 notification be released?
The notification is expected by the end of October 2026 on the official SSC website, ssc.gov.in.
What is the qualification for SSC MTS 2026?
A 10th / matriculation pass from a recognized board is the minimum qualification for MTS posts.
What are Havaldar posts in the SSC MTS exam?
Havaldar posts in the CBIC and CBN are recruited through the MTS examination cycle. They need the same 10th-pass qualification plus a physical efficiency and standards test.
Is there negative marking in the SSC MTS exam?
Session 1 of the CBT has no negative marking; Session 2 carries a negative marking of one mark per wrong answer, and Session 2 marks decide the merit.
What is the age limit for SSC MTS 2026?
Typically 18–27 years for MTS and 18–25 years for Havaldar, with relaxations for reserved categories. The exact reckoning date will be in the 2026 notification.
