How to File a Cat Insurance Claim (and Get Paid Faster)

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

You pay premiums every month — the claim is where the policy earns its keep. Most cat insurance claims are straightforward, but small mistakes (missing records, late filing) cause most delays and denials. Here's exactly how to file, what you'll need, and how to get your money back as fast as possible.

How cat insurance claims work

Unlike human health insurance, pet insurance is reimbursement-based: you pay the vet upfront, submit the claim, and the insurer sends you a check or direct deposit. The typical timeline:

  1. Vet visit — you pay the full bill (keep the itemized invoice).
  2. File the claim — usually via app, website, or email, within the filing deadline (often 90–180 days).
  3. Review — the insurer checks coverage, waiting periods, and your deductible status. Takes 2–15 business days.
  4. Reimbursement — payout of (eligible costs − deductible) × reimbursement rate, sent to you.

What you need to file

  • Itemized invoice from the vet — line-by-line charges, not just a total.
  • Medical records / SOAP notes for the visit — many insurers pull these directly from your vet with your permission.
  • Claim form — most providers now use in-app digital forms; a few still want PDFs.
  • Proof of payment — receipt or card statement for some providers.

Pro tip: ask your vet's front desk for the itemized invoice before you leave. Chasing paperwork weeks later is the #1 cause of delayed claims.

5 ways to get reimbursed faster

  1. File the same day. Fresh records, fresh memory, no deadline risk. Most apps let you snap a photo of the invoice in the parking lot.
  2. Pre-authorize your vet to share records. Sign the records release at enrollment so the insurer doesn't have to ask you mid-claim.
  3. Submit complete records the first time. Incomplete submissions get kicked back — the single biggest delay. Include the invoice and the visit notes.
  4. Use direct deposit. Checks add 5–10 days; direct deposit is usually 2–3 days after approval.
  5. Track your deductible. Know how much of your annual deductible is met so you're not surprised when an early-year claim pays $0.

Why claims get denied (and how to avoid it)

Denial reasonHow to avoid it
Pre-existing conditionEnroll early; know exactly how your provider defines "pre-existing"
Waiting period not metCheck accident vs. illness waiting periods before filing
Excluded service (e.g. routine dental)Read your policy's exclusion list once — it takes 10 minutes
Missing documentationAlways submit the itemized invoice + visit notes together
Late filingNote your provider's filing deadline; file within days, not months

If a claim is denied and you disagree, appeal: ask for the denial in writing, gather supporting vet notes, and resubmit. A meaningful share of appealed denials get reversed.

What about direct vet payment?

A few providers offer to pay your vet directly so you don't front thousands. It usually requires your vet's cooperation and pre-approval. Worth asking about before a planned surgery — for emergencies, reimbursement is still the norm.

Bottom line: File same-day with complete records, use direct deposit, and know your waiting periods and exclusions. Do that and most claims pay out within a week or two.
