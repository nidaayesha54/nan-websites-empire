How Much Does Cat Insurance Cost in 2026?

Updated October 2026 · 8 min read

The short answer: in the USA, cat insurance costs $15–$35 per month for a standard accident-and-illness plan in 2026. But the range is wide — a healthy kitten in Ohio might cost $14/month, while a 12-year-old Persian in California can exceed $70/month. This guide breaks down real average prices, the factors that move your quote, and seven practical ways to pay less.

5 factors that determine your premium

1. Age — the biggest factor

Age drives pricing more than anything else. Insurers price on risk, and older cats need more care:

Cat's ageTypical premium vs. baseline
Kitten (under 1)~20% below baseline
Young adult (1–5)Baseline
Middle-aged (6–10)~45% above baseline
Senior (11+)~110% above baseline

This is why enrolling early is the single best money move in pet insurance. A policy started at 1 year old often stays far cheaper for life than one started at 8.

2. Breed

Mixed-breed domestic shorthairs are cheapest to insure. Purebreds cost 20–30% more, and breeds prone to hereditary conditions (Persians with kidney disease, Bengals with heart issues, Sphynx with skin conditions) can cost 50%+ more. The reason is actuarial: insurers pay out more claims on these breeds.

3. Location

Vet costs vary enormously by state, and premiums follow. California, New York, Massachusetts, and DC sit at the top — often 25–35% above the national average. Midwest and Southern states are typically 10–20% below average. Insurers use your ZIP code, not just your state.

4. Coverage level

Accident-only plans are cheapest but exclude the illnesses that generate most claims — urinary blockages, kidney disease, cancer. Most owners get the best value from accident-and-illness plans. Wellness add-ons cover routine care but rarely "pay for themselves" mathematically.

5. Deductible and reimbursement choices

A $500 deductible with 70% reimbursement costs far less per month than a $100 deductible with 90% reimbursement. Higher deductibles and lower reimbursement rates mean lower premiums — but bigger bills when something happens. Use our plan comparator to find your sweet spot.

7 ways to lower your cat insurance cost

  1. Enroll young. Lock in low premiums before age-related pricing kicks in and before conditions become "pre-existing."
  2. Choose a higher deductible. Moving from $250 to $500 can cut premiums 20–30%. Keep the difference in an emergency fund.
  3. Pick 80% reimbursement. The jump from 80% to 90% reimbursement is disproportionately expensive for most owners.
  4. Skip the wellness add-on. Run the numbers: wellness plans rarely beat paying for checkups out of pocket.
  5. Pay annually. Many providers offer a 5–10% discount for annual payment.
  6. Insure multiple pets. Multi-pet discounts of 5–10% are common.
  7. Compare at least 3 providers. Quotes for identical coverage can differ by 40% or more between companies.

What about the cheapest plans?

Accident-only plans at $10–$15/month look tempting, but they exclude illness — and illness drives roughly 80% of pet insurance claims. A urinary blockage ($1,500–$3,000) or chronic kidney disease (thousands per year) would be entirely uncovered. For most cats, accident-and-illness is the minimum that makes financial sense.

Bottom line: Budget $20–$30/month for a young healthy cat, $35–$60/month for a middle-aged or purebred cat. Enroll early, choose a $500 deductible with 80% reimbursement, and compare multiple providers to get the best value.
