BMI Calculator: What Your Body Mass Index Really Means

Body Mass Index (BMI) is the world's most-used quick check for healthy weight. One number, four categories — but what does it actually tell you, and what are its limits? This guide covers the formula, the chart, and the caveats. Check your number with our free BMI calculator.

The BMI formula

Metric: BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²
Imperial: BMI = (weight (lb) ÷ height (in)²) × 703

Example: 70 kg at 1.75 m → 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.9 — squarely in the healthy range.

Where BMI falls short

  • Muscle vs. fat: BMI can't tell them apart. A muscular athlete can be "overweight" by BMI while having low body fat.
  • Fat distribution: Belly fat carries more health risk than hip/thigh fat — BMI ignores location entirely.
  • Age and sex: The same formula is used for everyone, though healthy composition shifts with age.
  • Not for kids the same way: Children's BMI is interpreted via age-and-sex percentiles, not adult cutoffs.

Better measures to pair with BMI

  • Waist circumference: over 40 in (men) or 35 in (women) signals higher risk regardless of BMI.
  • Waist-to-height ratio: keep your waist under half your height.
  • Body fat percentage: the most direct measure, via calipers, scales, or DEXA scans.

How to move your BMI into the healthy range

  1. Know your number — calculate it today with our free BMI calculator.
  2. Set a realistic target — even 5–10% weight loss improves health markers.
  3. Build sustainable habits — daily walking, more vegetables, consistent sleep.
  4. Recheck monthly — not daily; weight fluctuates naturally.
Important: BMI is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. This guide is for general information only — talk to a healthcare professional about your personal health.
