Age Calculator: How to Calculate Your Exact Age

"How old am I, exactly?" It's a simple question with a surprisingly tricky answer. Your age in completed years is easy — but your exact age in years, months, and days requires careful calendar math. The exact age formula

An age calculator does this in three steps:

  1. Years: current year minus birth year. If your birthday hasn't occurred yet this year, subtract one.
  2. Months: current month minus birth month (adjusted for the year step above).
  3. Days: current day minus birth day. If negative, "borrow" the number of days in the previous month — just like subtraction in school.
Example: Born March 10, 1995, and today is October 11, 2026 → 2026−1995 = 31 years; October is after March, so 31 years stands; months = 10−3 = 7; days = 11−10 = 1. Result: 31 years, 7 months, 1 day.

Why leap years matter

A year isn't exactly 365 days — it's about 365.25, which is why we add February 29 every four years. Age calculators handle this automatically by working with real calendar dates instead of multiplying by 365. Born on February 29? In non-leap years your "birthday" for counting purposes is typically treated as March 1.

Total days, weeks, and hours lived

Once you have the date difference, conversions are easy:

  • Days: the raw calendar-day count between the dates.
  • Weeks: total days ÷ 7.
  • Hours: total days × 24 (roughly 262,800 hours by age 30!).

Fun milestones to celebrate

  • 10,000 days old — happens around age 27.4. A great excuse for a party.
  • 1 billion seconds old — about 31.7 years.
  • Golden birthday — when your age matches your birth date (e.g., turning 25 on the 25th).

When you need your exact age

  • Legal documents and ID verification (some forms ask for age in years and months).
  • Medical dosing for children, which is often weight- and age-based.
  • School enrollment cutoffs and sports age divisions.
  • Retirement and pension eligibility calculations.
