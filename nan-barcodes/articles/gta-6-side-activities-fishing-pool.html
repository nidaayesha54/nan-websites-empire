GTA 6 Beyond the Story: Fishing, Pool and Side Jobs

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 is more than its story — and the proof is in what IGN's review showed between the missions. Fishing. Pool. Side jobs. Radio stations. An in-game phone and social media layer. GTA 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and its world is full of things to do that have nothing to do with the next mission marker. This guide covers what is actually confirmed about the world beyond the story — and, just as importantly, what is not confirmed yet, so you know the difference between a real activity and a guess dressed as a guide.

The honest framing matters, because the game is days old and the details are still emerging. What is confirmed is that the activities exist: IGN's review showed fishing, pool, side jobs, and radio stations as part of the world, alongside the heists and bank robberies and the missions that cannot be replayed. What is not confirmed is the depth of each one — how fishing works, what pool pays, which side jobs are worth doing. Any article or video claiming a complete guide to any of these this week is guessing. This guide sticks to what is verified, and says plainly where the verification ends.

Fishing and Pool: The Quiet Side of Vice City

Fishing and pool are the quiet side of GTA 6 — the activities that exist between the noise. IGN's review showed both as part of the world, and their presence says something important about what kind of game this is. A story about escape, told through two leads, needs moments of calm between the jobs — and fishing and pool are those moments. They are the texture of the life Jason and Lucia are trying to leave behind, and the game is at its best when you take the time to live in it between missions.

Side Jobs: Earning Between Missions

Side jobs are the working side of the world beyond the story — and they matter more than the quiet activities, because some missions in GTA 6 are money-gated. The next mission only opens once Jason and Lucia have earned enough money, a detail confirmed by Rockstar North's Rob Nelson speaking via Famitsu. If no mission marker appears and your cash is thin, the game is not broken — it is waiting for you to earn. Side jobs are one of the ways to earn, alongside the verified methods: jewelry robberies, car sales, and treasure dives.

The practical advice is the same as everywhere else in this game: earn steadily, bank what you earn, and save manually. Pocket cash is lost when you die or get arrested, and it does not carry into Chapter 2 — so deposit at ATMs after every payday, stash spare money under the floorboards in Jason's bedroom, and open the BuckMe bank account once the mission "Buck Me" is available. A side job that pays is only useful if the money survives to the next mission, and the bank is what makes it survive.

Radio Stations: The Soundtrack of Leonida

The radio stations are part of the world's texture too — and they come with a launch bug worth knowing about. Some players have reported radio music going silent while effects and dialogue still work. The verified checks are simple: check the in-game music and radio volume, make sure a station is actually selected, and match the console's speaker mode to your real speakers. On Xbox, Party Chat can suppress licensed music — leaving Party Chat and relaunching the game is the documented workaround, though it comes from GTA 5 behaviour and is unconfirmed for GTA 6.

The In-Game Phone and Social Media

The in-game phone and social media layer is the most important system outside the story — because it is not just decoration. Witnesses report crimes through it, word travels, and the world reacts to what it sees. The wanted system is built on observation: the police act only on what they witness, and a civilian with a phone is a witness. The phone is the reason a quiet job is a safe job, and the reason a loud crime in front of a crowd brings the whole city down on you.

The phone also means the world is watching in a way GTA 5's world never was. A crime committed where no one can see you may pass unnoticed entirely. The same crime in front of a crowd is a chase. Before a risky job, look around: who is watching, where are the patrols, and is there a quieter way to do the same thing? The players who treat the world as full of eyes will stay out of trouble far more often than the ones who treat it as empty.

How to Spend Your Time Between Missions

The best advice for the world beyond the story is simple: take your time. The story is about escape, but the world is worth staying in for a while first. Fish. Play pool. Do a side job. Listen to the radio. Drive the neon boulevards at night and watch the headlights pierce the dark. The missions will wait — and the missions cannot be replayed, so the time you spend between them is the time that makes the story yours instead of a checklist.

And save manually while you do it. GTA 6 gives you fifteen manual save slots — Pause, Game, Save Game, after every mission, rotating two or three slots — and the autosave cannot be trusted: it only fires after a mission ends, the prologue never autosaves, and crashes have already cost players hours, including streamer JasonTheWeen, who lost around ten hours. A quiet evening of fishing is still an evening the autosave might not remember. Five seconds of saving is the cheapest insurance in Vice City.
