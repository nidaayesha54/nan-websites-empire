Voice Script Examples That Actually Work

Four copy-ready sample scripts — a 30-second ad, a 60-second explainer, an IVR menu and an announcement — each with timing and a breakdown of why it works.

By the VoiceScriptWriter team · Updated October 12, 2026 · 6-minute read

The fastest way to learn voice scripting is to study scripts that work. Below are four complete examples you can copy, adapt, and record. Each follows the hook–body–CTA structure from our complete guide, and each is timed at the standard 150 words per minute. Want one written for your own product? Try the free script generator.

Example 1: 30-Second Radio Ad (72 words ≈ 0:29)

The Script — "FreshBrew Coffee"

[HOOK] What if your morning coffee tasted like it was brewed by a barista — without the five-dollar price tag?

[BODY] FreshBrew delivers small-batch beans to your door, roasted the same morning they ship. Just brew, sip, and taste the difference fresh makes.

[CTA] Order today at freshbrew dot com and get free delivery on your first bag. FreshBrew — mornings, upgraded.

Why it works

  • The hook opens a curiosity loop ("tasted like a barista made it?") in the first 3 seconds.
  • One benefit (freshness), one proof point (roasted the morning it ships) — no clutter.
  • The CTA has a verb ("order"), a destination spelled for audio ("freshbrew dot com"), and a reward (free delivery).
  • 72 words lands at ~29 seconds — safely inside a 30-second slot.

Example 2: 60-Second Explainer (148 words ≈ 0:59)

The Script — "CloudSync App"

[HOOK] Here's a number worth knowing: the average freelancer loses 6 hours a month hunting for files. CloudSync gives those hours back.

[BODY] CloudSync is the file manager that organizes itself. Drop in your documents, and it sorts them by project, client, and deadline — automatically. Need something? Just type what you remember, and CloudSync finds it in under a second. It works on your phone, laptop, and tablet, and everything syncs instantly. No folders to manage. No files to lose. And your data stays encrypted, so only you can see it.

[CTA] Download CloudSync free today — set up in under two minutes at cloudsync dot com. Stop searching. Start finding.

Why it works

  • Problem → solution → how it works → CTA: the classic explainer arc.
  • Short punchy sentences ("No folders to manage. No files to lose.") create rhythm and are easy to voice.
  • The stat hook earns authority; the "6 hours" is concrete and relatable.
  • 148 words ≈ 59 seconds — leaves a breath of room in a 60-second slot.

Example 3: IVR Phone Menu (38 words ≈ 0:18)

The Script — "BrightSmile Dental"

[GREETING] Thank you for calling BrightSmile Dental.

[MENU] For appointments, press 1. For billing questions, press 2. For anything else, press 0 to speak with our team.

[CLOSE] If this is a dental emergency, please hang up and dial 911.

Why it works

  • Calm, slow, neutral — exactly the tone a caller needs. No hype, no rush.
  • Three options max. Every extra option doubles caller frustration.
  • The emergency line is legally and ethically essential for health businesses — never skip it.
  • 38 words at a slower IVR pace (~130 wpm) runs about 18 seconds — short enough that callers don't hang up.

Example 4: Public Announcement (64 words ≈ 0:26)

The Script — "Library Renovation"

[OPENING] Attention, please — an important update from Maple Street Library.

[DETAILS] Starting Monday, our main reading room closes for renovation for three weeks. All books remain available at the front desk, and our digital library is open 24 hours a day.

[CTA] For full details and alternate pickup locations, visit maplestreetlibrary dot org. Thank you for your patience.

Why it works

  • Leads with the news ("attention… important update") — announcements must not bury the point.
  • Answers the listener's first question immediately: "can I still get books?" Yes — front desk and digital.
  • Warm close ("thank you for your patience") softens bad news.
  • 64 words ≈ 26 seconds — perfect for PA systems and pre-roll announcements.

Steal the patterns, not the words

These examples are templates, not scripts to copy verbatim — swap in your product, your proof points, your CTA. The patterns (hook in 3 seconds, one idea per sentence, one clear CTA, timed to the slot) work in every niche.
