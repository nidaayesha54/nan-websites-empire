5 Voice Script Mistakes That Kill Conversions (and How to Fix Them)

We analyzed hundreds of beginner scripts. These five errors show up again and again — and each one has a simple fix.

Home › Blog › 5 Voice Script Mistakes

By the VoiceScriptWriter team · October 12, 2026 · 5-minute read

Most voice scripts don't fail because the product is bad. They fail because of small, fixable writing errors that compound: a weak opening loses the listener, a missing CTA wastes the attention you earned, filler words make the brand sound amateur. Here are the five mistakes we see most — and exactly how to fix each one.

Mistake 1: Opening with "Hello and welcome"

"Hi, welcome to our video about…" burns your most valuable seconds — the first three, when the listener decides to stay or skip. The fix: open mid-action with a hook. A question ("What if your coffee made itself?"), a stat, or a bold claim. Generate five options in seconds with the hook writer.

Mistake 2: No call to action (or three of them)

Scripts that end with "thanks for listening" convert at roughly zero. Scripts that end with "visit the site, call us, and follow us" also convert at roughly zero — three actions means no action. The fix: end every script with exactly one concrete CTA: one verb, one destination, one reason to act now. The CTA builder ranks options by strength for your goal.

Mistake 3: Writing for the eye, not the ear

Twenty-five-word sentences, "furthermore", "utilize", "do not" instead of "don't" — all fine in a report, all deadly in audio. Listeners get one pass; they can't re-read. The fix: keep sentences to 8–14 words, use contractions, and read every line aloud. If you stumble, rewrite. Paste your draft into the tone analyzer to catch stiffness automatically.

Mistake 4: Filler words and weakeners

"Basically", "literally", "um", "very", "really", "just" — in a written script these are pure clutter. They add syllables without meaning and make the voice actor sound unsure. The fix: search your script for each one and delete ruthlessly. "It's really very fast" becomes "It's fast" — shorter, stronger, more confident.

Mistake 5: Wrong word count for the slot

A 110-word script will never fit a 30-second ad — it gets cut mid-sentence in production. The fix: write to a word budget using the 150-words-per-minute rule (75 words ≈ 30 sec, 150 ≈ 60 sec). The script generator shows live word count and duration so you always hit the slot.

The 2-minute fix workflow

Run every script through this loop before recording: generate the draft with the script generator → check tone with the tone analyzer → sharpen the opener with the hook writer → close with the CTA builder. Two minutes of tooling saves hours of re-recording.
