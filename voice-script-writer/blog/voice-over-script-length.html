How Long Should a Voice-Over Script Be?

The 150-words-per-minute rule and exact word budgets for every format — so your script always fits its slot.

Home › Blog › Voice-Over Script Length

By the VoiceScriptWriter team · October 12, 2026 · 4-minute read

The most common production disaster in voice-over is simple: the script doesn't fit the slot. A 110-word script crammed into a 30-second ad gets cut mid-sentence — or rushed so fast it sounds like a disclaimer. The fix is one number every voice writer memorizes: 150 words per minute.

The 150 WPM rule

Professional US voice-over runs at roughly 150 words per minute at a natural conversational pace. Fast-paced retail ads push 160–170; calm IVR and narration sit at 130–140. For planning purposes, 150 is the standard — and it's the pace our script generator uses for its duration estimates.

Word budgets by format

  • 15-second spot: ~37 words. One hook + one CTA. Brutal editing required.
  • 30-second ad: ~75 words. Hook, one benefit, one proof point, CTA.
  • 60-second explainer: ~150 words. Problem → solution → how it works → CTA.
  • 90-second video: ~225 words. Room for a short story beat.
  • 2-minute narration: ~300 words. Full mini-arc with examples.
  • IVR greeting: 20–40 words at a slower pace (~130 wpm). Shorter is always better.
  • Podcast intro: 50–80 words (~20–30 sec). Hook + who this is for.
  • Audiobook chapter: ~9,000 words per finished hour (narration runs slightly slower than 150 wpm).

Three timing traps to avoid

1. Forgetting the music and pauses

A script timed at exactly 30.0 seconds of speech will overflow once you add the opening sting, the pause before the CTA, and the tag. Leave 2–3 seconds of headroom: write 70 words for a 30-second slot, not 75.

2. Numbers and URLs eat time

"Freshbrew dot com slash fall-deal" takes far longer to say than it looks on paper — about 3 seconds for what reads as two words. Spell out every URL and phone number in your draft, then time the spoken version.

3. Energy changes pace

An excited read runs 10–15% faster than a calm one. If your ad is high-energy, budget 160+ wpm; if it's a luxury brand whisper-read, budget 130. When in doubt, record a phone-voice memo of yourself reading it — that's your true timing.

The 60-second timing workflow

  1. Pick your slot and its word budget from the table above.
  2. Draft with the script generator — it counts words and estimates duration live.
  3. Read it aloud with a timer. Trim or expand to land within ±5% of target.
  4. Re-time after adding pauses, music cues, and spelled-out URLs.
