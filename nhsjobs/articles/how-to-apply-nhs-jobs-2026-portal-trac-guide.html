How to Apply for NHS Jobs in 2026

The complete guide to the NHS Jobs portal and the TRAC application system — supporting statements, values-based interviews and pre-employment checks explained.

Published 13 October 2026 · NAN Jobs Portal editorial team

The NHS is the UK's largest employer, with over 1.3 million staff in England alone — and it recruits year-round. Whether you are a newly qualified nurse, an experienced healthcare assistant, an allied health professional or an administrator looking for your first public sector role, the application route is the same: the official NHS Jobs portal (jobs.nhs.uk) and, for many trusts, the TRAC recruitment system. This guide explains both, step by step.

The Official Portals: NHS Jobs and TRAC

NHS Jobs is the national vacancy site for NHS roles in England and Wales. You can search by keyword, location, pay band, staff group and contract type, save searches, and set email alerts. When you click "Apply" on a vacancy, one of two things happens: you either complete the application on NHS Jobs itself, or you are redirected to the recruiting trust's TRAC site to fill in its application form. TRAC is simply the trust's recruitment software — the process and questions are the same, so do not be alarmed by the handover.

Verify before you apply: NHS jobs are only ever advertised on official channels. Never pay a fee to apply, and never share bank details during recruitment — genuine NHS recruiters will never ask for either.

Step-by-Step: Submitting Your NHS Application

Create your account. Register on NHS Jobs with your personal details, employment history and referees. Completing your profile once saves time on every future application.
Search and shortlist. Filter by pay band, staff group and location. Read the full job description and person specification — NHS adverts list essential and desirable criteria explicitly.
Write your supporting statement. This is the heart of your application: address every essential criterion with a concrete example. See the section below for how to structure it.
Complete the TRAC form if redirected. Some trusts ask additional questions on TRAC — answer in the same detailed, evidence-based style.
Submit before the closing date. NHS adverts frequently close early when application volumes are high. Aim to submit at least a week before the deadline.

Writing a Supporting Statement That Gets Shortlisted

Shortlisters score your statement against the person specification — often point by point. Structure it with one headed paragraph per essential criterion, and for each, give a brief example: the situation, what you did, and the outcome. Weave in the NHS values (working together for patients; respect and dignity; commitment to quality of care; compassion; improving lives; everyone counts) — values-based recruitment means panels explicitly look for evidence that you live these values.

  • Keep it focused: most trusts expect 500–1,500 words. Quality beats length.
  • Use clinical and professional terminology correctly — but avoid unexplained jargon if applying for non-clinical roles.
  • Quantify where possible: caseloads managed, waiting lists reduced, audits completed, training delivered.
  • Never copy the job description back at the panel; show evidence you meet each point.

NHS interviews are values-based: expect questions like "Tell us about a time you showed compassion in a difficult situation" alongside role-specific scenarios. Prepare examples that demonstrate both clinical or professional competence and the NHS values.

Eligibility Notes

You must have the right to work in the UK. Clinical roles require registration with the relevant professional body — the NMC for nurses and midwives, the HCPC for allied health professionals, the GMC for doctors — and your PIN must be valid before you can start. Overseas applicants should check the trust's sponsorship policy: many trusts sponsor Skilled Worker visas for shortage roles such as nursing, but sponsorship is never guaranteed and is stated in the advert.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the official NHS jobs website?

NHS Jobs (jobs.nhs.uk) is the official recruitment portal for NHS vacancies in England and Wales. Many trusts also use the TRAC recruitment system, which links from the NHS Jobs advert.

What is TRAC in NHS recruitment?

TRAC is the recruitment management system used by many NHS trusts. When you click apply on an NHS Jobs advert, you are often taken to the trust's TRAC site to complete the application form.

What is an NHS supporting statement?

The supporting statement is the most important part of an NHS application. It is where you demonstrate how you meet each point of the person specification, usually in 500 to 1,500 words.

Do NHS jobs require a DBS check?

Most clinical and patient-facing roles require an enhanced DBS check. Many admin and corporate roles require a standard check or none at all — the advert always states the requirement.

Can overseas nurses apply for NHS jobs?

Yes — many trusts recruit internationally for nursing and other shortage roles and can sponsor Skilled Worker visas. You must hold (or be eligible for) NMC registration and meet English language requirements.
