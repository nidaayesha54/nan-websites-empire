NHS Careers 2026: Nursing, Healthcare Assistant & Admin Roles

From Band 2 healthcare assistants to Band 7 ward managers — the NHS roles recruiting in 2026, how Agenda for Change pay bands work, and the entry routes into each.

Published 13 October 2026 · NAN Jobs Portal editorial team

The NHS recruits across more than 350 different careers — but three staff groups dominate vacancy boards: nursing, healthcare assistants and administration. Together they account for the majority of adverts on the NHS Jobs portal at any given time. This guide explains what each role involves, how the Agenda for Change pay bands map onto them, and how to get your foot in the door.

Pay note: exact Agenda for Change pay values change with annual pay awards. The band descriptions below are accurate for 2026; always check the advertised salary on the vacancy itself, and verify current band values on the NHS Employers website.

Agenda for Change: How NHS Pay Bands Work

Almost all NHS staff in England are paid under Agenda for Change, a national framework of bands 2 to 9. Your band reflects the knowledge, responsibility, skills and effort your role demands — not your job title alone. Each band has a pay range with annual progression points, plus enhancements for unsocial hours, overtime and on-call work. Clinical roles recruiting year-round cluster in Bands 2–7:

Nursing Careers in 2026

Nursing remains the NHS's biggest recruitment priority. Newly qualified nurses enter at Band 5 after completing an NMC-approved degree and registering with the Nursing and Midwifery Council. From there, common progression routes include:

  • Band 6 specialist/senior nurse — leading shifts, mentoring juniors, developing a specialty such as intensive care or oncology.
  • Band 7 ward manager or advanced practitioner — running wards or practising at an advanced clinical level with prescribing rights.
  • Alternative routes — nursing associate (Band 4) apprenticeships let healthcare assistants train while earning, and degree apprenticeships offer a debt-free path to registration.

International recruitment continues for nursing: many trusts sponsor Skilled Worker visas for overseas nurses who hold NMC registration and meet English language requirements.

Healthcare Assistant Roles: The Accessible Entry Point

Healthcare assistants (HCAs) provide hands-on patient care — washing and dressing patients, monitoring observations, supporting mealtimes and assisting nurses. No formal qualifications are required to start; trusts value care experience, compassion and reliability, and provide the Care Certificate training on the job. HCAs typically sit at Band 2, with senior HCAs at Band 3.

What makes HCA roles attractive is the progression ladder: many trusts actively support HCAs onto nursing associate apprenticeships (Band 4) and then registered nurse training — a genuine earn-while-you-learn route into a professional career.

NHS Admin and Corporate Roles

Behind every ward is an army of administrators. NHS admin vacancies include medical secretaries, outpatient booking coordinators, receptionists, ward clerks, data and performance analysts, and executive assistants — typically Bands 2 to 5 depending on responsibility. These roles suit organised communicators and often require no clinical background. Trusts also recruit corporate professionals in finance, HR, IT, estates and communications, many of which mirror private-sector salaries at Bands 6–8.

How to Find and Apply for These Roles

Search NHS Jobs. Use jobs.nhs.uk filters for staff group (nursing, admin, support services), pay band and location. Set up email alerts for your target bands.
Read the person specification. NHS adverts split criteria into essential and desirable — your supporting statement must evidence every essential point.
Write a values-based statement. Address each criterion with examples that demonstrate the NHS values: compassion, respect, teamwork and commitment to quality care.
Prepare for values-based interview. Expect scenario questions and, for clinical roles, practical assessments or presentations.
Complete pre-employment checks. Enhanced DBS for clinical roles, occupational health clearance, references and right-to-work verification.

Eligibility Notes

Right to work in the UK is mandatory for all posts. Clinical roles require current professional registration (NMC, HCPC or GMC as applicable) before your start date. For overseas applicants, check each advert's sponsorship statement — trusts sponsor visas mainly for shortage roles, and sponsorship policies differ between trusts.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are NHS Agenda for Change pay bands?

Agenda for Change is the NHS pay system, with bands from 2 to 9. Your band reflects the knowledge, responsibility and skills your role requires, and each band has a pay range with annual progression points.

What band is a healthcare assistant?

Healthcare assistants are typically Band 2, with senior healthcare assistants and support workers at Band 3. Specialist support roles can reach Band 4.

What band is a newly qualified nurse?

Newly qualified registered nurses start at Band 5. With experience, nurses progress to Band 6 (specialist/senior nurse) and Band 7 (ward manager or advanced practitioner).

Do you need qualifications to be a healthcare assistant?

No formal qualifications are required to start as a healthcare assistant, though care experience and the Care Certificate are valued. Many HCAs later train as nursing associates or registered nurses.

What admin jobs are available in the NHS?

NHS admin roles include medical secretaries, receptionists, booking coordinators, ward clerks, data analysts and executive assistants, typically at Bands 2 to 5 depending on responsibility.
