Nottingham Hospitals Welcome 49 New Midwives: NUH Maternity Recruitment Wave October 2026

Nottingham University Hospitals is welcoming 49 new midwives over six weeks in October 2026 — a major maternity recruitment boost across Nottingham City Hospital and Queen’s Medical Centre.

✅ Updated October 19, 2026
Home » NHS Jobs UK » Articles

Nottingham University Hospitals NHS Trust (NUH) is in the middle of one of the biggest maternity recruitment boosts of the autumn: 49 new midwives are joining its hospitals over six weeks — a mix of newly qualified and experienced staff, including international midwives — arriving in five cohorts, with the final cohort joining by Monday 21 October 2026.

49 New Midwives — Key Facts

DetailInformation

Note: Figures and quotes are as announced by NUH in mid-October 2026. Recruitment followed a drive earlier in the year — trusts advertise individual vacancies on jobs.nhs.uk, so check the portal for current midwifery openings.

The International Intake

The 49 joiners follow 18 international midwives who arrived earlier this year from Ghana, Iran, Hong Kong, South Africa and Zambia. One of them, Ivy Armah — a midwife for three years in her home country of Ghana before moving to Nottingham — said of the team: “Everybody wants to help when they can, and I am really enjoying it so far. We are like family and rely on each other. It’s wonderful. It feels like home.” International recruitment continues to be a major channel for NHS maternity staffing.

Retention Is Turning Around

The recruitment wave lands on improving retention: NUH’s maternity staff turnover rate has fallen from 13.41% in July 2023 to 8.45% in July 2024, and the trust is pushing retention further with projects like self-rostering and careers events. Welcoming the new cohort, Sharon Wallis, Director of Midwifery, said: “We are delighted to welcome 49 new midwives to the Trust in the next six weeks. We know they will be a brilliant addition to our very dedicated and compassionate maternity team and look forward to supporting them as they develop in their midwifery careers.”

What It Signals for Midwifery Jobs

The Nottingham wave reflects a national picture of active midwifery recruitment: trusts competing for newly qualified and experienced midwives, plus sustained international hiring. At the same time, the market is uneven — a petition reported in mid-October 2026 highlighted nearly 150 Welsh nurse and midwife graduates still without jobs, showing that getting hired depends heavily on where and how you apply. The government’s graduate guarantee programme and £8m conversion funding are aimed at bridging exactly that gap. For jobseekers, the lesson is to apply broadly across trusts — Nottingham’s 49 hires show the jobs exist where recruitment is well run.

The Bottom Line

49 new midwives, falling turnover, self-rostering and a warm welcome from the Director of Midwifery — Nottingham shows what good maternity recruitment looks like. If you are a midwife looking for your next role, October 2026 is a strong month to be applying.
