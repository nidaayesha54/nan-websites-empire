NHS Apprenticeships 2026-27: Healthcare Support Worker Routes — Earn While You Train

NHS apprenticeships let you earn a salary while training towards a healthcare career — from Level 2 healthcare support worker apprenticeships to degree-level routes. Here is how the 2026-27 routes work and where to find live vacancies.

✅ Updated October 16, 2026
Home » NHS Jobs UK » Articles

An NHS apprenticeship is one of the most accessible routes into a healthcare career: you earn a salary while you train, gain a nationally recognised qualification and build experience inside an NHS trust — with no tuition fees. In the 2026-27 cycle, trusts continue to advertise apprenticeships from Level 2 (healthcare support worker) right through to Level 6/7 degree-level routes such as nursing and allied health professions.

Apprenticeships suit school leavers, career changers and existing support staff alike — and many trusts ring-fence apprenticeship places for local communities, making them a genuine social-mobility route into the NHS.

Healthcare Support Worker Apprenticeships — The Entry Route

The Level 2/3 Healthcare Support Worker apprenticeship is the classic NHS starting point. You work as a paid member of a clinical team — on wards, in community teams or in specialist services — while completing your apprenticeship standard, which typically includes the Care Certificate and a vocational qualification. Most adverts ask for basic literacy and numeracy (GCSE English and Maths or equivalent), a caring attitude and the right to work in the UK; prior care experience is helpful but not always required.

Progression — Where Apprenticeships Lead

  • Level 2/3 HCSW → Senior HCA → Nursing Associate (Level 5) or Assistant Practitioner.
  • Degree apprenticeships (Level 6) can lead to registered nurse or allied health professional status — a full professional qualification earned while employed.
  • Non-clinical apprenticeships exist too — business administration, data, estates, pharmacy and IT routes all run across trusts.

How to Find NHS Apprenticeship Vacancies

NHS apprenticeships are advertised like any other NHS vacancy. Follow these steps:

1
Search jobs.nhs.uk for “apprentice” or “apprenticeship”, filtered by your region.
2
Check the apprenticeship level, duration, pay and entry requirements on each advert — levels and salaries differ between trusts.
3
Look at individual trust websites too: many run dedicated apprenticeship pages and recruit in seasonal cohorts.
4
Apply through the trust’s system (NHS Jobs or Trac) with a supporting statement focused on motivation for healthcare and the apprenticeship standard.

Why Apprenticeships Stand Out

Unlike a university route, an NHS apprenticeship pays you from day one and guarantees structured clinical experience — and unlike a standard HCA role, it comes with a funded qualification and a defined progression ladder. For anyone weighing healthcare as a career without wanting student debt, the 2026-27 apprenticeship cycle is worth serious attention: search jobs.nhs.uk for “apprentice” in your area and compare the levels on offer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an NHS apprenticeship?

A paid training route where you work in an NHS trust, earn a salary and gain a nationally recognised qualification — from Level 2 (GCSE standard) to Level 6/7 (degree level) — with no tuition fees.

What is the entry-level NHS apprenticeship?

The Level 2/3 Healthcare Support Worker apprenticeship: paid ward or community work plus the Care Certificate and a vocational qualification.

Do I need experience to apply for an NHS apprenticeship?

Most entry-level adverts ask for basic English and Maths and the right attitude rather than prior care experience — check each advert's entry criteria.

Where are NHS apprenticeships advertised?

On jobs.nhs.uk — search ‘apprentice’ or ‘apprenticeship’ — and on individual NHS trust websites, which often run dedicated apprenticeship pages.

Can an apprenticeship lead to nursing?

Yes — progression runs from HCSW to senior HCA, nursing associate and degree-level registered nurse or allied health professional apprenticeships.
