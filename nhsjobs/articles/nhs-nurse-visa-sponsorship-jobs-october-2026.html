The Role: Renal Nursing in the Community

This is renal nursing outside the hospital — care for dialysis patients in the community at the Peterborough unit. Rather than acute ward shifts, the role blends clinical dialysis work with patient education and ongoing care management. It is permanent and flexible (full-time or part-time), which makes it genuinely attractive: sponsorship-welcoming adverts on permanent community posts are exactly the combination overseas applicants are looking for.

What the Day-to-Day Involves

  • Assessing, planning, implementing and evaluating nursing care for dialysis patients.
  • Teaching patients to carry out home peritoneal dialysis and home haemodialysis — a key community-nursing skill.
  • Maintaining accurate patient records in line with Trust and NMC standards.
  • Responding to urgent dialysis situations, managing clinical risk calmly and safely.
  • Mentoring and supervising students and support staff on placement.
  • Multidisciplinary working with renal consultants, dietitians, pharmacists and community teams.
  • Infection control compliance in a dialysis environment, where standards are critical.
  • Working within the Trust’s lone-worker policy where community visits are involved.

Who Should Apply — Requirements

  • Current NMC registration is essential — you must already hold it. This is not a role that can wait for registration.
  • RGN (first or second level) — registered general nurse qualification.
  • Valid driving licence and access to a car — this is a community role, and you need to get to patients.
  • Renal or community nursing experience preferred — dialysis experience is a clear advantage but the advert lists it as preferred rather than essential.
  • Evidence of continuing professional development (CPD) — expected for post-registration roles.
  • Overseas applicants needing Skilled Worker sponsorship — explicitly welcome, provided the NMC and experience criteria are met.

The Health and Care Worker Visa Route — A Quick Primer

When an NHS advert says Skilled Worker sponsorship is welcome, it generally means the Trust is a licensed sponsor able to issue a Certificate of Sponsorship under the Health and Care Worker visa route (the health-sector pathway within the Skilled Worker system). In broad terms: eligible health roles with a sponsoring employer can be filled by overseas workers, with reduced application fees and no Immigration Skills Charge for the employer. But the route still has salary and eligibility thresholds, and — critically — you must meet the NMC registration requirement independently: sponsorship does not substitute for professional registration. Treat this section as general background only; the live advert and the Trust are the authority on this post’s sponsorship terms.
