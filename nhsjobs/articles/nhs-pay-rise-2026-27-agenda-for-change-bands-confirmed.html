NHS Pay Rise 2026/27 Confirmed: Agenda for Change Bands & Nurse Salaries

The 2026/27 NHS pay award is confirmed: a 3.3% rise for England and Wales, with Scotland securing 3.75% under a two-year deal. Here is the full Agenda for Change pay table — from Band 2 support workers to Band 9 directors.

Published 14 October 2026 · NAN Jobs Portal editorial team

The NHS pay rise for 2026/27 has been confirmed, giving Agenda for Change staff in England and Wales a 3.3% pay increase. The award lifts every pay band — meaning a newly qualified Band 5 staff nurse now starts on £32,073 a year, rising to £39,043 at the top of the band through progression.

Scotland did even better: Scottish NHS staff secured a 3.75% rise as part of a two-year deal, worth roughly £1,300–£3,400 more than English counterparts at equivalent bands. If you are weighing up where in the UK to work, Scotland's deal is worth factoring in.

What this means for you: every salary figure on NHS Jobs adverts for 2026/27 already reflects the new award. If you are comparing job offers, check which pay step point is offered — two Band 5 nurses can earn thousands apart depending on their position on the spine.

NHS Pay Bands 2026/27 — The Full Table (England & Wales)

Every NHS clinical and non-clinical role (except doctors, dentists and senior managers) is paid on the Agenda for Change (AfC) system, which classifies jobs into bands by skill, responsibility and qualifications. The confirmed 2026/27 scales:

How Pay Progression Works

Within each band you progress through pay step points based on service and appraisal — a Band 5 nurse gains around £6,970 moving from entry (£32,073) to the top (£39,043). Night, weekend and on-call enhancements, plus London weighting, can add thousands more.

Scotland vs England: The Pay Gap

Scotland's 3.75% two-year deal means Scottish NHS staff earn roughly £1,300–£3,400 more than English counterparts at equivalent bands (the 3.3% figure applies to Wales). Compare the full package — NHS pension, annual leave and sick pay — not just the headline salary.

What the Pay Rise Means for Jobseekers

Target the right band. Newly qualified nurses enter at Band 5 (£32,073); experienced nurses should look at Band 6 (£39,959–£48,117); support workers at Band 2–3.
Check the pay step. Adverts state the band and often the step — negotiate where the advert allows by evidencing equivalent experience.
Factor in enhancements. Night, weekend and on-call payments can add thousands to base salary in acute and community roles.
Compare nations. Scotland's 3.75% deal makes it the best-paid AfC nation this year — worth considering if you are relocating.
Apply through NHS Jobs. Every advert shows its Agenda for Change band and salary range — filter by band to find roles matching your target pay.
