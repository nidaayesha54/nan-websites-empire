7 Hidden Costs of Owning a Dog (Nobody Warns You) 💸

Everyone budgets for food and vet visits. But these seven sneaky costs catch new dog owners off guard:

1. Emergency Vet Visits ($800–$5,000)

The #1 budget-killer. Swallowed toys, allergic reactions, and mystery limps don't wait for payday. This alone is why pet insurance exists.

2. Pet Deposits & Rent ($200–$500+)

Renting? Many landlords charge a non-refundable pet deposit plus $25–$50/month "pet rent."

3. Training ($150–$600)

Group classes run $150–$300 for a 6-week course. Private training for behavior issues: $75–$150/hour.

4. Boarding & Pet Sitting ($40–$80/day)

One week of vacation = $280–$560 in boarding. It adds up fast for frequent travelers.

5. Dental Cleanings ($300–$800)

80% of dogs show dental disease by age 3. Professional cleanings under anesthesia aren't cheap — but extractions cost far more.

6. Replacing Chewed Stuff ($??? )

Shoes, remotes, couch corners. Puppy-proofing saves money, but budget $100–$300 for "oops" replacements in year one.

7. End-of-Life Care ($500–$2,000)

Nobody likes thinking about it, but senior diagnostics, medications, and final vet visits are real costs of loving a dog well.
