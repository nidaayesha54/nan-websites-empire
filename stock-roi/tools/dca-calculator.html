What is dollar-cost averaging?

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) means investing a fixed dollar amount on a regular schedule — say $500 every month — regardless of market prices. When prices are high you buy fewer shares; when they're low you buy more. Over time, this smooths your average purchase price and removes the stress of "timing the market."

The math behind the projection

This calculator compounds monthly: each contribution grows at 1/12th of your assumed annual rate for every remaining month. It's a simplified model — real markets zigzag — but it shows the power of consistency plus time.

DCA vs lump sum: which wins?

Research (including Vanguard's well-known study) finds lump-sum investing beats DCA about two-thirds of the time in rising markets — because markets rise more often than they fall. But DCA wins on psychology: it's easier to stay invested through volatility. Read our full DCA vs lump sum investing guide.

FAQ

Is dollar-cost averaging a good strategy? +
DCA reduces timing risk and builds discipline, but historically lump-sum investing wins about two-thirds of the time in rising markets. Read our DCA vs lump sum guide for the full comparison.
How much should I invest monthly? +
A common rule is 15–20% of income toward long-term investing, after an emergency fund. The right amount depends on your goals, debts, and timeline.
