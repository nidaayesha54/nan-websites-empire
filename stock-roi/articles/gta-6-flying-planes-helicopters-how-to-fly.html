GTA 6 Flying: Planes, Helicopters and How to Fly

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and Leonida is a state you can cross by road, by water — and by air. Kotaku's review of the released game says Jason and Lucia can drive boats, planes, helicopters, dirt bikes, jet skis and more, and IGN's review adds a detail that tells you how open the flying is: you can still land a jumbo jet on the beach, though now you have to make your own way to the cockpit instead of appearing in it. This guide covers how flying works, the controls, the missions that teach it, and the honest limits of what is known so far.

Flying is not a side feature in GTA 6. The map is roughly twice the size of the one in GTA 5 by Rockstar's own description, and the fastest way to cross it is above it. The game gives you the aircraft early — a seaplane in Chapter 1 — and then leaves the rest of the sky for you to find.

The controls: throttle, pitch and yaw

The flying controls on PlayStation 5 are the same shape as the driving controls, with the throttle on the triggers. Hold R2 to throttle up and L2 to throttle down, steer pitch and roll with the left stick, and press Square to yaw left and Circle to yaw right. Press L3 to raise or lower the undercarriage, Triangle to exit the aircraft, and R1 to shoot if the aircraft is armed. The right stick looks around, and the D-pad brings up the phone or the objective marker, just as it does on foot.

For helicopters the same triggers do the climbing: hold R2 to climb and L2 to descend, with Square and Circle turning the nose. The useful trick, reported by players on launch day, is that releasing all the controls makes a helicopter hover — so if you are lining up a landing and everything feels too fast, let go for a second and let the aircraft settle. Landing is the hard part: pick a flat area with no obstacles, descend gently, and do not rush the last few feet. A plane that survives the flight and dies on the runway still fails the mission.

Clear Skies: the mission that teaches you to fly

The flying tutorial is not a tutorial at all — it is a story mission, and it is one of the best in Chapter 1. Clear Skies, the fifteenth story mission, sends Jason to Donnie at the Mile Zero Air Station, where Donnie is tinkering with a seaplane. Brian, at the bar up the street, needs someone to watch over Donnie on a job: escort a plane carrying Pilot Pete, and keep it off the radar. The trick is to fly directly above Pete's plane, hiding its radar signature under your own.

You take off from the water — taxi the seaplane off the road into the water first, because a take-off from the road works but is riskier — fly to the intercept point in the ocean to the southeast, catch up with Pete, and escort him to a landing strip in the swamps. Then Donnie wants a detour to a second airstrip in the northwest of the swamps, where his deal with The Final Chapter, a biker gang, goes wrong and Jason has to fight through the airstrip buildings to free him. It is a flying lesson, an escort mission and a shootout in one, and it is the moment the sky opens up.

Getting a helicopter early

Outside missions, the earliest aircraft players have reported is a helicopter on a private helipad east of the Hamlet region, close to Key Lento, where the game starts. GameRant's launch-day guide describes the spot: a compound with private security at a checkpoint gate, and a chain-link fence on the side you can climb to get in without being spotted. Inside, a helicopter sits on the pad, unlocked — walk up, climb in, and fly off, and the guide reports that flying away does not alert the security or trigger a wanted level.

Treat that as reported, not guaranteed: it comes from one outlet's launch-day playthrough, and the game may behave differently for you. But it is the best early flying spot anyone has published, and it is close enough to the starting area to reach in the first hours. The same guide notes the helicopter is the fastest way to clear the map fog and explore Leonida from above — and with a map this size, that matters more than it sounds.

What is still unconfirmed

Here is where the honest limits sit. Rockstar has not said how you get a plane or a helicopter outside missions, and no reliable report we have seen describes a shop, a price or an ownership system for aircraft. No flight school has been announced, and nothing about one has been reported in the released game. Whether aircraft can be stolen freely, bought, or only used in missions is unconfirmed — the GameRant helicopter is a reported steal, not a confirmed system, and the seaplane in Clear Skies is a mission vehicle, not a purchase.

What is confirmed is smaller and more useful: aircraft exist in the world, Rockstar's own images show a jet over a Vice City sign, a seaplane over the Keys and a news helicopter over Mount Kalaga, and the reviews confirm you can fly them. The rest — prices, hangars, ownership — is still unknown, and any guide that lists a plane shop or a fixed price is guessing. Fly what the missions give you, grab the reported helicopter if you want it early, and wait for the rest to be confirmed by players.

FAQ

Can you fly planes in GTA 6? Yes. The Clear Skies mission in Chapter 1 has you flying Donnie's seaplane, and reviews confirm planes and helicopters are flyable. Hold R2 to throttle up, L2 to slow down, and steer with the left stick.

Is there a flight school in GTA 6? Nothing about one has been announced, and no reliable report describes one in the released game.

Can you buy a plane or helicopter in GTA 6? No price, shop or ownership system for aircraft has been announced. The earliest reported helicopter is a steal from a private helipad east of Hamlet, near Key Lento.

The bottom line: GTA 6 lets you fly from Chapter 1, the controls are simple once you learn the throttle, and the sky is the fastest way across a state twice the size of GTA 5's map. Learn Clear Skies, grab the early helicopter if you want it, and do not pay anyone for a plane that does not exist yet.
