How to Read Soccer Odds in 2026

Guide · Updated 2026 · Educational

Soccer odds look confusing at first, but they all answer one question: how much do I win if I'm right, and how likely does the bookmaker think it is? In the US you'll mostly see American odds; internationally, decimal odds dominate; the UK still uses fractional odds. They're the same prices in different costumes — use our odds converter to switch instantly.

American odds: −150 vs +200

American odds center on $100. A minus number is the favorite; a plus number is the underdog.

  • −150: risk $150 to win $100. The team is favored.
  • +200: risk $100 to win $200. The team is the underdog.
  • +100 / −100: even money — $100 wins $100.

Soccer is a three-way market (win, draw, away win), so you'll see a trio like: Home −120 / Draw +240 / Away +320.

Decimal odds: 1.50, 2.00, 3.00

Decimal odds show the total return per $1 staked, stake included. Multiply: $20 × 2.50 = $50 back ($30 profit). The math is simplest here, which is why calculators use decimal format internally.

Fractional odds: 1/2, 2/1, 9/4

Fractional odds show profit relative to stake: 2/1 means $2 profit per $1 staked. Add your stake back for the total return (2/1 on $10 = $20 profit + $10 stake = $30).

Implied probability: what the odds suggest

Every odds format implies a probability: 1 ÷ decimal odds. Decimal 2.00 implies 50%. But beware the overround: bookmakers build in a margin, so the implied probabilities of all outcomes in a match sum to more than 100%. Odds are prices with profit baked in — not predictions.

Soccer-specific wrinkles

  • 3-way moneyline: bets settle on the 90-minute result (extra time excluded unless stated).
  • Draw no bet: your stake is refunded if the match is a draw — lower odds, lower risk.
  • Double chance: covers two of three outcomes (e.g. home win or draw) at short odds.
