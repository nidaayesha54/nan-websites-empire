How Landlord Insurance Claims Work

When a pipe bursts or a tenant calls about fire damage at 2 a.m., the difference between a smooth $30,000 payout and a denied claim comes down to what you do in the first 48 hours. Here's exactly how landlord insurance claims work — the process, realistic timelines, and the seven mistakes that get claims denied.

Step 1: Mitigate Further Damage (Immediately)

Your policy requires you to prevent additional damage. Shut off the water, board up the broken window, call the emergency plumber. Keep every receipt — emergency mitigation costs are reimbursable. What you must not do is start permanent repairs before the adjuster sees the damage.

Step 2: Document Everything (First 24 Hours)

Photograph and video everything before anything is moved or cleaned: wide shots of each room, close-ups of damage, the source of the problem (burst pipe, burn pattern). Save damaged materials. Note dates and times. Good documentation is the single biggest factor in how fast a claim settles — adjusters trust photos more than descriptions.

Step 3: File the Claim (Within Days)

Call your agent or the carrier's claims line with your policy number, the date of loss, and a brief description. You'll get a claim number immediately. For large losses, ask about advance payments — many carriers will release funds for emergency repairs and temporary tenant relocation before the full settlement.

Step 4: The Adjuster Inspection (Days to Weeks)

A field adjuster visits, typically within 3–10 business days for standard claims. Walk them through the damage, hand over your photos, and provide contractor estimates. Pro tip: get two independent contractor estimates before the visit — it anchors the negotiation and speeds approval.

Step 5: Settlement and Repairs

For straightforward claims (burst pipes, storm damage under $25K), expect settlement in 2–6 weeks. Complex claims — fires, liability disputes — can take 2–6 months. Replacement-cost policies pay the depreciated value first, then release the remaining recoverable depreciation after you complete repairs and submit receipts. This is normal; budget for it.

Loss of Rent Claims: The Part Landlords Miss

If damage makes the unit uninhabitable, file for loss of rental income in the same claim — many landlords forget. You'll need the lease, rent payment records, and evidence the unit was occupied or actively marketed. Coverage typically pays your monthly rent for up to 12 months while repairs proceed. It does not cover rent lost to tenant non-payment or normal vacancy.

7 Mistakes That Get Landlord Claims Denied

  1. Waiting too long to file. Late reporting gives the carrier an excuse — file within days.
  2. Throwing away damaged materials before the adjuster inspects.
  3. Not mitigating damage — letting a leak run for a week looks like negligence.
  4. Filing for maintenance issues — worn roofs and old plumbing aren't covered perils; filing anyway burns your claims history.
  5. Under-insuring the dwelling — below ~80% of rebuild cost triggers coinsurance penalties that shrink every payout.
  6. Vacancy violations — damage during a 60+ day vacancy may be excluded; notify your agent during turnovers.
  7. Filing tiny claims. A $1,200 claim after a $1,000 deductible nets $200 — and raises your premiums for years. Pay small losses out of pocket.

When to Hire a Public Adjuster

For claims above ~$50,000 or any denied claim you believe is valid, a licensed public adjuster (who works for you, not the carrier) typically charges 5–10% of the settlement but often recovers significantly more than the carrier's first offer. For small claims, they're not worth the fee.

Bottom Line

Fast mitigation, thorough photos, quick filing, and contractor estimates in hand — that's the entire playbook. Before your next renewal, verify your limits still match rebuild costs with the coverage calculator, check what you're paying with the quote estimator, and review what your policy actually covers so there are no surprises at claim time.
