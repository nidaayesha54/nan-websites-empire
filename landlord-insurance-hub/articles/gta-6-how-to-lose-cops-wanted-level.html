How to Lose the Cops in GTA 6: Wanted Level Guide

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and its wanted system is the biggest change to police chases since the series began. The old rule — outrun the circle and the stars fade — is gone. In GTA 6, the police remember what they know about you: your clothes, your car, your weapon, whether you were seen as a couple, and whether a camera caught you. Escaping is no longer about speed. It is about making yourself unrecognisable.

The change is easy to miss if you play the way you played GTA 5. Driving fast in the same car, in the same clothes, through the same area, is the worst thing you can do — because the police are not chasing your position. They are chasing your description.

How You Get a Wanted Level: Someone Has to See It

The first rule is that a crime has to be witnessed or reported before the police respond. Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson put it plainly: the wanted system requires that someone witness the crime, or that an alarm goes off. A witness pulls out a phone and calls it in. An alarm goes off during a robbery. A stolen vehicle with an advanced tracker reports itself. No witness, no alarm, no tracker — no police.

That means the seconds before a crime matter as much as the seconds after it. A quiet street with no one watching is a different place from a crowded boulevard at noon. Leave the scene before officers arrive, if they have not identified you, and you may avoid the chase entirely. The best escape is the one that never starts.

Six Stars, and What They Mean

GTA 6 brings back a six-star wanted level — up from GTA 5's five — and the look of the stars tells you what the police are doing. Hollow stars mean the police are responding to a reported crime without identifying the suspect: they do not know who you are, and that is your best chance to leave before becoming a direct target. Solid white stars mean the suspect is identified and in sight: officers are actively pursuing you. Flashing white stars mean officers have lost sight of you and are searching around your last known position. Red stars mean the pursuit has ended, but a hot area remains — the police still know what you look like, and can recognise you if you return.

That red state is the new part. Losing the cops does not wipe the slate clean: the police keep information about the crime and use it to recognise you around your last known location. You have escaped the chase, but not the heat.

Heat: What the Police Know About You

Below the stars, red icons show exactly what the police know: your description, your clothing, your vehicle, your weapon, whether Jason and Lucia were identified as a pair, and whether CCTV caught you. Those icons are a checklist, and clearing them is how you escape. A clothing change addresses the outfit lead — but it does not replace an identified car, or separate a pair of suspects. Each lead has to be dealt with on its own.

The fixes are practical. Change your clothes — spare outfits can be stored in your car trunk, so you can change without visiting a shop — and change your hairstyle at a salon or barber to clear the appearance icon. Put on a mask before a crime and take it off once you are out of sight: the police are looking for a masked person, so removing it lowers how well they recognise you. Drop the weapon. And if the two-person icon shows the police are looking for Jason and Lucia together, split up — it lowers how well they can place you.

Breaking Line of Sight and Leaving the Heat Area

Once the chase is on, first break line of sight: get around corners, into alleys, under cover, anywhere officers cannot see you. Use Focus to highlight nearby officers before moving between hiding places. In a stationary vehicle, hold Circle on PS5 or B on Xbox to duck down and hide — even if you jumped in from on foot. In rural areas, look for woodland and bushes.

Second, leave the heat area. Nelson told one interviewer that once you leave the area the police are searching, "the pursuing police disappear" — but inside it, the heat will not go away. Third, ditch or swap the car: the police know what you were driving, and jumping into a new vehicle means they will not know you are in it. If the car you stole has an advanced tracker, keep it moving fast or abandon it, because it pings your position when you slow down. In the story, Jason, Lucia and Raul abandon and burn their getaway car before switching to a van — the system, demonstrated.

What Not to Do

Do not turn a manageable pursuit into a firefight. Shooting your way through responding officers adds violence while keeping you exposed, and it escalates the response. Shooting out cop car tires slows a pursuit, and helicopters may retreat if shot at — but the goal is to disappear, not to win a battle. At three stars or more in the city, tight streets and dense traffic make car chases brutal, and ducking into alleys on foot is often smarter than driving.

Watch for K9 units: if dogs are called in, they track you relentlessly, so keep moving. And do not expect a cheat to save you — none of GTA 6's cheats lowers or clears your wanted level, and the two that touch the police, Raise Heat Level and Raise Wanted Level, do the opposite. Cheats only work after you finish the story anyway. There is no shortcut out of a wanted level, only the escape done properly.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I lose the cops fast in GTA 6?

Break line of sight, leave the heat area, ditch or swap your car, change your clothes from your trunk, take off your mask, and split Jason and Lucia up if you were seen together. Do those while out of sight and the search collapses. Hiding in the same area is not enough — you have to leave it.

What do the red icons under the stars mean?

They show what the police know about you: your appearance, clothes, vehicle, weapon, whether you were seen as a couple, and whether CCTV caught you. Clear each one — change clothes, swap cars, split up, drop the weapon — and they lose the ability to recognise you.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6's wanted system is about what the police know, not just where you are: six stars, a crime that has to be witnessed, and a heat system that remembers your clothes, your car and your partner. Break line of sight, leave the heat area, change your look, swap your car, split up — and do not shoot your way out. Escape is a process of becoming unrecognisable, and the players who learn it stop getting caught.
