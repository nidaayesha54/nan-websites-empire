How to Train a Puppy: Complete 2026 Guide

By NAN Marketing Agency · Updated October 12, 2026 · 9 min read

Bringing home a puppy is pure joy — and pure chaos. Those tiny teeth, the 3 AM potty runs, the chewed shoes. Here's the good news: puppies are learning machines, and with the right approach, you can shape a polite, confident adult dog in just a few months. This complete guide walks you through exactly how to train a puppy in 2026, from the first day home through six months old.

Forget outdated "dominance" theories. Modern dog training is built on positive reinforcement: rewarding the behaviors you want so your puppy chooses them again. It's faster, kinder, and backed by decades of behavioral science. Let's build your puppy's foundation.

1. When to Start Training Your Puppy

Start the day your puppy comes home — usually around 8 weeks old. You don't need formal "obedience classes" on day one. Training at this age means short, fun games: rewarding eye contact, teaching their name, and rewarding calm behavior. Puppies have a critical socialization window that closes around 14 weeks, so the earliest weeks matter enormously. Every positive experience now — new sounds, surfaces, gentle people, vaccinated dogs — builds a confident adult. Waiting until "they're old enough" wastes the most impressionable period of your dog's life.

💡 Golden rule: Keep sessions to 3–5 minutes, several times a day. End every session with a win — even an easy one — so your puppy always thinks training is the best game ever.

2. The 5 Foundations Every Puppy Needs

Before fancy tricks, nail these five foundations. They prevent 90% of the behavior problems owners complain about later.

Foundation 1: Name recognition

Say your puppy's name once. The instant they look at you, mark it ("Yes!") and deliver a treat. Repeat 10 times, twice a day. Within days, their name becomes the best sound in the world — the basis of all future recall.

Foundation 2: A marker word

A marker ("Yes!" or a clicker) tells your puppy exactly which behavior earned the reward. Charge it first: say the word, then treat, 20 times. Timing matters — you must mark within 3 seconds of the behavior you like.

Foundation 3: Socialization

Aim for 5 positive new experiences daily: umbrellas opening, vacuum cleaners at a distance, kids on bikes, different floor textures, car rides. Keep each exposure short and pair it with treats. A scared puppy should never be forced closer — let them observe from a comfortable distance.

Foundation 4: Potty training routine

Take your puppy out after waking, eating, playing, and napping — plus every 1–2 hours. Reward outdoor success immediately. Our free potty schedule generator builds a custom timetable from your puppy's age. Never punish accidents; they only teach puppies to hide them.

Foundation 5: Bite inhibition

Puppy teeth hurt! When teeth touch skin, end play for 20 seconds (a "reverse time-out") or redirect to a toy. Consistent, calm consequences teach gentle mouths far better than yelling ever could.

3. Your First 8 Weeks: Week-by-Week Roadmap

Weeks 1–2 (settling in): Focus on name, marker word, potty routine, and crate introduction. Let your puppy explore the house on leash. Start handling exercises: touch paws, ears, and mouth daily with treats — future vet visits will thank you.

Weeks 3–4 (first cues): Teach Sit (lure with a treat from nose up over the head) and start Down. Begin "leave it" with a treat in a closed fist. Continue socialization outings — carry your puppy in public places before vaccinations are complete.

Weeks 5–6 (leash & manners): Introduce the leash indoors with treats. Teach "touch" (nose to palm) — it's the foundation of recall. Start polite greeting practice: four paws on the floor earns attention; jumping earns a turned back.

Weeks 7–8 (real world): Practice cues in new locations with mild distractions. Begin recall games indoors with a partner. Introduce "place" (go to a mat). By now your puppy should offer sits automatically — reward every single one.

4. The First 5 Commands to Teach

Teach in this order — each builds on the last:

  1. Sit — the default polite behavior. Lure, mark, reward. Use it before meals, doors, and greetings.
  2. Down — from a sit, lure toward the floor between the paws. Harder for excitable puppies; be patient.
  3. Come — start as a game: two people calling back and forth with jackpot treats. Never call your puppy to punish them.
  4. Leave it — impulse control that could save their life around dropped food or trash.
  5. Stay — build duration slowly: 3 seconds, then 5, then 10. Release with a clear word like "OK!"

5. Common Puppy Training Mistakes

Mistake 1: Sessions too long. A bored puppy isn't stubborn — they're done. Quit while you're ahead.

Mistake 2: Repeating cues. Saying "sit, sit, SIT!" teaches your puppy to ignore the first two. Say it once, then help them succeed with a lure.

Mistake 3: Training only in the kitchen. Dogs don't generalize well. Practice every cue in at least 5 locations before calling it learned.

Mistake 4: Accidental reward of bad behavior. Laughing at jumping, chasing a puppy who stole a sock — all of it rewards the behavior. Manage the environment instead.

Mistake 5: Skipping naps. Puppies need 18–20 hours of sleep. An overtired puppy is a land-shark. Enforce crate naps — see our crate training guide.

⚠️ Avoid: alpha rolls, scruff shakes, spray bottles, and shock collars. Aversive methods suppress behavior through fear and damage trust — and they can create aggression. Positive reinforcement gets better results without the fallout.

6. When to Get Professional Help

Most puppy issues resolve with consistency, but call a certified professional dog trainer (look for CPDT-KA or IAABC credentials) if you see: resource guarding with growling over food or toys, intense fear that doesn't improve, separation distress (drooling, destruction, escape attempts), or any aggression. The earlier you intervene, the easier the fix. For barking problems specifically, see our guide on how to stop a dog from barking.

Remember: puppyhood is short, but the habits you build now last a lifetime. Train in short happy bursts, reward generously, and enjoy the process — you're not just teaching commands, you're building a relationship. 🐾
