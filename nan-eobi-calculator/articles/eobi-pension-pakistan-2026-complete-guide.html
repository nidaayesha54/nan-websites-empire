EOBI Pension in Pakistan 2026: Complete Guide — Eligibility, Formula & How to Check

Everything a Pakistani private-sector worker needs to know about the Employees' Old-Age Benefits Institution in 2026: the exact formula, the new Rs 11,500 minimum, the landmark 14.5-year rule, and how to check your own record online.

What EOBI is and who it covers

The Employees' Old-Age Benefits Institution (EOBI) is Pakistan's federal pension scheme for private-sector workers. If you work for a registered private employer, your employer contributes 5% of the minimum wage every month and you contribute 1%. In return, you earn the right to a monthly pension for life once you retire — plus invalidity and survivors' benefits for your family.

EOBI is not the same as a provident fund or gratuity. Those are one-time payments from your employer. EOBI is a lifetime monthly pension paid by the institution, and it continues for your spouse after your death as a survivors' pension. That makes it one of the most valuable benefits a private-sector worker in Pakistan can have — and one of the least understood.

Coverage applies to employees of registered establishments. The catch that trips up thousands of workers: only insured employment counts. If your employer never registered with EOBI or never actually paid the monthly contributions, those years don't exist in EOBI's records — no matter what your appointment letter says.

The EOBI pension formula, explained with examples

The official formula is refreshingly simple:

Monthly Pension = (Average Monthly Wage × Years of Insured Service) ÷ 50

In plain words: every year of insured service adds 2% of your average monthly wage (calculated on the last 12 months) to your pension. If the formula result is below the official minimum, you receive the minimum instead.

Worked examples (2026)

Average wage Rs 40,000 × 25 years ÷ 50Rs 20,000/month
Average wage Rs 30,000 × 15 years ÷ 50 = Rs 9,000Rs 11,500/month (minimum applies)
Average wage Rs 60,000 × 30 years ÷ 50Rs 36,000/month
Average wage Rs 25,000 × 20 years ÷ 50 = Rs 10,000Rs 11,500/month (minimum applies)

Notice the pattern: the minimum pension protects low-wage and short-service workers, while long-serving workers with decent wages get a genuinely meaningful monthly amount. Try your own numbers on our EOBI pension calculator — it applies the minimum automatically.

What changed in 2026

2026 brought the biggest EOBI overhaul in years, approved by the federal cabinet:

  • Minimum pension raised to Rs 11,500/month (up from Rs 10,000). Effective 1 January 2026, with arrears paid out in September 2026.
  • 15% increase on formula-based pensions. If your pension was calculated by the formula above the minimum, it rises by 15%.
  • The landmark 14.5-year rule. A court ruling established that 14 years and 6 months of insured service now legally counts as 15 years. Previously, even 14 years and 11 months meant rejection. Thousands of previously rejected applicants can now re-apply.
  • Long-serving pensioners (20+ years) may now receive over Rs 30,000 per month.
  • Pensions are credited between the 1st and 5th of every month, mostly through partner banks such as Bank Alfalah.

The federal cabinet has also formed a committee to explore extending EOBI coverage to informal-sector, domestic, and agricultural workers — a change that would be historic if implemented, though it is still at the discussion stage.

Eligibility: age, service and contributions

To draw the old-age pension you must satisfy all three conditions:

  • Age: 60 for men, 55 for women (verify your exact age). Mine workers qualify at 55 with at least 10 years of mining service.
  • Service: at least 15 years of insured contributions — now satisfied by 14.5 years under the rounding-off rule.
  • Contributions actually paid: your employer must have remitted the monthly contributions. Unpaid years don't count.

Invalidity pension

If you become permanently disabled during employment, you can qualify after only 5 years of insured service, subject to medical board approval. This is one of EOBI's most important but least claimed benefits.

Survivors' pension

When a pensioner dies, the spouse continues to receive the full pension — even after remarriage in some interpretations — followed by minor children and then parents. Your EOBI pension protects your family, not just you.

The old-age grant (under 15 years of service)

Workers with 2 to under 15 years of contributions don't get a monthly pension, but they aren't left empty-handed. They receive a one-time old-age grant equal to one month's average wage for each year of service. Example: 8 years of service at an average wage of Rs 35,000 = a one-time grant of Rs 280,000.

Important: if you are close to 15 years — say 13 or 14 — check whether your employer can regularize missing contributions. Crossing the 15-year line (or 14.5 under the new rule) converts a one-time grant into a lifetime monthly pension, which is worth far more.

How to check your EOBI record online

Do this years before you retire, not after. Missing years are far easier to fix while you're still employed.

  1. Visit the official EOBI website at eobi.gov.pk.
  2. Open "Insured Person Tracking" or "Individual Information".
  3. Enter your 13-digit CNIC number without dashes.
  4. Review your contribution history year by year.

If years are missing, take your salary slips and employment records to your employer's HR first, then to the EOBI facilitation center if needed. Employers are legally required to pay these contributions — the record is your proof.

Biometric verification: keeping your pension active

To prevent fraud, EOBI requires pensioners to complete proof-of-life biometric verification twice a year, typically in March and September. You can do it at any Bank Alfalah branch or an authorized HBL Konnect agent, carrying your original CNIC and EOBI pension card.

From 2026, NADRA also offers facial verification for pensioners whose fingerprints don't match — available at NADRA registration centers. Missing verification suspends your pension, so treat those two windows as non-negotiable appointments.

Common problems and how to fix them

"My employer deducted EOBI but never paid it." This is depressingly common. Your CNIC record on eobi.gov.pk will show the gap. Raise it with HR in writing; if unresolved, file a complaint with EOBI — the institution can recover dues from the employer.

"My application was rejected for short service." If you have 14.5+ years, re-apply under the 2026 rounding-off rule. Many rejections issued under the old strict 15-year interpretation are now reversible.

"My pension stopped." Nine times out of ten, it's a missed biometric verification. Complete it and payments resume — but don't wait months, because arrears paperwork gets slower.

"I worked at several companies." Your insured service is cumulative across all registered employers, as long as each one paid contributions. The CNIC record consolidates everything.

How to maximize your pension

  • Verify your record annually on eobi.gov.pk — catch missing contributions while you can still fix them.
  • Don't change jobs right before the 15-year mark without confirming the new employer is EOBI-registered.
  • Keep salary slips and appointment letters forever. They are your evidence if records go missing.
  • Understand the minimum. If your formula pension is near Rs 11,500, extra service years still help — but the biggest gains come from a higher recorded wage, so make sure your registered wage reflects reality — model different salary scenarios with our free salary tools.
  • Plan for the family. Ensure your spouse knows about the survivors' pension and where your EOBI documents are.
