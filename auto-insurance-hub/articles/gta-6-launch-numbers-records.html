GTA 6 Launch Numbers: The Biggest Game Launch Ever

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 is the biggest game launch in history — and the numbers prove it. GTA 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, thirteen years after GTA 5, and every metric that can be measured broke records: review views, search volume, video views, and the sheer number of people trying to play at the same time. This guide collects the verified launch numbers in one place — the review that everyone watched, the searches that show how big the demand was, and the midnight unlock that crashed a network — so you can see exactly how big this launch was.

Start with the search numbers, because they show the demand before a single copy was sold. "GTA 6" drew 53.8 million searches a month, up 509% — fifty-three million people a month looking for one game. "GTA 6 gameplay" hit 10.4 million searches a month, up 917%. "GTA 6 secrets" grew 1,700% in a single measurement period. "GTA 6 map" reached 879,000 searches a month, up 369%. Even the niche queries were enormous: "how to play GTA 6 on PC" at 243,000 a month, "how to play GTA 6 early" at 230,000, "how to make money with GTA 6" at 245,000. Thirteen years of waiting created a demand no game had ever seen.

The Review Everyone Watched

The single most-watched piece of GTA 6 content on launch day was IGN's review. "Grand Theft Auto 6 (GTA 6) Review" passed 7.44 million views, at 260,551 views per hour — a quarter of a million people an hour watching one review. It called the game a masterpiece, and the praise it carried — the jaw-dropping night lighting, the deep car culture, the nuanced wanted system, the dual leads Jason and Lucia — set the tone for everything that followed. When a review is watched 7.44 million times in a day, it is not just a review. It is the launch.

The other long-form videos were enormous too. GamingBolt's "GTA 6 Review" passed 937,000 views at 32,790 views per hour. TechyCrew's "WE PLAYED GTA 6!" reached 828,000 views. BeastBoyShub's "FINALLY PLAYING GTA 6" hit 441,000 views at 78,494 views per hour — the highest per-hour rate of any long-form video that day after IGN itself. And ohnepixel's raw "GTA 6 IS INSANE..." reached 382,000 views. The launch was not one video. It was every video, all at once, all enormous.

The Shorts That Dominated

The short-form numbers were just as extreme. GelNox's 8-second "GTA 6" Shorts dominated the format: one reached 898,000 views at 32,277 views per hour, another 239,000, another 154,000. Small channels won too — True Glow Scope, with only 10,100 subscribers, reached 25,700 views on a "GTA 6 Vacation" Short. The format worked for everyone because the demand was so large that every corner of it had an audience. Eight seconds of neon Vice City was enough to find tens of thousands of viewers.

That is also why the clip economy exploded the way it did. "How to clip GTA 6" drew 191,000 searches a month, up 9,361% — the fastest-growing search in the whole launch. "How to make GTA 6 clips" drew 57,000 a month. Everyone wanted to make clips, and everyone wanted to watch them. The honest ones — AI-generated concepts, clearly labelled, paired with real information — are the ones that last. The dishonest ones, the stream rips and the fake gameplay, are the ones that get channels removed.

The Midnight Unlock That Crashed a Network

The demand was so large that it broke things. At the midnight ET unlock, PlayStation Network went down: Downdetector spiked past 8,000 US reports, Sony's status page confirmed the outage, and it was marked resolved about twenty minutes later. The same thing had happened on November 18, and it was resolved the same day. The outage was not a GTA 6 bug — it was a network overwhelmed by millions of people trying to sign in at the same moment. The fix was to wait it out, check Sony's status page, and restart the console fully.

The download numbers tell the same story. The game is a 212 GB install, and the preload was one of the most-searched topics of the launch: "how to download GTA 6" at 46,000 searches a month, "how to preload GTA 6" at 29,000, "how to redeem a GTA 6 code on PS5" at 42,000. The most common launch-night "problem" was not a problem at all: the game shows a padlock until midnight local time in your store region, and players who deleted the 212 GB install to "fix" it only made their night worse. The padlock was the countdown, not a bug.

Thirteen Years in the Making

The scale of the launch is the scale of the wait. Thirteen years passed between GTA 5 and GTA 6 — the longest gap in the series, and the longest wait in modern gaming. In those thirteen years, the audience grew up, the platforms changed twice, and the game became a legend before it existed. The launch numbers are the measurement of that legend: 53.8 million searches a month, 7.44 million views on one review, 8,000 outage reports in a single hour, and a 212 GB download that millions of people started on the same night.

The game itself is built to be worth the wait. IGN's review praised the night lighting — headlights piercing the dark, city lights from above, beach storms rolling in — the deep car culture with custom neons and engine swaps, the wanted system in which the police act only on what they witness, and the dual leads Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos in a story about escape rather than empire-building. The numbers are the launch. The game is the reason the numbers exist.
