How Much Car Insurance Do I Need?

2026-10-12 · 7 min read

State minimums are legal minimums — not adequate protection. Here's how to pick limits that actually protect your assets.

Start with the numbers: 100/300/100

The widely recommended baseline is 100/300/100: $100k bodily injury per person, $300k per accident, $100k property damage. State minimums (often 25/50/25 or lower) can be exhausted by a single ER visit — after that, your assets pay the rest.

Liability: buy more than the minimum

One serious at-fault accident can easily cause $200k+ in medical bills across multiple people. If your net worth (home equity, savings, future wages) exceeds your limits, you're exposed. Match liability limits to what you have to protect.

When do you need full coverage?

  • Loan or lease: lenders require collision + comprehensive — non-negotiable.
  • Car worth $8k+: full coverage usually still pencils out.
  • Car under ~$4k: run our break-even tool — liability-only plus a repair fund often wins.

Don't forget uninsured motorist coverage

About 1 in 8 US drivers is uninsured. Uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM/UIM) coverage is cheap and protects you when the at-fault driver can't pay. Match it to your liability limits.

Consider an umbrella policy

If you have significant assets, a $1M umbrella policy typically costs just $150–$350/year and kicks in when auto (or home) liability limits run out. It's the cheapest asset protection in insurance.

Note: Review limits yearly: a raise, a new home, or a growing family all raise the amount worth protecting.
