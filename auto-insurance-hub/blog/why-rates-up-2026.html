Why Did My Car Insurance Go Up in 2026?

2026-10-12 · 6 min read

Your driving didn't change — but your premium did. You're not imagining it: US auto premiums have risen sharply, and the causes have little to do with you personally.

The 5 real reasons

  1. Repair costs exploded. Bumpers now hide radar sensors and cameras; a simple fender-bender can cost $3,000–$5,000. EVs and ADAS-equipped cars cost even more to fix.
  2. Medical & litigation costs rose. Bodily injury claims are bigger, and attorney involvement in claims keeps climbing.
  3. More severe accidents. Distracted driving and higher speeds raised claim severity even as cars got safer.
  4. Catastrophe losses. Hail, floods, and wildfires hammered comprehensive claims in many states.
  5. Insurers are catching up. Several years of underwriting losses forced double-digit rate filings — regulators approve them in batches, so increases arrive in waves.

6 ways to fight back

  1. Shop 5+ insurers — rate increases hit companies unevenly; someone is always cheaper for your profile.
  2. Raise collision/comprehensive deductibles to $1,000.
  3. Enroll in telematics if you drive carefully.
  4. Audit your discounts with our discount finder.
  5. Drop full coverage on low-value cars (check the break-even tool).
  6. Improve your credit-based insurance score where it's used — on-time payments matter.

Note: Never just accept a renewal increase. A 15-minute comparison shop is the highest-paid quarter-hour in personal finance.
