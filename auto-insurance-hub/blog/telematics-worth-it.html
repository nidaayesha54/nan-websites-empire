Telematics Insurance: Is It Worth It?

2026-10-12 · 6 min read

Telematics (usage-based insurance) tracks how you actually drive — then prices your policy on it. Safe drivers can save 15–25%. But is the trade-off worth it?

How it works

You install an app or plug a small device into your car's OBD-II port for a monitoring period (typically 3–6 months, sometimes ongoing). It scores braking, acceleration, cornering, speed, phone use, and time of day. Good scores earn discounts; most programs promise your rate won't rise from the data — only the discount varies.

What it actually tracks

  • Hard braking and rapid acceleration events.
  • Late-night driving (midnight–4am is highest risk).
  • Mileage and trip patterns.
  • Phone handling while driving (app-based programs).

Who should enroll

  • Low-mileage, smooth drivers: the ideal profile — biggest discounts.
  • Young drivers: telematics can undercut the brutal under-25 surcharge faster than waiting to age out of it.
  • Second cars that sit in the garage most days.

Who should skip it

  • Night-shift workers or frequent late-night drivers (scored as high risk).
  • Aggressive brakers in stop-and-go commute traffic.
  • Anyone uncomfortable with location and driving data on insurer servers — read the privacy policy first.

Note: Most programs give a small participation discount (5–10%) just for enrolling, with the full safe-driver discount after the monitoring period. If your score disappoints, you can usually opt out at renewal.
