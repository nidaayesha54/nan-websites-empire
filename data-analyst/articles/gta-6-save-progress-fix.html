GTA 6 Lost Progress? Save Your Game Like This

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PS5 and Xbox Series X|S — and within a day, the most painful story of the launch was progress vanishing. Streamer JasonTheWeen lost around 10 hours of playtime, according to NotebookCheck, and he is not alone. The cause is simple: GTA 6's autosave is unreliable, and in the Prologue it does not fire at all. Trust the game to save for you and you can lose hours without warning. This guide explains how saving works, the manual-save routine that protects you, and what to do if the game crashes.

Why GTA 6 Autosave Cannot Be Trusted

Most modern games autosave constantly — after every checkpoint and objective. GTA 6 does not. Its autosave fires only after a mission ends, so everything you do between missions — exploring Vice City and the wider Leonida state, earning money, driving across the map — sits unprotected until the next mission completes. Quit mid-mission, lose power, or hit a crash, and that time is gone.

Two details make this worse than players expect. First, autosaves appear only in the Load Game menu — never in the Save Game menu. If you open Save Game looking for proof the game saved, you will find nothing — and that empty list does not mean your autosave failed; you are looking in the wrong place. Second, and most important: the Prologue never autosaves. Not once. The entire opening has no safety net at all, which is how a streamer can lose around 10 hours before realizing anything is wrong.

The Manual-Save Routine: Pause, Game, Save Game

Manual saving is quick once you know the path:

  1. Pause the game. Open the pause menu whenever you are in free play, not in the middle of a cutscene.
  2. Go to Game. Select the Game tab in the pause menu.
  3. Choose Save Game. Pick a manual save slot and confirm. That is your save — done in seconds.

Now make it a habit with three rules:

  • Save after every mission. The moment a mission ends, pause and save manually. Do not rely on the autosave that may or may not have fired — make your own.
  • Save before tricky missions. If a mission looks hard, long, or risky, save first. Missions in GTA 6 cannot be replayed, so a manual save before you start is your only way back to a clean attempt.
  • Save before you quit, every single time. Never close the game from free play without a manual save. That one habit alone would have prevented most of the lost-progress stories from launch week.

The 15-Slot Rotation: Never Put Everything in One Save

GTA 6 gives you 15 manual save slots. The smart move is to rotate between two or three of them instead of overwriting the same slot every time: a single slot is a single point of failure. Save over your only good save right before a mission goes wrong — or a choice you regret, since missions cannot be replayed — and there is nothing to fall back to.

A simple rotation works like this:

  • Slot 1: your main save — the latest progress, updated after every mission.
  • Slot 2: your backup — updated every few missions, or before any big story beat.
  • Slot 3: your "before something risky" save — made before tricky missions or big decisions.

With that pattern, the worst case is losing a mission or two, not a whole evening. It costs ten extra seconds per save — the cheapest insurance in the game. And remember: autosaves live only in Load Game, so verify what the game saved on its own there, not in the Save Game menu.

The Prologue Warning: Play It Like Nothing Is Saved

The Prologue deserves its own warning, because it is where the most progress gets lost: it never autosaves, so there is no safety net at all. Treat it as unprotected time — avoid quitting mid-Prologue if you can, and make your first manual save as soon as the game lets you. The earlier you are in the game, the less the game protects you.

If you are starting fresh, plan your first session around this: set aside enough uninterrupted time to get through the Prologue and into the part of the game where manual saving is available, then save immediately. A streamer losing around 10 hours sounds extreme until you realize how much of that time was spent in sections the game simply never saved.

If the Game Crashes: Prologue Freezes and What Helps

Lost progress is not always your fault — sometimes the game itself crashes or freezes, and the Prologue is where players report it most. No fix has been announced for Prologue crashes and freezes, and Rockstar has not commented. That is frustrating, but these workarounds help:

  • Update everything first. Install the latest game update and make sure your PS5 is on system software 26.07-14.23.00, the version GTA 6 requires.
  • Do a full restart, not rest mode. Close the game completely and restart the console.
  • On PS5, clear the cache and rebuild the database. Boot into Safe Mode and use the Clear Cache and Rebuild Database options. This is the most thorough console-side fix available.
  • On Xbox, fully quit the game before relaunching. Make sure the game is completely closed, not suspended in Quick Resume, then start it fresh.

None of these is a guaranteed fix — Rockstar has not commented, so no one can promise one. But they give a crashing game its best chance, and with the manual-save routine they keep a crash from becoming a catastrophe. A crash that costs one mission is annoying; a crash that costs ten hours is a lost evening.

Your Save Checklist

  1. Finish the Prologue without quitting mid-way, and save manually as soon as you can.
  2. After every mission: Pause, Game, Save Game.
  3. Before every tricky mission: save first — missions cannot be replayed.
  4. Rotate two or three of your 15 slots; never rely on a single save.
  5. Before quitting for the night: save manually, then check Load Game to confirm it is there.
  6. If the game crashes in the Prologue: update the game and PS5 software to 26.07-14.23.00, restart fully, and on PS5 use Safe Mode to clear the cache and rebuild the database.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6's autosave fires only after a mission ends, never during the Prologue, and autosaves show up only in Load Game — never in the Save Game menu. That is how around 10 hours disappears. The fix is a habit: Pause, Game, Save Game after every mission and before every tricky one, rotate two or three of your 15 slots, and never quit without saving.
