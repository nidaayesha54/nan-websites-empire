What Is Data Analysis? A Beginner's Guide (With a Hands-On Example)

Data analysis is simply turning raw numbers into decisions. A shop owner looks at daily sales and decides what to stock. A marketer looks at ad clicks and decides where to spend. That's data analysis — no PhD required.

What analysts actually do day to day

  1. Collect — pull data from databases, spreadsheets, APIs or CSV files.
  2. Clean — fix duplicates, typos and missing values (the unglamorous 80% of the job).
  3. Analyze — compute summaries, spot trends, test hunches with statistics and SQL.
  4. Visualize — turn findings into charts and dashboards people understand at a glance.
  5. Communicate — explain what the data means and what to do about it, in plain language.

The 4 types of data analysis

TypeQuestion it answersExample
DescriptiveWhat happened?Sales were $50k in March.
DiagnosticWhy did it happen?Sales rose because of the spring campaign.
PredictiveWhat will happen?April sales will likely hit $55k.
PrescriptiveWhat should we do?Increase ad spend in the top 3 cities.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to know programming to start? ＋

No. Start with spreadsheets and the free tools on this site. Learn SQL next, then Python when spreadsheets feel too small.

Is data analysis the same as data science? ＋

Not quite. Analysts answer business questions with existing data; data scientists build predictive models and machine learning systems. Analysis is the foundation of both.
