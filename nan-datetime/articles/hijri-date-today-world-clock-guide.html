2. How the Hijri (Islamic) Calendar Works

The Hijri calendar — also called the Islamic calendar — is a lunar calendar of 12 months and about 354 or 355 days per year. That makes it roughly 10 to 11 days shorter than the Gregorian solar year, which is why Ramadan, Eid al-Fitr, and Eid al-Adha move "backwards" through the seasons, arriving about ten days earlier each Gregorian year.

The calendar's epoch — year 1 AH (After Hijrah) — marks the migration (Hijrah) of Prophet Muhammad ﷺ from Makkah to Madinah in 622 CE. Every Islamic date counts forward from that event, which is why the calendar is called "Hijri."

Lunar months: 29 or 30 days

Each Islamic month begins at sunset on the day the new crescent (hilal) is sighted and lasts 29 or 30 days. In the tabular Islamic calendar used for calculation, odd-numbered months have 30 days and even-numbered months have 29, with the 12th month gaining an extra day in leap years. A 30-year cycle contains 11 leap years of 355 days, keeping the calendar aligned with the moon's phases over the long term.

4. How to Convert Gregorian Dates to Hijri Dates

Converting a Gregorian date to the Islamic date by hand is tedious, which is why our tool does it instantly. Under the hood it uses the classic tabular algorithm: it converts the Gregorian date to a Julian Day Number, then maps that number onto the 30-year leap cycle of the Islamic calendar to derive the Hijri year, month, and day.

A quick mental shortcut used by many: Hijri year ≈ (Gregorian year − 622) × 33 ÷ 32. For 2026: (2026 − 622) × 33 ÷ 32 ≈ 1447.7, so we are in the year 1448 AH for most of late 2026. This gets you the year; the exact month and day need the full calculation, which our converter handles for you on the main page.

Why do different websites show slightly different Hijri dates?

You may notice one site says 7 Jumada al-Ula while another says 8. This is normal and comes from three sources: (1) observational vs. tabular methods, (2) different countries announcing the crescent on different evenings, and (3) whether the "day" is counted from sunset or midnight. A one-day difference between sources is expected — never a sign that a converter is broken.

6. Pakistan Time Now (PKT Explained)

Searching for Pakistan time now? Pakistan Standard Time (PKT) is UTC+5, observed uniformly across the country — from Karachi to Lahore to Islamabad — with no daylight saving changes since 2009. That stability is exactly why so many people bookmark a Pakistan clock: the offset never shifts.

Common conversions people look up daily:

  • Pakistan to India: India (IST, UTC+5:30) is 30 minutes ahead of Pakistan.
  • Pakistan to Saudi Arabia: Saudi Arabia (AST, UTC+3) is 2 hours behind Pakistan — useful for prayer-time and Umrah planning.
  • Pakistan to UAE: Dubai is 1 hour behind Pakistan.
  • Pakistan to USA East Coast: 9–10 hours behind Pakistan depending on US daylight saving.

Is the Islamic date today the same in every country?

Usually, but not always. Because months begin with the local crescent sighting, neighboring countries can differ by a day. Saudi Arabia's Umm al-Qura calendar, Pakistan's Ruet-e-Hilal Committee, and other national bodies occasionally announce different start dates.

How many days are in a Hijri year?

354 days in a common year and 355 in a leap year — about 10–11 days shorter than the Gregorian year, which is why Islamic months drift through the seasons.
