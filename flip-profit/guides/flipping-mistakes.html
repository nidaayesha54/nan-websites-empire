7 Costly House Flipping Mistakes Beginners Make

Updated October 2026 · 9-minute read · By FlipProfit

Ask any experienced flipper about their first deal and you'll hear the same thing: "I paid tuition to the market." The tuition is optional. Nearly every beginner loss traces back to one of these 7 mistakes. Learn them here — free — instead of funding them with your own capital.

The 7 mistakes

  • 1. Overpaying — ignoring the 70% rule
  • 2. Fantasy ARV numbers
  • 3. Underestimating rehab costs
  • 4. Forgetting holding & selling costs
  • 5. Renovating for yourself, not the buyer
  • 6. Letting the timeline slip
  • 7. Skipping the inspection & due diligence

1. Overpaying — ignoring the 70% rule

The #1 killer. Beginners fall in love with a property and "stretch" to 80–85% of ARV, leaving zero margin for anything to go wrong — and something always goes wrong. The fix: make the 70% rule your hard ceiling. Maximum offer = (ARV × 0.70) − rehab. If the seller won't take it, walk away. There is always another house. Check every deal with our flip calculator before you offer.

2. Fantasy ARV numbers

Beginners pick the highest comp in the neighborhood and call it ARV. Pros use the median of 3–5 truly comparable sold homes — and then shade it down 2–3%. The fix: only use sold comps (never listings), match bed/bath/sqft within 20%, stay within half a mile, and when comps range $250k–$270k, underwrite at $255k. A $10,000 ARV fantasy becomes a $10,000 loss at closing.

3. Underestimating rehab costs

The classic: "It's mostly cosmetic — $25,000 should cover it." Then the electrician finds knob-and-tube wiring, the plumber finds galvanized pipes, and the "cosmetic" flip needs $55,000. The fix: get 2–3 contractor bids before you close, price every line item, and add a 10–15% contingency on top. If you can't walk the property with a contractor, don't buy it.

4. Forgetting holding & selling costs

Beginners model purchase + rehab and call it a budget. They forget: 6 months of hard-money interest, property taxes, insurance, utilities ($1,000–$2,000/month), plus ~9% of ARV in agent commissions and closing costs on the sale. The fix: on a $260k ARV flip, budget at least $25,000–$35,000 for holding + selling before you count a dollar of profit.

5. Renovating for yourself, not the buyer

Marble waterfall islands, custom tile mosaics, smart-home-everything — in a starter-home neighborhood where buyers want clean, neutral, and affordable. Over-improving is spending money the appraisal will never return. The fix: renovate to the neighborhood standard, not your Pinterest board. Tour 3 recently flipped comps and match their finish level — nothing more.

6. Letting the timeline slip

Every extra month costs $1,000–$2,000 in holding costs and risks a market shift. Beginners hire the cheapest contractor (who disappears for weeks), pull permits late, and make design decisions mid-project. The fix: have materials selected before demo day, use contractors with flip experience and penalty clauses for delays, and manage the schedule weekly. Speed is profit.

7. Skipping inspection & due diligence

"It's a teardown price, why inspect?" Because foundation cracks, mold, and unpermitted additions turn a teardown price into a money pit. The fix: always get an inspection — even on ugly houses, especially on ugly houses. Check title for liens, verify zoning and permit history with the city, and confirm the property can actually be financed and insured.
