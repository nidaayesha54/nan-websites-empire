GTA 6 Flipping Cars: Moses, Trackers and $30,000 Sales

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and the steadiest way to make money in the early game is not a heist — it is selling cars. GTA 6 has 557 vehicles, 277 standard models and 280 featured variants according to the in-game log, and almost all of them can be stolen and sold. But the selling is not simple: every car has a tracker status, every buyer has rules, and the difference between a $1,000 sale and a $30,000 sale is knowing which buyer to take which car to.

That matters because money in GTA 6 is tight at the start, and cars are everywhere. The game's own money guides call vehicle sales one of the best early income methods, and the system rewards players who learn it properly. This guide explains the whole ladder — from the first fence in Chapter 2 to the $30,000 Wish List cars in Chapter 3 — and the mistakes that cost you money.

Step One: Moses and the Untracked Cars

The ladder starts in Chapter 2 with Moses Watkins. After you complete his mission — stealing an '82 Crawler and bringing it to him — Moses becomes your fence, and he will buy any untracked car you bring him. A typical sale brings in $1,000 or more, and the whole process takes about one to two minutes. His garage has a cooldown of approximately two minutes after a sale, so you cannot dump cars on him one after another without a pause.

Moses has rules, and they are strict. He does not accept vehicles with trackers, and he does not accept vehicles stolen too close to his garage — drive the car away from the area before you bring it in. He also gives you a Slim Jim when you meet him, which lets you open locked cars without smashing a window and setting off the alarm. And he sometimes texts boost requests for specific cars, which pay extra plus a condition bonus — so a car he specifically wants is worth more than the same car on an ordinary day. Condition matters in every sale: fences pay more for vehicles in good shape, and may refuse one that is too damaged.

Step Two: The WAiNK App and the Tracker System

Before you steal a car, check what it is worth. The WAiNK app on your in-game phone lets you scan a vehicle before you commit: hold Up on the D-pad to open the app, point the phone at the car and press R1 or RB to scan it. The scan lists the sell value, whether any particular fences would be interested, and the details of the vehicle's condition and security features — including whether it has a tracker.

The tracker is the whole system. Cars without a tracker go to Moses. Cars with a Standard Tracker cannot go to Moses at all, and they have to wait for Chapter 3, when you can sell them at any Pay 'n' Spray for around $4,000 per sale. The map index lists the fences you have unlocked under Robberies, along with how much each will pay for a car, so you can plan a route before you ever touch a door handle. And remember that unregistered stolen cars marked with a grey icon will eventually disappear — only registering a car at a Pay 'n' Spray makes it permanently yours.

Step Three: Pay 'n' Spray, Randy and the $30,000 Wish List

Chapter 3 is where car flipping becomes serious money. Pay 'n' Spray locations, which unlock during the "Just a Scratch" mission in Chapter 1, will buy cars with Standard Trackers — but there is a limit of three cars per day, so you cannot farm them endlessly. Pay 'n' Spray also repairs cars and resprays them to reduce vehicle heat, and you can sell your own registered vehicles there too, with the money going straight into your BuckMe account. If you have hit your balance limit, the sale will not go through, so bank your cash first.

The top of the ladder is Randy. Partway through Chapter 3, after the "Clonin' Keys, Makin' GS" mission where you steal the Zorrusso for Randy, you get the Key Cloner — and Randy gives you his Wish List of 18 high-end sports cars. Find one of those cars and deliver it in perfect condition, and it can pay $30,000 or more, making it one of the strongest vehicle-based income sources before the large bank robberies open up in Chapter 4. The big bank vault robberies near the end of Chapter 4 can reportedly pay up to $205,000 in around five minutes, but until you get there, cars are the money.

Frequently Asked Questions

Where do you sell stolen cars in GTA 6?

To Moses from Chapter 2 if the car has no tracker (about $1,000 or more). From Chapter 3, at any Pay 'n' Spray for cars with a Standard Tracker (about $4,000, three per day), and to Randy for the 18 sports cars on his Wish List ($30,000 or more).

How do you check a car's value before stealing it?

Use the WAiNK app on your phone: point it at the car and scan. It shows the sell value, whether fences are interested, and the car's condition and tracker status.

Can you sell your own cars in GTA 6?

Yes. Drive a registered personal vehicle to a Pay 'n' Spray and choose the sell option. The money goes into your BuckMe account, and you can sell stored vehicles from the Stored Vehicles tab.

The Bottom Line

Car flipping in GTA 6 is a ladder, and every rung has a rule: untracked cars to Moses, tracked cars to Pay 'n' Spray, and the Wish List cars to Randy for the real money. Scan before you steal, keep the car in good condition, respect the daily limits — and bank the cash, because a car you sell is only money once it is in your account.
