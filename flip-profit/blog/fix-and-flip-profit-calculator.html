Fix and Flip Profit Calculator: How to Estimate Any Deal in 5 Minutes

October 11, 2026 · 8-minute read · By FlipProfit

A fix and flip profit calculator is the fastest way to separate real deals from money pits. But most investors type in fantasy numbers and get fantasy profits. This article walks you through every input — what it means, where beginners go wrong, and what honest numbers look like — using a real example deal.

The 6 inputs, explained

1. Purchase price

What you'll actually pay, including your closing costs on the buy (typically 1–3% extra for title, recording, and lender fees). Beginners enter the list price and forget closing costs — that's $2,000–$5,000 of phantom profit.

2. Rehab cost

Your contractor's bid plus a 10–15% contingency. If the bid is $40,000, enter $46,000. Surprises aren't possible in old houses — they're guaranteed. Never use a guess; get at least two written bids before you close.

3. After-repair value (ARV)

The most abused input. ARV must come from 3–5 sold comps — similar size, beds/baths, and condition after renovation, sold within 6 months and half a mile. Use the conservative end of the range. Every $10,000 of ARV optimism is $10,000 of imaginary profit.

4. Holding period (months)

Renovation time + listing time + closing time. Beginners enter 3 months; reality is 5–7. Be honest: add 2 months to your contractor's estimate, because contractors estimate like optimists and renovate like realists.

5. Monthly holding cost

Everything you pay each month you own the house: loan interest, property taxes, insurance, utilities, HOA. With hard-money loans at 10–12%, this is usually $1,000–$2,000/month — the silent profit killer.

6. Selling costs (%)

Agent commissions (5–6%) + seller closing costs + staging + concessions. Budget 8–10% of ARV. On a $260,000 sale, that's $21,000–$26,000 most beginners never model.

Real example: the $150,000 deal

Let's run a typical Midwest deal through our fix-and-flip calculator:

InputValue
Purchase price$150,000
Rehab cost$40,000
ARV$260,000
Holding period6 months
Monthly holding cost$1,200
Selling costs9%

The math:

  • Holding costs: 6 × $1,200 = $7,200
  • Selling costs: 9% × $260,000 = $23,400
  • Total costs: $150,000 + $40,000 + $7,200 + $23,400 = $220,600
  • Profit: $260,000 − $220,600 = $39,400
  • ROI: $39,400 ÷ $197,200 invested = 20%
  • 70% rule check: $150,000 vs. max ($260,000 × 0.70 − $40,000) = $142,000 → slightly over, negotiate down $8,000

A $39,400 profit with 20% ROI is a solid deal — but note it fails the strict 70% rule by $8,000. That's exactly the kind of nuance a calculator reveals and gut feeling misses.

How to read the results

  • Profit ≥ $20k–$30k minimum: below this, one surprise wipes you out. Walk away from thin deals.
  • ROI ≥ 15–20%: you're taking real risk; demand real returns. Under 10% isn't worth it.
  • 70% rule PASS: your margin of safety is intact. FAIL means renegotiate or walk.
  • Max offer price: the highest you can pay while keeping a 15% profit margin — your walk-away number in negotiations.

How accurate are fix and flip calculators?

A calculator is only as accurate as your inputs. With honest ARV comps, real contractor bids, and full holding and selling costs, estimates typically land within 10–15% of the actual outcome. The calculator doesn't make you money — honest inputs do. Garbage in, foreclosure out.
