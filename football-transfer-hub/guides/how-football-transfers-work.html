How Football Transfers Work

Every summer, billions change hands as players move between clubs. But how do football transfers actually work? Here is the full process, step by step.

1. Scouting and Interest

It starts with data and scouts. Clubs identify targets, often through agents who quietly sound out interest. At this stage you will see "interest" rumors — usually the least reliable, since agents use leaks as leverage.

2. The Bid

The buying club makes a formal offer to the selling club. Negotiation covers the fee, payment structure (installments are normal), add-ons (appearances, trophies) and sell-on clauses. Our transfer fee calculator estimates what a fair fee looks like.

3. Personal Terms

Once clubs agree a fee, the player negotiates wages, bonuses and contract length with the buying club — often via their agent. Use the wage calculator to convert weekly wages to annual pay.

4. Release Clauses

Some contracts include a release clause (buyout clause): a fixed fee that, if met, forces the selling club to accept. Famous in Spain, they are reshaping the market — see our article on release clauses changing the transfer market.

5. The Medical

Before signing, the player undergoes a medical. A failed medical can collapse or renegotiate a deal — it is the last real hurdle.

6. Signing and Registration

Contracts are signed, the fee is processed, and the player is registered with the league and federation — which can only happen inside the transfer window. International moves also need a transfer certificate via FIFA's TMS system.

Who Takes a Cut?

  • Agents: typically earn a commission from the player, the buying club, or both.
  • Selling club: receives the fee, often in installments over several years.
  • Previous clubs: FIFA solidarity payments send a small percentage to clubs that developed the player.
  • Sell-on clauses: a former club may be owed a cut of any future profit.

Why Some Transfers Collapse

Deals die over fee disagreements, wage demands, failed medicals, work permits, or simply the window closing. Tracking the reliability of each rumor helps — try our rumor tracker and read how to spot a reliable transfer rumor.
