How to Spot a Reliable Transfer Rumor

Every transfer window, thousands of football transfer rumors flood social media. Most are noise. Here is how to separate signal from clickbait.

The Source Tier System

Fans rank sources by track record. Our rumor tracker uses the same five tiers:

  • Tier 1: top-tier journalists with direct club contacts and near-perfect records.
  • Tier 2: reputable national media — good but occasionally wrong.
  • Tier 3: decent outlets with a mixed hit rate; treat as "possible".
  • Tier 4: aggregators that rewrite others' stories, often adding spin.
  • Tier 5: random accounts, gossip pages, "my uncle works at the club" energy.

Green Flags (Likely Reliable)

  • The journalist has a long, public track record of correct exclusives.
  • Multiple tier 1–2 outlets report the same story independently.
  • Specific details: medical dates, fee structure, contract length.
  • The player's agent or club-adjacent reporters confirm interest.

Red Flags (Probably Fake)

  • No named source — "reports suggest", "according to sources".
  • Only aggregators carry it; no original reporting exists.
  • Impossible timing — announced outside the transfer window as a done deal.
  • The account has a history of deleted wrong predictions.
  • It exists mainly to farm engagement during a slow news day.

The Golden Rule

No rumor is real until the club announces it. Until then, score every story with our rumor tracker, learn how transfers work, and enjoy the window for what it is — the best soap opera in sport.
