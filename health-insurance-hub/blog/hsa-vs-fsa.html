HSA vs FSA: Which Saves You More?

2026-10-12 · 6 min read

Both let you pay medical bills with pre-tax dollars — but they work very differently. Pick the wrong one and you can literally lose money.

HSA: the triple-tax superstar

  • Triple tax advantage: contributions are pre-tax (or deductible), growth is tax-free, and withdrawals for medical expenses are tax-free.
  • The money is yours forever — it rolls over year after year and stays with you if you change jobs.
  • Catch: you must be enrolled in a qualifying high-deductible health plan (HDHP).
  • 2026 contribution limits are roughly $4,300 (individual) / $8,550 (family), plus a $1,000 catch-up at 55+.

FSA: use it or lose it

  • Also pre-tax for medical expenses — same immediate tax saving as an HSA.
  • Use-it-or-lose-it: funds generally don't roll over (employers may allow a small rollover ~$640 or a grace period — check yours).
  • Available with any plan type — no HDHP required.
  • Lower contribution limit (~$3,300 in 2026) and you lose it if you leave the job.

Which saves more?

If you qualify for an HSA, it wins on almost every axis: higher limits, permanent ownership, investment growth, and portability. An HSA used as a retirement vehicle (pay medical bills from cash, let the HSA compound) is one of the best tax shelters available to middle-income earners.

Choose the FSA when: you don't have an HDHP, you have predictable expenses (braces, LASIK, known prescriptions), and you're confident you'll spend the balance within the plan year.

Note: You generally can't contribute to both a full HSA and a general-purpose FSA in the same year (a limited-purpose FSA for dental/vision is the exception).
