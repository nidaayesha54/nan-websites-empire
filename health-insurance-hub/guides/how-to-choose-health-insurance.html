How to Choose Health Insurance in 2026

2026-10-12 · 8 min read

Choosing a health plan feels like gambling because most people compare only the monthly premium. The premium is just one of four numbers that decide what you'll actually pay. Here's the full framework.

Step 1: Know the metal tiers

Marketplace plans come in Bronze, Silver, Gold, and Platinum — tiers describe cost-sharing, not quality of care:

  • Bronze: lowest premium, highest deductible (~$7,000). Best if you're healthy and want catastrophe protection.
  • Silver: the middle path — and the only tier where cost-sharing reductions apply if your income qualifies.
  • Gold: higher premium, lower deductible (~$1,500). Best if you expect regular care.
  • Platinum: highest premium, lowest out-of-pocket costs. Rarely the best value unless you have major planned care.

Step 2: Pick the network type

  • HMO: cheapest; you need a primary-care referral for specialists and there's (almost) no out-of-network coverage. Fine if your doctors are in-network.
  • PPO: pricier; see specialists without referrals and get partial out-of-network coverage. Worth it if you travel or want specific doctors.
  • EPO: the middle ground — no referrals, but no out-of-network coverage either.

Step 3: Compare TRUE yearly cost, not premiums

Add 12 × premium + expected out-of-pocket costs for your situation. Our health plan cost comparator does this across low, medium, and high usage scenarios — the "cheap" Bronze plan often loses once real medical bills appear.

Step 4: Check your doctors and drugs

Before enrolling, verify two things in the plan's directory: (1) your primary-care doctor and any specialists are in-network, and (2) your prescriptions are on the plan's formulary at a reasonable tier. A plan that excludes your $400/month medication isn't a bargain.

Step 5: Factor in subsidies and HSAs

If your income is under ~400% of the poverty level, check your ACA subsidy estimate — subsidies can make a Silver plan cheaper than Bronze. And if you pick a qualifying high-deductible plan, an HSA gives you triple tax advantages on medical savings.

Note: Never choose on premium alone. A $200/month cheaper premium with a $5,000 higher out-of-pocket max is a bad trade the first time anyone in the family needs surgery.
