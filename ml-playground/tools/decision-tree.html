What's happening here?

A decision tree is a flowchart learned from data: at each node it asks the yes/no question that best splits the examples (maximizing "information gain"). You just traced a path from root to leaf — that's exactly how a trained tree classifies a new input, except its questions are learned from thousands of examples instead of hand-written.

  • Strength: fully interpretable — you can explain every prediction.
  • Weakness: single trees overfit easily, memorizing noise.
  • The fix: Random Forests — grow hundreds of trees on random data samples and let them vote. That's the "Gradient Boosting / Random Forest" leaf you may have landed on.
