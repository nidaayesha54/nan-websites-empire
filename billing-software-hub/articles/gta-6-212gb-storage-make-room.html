GTA 6 Is 212 GB: How to Make Room Before You Play

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S — and before you can play a single minute of it, you have to fit it on your console. The install is 212 GB. That is the biggest game install of this console generation, and for a lot of players it is bigger than the free space they have left. The preload alone needs up to 212 GB depending on the device, and the game will not start until the whole thing is on the drive. Making room is a ten-minute job if you know what to delete first — and a miserable one if you delete the wrong things.

The Number: 212 GB, and Why It Is That Big

Two hundred and twelve gigabytes is not a typo. Rockstar's own store pages list the preload at up to 212 GB, and the figure has been consistent since preloading opened: the full game, in one download, on both consoles. It is the price of the world Rockstar built. Leonida is not one city — it is six regions, from Vice City and the Leonida Keys to the Grassrivers swamps, Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia and Mount Kalaga National Park, with dense traffic, detailed interiors, wildlife, weather and a soundtrack that reviewers have singled out for praise. Every one of those systems lives on your drive, and none of it streams in from the internet while you play.

For comparison, most big games of this generation land between 80 and 150 GB. On a base PS5 with 667 GB of usable space, GTA 6 eats about a third of the whole drive; on an Xbox Series S, with 364 GB usable, it eats well over half. Start the download with 100 GB free and you will be staring at an error message instead of the opening mission.

Check Your Free Space First

Before you delete anything, check what you actually have. On PS5, go to Settings, then Storage, then Console Storage — the screen shows a bar with games, media and system data, and the free space at the end. On Xbox, open My Games and Apps, then Manage, then Free Up Space, which lists how much is free and what is taking the rest. Do this before you buy the game, not after: a download that stops halfway because the drive filled up wastes the hours you already spent downloading it.

While you are there, check for a pending system update too. GTA 6 needs PS5 system software 26.07-14.23.00 to run properly at launch, and an update downloading at the same time as a 212 GB game is the slowest possible way to spend launch night. Install the system update first, then start the game download, and leave the console alone while it works.

What to Delete First

Start with the games you have finished and will not replay this month. A finished story game is the easiest space on any console: select it, delete it, and the space comes back immediately. Your save data is stored separately and stays put, so you can reinstall the game later and pick up where you left off. On PS5 you can also move PS4 games to an external USB drive instead of deleting them — they run from USB, so they cost you nothing on the internal drive. PS5 games cannot run from USB, but they can be stored there and moved back later, which is still faster than re-downloading them.

Next, the capture gallery. A year of 4K clips can easily be tens of gigabytes: on PS5 check Media Gallery, on Xbox check Captures, and delete the clips you have already shared. After that, look at apps and games you do not use. Delete the game, not the save: on both consoles those are two different actions, and only one of them is reversible.

What Not to Delete

Do not delete your save data to make room. Saves are tiny — a few megabytes each — and deleting them destroys progress you cannot get back. If a game asks whether to delete its saved data along with the game, say no. The same goes for your GTA 6 save once you start playing: the autosave is unreliable and manual saving matters, so the save file is the most valuable few megabytes on your console. Protect it.

Do not delete system software, and do not factory-reset the console to “start clean” — that costs you every setting, login and download on the machine. And do not try to run GTA 6 from an external USB drive: PS5 games must run from the internal SSD, and Xbox Series X|S games from the internal drive or an official expansion card. An external drive can store the game, but it cannot play it.

Preload, Download, and Launch Night

If you bought the game digitally, preloading is the way to skip the launch-night queue. On PS5, preloading opened at midnight local time on November 10; on Xbox, at 00:00 GMT the same day. Code-in-box physical copies — the boxed version is a download code, not a disc — could preload from November 12. A preloaded game still stays locked until midnight local time in your store region on launch night, but the 212 GB is already on your drive when the clock turns over, and you play instead of waiting.

If you did not preload, start the download as early as you can on launch day and leave the console in rest mode with downloads enabled. A 212 GB download is hours on most home connections. The game unlocks at midnight whether the download finished or not — but an unfinished download means an unfinished game.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the install really 212 GB on both consoles?

The store pages say the preload requires up to 212 GB depending on the device, so the exact figure can vary slightly between PS5 and Xbox — but plan for the full 212 GB on either console. Freeing less than that and hoping is how launch night gets ruined.

Can I play GTA 6 from an external hard drive?

No. On PS5, PS5 games must run from the internal SSD; on Xbox Series X|S, they must run from the internal drive or an official storage expansion card. You can store the game on a USB drive, but you have to move it back to the internal drive before you can play it.

Will there be a day-one patch on top of the 212 GB?

Rockstar has not announced a separate day-one patch size, and the preload is the full game. If an update does appear at launch, it will be small compared with the install itself — the 212 GB is the number to plan around.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6 is 212 GB, and that is a real number with real consequences: a third of a base PS5 drive, and more than half of a Series S. Check your free space now, delete finished games and old clips first, never touch your save data, and start the download early. Do the ten minutes of clearing today, and launch night is yours.
