In-House vs Outsourced Medical Billing: The Honest 2026 Guide

Updated for 2026 · 7 min read

Every practice eventually asks: hire our own billing staff, or hand it to a billing company? The honest answer: it depends on your size, volume, and specialty — and the math is simpler than vendors make it sound.

In-house billing: the real cost

A single experienced biller costs roughly $50,000–$70,000/year fully loaded (salary + benefits + payroll tax). Add billing software ($12,000–$25,000/year for a small practice), clearinghouse fees, and management time. A 3-provider practice typically spends $140,000–$180,000/year all-in on in-house billing.

Pros: full control, instant communication, staff knows your providers and patients, easier for complex specialties.

Cons: turnover risk (one resignation = crisis), training burden, fixed cost even in slow months, limited after-hours coverage.

Outsourced billing: the real cost

Billing companies typically charge 4–8% of collections. A practice collecting $200K/month pays $8,000–$16,000/month ($96K–$192K/year). The fee scales with you — slow month, lower fee.

Pros: no hiring or turnover risk, built-in expertise across payers, 24/7 operations at larger firms, performance incentives aligned (they earn more when you collect more).

Cons: less day-to-day control, communication lag, quality varies wildly between firms, contract lock-ins.

The break-even rule of thumb

  • 1–2 providers: outsourcing almost always wins on cost.
  • 3–5 providers: the crossover zone — run the numbers.
  • 6+ providers: in-house usually wins IF you can hire and retain good billers.

But cost isn't everything. Factor in denial rates: a good billing company that cuts your denial rate from 12% to 6% can pay for itself entirely. Our ROI calculator includes denial-rate math.

Hybrid: the option nobody mentions

Many practices keep front-end billing in-house (eligibility, charge entry) and outsource back-end AR follow-up (denials, appeals, old balances). This gives you control where it matters and expertise where it's hardest. Ask billing companies if they offer AR-only contracts — many do at 3–5% of recovered amounts.

Vetting an outsourcing partner

  1. Ask for 3 references in your specialty — then actually call them.
  2. Get the denial rate and days-in-AR they achieve for similar clients, in writing.
  3. Confirm who owns your data and how you get it back if you leave.
  4. Cap the contract at 12 months with a 90-day out clause.
General information only. Staffing costs and billing company fees vary by market — verify with local data before deciding.
