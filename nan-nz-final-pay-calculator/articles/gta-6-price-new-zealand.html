GTA 6 Price in New Zealand: What Kiwis Pay

November 20, 2026 · 4 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 costs NZ$139.95 for the Standard Edition and NZ$174.95 for the Ultimate Edition in New Zealand, on both the PlayStation Store and the Microsoft Store, with tax included. That is the price Kiwis pay — not the US price converted, but the price New Zealand's own stores list. This guide covers the two editions, the launch time, how the price compares with the rest of the world, and the early-play trick that makes New Zealand famous every launch.

The prices come from the stores' own listings, checked repeatedly since pre-orders opened on June 25, 2026. They have not changed since — no price rise, no launch discount, no regional adjustment. What you see in the store is what you pay.

The two editions, side by side

The Standard Edition at NZ$139.95 is the full game: the story of Jason and Lucia across Leonida, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. The Ultimate Edition at NZ$174.95 adds the premium extras — extra vehicles, weapons, apparel and in-game content, including 16 story-mode extras. The NZ Herald lists the same two prices, and both stores charge the same in New Zealand, so there is no cheaper store to hunt for between PlayStation and Xbox.

In US dollars, the Standard Edition is $79.99 and the Ultimate Edition is $99.99. New Zealand's NZ$139.95 converts to roughly $81 at November 2026 rates, which puts it near the top of the world price table in dollar terms — behind the UK at about $92, but ahead of most of Europe. The reason is simple: each country's store sets its own local price, and New Zealand's is set high.

Launch time: first in the world

New Zealand launched GTA 6 first. The game went live at midnight NZDT on Thursday, November 19 — 18 hours before the US store opened. Auckland sits 18 hours ahead of New York, and the midnight local launch meant Kiwis were playing while Americans were still waiting for Wednesday evening. It is the same pattern as every big launch: the country furthest ahead in time gets the game first.

That head start is also why New Zealand is famous among players worldwide. Every launch, the same trick circulates: make a New Zealand account, buy the game there, and play hours early. For GTA 6, the trick works the same way it always has — and it costs the same as buying the game twice.

The early-play trick, honestly explained

The trick needs three things: a New Zealand account, New Zealand money in that account's wallet, and console sharing to carry the game over to your main profile. On PlayStation, the New Zealand store listing opens the game at midnight Auckland time, and a second purchase on that account unlocks it early on your console through sharing.

Here is the honest cost. A New Zealand copy is a second purchase at NZ$139.95 for Standard or NZ$174.95 for Ultimate, on top of any pre-order you already hold. Wallet funds cannot be transferred between accounts, so the New Zealand dollars stay on that account. And the account's country cannot be changed once it is created, so the New Zealand account is a permanent second account, not a temporary trick. For most players, 18 hours early is not worth a second full-price purchase. For the players who want it anyway, those are the real terms.

Digital vs boxed: where Kiwis save money

Buying digitally costs more in New Zealand than buying a boxed code at a shop. The digital prices are fixed at NZ$139.95 and NZ$174.95, but retail prices can change, and launch-week reporting showed boxed codes cheaper than digital in New Zealand stores. The box holds a download code instead of a disc, and boxed copies reached stores on November 12, a week before launch — with pre-loading from the code opening the same day.

The practical advice for Kiwi buyers is simple: check the shop price before you buy digital. The stores' digital prices have been unchanged since October, so there is no rush and no discount to wait for — but a boxed code from a retailer can still beat the digital price on launch week. And whatever you buy, check the price in your own store before paying, because store prices can change without notice.
