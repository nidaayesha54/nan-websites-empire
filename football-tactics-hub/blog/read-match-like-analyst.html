How to Read a Football Match Like a Tactics Analyst

October 2026 · 7 min read

You don't need a coaching licence to see the game the way analysts do — just a TV, curiosity, and the right things to look at. Here are seven.

1. Watch the shape, not the ball (first 10 minutes)

For the opening minutes, ignore the ball and watch one team's shape: how high is the defensive line? Where does the width come from? You'll identify the formation (see our formations guide) faster than any commentator will tell you.

2. Spot the pressing triggers

Teams don't press randomly. Watch for the cue that starts the press — a back-pass to the keeper, a touch toward the touchline, a heavy first touch. Once you see the trigger, you'll predict the press before it happens.

3. Count the midfield

The team with more bodies in the middle usually controls the game. Is it 3v2? Has a striker dropped in to even it up? Midfield numbers explain most of what happens.

4. Check the rest defence

When a team attacks, glance behind the ball: how many players stayed back? Three or four means they're protected; two means they're gambling — and a turnover could be fatal.

5. Find the half-spaces

The channels between wing and centre are where modern attacks live. Notice which players keep receiving the ball there — that's the team's creative hub.

6. Watch what changes after 60 minutes

Substitutions reveal the manager's read of the game. A winger for a full-back means chasing a goal; a midfielder for a striker means protecting a lead. Ask yourself: what problem is the manager solving?

7. Rewind one attack

Pick one goal and rewind 30 seconds. Trace it backwards: where did the space come from? A failed press? An overload? A full-back caught high? One rewound attack teaches more than an hour of highlights.
