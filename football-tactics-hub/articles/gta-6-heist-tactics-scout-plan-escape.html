GTA 6 Heist Tactics: Scout, Plan and Get Away Clean

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and robbery is at the centre of the game. Rockstar North co-head Rob Nelson has said you can rob pretty much anything, and the game's demonstrations show why planning matters: in one demo, Nelson scouted a bank's interior, decided it had too many guards, and walked out without robbing it. In another, he robbed a headphone shop he had walked into, and realised as the alarm went off that he should have prepared. The lesson of both demos is the same: in GTA 6, a robbery starts before you walk in the door.

That matters because GTA 6's robberies are not the GTA 5 heists. There is no planning board, no crew roster, no loud-or-quiet menu. There is a ladder of jobs — from a corner store to a bank vault — and the tactics that work are the ones you bring with you: scouting, preparation, a getaway car and a plan for the money afterwards.

Step One: Scout the Target Before You Touch It

Scouting is the tactic the game rewards most, and it is the one Nelson demonstrated first. Walk into a bank and look at it the way a robber would: how many guards, how many staff, where the alarm is, whether there is a back way in. Nelson's demo is the perfect example — he scouted a bank, counted the guards, decided the job was not worth it, and left. A robbery you walk away from costs you nothing. A robbery you walk into blind can cost you the take, the getaway and the cash in your pocket.

The game's wanted system makes scouting matter more, not less. The police act only on what they actually witness, or what a witness reports — there is no magic GPS that knows where you are. If nobody sees the crime and no alarm goes off, the response is different from a job that starts with a screaming teller and a panic button. Coming through the back lets you reach the security cameras and the alarm before anyone out front knows what is happening, and you can destroy the footage and switch off the alarm. In Chapter 3, the story hands you a snap gun, which picks door locks without setting off the alarm — and that makes the quiet approach far easier.

Step Two: The Safe, the Alarm and the Getaway Car

Inside the target, three things decide how much you make. First, the safe: the code is often written down somewhere on the premises, or you can threaten an employee until they give it up. Blowing a safe open with explosives is faster, but Nelson warned that an explosive would damage some of the take inside it — so the quiet code is worth more than the loud explosion. Second, the alarm: a bank has more staff and more security than a shop, which means more people who can panic and hit the alarm, so control the room before you touch the safe. Aim at the clerk or guard and shout them down so they cannot hit the panic button, and aim at more than one person to keep a standoff under control while your partner works.

Third, the getaway car — and this one comes first, not last. Nelson's own words are the guide: "I should really be preparing for this robbery. Like by putting a vehicle out front." Park the car before you go in, hide your gun behind your back, put on a mask or face wrap, and know your exit before you enter. The partner matters here rather than being scenery: Nelson has said the partner can drive well, with one character shooting while the other drives, and you can switch to take the wheel yourself. A back way out matters because the police form up at the front — and the game's own footage shows torching the getaway car and switching vehicles to break the police description of your outfit, your car and your face.

Step Three: The Big Jobs and the Money After

The ladder goes up from there. At the bottom you can rob almost any store, gas station or diner in free roam. Above that are jewellery stores and armoured cars, and at the top are the story bank and vault heists. The biggest named jobs belong to Raul Bautista, a seasoned bank robber who pulls Jason and Lucia in as getaway drivers: the Fleeca heist, where one mission hits three Fleeca banks in a row with Karim on comms cutting the alarms and a clean car waiting at each bank, paying $31,860 in marked bills; and the Cartel heist at the Sinfrontera National Bank, worth $150,000, with eight setup missions — scoping the bank, a security expert, heist planning, and rigging cell tower bombs that buy a longer time limit, with the clock starting at 10:47 when all three towers are rigged.

And the job is not done when you leave the building. Cash goes in your pocket, and anything else fills a duffel bag with its own value on the HUD — and the money is not safe until it is banked. Nelson has said stolen money disappears if you are killed before depositing it, and he recommends putting cash into Jason and Lucia's bank accounts at an ATM before taking on dangerous situations. Marked bills from bank jobs cannot be spent at all until you sell them to a fence. A robbery is not really over until the cash is deposited and the loot is fenced.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a planning board for heists in GTA 6?

No. A GTA 5-style planning board has not been shown. Missions offer several approaches and escape routes, but the planning is something you do in the world — scouting, preparation and positioning — not on a menu.

Should you blow the safe open or find the code?

Find the code. It takes longer, but blowing a safe open with explosives damages part of the cash inside, so the quiet method pays more.

What happens to robbery money if you die?

Stolen cash in your pocket is lost if you are killed before you deposit it. Bank it at an ATM as soon as you can, and sell marked bills and duffel-bag loot to a fence.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6 heists reward the boring work: scout the target, park the getaway car, control the room, crack the safe quietly, and bank the money before you do anything dangerous. The big jobs with Raul pay the most, but the tactics are the same at every level — and the player who prepares is the player who keeps the take.
