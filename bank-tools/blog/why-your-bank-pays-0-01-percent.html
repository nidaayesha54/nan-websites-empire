Why Your Big Bank Pays 0.01% (and What to Do About It)

By BankTools · October 2026 · 5 min read

Your bank isn't struggling. It's choosing to pay you almost nothing — because it can. Here's the business logic, and the 10-minute fix.

The math from the bank's side

Big banks earn ~4–5% lending your deposits out (or parking them at the Fed) while paying you 0.01%. On $10,000, that's ~$450 a year of profit from your money, of which you see $1. Branches, marketing, and inertia make this model work.

Why they get away with it

  • Inertia: most people never move their first checking account.
  • Bundling: direct deposit, bill pay, and branches create switching friction.
  • Rate blindness: 0.01% vs 4.50% feels abstract — until you see it's $1 vs $450 a year on $10,000. Make it concrete with the APY comparison calculator.

The 10-minute switch

  1. Open a high-yield savings account online (see what to compare).
  2. Link your checking account and transfer savings over.
  3. Keep checking where it is; move emergency + short-term money to the HYSA.
  4. Set a calendar reminder to glance at rates once a year.

Same FDIC insurance. Same dollars. Roughly 400× the interest. Inertia is the only thing standing between you and it.
