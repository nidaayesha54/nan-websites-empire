How Much Emergency Fund Do You Really Need?

By BankTools · October 2026 · 6 min read

'3–6 months of expenses' is the standard advice — but your number depends on your job, your household, and your safety nets. Let's find yours.

Start with monthly essentials

Add up must-pay monthly costs: housing, food, transport, insurance, minimum debt payments. Not vacations, not dining out. Say it's $4,000 — the textbook range is $12,000–$24,000.

Lean toward 3 months if…

  • Dual-income household with stable jobs.
  • Strong safety nets (family support, liquid investments).
  • You're carrying high-interest debt — a smaller fund plus aggressive payoff wins.

Lean toward 6–12 months if…

  • Single income, freelancing, or commission-based pay.
  • Specialized job that'd take months to replace.
  • Dependents, chronic health costs, or an older home/car.

Where to keep it

A high-yield savings account: FDIC-insured, earning 4%+, withdrawable in days. Not stocks (could crash when you need it), not a CD ladder's long rungs (penalties). Project yours with the HYSA projector.

When to stop adding

Once the fund is full, redirect that monthly deposit to investing or debt. An emergency fund is insurance, not an investment — its job is to sit there, boring and ready.
