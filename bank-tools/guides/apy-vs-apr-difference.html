APY vs APR: What's the Difference?

By BankTools · October 2026 · 5 min read

Banks quote APY on savings and APR on loans — and the difference isn't marketing trivia. It changes the actual dollars.

The one-sentence version

APY (annual percentage yield) = what your savings earn, with compounding included. APR (annual percentage rate) = what borrowing costs per year, without compounding.

APY: compounding included

If a savings account compounds monthly at a 4.4% nominal rate, the APY is (1 + 0.044/12)12 − 1 ≈ 4.49%. APY is the number that tells you what $10,000 becomes in a year: $10,449. Compare two APYs head-to-head in our APY comparison tool.

APR: compounding not included

A credit card's 24% APR compounded monthly actually costs (1 + 0.24/12)12 − 1 ≈ 26.8% effective. APR understates the true cost of debt that compounds — one more reason to kill high-APR balances first.

The trap to watch

Some banks advertise a high interest rate but a lower APY, or vice versa. Always compare APY to APY on savings and APR to APR on loans — never cross-compare. Then project the winner with the HYSA projector.
