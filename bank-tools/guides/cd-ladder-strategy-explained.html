CD Ladder Strategy Explained

By BankTools · October 2026 · 6 min read

A CD ladder gives you the best of both worlds: long-term CD rates with money freeing up every single year. It takes 20 minutes to set up and runs itself afterward.

How it works

  1. Divide your savings into five equal parts.
  2. Buy 1-, 2-, 3-, 4-, and 5-year CDs at the same time.
  3. When the 1-year CD matures, roll it into a new 5-year CD.
  4. Repeat yearly: from then on, a CD matures every 12 months earning 5-year rates.

When a ladder beats a single CD

  • Rising rates: each maturing rung re-prices at new higher rates.
  • Possible cash needs: yearly maturities mean never paying early-withdrawal penalties.
  • Rate uncertainty: you stop trying to time the 'perfect' lock-in.

When to skip the ladder

  • You need the money within a year — use an HYSA instead.
  • Rates are clearly falling and you want to lock everything long now.
  • The dollar amounts are small enough that the extra yield isn't worth the accounts.

Penalties and fine print

Breaking a CD early typically costs 3–12 months of interest, and some 'no-penalty' CDs pay lower rates for the privilege. Laddering exists precisely so you rarely need to break one.
