GTA 6 Reviews: What Critics Actually Said at Launch

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, to the kind of reviews most games never get. IGN called it a masterpiece. Its review passed 7.44 million views on launch day, at 260,551 views per hour — a quarter of a million people an hour watching one review. But what did the critics actually say, underneath the headline? Not just the praise — the caveats, the frame rate, and the things they warned about. This guide collects what the launch reviews actually said, built only on verified launch reporting, so you can see the full picture instead of the score.

Start with IGN, because it is the review that defined the launch. The praise was specific, not generic: the jaw-dropping night lighting — headlights piercing the dark, city lights from above, beach storms rolling in. The deep car culture — custom neons, engine swaps, named boats. The nuanced wanted system, in which the police act only on what they witness, with no magic GPS. The dual leads, Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos, in a story about escape rather than empire-building. The driving — realistic street widths, real traffic and roadworks, Hollywood-style slides. The world — Vice City and the wider Leonida state, neon beachfront boulevards, beaches, swamps, and countryside, dense and living. That is what "masterpiece" meant: not a perfect game, but a world that felt alive in a way no open world had before.

What the Other Reviews Said

IGN was not alone. GamingBolt's "GTA 6 Review" passed 937,000 views at 32,790 views per hour. TechyCrew's "WE PLAYED GTA 6!" reached 828,000 views. BeastBoyShub's "FINALLY PLAYING GTA 6" hit 441,000 views at 78,494 views per hour — the highest per-hour rate of any long-form video that day after IGN itself. And ohnepixel's raw "GTA 6 IS INSANE..." reached 382,000 views. The consensus across all of them was the same shape: awe at the world, praise for the story and the systems, and the same single caveat, repeated everywhere.

That caveat was the frame rate. GTA 6 runs at 30 fps on every console, and there is no 60 fps performance mode — Rockstar confirmed it to Kotaku the day before launch. The reviews did not treat it as a deal-breaker, but they did not ignore it either. Base PS5 dips below 30 in heavy combat and explosions. The Xbox Series S is roughest, dropping to around 18 to 20 frames per second in shootouts. The praise and the caveat were always together: the most detailed world Rockstar has ever built, at a frame rate that some players will feel in their stomachs.

The Systems the Critics Kept Coming Back To

Three systems came up in every review, and they are the three that change how the game plays. The first is the wanted system: police who act only on what they witness, witnesses who report what they see, and an in-game phone and social media layer that spreads the word. The second is the dual-lead story: two perspectives, one escape, and a structure that makes the consequences feel personal. The third is the world itself: the activities — fishing, pool, side jobs, radio stations — the heists and bank robberies, and the missions that cannot be replayed, which make every choice permanent.

The no-replay rule is the one that divided opinion most. Some reviewers called it the game's bravest choice — a story that means something because the consequences are real. Others called it the game's biggest risk — a mission gone wrong is a mission gone forever, and the only safety net is the manual save. Both are true. GTA 6 gives you fifteen manual save slots — Pause, Game, Save Game, after every mission — and the autosave cannot be trusted: it only fires after a mission ends, the prologue never autosaves, and crashes have already cost players hours, including streamer JasonTheWeen, who lost around ten hours.

What the Reviews Did Not Say

Just as important is what the launch reviews did not say. None of them published a ranked list of the best cars — the game was days old, and the customization is too deep for a definitive ranking yet. None of them published a complete wanted-level breakdown — Rockstar has not released one, and what IGN confirmed is the principle, not the numbers. None of them found a PC version, because there is not one at launch: GTA 6 runs only on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and any "GTA 6 PC download" link is a scam.

The reviews also did not find a 60 fps mode, a mission replay menu, or a fix for the launch bugs — the prologue crashes, the unreliable autosave, the early Lucia mission softlock after leaving a bank. Those are real, and the workarounds are real too: install the latest game update and PS5 system software 26.07-14.23.00, do a full restart, clear the cache in Safe Mode, and reload a manual save from before the broken mission. The reviews praised the game despite these problems, not because they did not exist.

How to Read a Launch Review

Launch reviews are written in the first days, and the first days are the least reliable time to judge a game this big. The systems are deep, the world is dense, and the bugs are still being found. The right way to read them is to separate the verified from the guessed: the night lighting, the car culture, the wanted system, and the dual leads are verified by multiple reviews. The "best car" lists, the "complete wanted level guides", and the "secret locations" are not — they are guesses dressed as guides, and they will be wrong as often as they are right.

Give it a week. The reviews that matter are the ones written after the launch dust settles, when the systems have been tested, the bugs have been worked around, and the world has been lived in. The launch reviews tell you the game is a masterpiece. The second-week reviews will tell you whether it stays one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did IGN really call GTA 6 a masterpiece?

Yes — IGN's review called GTA 6 a masterpiece, and it passed 7.44 million views on launch day at 260,551 views per hour. The praise focused on the night lighting, the car culture, the wanted system, and the dual-lead story.

Did any review mention a 60 fps mode?

No — there is no 60 fps mode. GTA 6 runs at 30 fps on every console, and Rockstar confirmed it to Kotaku the day before launch. The reviews treated it as a caveat, not a deal-breaker.

The Bottom Line

The launch reviews agree: GTA 6 is a masterpiece of a world — jaw-dropping light, deep car culture, a wanted system that only chases what it sees, and two leads in a story about escape. The caveats are real too: 30 fps everywhere, no mission replays, and launch bugs that demand manual saves. Read the reviews for what they verified, ignore the guides that guess, and give the game a week to show you what it really is.
