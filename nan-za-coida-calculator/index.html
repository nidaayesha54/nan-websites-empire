How the COIDA Assessment Works

Every COIDA-registered employer pays an annual assessment to the Compensation Fund, calculated from the Return of Earnings (ROE) declaration:

Assessment fee = (total remuneration ÷ 100) × industry tariff.
The Fund places each employer in an industry class by risk: office and professional services carry the lowest tariffs (around 0.18%), retail and hospitality more (around 0.81%), and construction, transport, security and underground mining the highest (around 2.65%–2.71%).
Earnings ceiling: only the first R633,168 of each employee's annual earnings counts for the 2025/2026 year (1 March 2025 – 28 February 2026); from 1 March 2026 the ceiling rose to R668,000.
Minimum assessment: R1,621 for commercial employers and R560 for household/domestic employers — you pay the minimum even if the formula gives less.

Worked Example

A small construction company with 5 employees on combined annual remuneration of R800,000 (R160,000 each — under the ceiling) at a 1.9% tariff: (R800,000 ÷ 100) × 1.9 = R15,200 for the year. A professional-services firm with 3 employees on R450,000 combined at 0.2% would calculate R900 — but the minimum applies, so it pays R1,621.

Who Must Register?

Every employer with employees must register with the Compensation Fund — sole proprietors with no staff do not. Since the 2020 Constitutional Court ruling, domestic workers are covered too, so households employing domestic staff must register and file ROE like any commercial employer. Registration is free; the assessment is the annual cost.

The Letter of Good Standing

Once your assessment is paid and your ROE is up to date, the Compensation Commissioner issues a Letter of Good Standing — proof of compliance frequently required for tenders, site work and contractor onboarding. Missing the ROE deadline can mean penalties, interest and loss of good standing.

FAQs

When is the ROE due?

The Return of Earnings covers 1 March to the end of February each year and is typically submitted early in the new assessment year — file it as soon as the window opens so the assessment and Letter of Good Standing arrive in time for tender season.

What counts as remuneration for COIDA?

Salaries, wages, overtime payments, bonuses, commissions and other cash earnings — subject to the per-employee ceiling. Earnings above the ceiling (R633,168 for 2025/26, R668,000 from 1 March 2026) are excluded from the calculation.

Where do I find my tariff?

Your industry class and tariff are on your assessment notice from the Compensation Fund. The calculator above uses representative Schedule A rates — always confirm your own class before filing.

Do domestic employers really pay COIDA?

Yes. Since 2020, COIDA covers domestic workers, so households must register, submit a Return of Earnings and pay the annual assessment (minimum R560).

What if I don't register or file?

Non-registration is an offence, the Fund can assess you on estimated earnings, and you lose the Letter of Good Standing — while remaining fully exposed if an employee is injured on duty.

Is the assessment the same as UIF or SDL?

No. UIF (1% + 1% to R17,712/month) and SDL (1% above R500,000 payroll) go to SARS via the EMP201; the COIDA assessment is a separate annual levy paid to the Compensation Fund via the ROE.
