Term vs Whole Life: 30-Year Cost Comparison

Whole life can cost 10–15× more than term. This tool puts real estimated numbers on that gap over 30 years so you can decide with math, not marketing.

Disclaimer: Educational purposes only — not financial advice. Estimates, not quotes.

How the comparison is calculated

We estimate a healthy non-smoker's annual term premium per $1,000 of coverage by age band (roughly $0.60 in your 20s, $0.75 in your 30s, $1.50 in your 40s, $3.60 in your 50s, $9.00 in your 60s), multiply by your coverage, then project over 30 years. Whole life is estimated at 11× the term annual premium — the industry rule of thumb is 10–15×.

  • Term total = annual term premium × term length (you only pay while covered).
  • Whole life total = annual whole premium × 30 (it never expires and builds cash value).
  • The verdict divides the two totals to show the true price multiple.

Note: Term looks dramatically cheaper because it covers a limited window. If you outlive the term and still need coverage, a new policy at an older age costs far more — factor that into long-term planning.
