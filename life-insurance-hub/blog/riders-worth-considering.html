5 Life Insurance Riders Actually Worth Considering in 2026

2026-10-12 · 5 min read

A rider is an add-on that customizes your life insurance policy. Most are upsells — but a handful genuinely earn their keep.

1. Waiver of premium

If you become disabled and can't work, the insurer pays your premiums for you while coverage stays in force. It's usually only a few dollars a month — cheap protection for your protection.

2. Accelerated death benefit

Lets you access part of the death benefit early if diagnosed with a terminal illness. Many insurers now include a basic version for free — check before paying extra.

3. Child rider

A small amount of coverage (often $10k–$25k) for all your children under one rider, usually under $10/month total. It also typically lets each child convert to their own policy later without a medical exam.

4. Guaranteed insurability

Lets you increase coverage at set life events (marriage, new child) without new underwriting. Valuable if your health might decline — you're buying future insurability at today's health status.

5. Long-term care / chronic illness rider

Allows the death benefit to pay for long-term care costs. Standalone LTC insurance is expensive and getting harder to buy; this hybrid approach is worth pricing if you're in your 40s–50s.

Note: Skip: accidental death riders (narrow coverage, poor value) and most "return of premium" add-ons (expensive for what amounts to a forced savings account with low returns).
