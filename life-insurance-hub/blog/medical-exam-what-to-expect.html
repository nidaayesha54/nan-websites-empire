The Life Insurance Medical Exam: What to Expect

2026-10-12 · 5 min read

For the best rates on fully underwritten life insurance, you'll take a free paramedical exam — usually at your home or office, in about 20 minutes. Here's exactly what happens.

What they actually do

  • Record height, weight, blood pressure, and pulse.
  • Draw blood and collect a urine sample.
  • Ask a health questionnaire: medications, conditions, family history, tobacco/alcohol, risky hobbies.
  • Sometimes an EKG for larger policies or older applicants.

What the labs test for

Cholesterol and lipids, blood sugar (diabetes), liver and kidney function, HIV, and nicotine/cotinine — plus drug screening. Everything maps to an underwriting class (preferred, standard, substandard), which sets your price.

How to prepare (it matters)

  1. Fast 8–12 hours before a morning exam for accurate cholesterol/glucose readings.
  2. Avoid alcohol for 48 hours and heavy exercise for 24 hours (both skew liver enzymes and blood pressure).
  3. Drink plenty of water — dehydration concentrates urine and blood markers.
  4. Get good sleep; schedule morning when blood pressure is naturally lower.
  5. Have your medication list and doctors' contact info ready.

Can you skip it?

Yes — simplified-issue and no-exam policies skip the exam but cost 20–40% more and cap coverage lower. If you're reasonably healthy, the 20-minute exam usually pays for itself many times over.

Note: Be honest on the questionnaire. Insurers check prescription databases and medical records — undisclosed conditions found later can void a policy's payout.
