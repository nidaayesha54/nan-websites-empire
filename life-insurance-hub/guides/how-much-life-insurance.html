How Much Life Insurance Do I Need in 2026?

2026-10-12 · 7 min read

Most people either buy far too little life insurance or put off the decision entirely because the math feels intimidating. It isn't. Here is the exact framework financial planners use — and it takes about ten minutes.

The short answer

A common rule of thumb is 10–12 times your annual income, plus your mortgage balance. If you earn $80,000 and owe $250,000 on your home, that's roughly $1–$1.2 million in coverage. It's a decent starting point, but it ignores debts, education costs, and assets you already have.

The DIME method (the thorough way)

Financial planners prefer the DIME method: add up Debts, Income replacement (annual income × years your family needs support), Mortgage balance, and Education costs — then subtract existing assets and coverage. Our DIME life insurance calculator runs this for you in under a minute.

  • Debts: credit cards, car loans, student loans — everything except the mortgage.
  • Income: multiply your salary by 10–20 years (until kids are independent or the mortgage is gone).
  • Mortgage: the full remaining balance so your family can keep the home.
  • Education: projected college costs per child.

Three mistakes that leave families underinsured

  1. Only covering the mortgage. The house is paid off but there's no income replacement — the most common gap.
  2. Forgetting the stay-at-home parent. Childcare, cooking, and household management would cost $50k+/year to replace.
  3. Never updating the policy. A $250k policy bought at 28 rarely fits life at 38 with two kids and a bigger mortgage.

When to revisit your number

Recalculate after every major life event: marriage, a new child, buying a home, a big raise (or job loss), and divorce. Term life is cheap enough that layering a second policy later is usually smarter than guessing perfectly today.

Note: Round up to the nearest standard face amount — policies are sold in round figures like $250k, $500k, and $1M, and the price difference between bands is often tiny.
