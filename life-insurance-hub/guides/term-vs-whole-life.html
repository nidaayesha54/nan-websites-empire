Term vs Whole Life Insurance: Which Is Right for You?

2026-10-12 · 8 min read

This is the most-searched life insurance question in America, and agents have a financial incentive to push you toward the expensive answer. Here's the honest breakdown.

Term life in 30 seconds

Term life covers you for a set period — 10, 20, or 30 years. If you die during the term, your beneficiaries get the payout. If you outlive it, coverage ends and you paid for peace of mind. It's pure protection, no savings component, and it's cheap: a healthy 30-year-old can get $500k for roughly $25–$35/month.

Whole life in 30 seconds

Whole life covers you for your entire life and builds cash value you can borrow against. The catch: it costs roughly 10–15 times more than term for the same death benefit. That same $500k policy might run $400–$500/month.

The real 30-year cost gap

Run your own numbers with our term vs whole 30-year cost comparison tool. A typical result: 20-year term at 35 costs about $7,500 total over the term; whole life costs about $124,000 over 30 years. The most common strategy among fee-only planners: buy term and invest the difference in a retirement account, which historically outperforms whole life's cash value growth.

Who should choose term?

  • Parents who need maximum protection during working/child-raising years.
  • Anyone on a budget — being underinsured with whole life is worse than being well-insured with term.
  • People who already invest separately for retirement.

Who might choose whole life?

  • High-net-worth families using it for estate planning and tax-advantaged wealth transfer.
  • People who need permanent coverage (e.g., a special-needs dependent).
  • Those who've maxed out other tax-advantaged accounts and want another vehicle.

Note: Beware of agents presenting whole life as an "investment." Compare its projected cash-value growth against simply buying term and investing the premium difference — the math rarely favors whole life for middle-income families.
