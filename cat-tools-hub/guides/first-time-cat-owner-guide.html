First-Time Cat Owner Guide

Congratulations — you're about to gain a tiny roommate with strong opinions. This guide covers what to buy, what to schedule, and how the first two weeks should go.

Vet schedule for a new cat

  • Within 72 hours: wellness exam, FeLV/FIV test, deworming, vaccine plan, microchip check.
  • Weeks 1–4: FVRCP vaccine series (kittens), FeLV vaccine if recommended, flea prevention.
  • Month 2–6: spay/neuter if not already done (most shelters do this pre-adoption).
  • Ongoing: annual exam + vaccines; twice-yearly after age 10.

The first two weeks: go slow

Days 1–3: the safe room

Set up ONE room with everything: litter box, food, water, hiding spots. Let the cat come out of the carrier on their own — don't drag them out. Sit in the room reading or scrolling so they learn your smell and voice. Hiding is normal and healthy.

Days 4–7: open the door

Once they're eating, using the box, and exploring the room confidently, leave the safe-room door open. Let them expand territory at their own pace. Start short wand-toy play sessions — play builds trust faster than treats.

Week 2: routine settles in

Set the rhythm: play → feed, morning and evening. Scoop the box daily. This is the pattern for the next 15 years, so build it right. If you haven't yet, calculate their daily food portion so you don't overfeed from day one.

House rules that prevent 90% of problems

  • Litter boxes: one per cat plus one, scooped daily, full change weekly. Use our litter calculator to plan.
  • Scratching: never punish — redirect to a post and reward. Declawing is banned or restricted in many places and causes chronic pain; skip it.
  • Toxic stuff: lilies (deadly even in tiny amounts), chocolate, onions/garlic, essential oils, many houseplants. Google every plant before it enters your home.
  • Windows and balconies: screens on windows, no unsupervised balcony access. "High-rise syndrome" is a real ER category.
  • Indoor life: indoor cats live dramatically longer. Read indoor vs outdoor: the full comparison.

Reading your cat: the 5-minute crash course

  • Slow blink = "I trust you." Slow-blink back. 🧡
  • Tail up with a quiver = happy greeting.
  • Ears flat + tail swishing = overstimulated — stop petting before the "love bite."
  • Kneading = contentment, a leftover kitten behavior.
  • Belly shown = trust, NOT an invitation to rub (usually).
🐾 The golden rule: let the cat set the pace. Trust built slowly in week one becomes a 15-year bond. Rushing creates a cat who hides from you for months.
