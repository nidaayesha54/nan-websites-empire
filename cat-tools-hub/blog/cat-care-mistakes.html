12 Cat Care Mistakes New Owners Make (And How to Fix Them)

Almost every new cat parent makes at least three of these. The good news: every one is easy to fix once you know.

  1. Free-feeding dry food. The bag says "feed freely" because they sell bags, not health. An indoor cat needs a measured portion — get yours from our food portion calculator. Fix: measure twice a day, 10-minute meals.
  2. One litter box per house. The rule is boxes = cats + 1. Under-boxed cats pee on laundry, and it's not spite — it's math. Fix: add boxes; scoop daily.
  3. Skipping the first vet visit. That "healthy" shelter cat still needs an FeLV test, deworming, and a vaccine plan within 72 hours. Fix: book before adoption day.
  4. Punishing scratching. Spraying water teaches the cat to fear you, not the sofa. Scratching is stretching + nail care + marking — it's non-negotiable behavior. Fix: a tall, sturdy post next to the sofa + treats for using it.
  5. Declawing. It's amputation of the last toe bone, causes chronic pain, and is restricted or banned in many US cities and states. Fix: trim nails every 2–3 weeks.
  6. Letting them roam outside unsupervised. The lifespan gap is brutal: 13–17 years indoors vs. 2–5 outdoors. Fix: catio, harness walks, window perches — see indoor vs outdoor.
  7. Ignoring the water bowl. Cats evolved from desert animals and under-drink, which stresses kidneys. Stale water in a plastic bowl doesn't help. Fix: fresh water daily, ceramic/steel bowl, or a fountain — many cats drink far more from running water.
  8. No play routine. Two 10-minute wand sessions a day prevents obesity, 3am zoomies, and "attack the ankles." Fix: play at dawn and dusk, then feed — the hunt-eat-groom-sleep cycle.
  9. Toxic plants and lilies. Lilies can kill a cat from pollen on their fur. Also dangerous: many essential oils, chocolate, onions, garlic. Fix: Google every plant before it enters your home.
  10. Assuming "independent" means "no vet needed." Cats hide illness — it's a survival instinct. By the time a cat looks sick, it's often serious. Fix: annual exams, twice-yearly after 10.
  11. Changing food overnight. A sudden switch = vomiting and diarrhea. Fix: transition over 7–10 days (25/75 → 50/50 → 75/25).
  12. No budget plan. The first emergency vet bill averages $800–$1,500. Fix: know your numbers with the cat cost calculator, and keep a $500+ emergency cushion or insurance.
🐾 Made one of these? Don't feel bad — vets say nearly every new owner does. Fix it today and your cat won't hold a grudge (probably).
