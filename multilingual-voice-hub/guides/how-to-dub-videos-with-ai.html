🎬 How to Dub Videos with AI (2026 Workflow)

Updated October 2026 · 7-minute read

AI dubbing turns one video into five, ten, or twenty — each speaking the viewer's own language. US creators are quietly doubling their audiences by dubbing into Spanish alone. Here is the complete workflow, from script to published dubbed video.

Step 1: Prepare a dubbing-friendly script

Dubbing starts before translation. Short sentences (12–18 words), one idea per sentence, and no untranslatable idioms ("it's raining cats and dogs" becomes "it's raining heavily"). Paste your script into our dubbing planner to get your source word count and spoken duration — you will need both in step 3.

Step 2: Translate — then adapt, don't just translate

Machine translation gets you 80% there; a native speaker (or a careful review pass) gets you the last 20% that keeps viewers watching. Build a translation glossary first: your brand terms, product names, CTAs, and recurring phrases, translated identically every time. Inconsistent terms are the fastest way to sound amateur in a second language.

Step 3: Fix the timing problem

This is where most first dubs fail. Translated speech is rarely the same length as the original: Spanish runs ~20% longer than English, Arabic ~25% longer, while Japanese and Mandarin run ~15–20% shorter. Our dubbing planner calculates per-language word targets and tells you whether to condense the script, raise delivery speed 5–10%, or add pauses and reaction beats to fill scenes.

Step 4: Generate the voices

Pick your tool based on the job (see our 2026 tools comparison):

  • Talking-head with lip-sync: HeyGen or Rask AI — they match mouth movements to the new audio.
  • Narration / voiceover: ElevenLabs or Murf — most natural delivery.
  • Short-form: Captions — dub Reels and Shorts from your phone.

Clone your own voice when the tool supports it — audiences trust a consistent voice across languages far more than a rotating cast of stock voices.

Step 5: Sync, mix, and review

Even the best AI dubbing needs a human review pass: watch each language version at full speed, check that emphasis lands on the right words, and confirm background music and sound effects sit correctly under the new voice. Budget 30 minutes of review per 10 minutes of video — this is the step that separates professional dubs from obvious AI output.

Step 6: Disclose the AI — it's required

YouTube requires disclosure for content with AI-altered voices or lip-sync (check the "altered content" box in YouTube Studio). TikTok and Meta have similar AI-label rules. Disclose every time: undisclosed synthetic dubbing risks demonetization or removal, and honest labeling does not hurt performance — viewers care about value, not how the voice was made.

Step 7: Publish and measure per language

Publish each language as its own video (or use YouTube's multi-language audio tracks for one video with many dubs). Track watch time per language for 30 days, then double down on the winners. Most US creators find Spanish dubs deliver the fastest ROI — 43+ million US Spanish speakers are waiting.

Quick checklist

  • Script written dubbing-friendly (short sentences, no idioms)
  • Glossary built and shared with translators
  • Word targets + timing calculated per language
  • Voice cloned for consistency across languages
  • Full-speed human review of every language version
  • AI disclosure set on every platform
