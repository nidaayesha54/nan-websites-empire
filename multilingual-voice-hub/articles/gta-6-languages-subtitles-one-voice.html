GTA 6 Languages: 13 Subtitles, One Voice

November 20, 2026 · 4 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and one of the most-asked questions about it has a short answer that surprises people: the game supports 13 languages, but it speaks only one of them. Every voice in Leonida is English. The other twelve languages live in the text — menus, HUD and subtitles — while Lucia, Jason and everyone around them sound the same in Tokyo, Warsaw and Sao Paulo alike. This guide lists all 13 languages, explains how to switch yours, and answers the question everyone asks: is there a dub?

The list comes from the store listings themselves, not from a Rockstar announcement. The Xbox Store listing separates languages into audio, interface and subtitles, and the Rockstar Store's own product page carries the same 13 under its supported-languages block. The 13 are English, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Polish, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Spanish (Mexico) and Spanish (Spain). Take-Two CEO Strauss Zelnick confirmed the 13 languages at the company's shareholder meeting on September 17, 2026. The only visible change from GTA V's list is a rename: what GTA V called "Spanish (Latin America)" is now "Spanish (Mexico)" — a fitting change for a game starring Lucia Caminos. Nothing was dropped, nothing was added.

Why nobody gets a dub

There is no dubbing in French, Spanish, German or any other language, and that is not a GTA 6 cutback. It is how Rockstar has always done it. Rockstar has never dubbed a mainline GTA — not San Andreas, not GTA V — and Red Dead Redemption 2 did not get a dub either. The usual explanation is the one animators give: the performances are the game. Lucia and Jason were shot with full performance capture — face, body and voice recorded together — and replacing the audio track would throw away half of what the actors did. When an eyebrow, a pause and a line land as one take, you subtitle it, you do not paint over it. So when you hear Lucia in the trailers, that is how the whole world hears her. The subtitles are where your language lives. A fan-made recreation of GTA V with Spanish voices exists, but it has nothing to do with Rockstar and changes nothing about what has been announced.

The full list, and Lucia's Spanish

Here is the complete list, so you can check your language before you buy: English, French, German, Italian, Japanese, Korean, Polish, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, Simplified Chinese, Traditional Chinese, Spanish (Spain) and Spanish (Mexico). The two Spanish versions matter more than they look. Spanish (Spain) and Spanish (Mexico) are separate translations, and the Mexico version is the one aimed at Latin American players — a fitting choice for a game starring Lucia Caminos, a Latina lead in a Miami-inspired city. The setting plays a part in the language too: Leonida is Rockstar's take on Florida, and much of the flavor comes from accents, slang and the mix of English and Spanish you would hear in a city like Miami. Lucia herself is Latina, which is one more reason Rockstar is unlikely to replace the original performances with dubs — the Spanish you hear in the game is part of the English voice track, not a separate recording.

That is the heart of it. The game speaks one language and reads in thirteen, and the split is deliberate. Performance capture records face, body and voice together, and the performances are the game — an eyebrow, a pause and a line landing as one take. Subtitles carry your language; the voices carry the acting. If you play in French, German or Japanese, you will hear Lucia and Jason in English and read them in your own language, exactly as players of GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2 did. And if your language is not among the thirteen, the honest answer is to check your store listing before you buy, because on consoles there is no way to add it yourself.

How to switch, and what is missing

On consoles, Rockstar games traditionally follow the system language. Set your PS5 or Xbox to the language you want and the game picks it up — set the console to Spanish and you get Spanish menus and subtitles over the English voices. Subtitle display and language choice can also be separate settings: on PS5, open Pause, then Settings, then UI, then Subtitles and Captions, to change subtitle size, the background and speaker names. One honest caveat: Rockstar has not shown GTA 6's own settings menu yet, so an in-game language picker cannot be confirmed either way, and regional listings can differ — the UAE PlayStation Store listing for the Ultimate Edition lists 12 screen languages and omits Japanese. The part that stings is what is not on the list at all. No Turkish, no Arabic, no Ukrainian, no Hindi, no Dutch, no European Portuguese. If your language is not among the 13, there is no mod folder on consoles to fix it — check the languages on your store listing before you buy, and know that publishers do sometimes add languages after release, though Rockstar has promised nothing.
