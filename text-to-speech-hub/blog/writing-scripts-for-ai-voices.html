Writing Scripts for AI Voices: 7 Rules That Work

Oct 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Creators obsess over which TTS engine to use, then feed it a script written for silent reading. The script is 80% of the result. These seven rules fix that.

1. One idea per sentence

Written prose loves compound-complex sentences. AI voices don't. Split them: "Because the market shifted, companies that adapted quickly survived" becomes "The market shifted. The companies that adapted quickly? They survived." Same meaning, twice the impact.

2. Write numbers the way they're spoken

TTS engines guess at digits — badly. Write "twenty twenty-six" instead of "2026", "half past three" instead of "3:30" when the exact format doesn't matter, or wrap exact figures in <say-as> tags via our SSML helper.

3. Use contractions everywhere

"You will love this" sounds like a textbook. "You'll love this" sounds like a person. Go through your draft and contract every "do not", "cannot", and "it is" — unless you're deliberately aiming for formal.

4. Plant signposts for listeners

Readers can scan back; listeners can't. Use explicit structure: "First…", "Here's why that matters…", "The bottom line is…". These phrases also give TTS engines natural intonation cues.

5. Read it aloud yourself first

If you stumble reading your own script, the AI will too. Read every draft out loud once — anywhere you pause, add a comma or an SSML break; anywhere you stress a word, add emphasis.

6. Front-load the hook

Spoken intros have about 5 seconds before listeners decide. Put the payoff first: "This one setting doubles your voiceover quality" beats "In today's video, we're going to discuss various settings…"

7. End sentences on strong words

English intonation falls at the end of statements. Ending on a filler word ("…you know?") or preposition leaves the voice hanging awkwardly. Rewrite so the final word carries weight.
