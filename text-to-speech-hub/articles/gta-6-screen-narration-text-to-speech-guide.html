GTA 6 Screen Narration: The Text-to-Speech Setting Explained

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Many GTA 6 players meet the game's text-to-speech voice before they meet the game itself. On a fresh start, the first setup screen offers a narration switch, and if it gets turned on by accident, every menu starts talking. The voice reading your menus aloud is called Screen Narration, and it is a deliberate accessibility feature — not a glitch, and not something stuck in your console. This guide explains what it does, where the switch lives, and how to turn it on or off in seconds.

Screen Narration is the part of GTA 6's accessibility options that matters most to players who cannot read the screen easily, and to everyone else it is the setting they most often need to find in a hurry. Either way, the answer is the same menu, and it takes less than a minute once you know where to look.

What Screen Narration does

Screen Narration reads on-screen text aloud for most menus and the game's interface. Turn it on, and a synthetic voice speaks the menu items, the settings labels, and the on-screen prompts as you move through them. The game describes the option in its own words as narrating on-screen text for most menus and UI — and it adds one important limit: the narration is only available in English, even if you play the game in another language.

The game offers the feature on your very first start, before screens such as the minimap choice. At the bottom right of that first setup screen it reads Screen Narration (Off) with a button beside it — Square on PlayStation — and pressing it switches the narrator on or off right there. If you tapped that button without meaning to, that is why your menus are being read to you, and the Audio tab switch below undoes it at any time.

How to turn it on or off

Press Options on PlayStation or Menu on Xbox to open the pause menu, then select Settings. Move to the Audio tab with R1 on PlayStation or RB on Xbox — the tabs run Control Scheme, Controller, Gameplay, Display, UI, Camera and then Audio. Scroll all the way to the bottom, where the last section is called Narration, and switch Screen Narration to On or Off. That is the whole process, and it works at any point in the game.

If a voice is still reading out your console's own menus after you leave the game, that is not GTA 6 at all — it is your console's screen reader, which has its own switch in the PlayStation or Xbox accessibility settings. The in-game switch only controls the voice inside GTA 6.

Audio Descriptions and GPS Voice

Narration is not the only spoken option in the Audio tab. Sitting right under Screen Narration in the same Narration section is Audio Descriptions, a separate toggle that describes what is happening on screen during play. It is off by default, and it is a different feature from the menu narration above it — one reads the menus, the other describes the action.

GPS Voice is the third spoken option, and it lives in the Setup section at the top of the Audio tab. It is the voice that reads out driving directions as you follow a route, and it is off by default, with four options to choose from. Switch it on if you keep missing turns while driving, and switch it off if you would rather navigate by the minimap alone.

The rest of the accessibility audio

The Audio tab holds more than voices. Subtitles and captions can be resized, given a background, and shown with speaker names so you know who is talking. A tinnitus-reduction toggle cuts the ringing effect after explosions, and a mono audio toggle helps if you play with one earbud or have hearing loss in one ear. There are separate volume sliders for dialogue, music, sound effects, UI and media, so you can raise the dialogue if the music drowns out conversations.

Beyond audio, the game has a high contrast mode that shows characters, enemies and interactable items in contrasting colours, and there are no difficulty levels — instead, the Gameplay tab has Accessible Combat, which makes enemies weaker and lowers the damage you take, plus Reduced Vehicle Damage for gentler getaways. There is also help text and reminders, and you can review mission objectives and recent dialogue at any time.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why is GTA 6 reading the menus out loud?

Screen Narration is on. It can be switched on with a single button press on the first setup screen, so it is easy to enable by accident. Turn it off in Settings, under the Audio tab, in the Narration section at the bottom.

Does narration work in other languages?

No. The game says Screen Narration is only available in English, even when the rest of the game is set to another language.

Is there a separate audio description track?

Yes. Audio Descriptions sits directly under Screen Narration in the same Narration section, and it describes on-screen action rather than reading menus.

The bottom line: Screen Narration is GTA 6's built-in text-to-speech voice for its menus, it is English only, and the switch is one scroll away in the Audio tab. Turn it on if you need it, turn it off if you tapped it by accident, and check the toggles underneath it before you assume the game is broken.
