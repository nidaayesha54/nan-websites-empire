How to Make Text-to-Speech Sound Natural

Robotic TTS isn't a voice problem — it's a technique problem. Professional voiceover artists using ElevenLabs or Murf follow the same playbook every time. Here are the 8 techniques, from highest to lowest impact.

1. Write for the ear, not the eye

Text written for reading sounds stiff when spoken. Rewrite with contractions ("you'll" not "you will"), spoken numbers ("twenty twenty-six" not "2026"), and conversational transitions ("here's the thing…"). Aim for a Flesch Reading Ease of 70+ — our readability scorer checks this in seconds. Short sentences are the single biggest naturalness lever: cap them at 20 words.

2. Add pauses with SSML breaks

Humans breathe; robots don't. Insert <break time="400ms"/> at commas, 600–800ms before reveals, and full <p> paragraph breaks between sections. Strategic silence makes the words around it feel intentional. Build snippets instantly with our SSML helper.

3. Match the voice to the content

A deep dramatic voice reading a kids' story — or a bubbly voice reading a legal disclaimer — breaks immersion instantly. Ask: who would a human producer cast for this? Then pick the tone with our voice style guide and keep the same voice across an entire series for brand consistency.

4. Tune rate and pitch deliberately

Defaults are compromises. Tutorials land best at 90–95% rate (listeners need time to follow steps); energetic intros shine at 105–110%; bedtime stories drop to 80% with pitch slightly lowered. Change one variable at a time and A/B test on real listeners — what sounds fine to you at your desk may drag on a commute.

5. Fix every pronunciation once, forever

Nothing exposes AI faster than a mangled brand name. Build a pronunciation glossary: test each tricky word, then lock it with <sub alias="…"> or phoneme tags. Phone numbers get <say-as interpret-as="digits">; acronyms get spelled out ("C. E. O."). Reuse the glossary across projects.

6. Emphasize like a human, not a robot

Humans stress content words — verbs, nouns, surprising adjectives — never prepositions. Wrap 1–2 key words per sentence in <emphasis level="moderate">. Over-emphasis sounds theatrical; under-emphasis sounds flat. Read the sentence aloud yourself first and mark where you naturally stress.

7. Break long scripts into scenes

A 20-minute render in one pass drifts in energy and pacing. Split scripts into 2–3 minute scenes, render each separately, and normalize loudness in a free editor like Audacity. This also makes fixes cheap: re-render one scene, not the whole episode.

8. Always do a full 1x listen-through

TTS misreads are subtle — a wrong homonym ("read" vs "read"), an awkward pause, a name flipped. Listen to every final render at normal speed before publishing. It's 10 minutes of work that protects your credibility with every listener.

The 10-minute naturalness checklist

  1. Script scores 70+ on Flesch Reading Ease ✓
  2. Sentences under 20 words ✓
  3. Voice matches content tone ✓
  4. Breaks added at commas and reveals ✓
  5. Key words emphasized (1–2 per sentence) ✓
  6. Pronunciations tested and locked ✓
  7. Rate/pitch tuned for the format ✓
  8. Full listen-through at 1x ✓
