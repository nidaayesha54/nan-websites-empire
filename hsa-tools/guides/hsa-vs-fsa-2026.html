HSA vs FSA in 2026: Which Is Better?

By HSATools · October 2026 · 6 min read

HSAs and FSAs both let you pay medical costs with pre-tax dollars, but they disagree on almost everything else. Pick the wrong one and you could forfeit hundreds of dollars — or miss the best tax shelter in the code.

Eligibility: the first filter

An HSA requires a qualifying high-deductible health plan (HDHP). No HDHP, no HSA — decision made. An FSA (healthcare FSA) is offered by your employer alongside any plan type, so it's available to far more people.

The HSA's limits are dramatically higher, especially for families — and only the HSA lets you invest the balance for long-term growth.

Use-it-or-lose-it vs keep-it-forever

This is the big one. FSA funds generally expire at year-end (some plans allow a ~$660 rollover or a 2.5-month grace period). HSA funds roll over forever, stay yours if you change jobs, and can be invested. If there's any chance you'll switch employers, the HSA's portability is a superpower.

The tax treatment, side by side

Both skip federal income tax on contributions. HSA payroll contributions also skip FICA (Social Security/Medicare) taxes; FSA contributions skip FICA too. The HSA pulls ahead on growth: FSA money can't be invested, HSA money can compound tax-free for decades.

So which should you choose?

  • Choose the HSA if you have an HDHP, want to invest long-term, might change jobs, or are in a higher tax bracket.
  • Choose the FSA if you have no HDHP, your costs are small and predictable, and you'll spend it all this year.
  • Use both? With an HDHP you can pair an HSA with a limited-purpose FSA for dental and vision only — check your plan documents.
