GTA 6 BuckMe: Your Bank, Your ATMs and Your Savings

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and it has a banking system that is stricter than any GTA before it. Your money lives in three places: the cash in your pocket, the loot in your duffel bag, and the balance in your BuckMe account — and only one of those three is safe. The cash in your pocket is lost when you die. The loot in your bag has to be sold to a fence before it becomes money at all. And the bank is the only place where your earnings are protected, which is why learning how BuckMe works is the first financial lesson in Leonida.

That matters because GTA 6 makes banking a mechanic, not a menu. You cannot deposit money from your phone. You have to walk to an ATM. Your checking account has a limit. Your savings account locks money away forever. And the game teaches you all of it in a single early mission, in a bank in Key Lento, before you have earned enough for any of it to matter — which is exactly when you need to learn it.

Opening the Account: The Buck Me Mission

BuckMe starts in Chapter 1, in the mission called Buck Me, when Jason and Lucia go to the bank together. The bank is a Fleeca branch in Key Lento, according to PowerPyx's walkthrough, and the mission ends at Jason's house, where the BuckMe app is set up on the phone. It comes with a $100 welcome gift, and the first thing the game asks you to do with it is use Buck Yourself to move that $100 into Family Savings — a small transfer that teaches you the most important rule in the whole system before you have any real money to lose.

Before that mission, there is no account at all. Everything you earn is cash, and the only safe place for it is under the loose floorboards in Jason's bedroom: walk up to the bed, press Triangle at the Stash prompt, select Cash, set the amount, and deposit it. After Buck Me, the ATMs take over. Cash goes in at an ATM, and money comes back out as cash at an ATM when you need it in hand — and the BuckMe app on the phone, which opens with Up on the D-pad, has an ATM Finder that marks the nearest machine for you.

The Three Rules: Cash, Checking and Savings

BuckMe has three rules, and they are all about limits. First, cash has a ceiling: shops take pocket cash only up to $999, and anything that costs more is paid through BuckMe. Second, cash is at risk: you lose your pocket cash when you die, and the hit to your checking balance is smaller — which is why the game's own advice is to deposit cash before doing anything dangerous. Third, checking has a limit: when it is full, incoming payments wait until you spend money or move some of it to savings.

Family Savings is the strange one. It is money you put away, and you cannot take it back out — ever. Money in Family Savings can never be withdrawn, and it can never be lost, which makes it the safest place in the game and the least useful one at the same time. The reason to use it is that savings goals raise your checking limit and add perks: according to one walkthrough, $5,000 saved raises the checking limit to $15,000, $10,000 saved raises it to $100,000 with 20% off RydeMe, and BuckMe Premium is assigned at the start of Chapter 4, with a $2,000,000 limit and 10% off liveries. Those tier figures come from a single source, so treat them as reported rather than confirmed — but the principle is the same in every account: savings you cannot touch buy you a bigger checking account you can spend from.

The ATMs and the $20,000 Question

Leonida has 454 ATMs, and they are the only way money gets into the bank. Previews describe ATMs as the only way in, with no option to send pocket cash to the bank from the phone, so a big take stays exposed until you find a machine — and the walk from the store to the ATM is where you are most likely to lose the money. Rockstar's own presenters and four separate preview breakdowns agree on that mechanism, and Bloomberg's Jason Schreier added a first-hand data point in October, watching Jason walk cash to an ATM during a Rockstar North demo. An ATM card even exists as its own inventory item, though whether it can be lost or lifted is a question nobody has answered yet.

Then there is the number everyone quotes and nobody can source: a $20,000 cap on the cash in your pocket. The figure is widely repeated online, and HUD analysis of Rockstar's Extended Look footage points to it — go over it and the extra has to be deposited in your bank. But several previews say Rockstar never gave a figure, and the finance guides are careful about it: a limit of $20,000 on carried cash is widely quoted in videos and guides, but it does not trace back to Rockstar. Treat it as reported, not confirmed — and whatever the real number is, the rule is the same: when your pockets are full, walk to an ATM.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you withdraw money from Family Savings in GTA 6?

No. Money in Family Savings can never be withdrawn and can never be lost. It exists to raise your checking limit and unlock perks, not to be spent.

How do you deposit money in GTA 6?

Walk up to any ATM and pay the cash you are carrying into your BuckMe account. You cannot deposit from the phone — only at a machine. Before the Buck Me mission, stash cash under the floorboards in Jason's bedroom.

What happens when your checking account hits its limit?

Incoming pay is held until you spend money or move some of it to Family Savings. Savings goals raise the checking limit, so saving is how you make room for more income.

The Bottom Line

BuckMe is GTA 6's bank, and its rules are simple: pocket cash is at risk, checking has a limit, and savings are locked away forever in exchange for a bigger account. Walk your cash to an ATM, keep your spending money in checking, and put the rest in Family Savings before a dangerous job — because in Leonida, the money in your pocket is the money you can lose, and the money in the bank is the only money that is really yours.
