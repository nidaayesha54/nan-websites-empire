Bangladesh Overtime Rules: Double Pay, Hour Limits & Your Rights

Overtime in Bangladesh is paid at double the ordinary rate: the legal limits, the calculation formula, and what to do if underpaid.

In-depth 2026 guide~6 min readFree

Bangladesh has one of the most worker-friendly overtime rules in South Asia: every overtime hour must be paid at twice the ordinary rate. No 1.5× compromise, no "time off in lieu" substitute — double pay, set down in Section 108 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006. Yet underpayment is widespread, usually because the "ordinary rate" is calculated on the wrong salary base, or because workers don't know the legal hour limits that trigger the double rate in the first place.

This guide covers the overtime rules end to end: the Section 108 formula, the Section 100/102 hour limits, how to derive your hourly rate correctly, and what to do when the numbers on your pay slip don't add up. Check your own figures with our Bangladesh Overtime Calculator.

The core rule: Section 108 — double the ordinary rate

Section 108 ("Extra-allowance for overtime") provides that where a worker works more than the hours fixed under the Act on any day or in any week, the worker is entitled to an overtime allowance at twice the ordinary rate of basic wage and dearness allowance (plus any ad-hoc or interim wage).

The critical phrase is basic wage and dearness allowance — not gross salary. The legally correct overtime base excludes house rent, medical allowance, conveyance, and other allowances. This is the single biggest source of disputes:

  • Correct: overtime hourly rate = (monthly basic + DA) ÷ standard monthly hours × 2
  • Common error (overpays): using gross salary as the base inflates the rate
  • Common error (underpays you): using an outdated basic, or a "basic" artificially shrunk by loading allowances

The standard divisor: monthly hours are typically derived from the 8-hour day — (basic ÷ 30 ÷ 8) gives the ordinary hourly rate, which is then doubled. A worker with a BDT 20,000 basic works out to BDT 83.33/hour ordinary, BDT 166.67 per overtime hour.

When does overtime start? The legal hour limits

Overtime is not just "staying late" — it is any work beyond the statutory limits:

LimitRule (Labour Act, 2006)
Normal working day8 hours (Section 100); extendable to 10 hours with overtime paid
Normal working week48 hours (Section 102)
Maximum with overtime60 hours/week, and an average of 56/week over the year must not be exceeded
Spread-overWork + rest intervals must not spread over more than 10 hours (Section 105)
Weekly holiday1.5 days for shops/commercial/industrial establishments; 1 day for factories (Section 103)

So the 9th and 10th hours of a day are overtime hours (paid double), and anything beyond 48 hours in a week is overtime even if no single day exceeded 8 hours. Work on your weekly holiday is overtime too. The 60-hour weekly ceiling is absolute — no amount of pay makes hours beyond it lawful — and the 56-hour annual average prevents employers from running workers at the maximum indefinitely.

Rest intervals and night work

Section 101 requires at least a 1-hour rest interval after 6 hours of work (or half an hour after 5 hours). These intervals are not counted as working time for the overtime calculation — but they do count toward the 10-hour "spread-over" limit, which is why a nominally 8-hour day with long breaks can still breach the rules.

Women workers have additional protections: Section 109 provides that no woman shall be required to work between 10 p.m. and 6 a.m. without her consent. Where night work is agreed, the overtime provisions apply to it in the same way.

The overtime register: your employer's legal duty

Section 108 empowers the government to prescribe a register of overtime that every establishment must maintain. In practice, this means your employer should be recording overtime hours systematically — not reconstructing them from memory at payroll time. If your workplace has no overtime register, that is itself a compliance failure, and it weakens any dispute about how many hours you worked.

Protect yourself: keep your own contemporaneous record — entry/exit times, a photo of the attendance register, or even a dated notebook. In a dispute before the Labour Court, consistent personal records carry real weight against an employer's bare denial.

Worked example: a full month's overtime

Take a factory worker with a monthly basic of BDT 18,000 and dearness allowance of BDT 2,000 (overtime base: BDT 20,000). She works 2 extra hours daily for 22 working days = 44 overtime hours.

  • Ordinary hourly rate: 20,000 ÷ 30 ÷ 8 = BDT 83.33
  • Overtime hourly rate: 83.33 × 2 = BDT 166.67
  • Overtime pay for the month: 44 × 166.67 = BDT 7,333

That is more than a third of her basic — which is exactly why employers have an incentive to miscalculate the base, and why verifying it matters. Run your own numbers in the calculator and compare against your pay slip line by line.

What to do if you're underpaid

1. Verify the base first. Most disputes dissolve here. Ask payroll — in writing — which figure they use as the overtime base and how they derive the hourly rate. Compare it against your appointment letter's basic.

2. Check the hour count. Compare your own records against the pay slip. Missing hours are more common than wrong rates.

3. Raise it internally in writing. Frame it as a query, not an accusation: "My calculation under Section 108 gives X; my pay slip shows Y — could you help me understand the difference?" Written queries create a paper trail.

4. Escalate to DIFE. The Department of Inspection for Factories and Establishments inspects overtime compliance and can order payment of arrears.

5. Labour Court. Unpaid overtime is a recoverable wage claim. Keep every pay slip — they are your primary evidence.

A final note on "off the clock" work: any work the employer suffers or permits counts. If your supervisor expects you at the machine at 8 a.m. but the register opens at 8:30, those 30 minutes are working time, and the overtime rules apply to them like any other hours.

FAQs

Can my employer give time off instead of overtime pay?

The Act provides for overtime allowance — monetary payment at double the rate. "Time off in lieu" is not a statutory substitute under Section 108, though it may exist in company policy for hours within the normal limits.

Does overtime apply to monthly-rated (salaried) staff?

Yes. The Act's overtime provisions apply to "workers" as defined in the Act regardless of whether pay is calculated hourly, daily, or monthly — the hourly rate is derived from the monthly basic.

Are managers entitled to overtime?

The Act's definition of "worker" excludes persons employed in a managerial or administrative capacity. True managers fall outside Section 108; misclassified supervisors may still be covered.

What about work on festival holidays?

The Act provides 11 festival holidays with full wages (Section 118). Work performed on these days beyond normal hours attracts the overtime provisions; many establishments also have enhanced holiday policies.

Is there a daily overtime cap?

A worker may work up to 10 hours a day (8 normal + 2 overtime). Beyond 10 hours in a day is not permitted even with overtime pay, and the 60-hour weekly ceiling is absolute.
