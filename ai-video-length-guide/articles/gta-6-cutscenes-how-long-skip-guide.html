GTA 6 Cutscenes: How Long Are They and Can You Skip Them?

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

GTA 6 is a long game with a lot of story in it — and a lot of that story arrives as cutscenes, the cinematic sequences where you put the controller down and watch. Some players love them. Some players want to skip straight to the action. Either way, the same question comes up in the first hour: how long are the cutscenes, and can you skip them?

The short answers are that the cutscenes are long, yes you can skip them, and you should think twice before you do. There is no mission replay in GTA 6, so a scene you skip is a scene you will not see again unless you reload an old save. This guide covers the skip controls, how long the story actually takes, and how to watch the cutscenes without losing them.

How to skip a cutscene

A cutscene in GTA 6 can be skipped by holding X on PlayStation or A on Xbox. Hold the button — do not just tap it — and the scene fast-forwards past the cinematic and drops you back into gameplay. It is the same kind of hold-to-skip the series has used before, and it works on story cutscenes throughout the campaign.

There is one trap to avoid. Opening the menu or the map by mistake does not show the skip option, and players who pause in the middle of a scene sometimes think the skip is missing. It is not missing — it is tied to the hold prompt, not the pause menu. If you want out of a scene, hold the button and wait for the skip to engage.

How long is the story?

The campaign takes 50 to 100 hours, according to IGN, which played the finished game — and one reviewer's first playthrough took about 70 hours. That range covers the main story plus the side content most players will do along the way, and it makes GTA 6 one of the longest campaigns Rockstar has made. The cutscenes are a meaningful part of that time: reviews describe lengthy cinematic sequences that carry the story, especially in the opening hours, before the game opens up and hands you control of Jason's day-to-day life.

The opening is the slowest part. The first hours are heavy with story — Lucia's release from prison, Jason's life in the Keys, the meeting that sets the whole plot in motion — and the game asks you to watch before it lets you roam. Once the world opens up, the balance shifts toward play, but the story never stops interrupting with scenes. If you skip them all, you will finish faster and understand less, and the game is built on the assumption that you are watching.

The trailers: how long is each video?

Rockstar released four videos for GTA 6, and their lengths tell you something about how the studio tells a story. The first trailer, from December 2023, runs about 1 minute 31 seconds — a short, punchy introduction that gave the world its first look at Lucia and Vice City. The second trailer, from May 2025, runs about 2 minutes 47 seconds and introduces Jason properly, with the two leads sharing the frame for the first time.

The longest video is the Extended Look showcase from August 27, 2026, which runs 26 minutes 48 seconds. It is not a trailer — it is a full gameplay showcase, blending story scenes, a 36-second gameplay reel and a two-minute activity montage, and it is the single best preview of what the game actually plays like. The launch trailer from October 2026 completes the set. Four videos, four different lengths, one story told in pieces.

No mission replay: why skipping costs you

The most important fact about cutscenes in GTA 6 is not the skip button — it is that the game does not let you replay story missions from a menu. That feature, common in Rockstar's recent games, is absent at launch, and there are no bronze, silver or gold medals for mission performance either. If you skip a cutscene, the only way to see it again is to reload a save from before it played.

That is why the game gives you 15 manual save slots, and why IGN recommends making a new save roughly every 7% of the story. Keep a spread of saves across the campaign and a skipped scene costs you a reload, not a lost moment. Save before the missions that matter, and keep one slot free for the finale.

Watching without losing the thread

If you do want to watch everything, the game helps you. Subtitles can be resized, given a background and labelled with speaker names, so dialogue stays readable even in fast scenes. The screen narration setting reads on-screen text aloud, and the audio descriptions toggle adds description for visual moments. Both sit in the Audio section of Settings, and both are worth turning on if you watch cutscenes the way you would watch a film.

And if you do skip, skip with a plan. Skip the scenes you have already seen on a second playthrough, or the ones in a mission you are replaying for a better result — not the first-time story beats. The cutscenes are where Jason and Lucia become people instead of avatars, and the game is at its best when you let them talk.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you skip cutscenes in GTA 6?

Yes. Hold X on PlayStation or A on Xbox to skip a cutscene. Opening the menu or map by mistake does not show the skip option — use the hold prompt.

How long is GTA 6's story?

50 to 100 hours per IGN, with about 70 hours for one reviewer's first playthrough. The opening hours are the most cutscene-heavy part of the game.

Can you rewatch cutscenes in GTA 6?

There is no mission replay at launch, so a skipped scene is gone unless you reload an older save. Keep saves across the campaign — 15 manual slots, one roughly every 7% of the story.

The bottom line: the skip button is there, and it works — but in a game with no mission replay, every skip is permanent. Watch the first time, skip the second.
