Video Length Benchmarks 2026: What the Data Actually Says

Every year creators ask the same question — "how long should my video be?" — and every year the honest answer is the same: as long as it's interesting, not a second longer. But platform data does reveal clear patterns. Here's what the 2026 benchmarks show across the four feeds that matter.

TikTok: the 21–34 second band

Completion rate is TikTok's heaviest ranking signal, and it peaks in the 21–34 second window. Shorter clips finish before viewers invest; longer ones bleed completion unless the narrative is airtight. The 1–3 minute format works for storytimes and tutorials, but only when average watch time clears ~50% — a bar most padded videos miss.

Instagram Reels: 18–30 seconds, looped

Reels skew slightly shorter, and the loop matters more here than anywhere else. A clean 20-second loop can post 150–200% average watch percentages — the single strongest signal you can send the Explore algorithm.

YouTube Shorts: 25–35 seconds

Shorts under 40 seconds dominate the shelf, but YouTube's audience tolerates — and rewards — slightly more substance than TikTok's. The 25–35 second band balances feed velocity with meaningful average view duration.

YouTube long-form: 8–12 minutes

The classic holds: 8–12 minutes supports mid-roll ads while sustaining 40–50% retention with good pacing. Past 15 minutes, only strong chapter structure and pattern interrupts every ~90 seconds keep viewers aboard.
