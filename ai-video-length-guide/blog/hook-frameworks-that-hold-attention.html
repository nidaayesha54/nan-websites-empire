5 Hook Frameworks That Hold Attention Past 3 Seconds

Great hooks aren't inspiration — they're engineering. These five frameworks show up in viral videos across every niche because they exploit how the scrolling brain works. Steal the structure, fill in your topic.

1. Payoff-first

"This $3 trick fixed my sleep in 2 nights." Lead with the result, then explain. The brain hates an unresolved promise and will wait to see how you got there.

2. The mistake callout

"You're doing step 2 of your routine wrong." People will watch anything that might reveal they're making an error — loss aversion is stronger than curiosity.

3. Contrarian reframe

"Stop saving money — do this instead." A credible-sounding reversal of common advice forces a pause. You must actually deliver the reframe, or trust collapses.

4. Mid-action cold open

Start at the most visual second of your video, then rewind to explain. Movement in frame one is the purest scroll-stopper there is.

5. The ranked list tease

"I tested 5 methods — #2 shocked me." Rankings create a built-in reason to watch to the end, and the teased surprise anchors retention through the middle.
