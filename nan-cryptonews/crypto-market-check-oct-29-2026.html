Crypto Market Check (29 Oct 2026): Bitcoin Dips Below $80,000 After the Fed Holds Rates

29 October 2026 · Market Check

Bitcoin slipped below $80,000 during Thursday's Asian trading session on 29 October 2026, reversing a two-day rebound, after the US Federal Reserve held interest rates steady on 28 October and a tech-led sell-off spread from Asian equities into crypto. No new Binance spot listing was announced for 29 October itself — so today's article is a market check built on that day's reported data, not a listing story.

What the Fed decided on 28 October

The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) kept the federal funds target range at 3.75%–4.00% at its October meeting, in a 10–2 vote. Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented, preferring a quarter-percentage-point rate increase. The FOMC statement upgraded its assessment of economic activity from "solid" to "strong", while repeating that inflation "remains elevated" against its 2% target. Market reports described the hold as hawkish: rates unchanged, but with little in the statement to suggest an immediate move toward easing, and with December rate-hike talk still alive in traders' pricing.

Why Bitcoin fell on 29 October

CoinDesk reported that the sell-off began in Asia, where Samsung shares fell more than 5% after its semiconductor division missed elevated investor expectations: operating profit surged to 106.5 trillion won for the three months through September, but that fell just short of the average analyst estimate of 108.5 trillion won. South Korea's Kospi fell more than 2% and Nasdaq futures declined 1.4%. That weakness rippled into crypto, dragging Bitcoin, Ether, XRP, Solana and other tokens lower. Bitcoin fell from above $81,700 to around $79,800, according to CoinDesk.

Liquidation figures reported for the sell-off differ by data provider and time window, so they are given here separately rather than combined: CoinDesk reported $356 million in crypto futures liquidated over 24 hours, with leveraged long traders bearing the brunt; CoinEdition, citing CoinGlass data, reported $172 million in liquidations over 24 hours, split $116 million in shorts and $56.14 million in longs, with the largest single liquidation, $6.35 million, hitting a BTC-USD position on Hyperliquid.

Binance deadlines landing on 29–30 October

EPIC and QUICK — deposit cut-off on 29 October. Following the delisting of Epic Chain (EPIC) and QuickSwap (QUICK) spot trading on 28 October at 03:00 UTC, deposits of those tokens stopped being credited after 29 October at 03:00 UTC. Withdrawals remain supported until 28 December 2026 at 03:00 UTC, and Binance said that after 29 December it may convert remaining balances into stablecoins on users' behalf, while cautioning that conversion is "not guaranteed".

12 Bitcoin spot pairs end on 29 October. Binance will close 12 spot trading pairs quoted against Bitcoin at 11 p.m. ET on Thursday 29 October (03:00 UTC on Friday 30 October): AAVE/BTC, ATOM/BTC, CAKE/BTC, DASH/BTC, ETC/BTC, ICP/BTC, PENDLE/BTC, RENDER/BTC, TAO/BTC, THETA/BTC, XAUT/BTC and XTZ/BTC. Spot trading bot services for those pairs end at the same cutoff. The affected tokens remain available through other Binance spot pairs — only the BTC-quoted pairs are closing.
