Binance to Close French Accounts on 31 Dec 2026 as Bitcoin Steadies Near $76,000

7 November 2026 · Binance News

No new Binance spot listing was announced for 7 November 2026 — reporting on 5–7 November noted that no new spot coin has appeared on Binance since PREDICT on 29 October, with the exchange's recent notices focused on restoring services after its 29 October security incident. Today's article therefore covers the day's biggest verified development: Binance is closing its French customer accounts at the end of the year, alongside a calmer market after Friday's sharp sell-off.

Binance France accounts to close on 31 December 2026

Binance has told its French customers that their accounts will close on 31 December 2026, according to emails sent on 6 November and reported by French crypto outlets Cryptoast and Cryptoactu. From 29 November, the exchange will charge a monthly fee of 0.025% of the balance left in an account, with a 10 USDC minimum. The first deduction comes within three days of 29 November and the second after 24 December. Users who withdraw everything before 29 November pay nothing.

French accounts are currently withdrawal-only; deposits are blocked and trading has already ended. The cause is the missing MiCA licence: Binance did not secure authorisation under the European Union's Markets in Crypto-Assets framework by the 30 June 2026 deadline, and has been winding down services for French users since 1 July. France's financial markets regulator, the AMF, struck the firm from its PSAN list on 2 July 2026. Cryptoast quoted the email as saying assets left after 31 December would be dealt with under the closure terms. CoinLab noted it found no official Binance announcement page at the time of writing, so the details come from customer emails quoted by the press. Only Binance France accounts are affected, not the global Binance platform.

Bitcoin steadies near $76,000 after Friday's fall

Bitcoin traded near $76,000 on 7 November, up about 0.5%–0.8% over 24 hours, after falling below $76,000 the previous day. Finnhub's Binance feed showed Bitcoin at $76,016.74, with a day range of $74,888 to $76,378. A daily market brief put Bitcoin's close on 6 November at $75,844, down 0.32% on the day. CoinLab reported Bitcoin near $76,023 in the early hours of 7 November, after Thursday's rout had steadied at the 50-week average of about $75,559, though the day's low set a fresh 30-day low. Moneycontrol, citing Bloomberg, reported Bitcoin trading mostly unchanged near $75,600 after the previous day's sell-off. InteractiveCrypto put Bitcoin at $75,835.41, barely above a technical support level of $75,775, and noted the coin remains nearly 40% below its all-time high of $126,080. Those price moves are reported market facts, not a forecast of where prices will go next.

Ether, Solana and the wider market

Ether traded at about $2,239–$2,243, up about 0.03%–0.90% over 24 hours, while Solana was up about 0.90% at $90.13, according to the daily brief. The total crypto market capitalisation stood at $2.57 trillion, down 2.25% over 24 hours, with $64.01 billion in 24-hour trading volume. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index read 25 (Extreme Fear), down 3 points from the previous day and down from 30 a week ago. Bitcoin's daily RSI was 37.9, inside the neutral 30–70 band, and BTC open interest was down 0.7% at $2.63 billion, suggesting leverage is not building up, the brief reported.

ETF outflows continue; December rate-hike odds rise

US spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded $412 million in net outflows on Friday, CoinLab reported, while Bloomberg data cited by Moneycontrol put Thursday's net outflows at $430 million. CoinLab also noted that December Federal Reserve hike odds had climbed to about 80% after a hot US jobs report, adding to the headwinds for Bitcoin. The US October employment report was due on 6 November, as reported the previous day.

US Treasury drops plan to lower crypto transfer reporting threshold

The US Treasury Department and the Federal Reserve withdrew a proposal on 6 November to expand data collection on international crypto transfers as small as $250, keeping the current $3,000 threshold in place, according to News Bitcoin via TradersUnion. The withdrawn proposal would have lowered the reporting threshold from $3,000 to $250 for crypto transfers.

Binance recovery: more withdrawals reopened on 6 November

Binance's ninth recovery update, published on 6 November, reopened withdrawals for more assets from 04:00 UTC, including USDE, PENDLE, ZRO, XRP and CAKE among others. The exchange said it is working to reopen withdrawals for HYPE, INJ, APT, PLASMA, NEAR and LTC by the end of next week, and aims to reopen withdrawals for all tokens on networks that have already been restored — including BNB Smart Chain, Ethereum, Polygon, Arbitrum and Optimism — by the same point. Binance said about 95% of the affected assets had been replenished, up from 90% in its previous update, after the 29 October incident in which about $6.3 billion in digital assets was stolen from hot wallets. Separately, Binance announced a spot taker programme running from 9 November to 3 January, returning 5%, 10% or 15% of fees to its largest spot traders.

What to watch next

With US markets shut for the weekend, attention turns to whether Bitcoin can hold the $75,775 support level through the weekend. CoinLab's base case for the 7 November close was sideways trading in a $74,800–$77,500 range, with a Polymarket median for Sunday of $76,129; those are reported analyst views, not a prediction by this site. Binance's spot taker programme begins on 9 November, and the exchange's next withdrawal-recovery targets — HYPE, INJ, APT, NEAR and LTC — are due by the end of next week.
