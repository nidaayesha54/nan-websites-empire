Bitcoin Rebounds Above $65,000 on 16 November 2026 After Weekend Crash to $62,000 as Fear Stays Extreme

16 November 2026 · Market Check

Bitcoin rebounded above $65,000 on Monday, 16 November 2026, as bargain-hunting emerged after a brutal weekend sell-off that pushed the largest cryptocurrency to about $62,000, its lowest level since July. CoinStats data captured at 00:00 UTC on 16 November showed Bitcoin at about $65,286, up about 1.9% over 24 hours but down about 12.9% over seven days. BusinessToday reported Bitcoin rising about 1.9% to about $65,359 in early Asian trading, recovering from the weekend low near $62,000. No new Binance spot listing was announced for 16 November 2026.

The weekend flush in numbers

The sell-off peaked on 15 November, when Bitcoin fell to about $62,000, its weakest level since July, after falling below $70,000 on 13 November. BeInCrypto reported that crypto liquidations topped $2 billion over 24 hours during the sell-off, with long positions making up about 87%, according to CoinStats. Total crypto liquidations over the last 24 hours then eased to about $56 million, with about $38.15 million, or 68.2%, coming from short positions as prices bounced, according to CoinStats data.

The recovery was market-wide but uneven. Ether climbed about 3.8% to about $1,837 after plunging to about $1,713 on 15 November, its lowest level since July, according to BusinessToday. Crypto.news market data showed Bitcoin trading around $65,200 on 16 November, with Ether near $1,830, BNB around $596, XRP close to $0.97 and Solana near $72. Despite the rebound, Bitcoin was still nearly 12% lower over seven days, while Solana had lost more than 15%. Bitcoin remains about 48% below its all-time high of $126,080, set on 6 October 2025.

The global crypto market capitalisation stood at about $2.27 trillion at 04:00 UTC on 16 November, down 0.8% over 24 hours, with total trading volume of about $75.3 billion, according to CoinGecko data cited by CoinGabbar. Open interest in Bitcoin futures fell 18.42% over the past seven days to about $41.66 billion, a decline of about $9.40 billion, according to CoinStats.

The Crypto Fear & Greed Index stood at 10 on 16 November, in Extreme Fear, according to KuCoin's daily market report and CoinGabbar's CoinGecko-based update. CoinMarketCap's version of the index read 13 on the day, also in Extreme Fear, according to BigGo Finance.

What drove the drop — and what still weighs on the market

The downturn was fuelled by heavy liquidations of leveraged positions, continued withdrawals from US-listed spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) and concerns over tighter US monetary policy, according to BusinessToday. US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded seven consecutive sessions of net outflows from 5 to 13 November, totalling about $1.91 billion, based on Farside Investors data cited by crypto.news. Investors will watch Monday's ETF flows for the first sign of whether the selling is easing.

Rate expectations remain the main macro driver. The CME FedWatch Tool showed traders pricing an 88% chance of a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate increase in December after hotter-than-expected inflation data, and the US Dollar Index rose above 103, its highest since April 2025, according to CoinDesk reporting cited by CoinStats.

MSCI's decision to remove Strategy, the largest corporate Bitcoin holder, from its benchmark indexes is the other overhang. MSCI published the results of its November 2026 Index Review on 11 November, putting Strategy's Class A shares on the deletion list for the MSCI USA Index, which also takes the stock out of the MSCI World Index and the MSCI ACWI Index. The change takes effect at the close of trading on 30 November 2026, with the rebalanced indexes going live on 1 December. JPMorgan estimates selling of roughly $2.8 billion from MSCI-tracking funds alone, a figure the bank says could climb to as much as $8.8 billion if other major index providers adopt similar rules, according to reporting by The Block and KuCoin. Strategy held 850,364 BTC as of 8 November 2026, according to its 9 November SEC filing cited by KuCoin. The removal changes Strategy's index eligibility, not its Nasdaq listing or its Bitcoin holdings.

Binance: no new spot listing; Blast wallet support ends 30 November

No new Binance spot coin listing was announced for 16 November 2026; no new spot coin has been listed since PREDICT on 29 October. Binance's most recent futures additions, the HYPEUSDC and TRXUSDC perpetual contracts, launched on 11 November 2026 at 09:30 and 09:35 UTC with up to 75x leverage and USDC settlement.

Binance has also announced that support for the Blast network on Binance Wallet will cease at 10:00 UTC on 30 November 2026. After that date, users will no longer be able to view, send or receive Blast assets within the Binance Wallet interface, although holdings can still be managed via the official Blast site. Blast, a Layer 2 network on Ethereum, announced in October 2026 that it was winding down operations after just over two years, citing decreasing network activity and unsustainable operating costs. The change affects only the Blast network; other networks supported by Binance Wallet are unaffected.

What to watch next

This week brings a run of US events that could move Bitcoin. Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh delivers opening remarks at a San Francisco Fed conference on productivity and employment at noon Eastern on Monday, 16 November. The US Census Bureau publishes October retail sales at 8:30 a.m. Eastern on Tuesday, 17 November, and Nvidia reports earnings the same day — its first since chief executive Jensen Huang resigned. The Federal Reserve publishes the minutes of its 27–28 October meeting at 2 p.m. Eastern on Wednesday, 18 November. Anthropic's anticipated $64.4 billion initial public offering and Friday's consumer sentiment report could also influence risk appetite, according to crypto.news and The Kobeissi Letter. Those are reported scheduled events, not a prediction by this site.

Two token unlocks are also scheduled for Monday: CRO, an unlock equivalent to 2.3% of circulating supply valued at about $51.4 million, and ARB, an unlock equivalent to 1.53% of circulating supply valued at about $12.5 million, according to KuCoin. The immediate market question is whether Bitcoin can hold above $65,000 and whether the seven-session run of ETF outflows breaks.
