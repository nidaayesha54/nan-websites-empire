Bitcoin Crashes to $62,000 on 15 November 2026 as Fear Hits 10 and $1.3 Billion in Longs Are Wiped Out

15 November 2026 · Market Check

Bitcoin crashed to about $62,000 overnight into Sunday, 15 November 2026, its lowest level in about three months, as a wave of forced selling swept through the crypto market. Bitcoin traded at about $62,199 at 01:44 UTC, down about 8.9% over 24 hours and 16.3% over the week, according to CoinGecko data cited by CryptoCompass. It later recovered toward about $64,872 by 12:30 AM ET, according to CoinLab. No new Binance spot listing was announced for 15 November 2026.

The sell-off in numbers

The selling accelerated on Saturday evening in the United States. Bitcoin fell 5.7% during the US session on 14 November and dropped a further 1.7% in the Asian session on 15 November, touching an intraday low of about $62,006–$62,112, according to Bloomberg data cited by The Economic Times and Vietnam News Agency. Bitcoin's 24-hour range ran from about $62,006 to $67,523, according to CoinLab. Bitcoin has now fallen about 25% since 21 October. Its all-time high is $126,080, set on 6 October 2025, so at $62,199 it was trading about 50.7% below its record. Its low for 2026 so far is $57,742, reached on 1 July.

The flush wiped out about $1.3 billion in long positions market-wide on Saturday evening, according to CoinLab, as traders betting on rising prices were forced to close. The sell-off was market-wide: Ether lost about 10.9% to about $1,718, Solana about 12.4% to about $67.79, and XRP about 10.5% to about $0.90, according to CoinGecko data cited by CryptoCompass. Ether later fell to about $1,731, its lowest since 9 July, according to Bloomberg. Total crypto market capitalisation dropped about 10.3% to about $2.14 trillion.

The Crypto Fear & Greed Index fell to 10 on 15 November, in Extreme Fear, according to Alternative.me data cited by CryptoCompass and CoinStats. It had read 13 on 14 November and 28 two days earlier.

What drove the drop

US rate expectations are the main driver, according to analysis cited by CryptoCompass. The CME FedWatch Tool showed traders pricing an 88.5% probability of a quarter-point Federal Reserve rate hike in December, with 11.5% priced for a half-point move. The US Dollar Index rose above 103, its highest level since April 2025, according to CoinStats. The 10-year US Treasury yield stood at about 5.15%, according to CoinLab.

US spot Bitcoin ETFs also recorded a seventh consecutive session of net outflows, with about $185.4 million withdrawn on Friday, 13 November, according to Cointelegraph data cited by CoinStats. About $1.07 billion left the funds over five trading days, according to Farside Investors data cited by Coinpaper.

Rich Rosenblum, co-founder of crypto market maker GSR, told Bloomberg the move was primarily technical: Bitcoin broke below its 200-day moving average at about $73,000, which triggered further selling. Bitcoin also broke below its 200-week simple moving average near $67,992, and its daily RSI fell to about 22.96, according to CoinStats.

Binance: withdrawals reopen on 87 networks, $1.2 billion of stolen funds targeted by US seizure order

In a recovery notice published on 14 November, Binance said it has reopened cryptocurrency withdrawals across 87 networks this week, including Litecoin, NEAR, Cardano, Stellar and Avalanche, and aims to restore the remaining networks and assets by the end of next week. The update follows the 29 October security breach in which about $6.3 billion in tokens was stolen. Binance said in a notice signed 13 November that the replacement of customer assets lost in the incident is complete, and it published a reserve snapshot taken at 00:00 UTC on 12 November covering 198 tokens. Binance said it committed more than $6 billion in company funds to cover the losses.

Separately, the US Department of Justice obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion stolen in the Binance hack, according to CNBC reporting cited by CoinStats.

Binance also announced on 13 November that it now offers more than 7,000 US stocks and ETFs in its app alongside crypto, with 24/5 trading and settlement in stablecoins, and that its tokenized-securities product bStocks has crossed $1 billion in assets under management within 125 days of its June 2026 launch, according to Blockchain.news and BigGo Finance. No new Binance spot coin listing was announced for 15 November 2026; no new spot coin has been listed since PREDICT on 29 October.

What to watch next

The next focus is whether Bitcoin holds the $60,000 area, whether the US spot Bitcoin ETF outflows ease after seven consecutive sessions of withdrawals, and how December rate expectations move before the Federal Reserve's 8–9 December meeting. The Federal Reserve publishes the minutes of its latest meeting on Wednesday, 18 November, the Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes October Personal Consumption Expenditures data, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, on Wednesday, 25 November, and the Bureau of Labor Statistics releases November consumer price data on 10 December. Those are reported scheduled events, not a prediction by this site.
