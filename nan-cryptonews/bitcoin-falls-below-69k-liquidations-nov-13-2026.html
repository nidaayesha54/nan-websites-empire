Bitcoin Falls Below $69,000 on 13 November 2026 as $1.41 Billion in Positions Are Liquidated

13 November 2026 · Market Check

Bitcoin fell below $69,000 on Friday, 13 November 2026, extending a week-long slide and reaching its lowest level since 20 August, according to The Block. Bitcoin traded at about $68,663 at around 04:15 UTC, down about 6.8% over 24 hours, according to CoinGecko data cited by The Crypto Times. About $1.41 billion in crypto derivatives positions was liquidated over 24 hours, with long positions — traders betting prices would rise — making up 89% of the total. No new Binance spot listing was announced for 13 November 2026.

The sell-off in numbers

The drop came in stages through the Asian session. Bitcoin's 24-hour range ran from about $68,675 to $73,738, and the price later steadied near $69,297, leaving it down about 6% over 24 hours, according to TradingView's report on the move. An intraday low of $68,943 was recorded during the session. A market that was trading above $76,000 earlier in the same week has now lost roughly $7,000 in a few days: Bitcoin was already at about $70,796 on 12 November, down 4.31% over the prior 24 hours.

CoinGlass data showed 172,922 traders liquidated over 24 hours for a total of about $1.41 billion as of 04:46 UTC on 13 November. A separate CoinGlass reading cited by The Block put the total at $1.47 billion with 175,299 traders liquidated. The single largest liquidation was a $31.09 million long position on Hyperliquid. Ethereum saw about $120 million in liquidations and Solana about $23 million. Binance accounted for 36.2% of total liquidations, according to The Crypto Times. Ether fell about 6% to about $1,921–$1,936, BNB slid 6.3% and XRP lost 3.5%, according to The Block and CoinGecko data.

The global crypto market capitalisation stood at $2.45 trillion, down 3.8% over 24 hours, with total trading volume of $68.6 billion, according to CoinGecko data recorded on 13 November 2026 at 04:00 UTC. Bitcoin's market capitalisation stood at about $1.38 trillion. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index fell to 20, in Extreme Fear, down from 28 on 12 November.

What drove the drop

Three forces lined up against the market at the same time. First, US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded a fifth straight session of net outflows, with about $1.44 billion withdrawn across the five sessions through 11 November, according to estimates cited by Mudrex and CoinSwitch. About $300 million left on 11 November alone, and US spot Ethereum ETFs saw withdrawals of about $145 million the same day.

Second, expectations of tighter US monetary policy kept building after the hot October consumer price index report, which showed inflation rising 0.4% against 0.3% expected. Traders are now watching the US October producer price index, due today, 13 November, at 13:30 UTC, for the next clue on inflation and the direction of Federal Reserve policy. The December Federal Reserve meeting on 8–9 December is in focus, with traders pricing a high chance of a rate hike.

Third, technology stocks came under pressure after Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive on 11 November, effective immediately, after the company launched an investigation into his relationship with a suspected Chinese spy, according to a filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission. The resignation weighed on tech shares and spilled into crypto sentiment. Glassnode data cited by KuCoin showed stablecoin supply has declined by $14.2 billion over the past 30 days — the largest contraction since June 2022 — indicating less capital available to buy crypto assets.

Technically, Bitcoin has fallen below its 200-day moving average near $73,100, and its relative strength index sits near 29, in oversold territory, according to analysts cited by CNBC TV18. Analysts quoted in the same coverage put immediate support at $69,800–$70,000, followed by $68,000–$68,500, with resistance around $73,000–$73,993. Those are reported analyst views, not a prediction by this site.

Binance today: no new spot listing, Blast support ends 30 November

No new Binance spot listing was announced for 13 November 2026. Binance's announcement list shows no new spot coin since PREDICT on 29 October, according to TechBullion's review of the exchange's notices on 11 November.

Two Binance changes take effect around today. Binance Wallet announced on 12 November that it will permanently discontinue all support for the Blast network, an Ethereum layer-2, effective 30 November 2026 at 10:00 UTC. Separately, Binance is raising the collateral ratios for Filecoin (FIL) and Hedera (HBAR) under Cross Margin and Portfolio Margin from 50% to 60%, effective today, 13 November at 06:00 UTC, according to the exchange's announcement published on 10 November.

The PREDICT airdrop's next scheduled release date is today, 13 November, but the Predict.fun token generation event remains delayed with no fixed date, and no listing has been confirmed. In other exchange news, the SIGN token rose about 60% on Tuesday after being listed on Korean exchange Upbit, following a muted start on Binance, according to CoinDesk.

Coverage circulating today about Binance's multi-asset expansion — more than 7,000 US stocks and ETFs plus the bStocks tokenized securities product — describes the same offering Binance announced in June and July 2026; bStocks launched on 11 June 2026. It is not a new announcement today.

What to watch next

The next focus is the US October producer price index at 13:30 UTC today, whether Bitcoin holds the $68,000–$70,000 area, and whether the US spot Bitcoin ETF outflows ease after five consecutive sessions of withdrawals. An AZTEC token unlock equivalent to about 31.81% of circulating supply, valued at about $10.3 million, is also scheduled for today. Those are reported scheduled events and analyst views, not a prediction by this site.
