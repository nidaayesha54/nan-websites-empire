Binance Launches NU and FRHC Perpetual Contracts on 12 November 2026 as Bitcoin Holds Near $73,700

12 November 2026 · Binance News

No new Binance spot listing was announced for 12 November 2026. Today's verified Binance news is a futures launch: Binance Futures is listing two new USDⓈ-margined perpetual contracts today — NUUSDT, tracking shares of Nu Holdings, opening at 09:30 UTC, and FRHCUSDT, tracking shares of Freedom Holding, opening at 09:45 UTC — according to the exchange's announcement published on 11 November 2026. These are perpetual futures contracts, not new spot listings. Bitcoin traded near $73,700 on 12 November, little changed over 24 hours but about 8.3% lower over the past week.

Two equity perpetuals go live, fifteen minutes apart

The contracts track Nu Holdings Ltd. Class A ordinary shares, which trade on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker NU, and Freedom Holding Corp. common stock, listed on Nasdaq as FRHC. Both contracts are settled in USDT and trade around the clock, unlike the cash equities they track, which stop at the closing bell, according to MyToken's report on the announcement. The contracts are admitted to trading on Binance RIE and to clearing and settlement by Binance RCH, the exchange's own venue and clearing entities, under their Exchange and Clearing Rules — not on a US or European exchange.

The published contract terms are: USDⓈ-margined perpetuals with a 0.01 tick size, a minimum trade of 0.01 contract units and a minimum notional value of 5 USDT. Maximum leverage is 20x. Funding settles every eight hours with an interest-rate component of 0% and a capped funding rate of plus or minus 2.00%. Multi-assets mode is supported on both contracts, and the contracts carry no expiry date. Binance RIE may adjust trading parameters under its Exchange Rules and Binance RCH may adjust leverage, margin and funding parameters under its Clearing Rules.

A product line built in six weeks

This is not Binance's first equity-linked contract. In late September the exchange announced five TradFi perpetual contracts in a single notice, and the 12 November notice brings the count of TradFi contracts announced since late September to seven, according to MyToken. By comparison, Coinbase Derivatives filed in September to list stock and ETF perpetual futures in the US market, a route that runs through the derivatives regulator rather than through an exchange's own clearing rules.

The announcement does not address how dividends or corporate actions on NU and FRHC are handled inside a perpetual that never expires, nor does it specify how the price index is constructed beyond naming the underlying equity. It repeats the standard regional caveat that the products "may not be available in your region," and Binance has not said which jurisdictions will see NUUSDT and FRHCUSDT, or that they are available to retail users in any particular market.

Binance lets users buy tokenized stocks with fiat

On 11 November Binance enabled fiat purchases of bStocks — its tokenized stocks — through its Convert and Buy/Sell pages, with up to 49 fiat currencies supported for buying. Users can also sell bStocks into eligible fiat currencies without first converting into crypto, according to the exchange notice. Before the change, the route into a bStocks position ran through a crypto asset; the announcement lists "no intermediate crypto conversion" as one of the new features. Funding can now come from a fiat balance, a card, Apple Pay or Google Pay.

The product being bought is not a share. Binance's disclosure is explicit: bStocks are tokenized securities issued by BTech Holdings Limited, a Binance group affiliate, classified as certificates representing certain financial instruments under the Abu Dhabi Global Market's Financial Services and Markets Regulations, and offered through an approved prospectus. Holders do not directly own a share in the underlying listed company. The 49-currency list includes the US dollar and the euro but not the Indian rupee, Indonesian rupiah, Thai baht, Singapore dollar, South Korean won, Turkish lira, Nigerian naira, Egyptian pound or Norwegian krone, and Binance did not explain the selection.
