GTA 6 Crypto Coin: The Rumour, the Scam and the Truth

20 November 2026 · Fact Check

Ask a crypto trader about GTA 6 and you will hear a confident story: the biggest game ever made is about to accept USDT, run its economy on Solana, and launch a coin called Sharkbux. The story has everything — documents, a token, a price chart. It has one problem. None of it is true, or more precisely, no verifiable evidence exists that any of it is true. Rockstar Games and Take-Two Interactive have never announced a cryptocurrency, a token, a blockchain feature, or any play-to-earn system for GTA 6. This article takes the rumour apart piece by piece, because the way it was built is a lesson in how crypto hoaxes work — and because real money has already been lost to it.

Start with the claim itself. The suggestion that GTA 6 would accept USDT alongside Bitcoin and Ethereum is not new. It traces back to a claim that circulated in the summer of 2024, spread by a pseudonymous influencer alongside a trailer that turned out to be fake. The claim resurfaces every few months, each time with a new detail attached, and each time without a source. Neither Rockstar, the developer, nor Take-Two, the publisher, has ever confirmed any of it. The reference to USDT does not come from a company press release or an earnings filing. It comes from an old corridor rumour that never found confirmation.

The "Sharkbux" documents were never a filing

The most convincing layer of the rumour arrived in August 2026: documents describing a supposed GTA 6 in-game currency called Sharkbux, represented as a token on the Solana blockchain, presented as if they were a filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission. They were not. The PDFs had simply been uploaded to the SEC's public comment section for a proposed rulemaking — a consultation open to anyone at all. Any member of the public can upload a file there. The authenticity and corporate authorisation of those documents was never established, and as of this writing the files are no longer available. The accurate description is "unauthenticated documents uploaded to a public comment section", not "a filing". Hosting is not endorsement.

The CYBERLEEK memecoin: the rumour was the advertising

Behind the documents was a coin. In August 2026, an anonymous account began publishing unauthorised GTA 6 footage — 15 clips over 9 days, each watermarked with a ticker and a QR code — while promoting a Solana memecoin called CYBERLEEK. The coin launched on Pump.fun on August 15, 2026, about three days before the first footage surfaced. It peaked near a $25 million market cap on August 23. Then its creator cashed out roughly $250,000 to $270,000 on August 27, and the price collapsed. The operation later escalated into an extortion-style ultimatum aimed at Take-Two, and by mid-October 2026 reports said it had wound down. The pattern is worth remembering: the coin was the product, and the "GTA 6 crypto" story was the advertising.

8,800 scam tokens and counting

The CYBERLEEK scheme was not alone. Security firm Blockaid tracked hundreds of social posts pushing malicious GTA-themed airdrop, pre-order and Ultimate Edition pages, and more than 8,800 GTA-themed tokens on Solana flagged as malicious, warning or spam. The funnel is always the same: a polished page with Vice City art and a Check Eligibility or Pre-Order button, and the button opens a wallet-connect window — and the wallet connection is where the theft happens. One example was a near-perfect clone of the PlayStation Store, hosted on an unrelated domain, listing the Ultimate Edition for $89.99. Scam activity spiked in the weeks of August 17 and 24, 2026, again in late September, and climbed through October into the November 19 launch.

What Rockstar has actually said about crypto

Rockstar has spoken about crypto exactly once in a policy sense, and it said no. In November 2022, its roleplay server policy prohibited cryptocurrencies, NFTs and loot-box-for-cash schemes on fan-run servers. That ban has never been lifted or revised. Older rumours claimed some GTA 6 missions would pay out in Bitcoin; the insider behind that claim later said the idea had been dropped years earlier. And there is a genuine crypto joke inside the game's world: a stunt plane in the launch trailer carries branding for a parody coin, played as satire of meme-coin culture. A joke painted on a plane is not a payment system, and tokens using the name have no connection to Rockstar. Press reporting also says the story mode carries no microtransactions at launch. Take-Two's own numbers make the same point: GTA Online earned more than $5 billion through Shark Cards — in-game cash bought with real money — using zero blockchain. A studio at that scale does not need a Solana memecoin.

Frequently asked questions

Does GTA 6 have a crypto coin? No. No cryptocurrency, token or blockchain feature has ever been announced by Rockstar or Take-Two.

What is Sharkbux? A name from unauthenticated documents uploaded to a public SEC comment section in August 2026. It is not a filing and not a currency.

Can you make real money from GTA 6? No. Nothing you earn in the game can be cashed out, and Rockstar has announced no crypto, NFT or play-to-earn feature of any kind.

What was CYBERLEEK? A Solana memecoin that used unauthorised GTA 6 footage as promotion, peaked near a $25 million market cap, and collapsed after its creator cashed out around $250,000 to $270,000.

How do I stay safe? Never connect a wallet to a page promising a GTA 6 coin, airdrop or pre-order. Verify everything on Rockstar's own site, and treat any GTA 6 token as a scam until proven otherwise.

Bottom line: GTA 6 has no coin, no chain, and no crypto payout — and the loudest claims otherwise came from people selling one. The rumour was built to move a token, not to tell the truth.
