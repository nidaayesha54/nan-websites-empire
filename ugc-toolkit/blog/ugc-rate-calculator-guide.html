UGC Rates Explained: How Much to Charge Per Video in 2026

Pricing guide · 8 min read · Updated Oct 2026

Pricing is the #1 anxiety for new UGC creators. Charge too little and you burn out; charge too much and you hear crickets. This guide gives you real 2026 numbers — no fluff — plus the exact formula to price every project with confidence. Want the shortcut? Use our free UGC rate calculator.

2026 UGC rate benchmarks

Based on current market data from creator communities, brand budgets, and platform reports, here are the going rates for a single 15–30 second UGC video:

  • Beginner (0–10 videos delivered): $150–$250
  • Intermediate (10–50 videos): $250–$400
  • Pro (50+ videos, proven results): $400–$750+
  • Monthly retainers: $1,500–$3,000/month (typically 8–30 videos)

Longer videos (60s) command 50–60% more. Niche matters too — finance and tech pay 20–25% above beauty and lifestyle averages.

The 5 factors that determine your rate

1. Usage rights (the biggest lever)

A video the brand posts organically is worth far less than one they run as a paid ad reaching millions. Standard multipliers:

  • Organic social only: base rate (1.0x)
  • Paid ads, 3 months: 1.6x base
  • Paid ads, 12 months: 2.2x base
  • Whitelisting (running ads from your account): add 30–50%

2. Video length and complexity

A 15-second talking-head testimonial is a different product than a 60-second scripted ad with 8 scenes. Price accordingly — roughly 25% more for 30s, 60% more for 60s versus your 15s base.

3. Your experience and proof

Every delivered video raises your floor. Keep a simple log: videos delivered, brands worked with, any performance stats brands share. After 10 paid videos, raise rates 20%.

4. Niche and audience value

A creator who authentically fits a high-ticket niche (finance, B2B software, luxury) commands more than a generalist. Specialization is a pricing superpower.

5. Turnaround and add-ons

Rush delivery (48 hours), raw footage, extra hook variations, and additional revisions are all billable. Standard add-ons: hook variations +$25, raw footage +$50, rush +30%.

How to quote: the exact script

When a brand asks "what are your rates?", don't freeze. Send a clean rate card (grab our rates pitch template):

Single video (15–30s): $___
Bundle of 4: $___
Monthly retainer: $___
Includes filming, editing, captions, 1 revision round, 3-month paid usage.

Bundles should discount ~15–20% versus single-video pricing; retainers ~20% off the bundle rate. Discounts reward commitment — never discount single videos without getting something (longer usage, testimonial, referral) in return.

Beginner pricing strategy (first 10 videos)

  1. Start at $150 minimum. Below $100 signals low quality and attracts the worst clients.
  2. Offer ONE free sample to serious prospects — a 15-second concept video, not a finished ad.
  3. Raise to $200 after 5 paid videos, $250 after 10. Your portfolio is your leverage.
  4. Never work "for exposure." Exposure doesn't pay rent; spec videos build your portfolio for free instead.

Common pricing mistakes

  • Charging hourly. UGC is value-priced per video, not per hour. Hourly punishes efficiency.
  • Unlimited revisions. Cap at 1–2 rounds; charge per extra round.
  • Vague usage terms. "Can we use it everywhere forever?" should never be free. Define platforms, duration, and paid vs. organic in writing.
  • Forgetting whitelisting fees. If they run ads from your handle, that's premium — charge for it.

FAQ

What is the average UGC rate in 2026?

In 2026, beginner UGC creators charge $150–$250 per 15–30 second video, intermediate creators $250–$400, and experienced pros $400–$750+. Monthly retainers range from $1,500 to $3,000.

Should I do free UGC work to build my portfolio?

Do spec work (self-made sample videos) instead of free client work. Spec videos build your portfolio without devaluing your rates. One free sample for a serious prospect is fine; ongoing free work is not.

How do usage rights affect UGC pricing?

Usage rights are the biggest price lever. Organic-only usage is the base rate; paid ad usage typically adds 60–120% because the video directly generates revenue. Always define usage length (3, 6, or 12 months) in writing.
