GTA 6 Creator Tools: What Exists and What Doesn’t

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

If you make videos, screenshots or fan content, the first question about GTA 6 is what tools the game gives you to create with. The honest answer is two-sided: no creator mode, mission editor or mod support has been announced for GTA 6, and yet the game still hands creators several powerful tools — a full photo studio, a phone camera, a social feed to browse, and console capture for clips. This guide lists what actually exists, what does not, and how to use the tools you do have, without inventing features the game never promised.

That honesty matters, because the gap between what creators expect and what the game provides is where most of the bad advice lives. Guides that describe a creator mode or a mod scene for GTA 6 are describing something that has not been announced. Here is the real inventory, as of launch day.

What does not exist

Start with the missing pieces, because they shape every plan you make. No GTA 6 creator tool has been announced: the mission and race creators that exist belong to GTA Online, not to GTA 6, and there is no app builder, phone customisation tool or way to add apps to the in-game phone. There is no mod support announced, and there is no PC version at all — the game launched on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S only, so there is no PC mod scene to join.

When the studio was asked about user-generated content, the answer given was that questions like that are addressed in the fullness of time by its labels — which is a polite way of saying nothing has been decided in public. The same applies to roleplay servers: none have been announced for GTA 6, and nothing has been said about the existing FiveM platform working with it. Plan your content around the tools below, not around features that may never arrive.

Photo Studio: the real creative tool

The strongest creative tool in the game is Photo Studio, the photo mode built into the pause menu. Open the pause menu and choose Photo Studio, or press Square on PlayStation or X on Xbox as a shortcut. It is available from the start of playable story content, and it even works during cutscenes, though with limited functions there.

Photo Studio has five tabs. Camera lets you rotate and zoom, change the field of view up to 48.5 degrees, pick from eight aspect ratios, and adjust depth of field or hide your character. Effects covers exposure, contrast, tint and bloom. Lighting lets you place up to five separate light sources, with presets including stage lights and camera flashes, and control their colour and intensity. Vehicle toggles your vehicle's lights, and Overlay adds vignette and filter intensity, with looks including Cartoon Universe, Found Footage and Pixel Art. One limit to know: the studio has no control for the hour or the weather, so you cannot change the time of day for a shot. Press Triangle or Y to hide the interface and take a clean picture, and your photos save to the phone's Gallery in the game.

Snapmatic: look, do not post

Snapmatic, the social media parody from GTA V, is back in GTA 6 — but it works differently from what many creators expect. You can scroll the feed, follow accounts and watch what other people post, but you cannot post your own photos as Jason or Lucia. The reason, given by the studio's co-head in an interview, is that the two are a couple on the run: posting location updates would leave a digital trail, so the game lets you lurk and nothing more.

The world around Snapmatic is still useful for creators. Leonida has livestreamers whose streams you can open, with a viewer count and a chat visible on screen, and the phone itself is now a physical object in the character's hand, with the camera moving in on it. The social layer is there to watch and to learn from — for ideas, for locations, and for the way the game's own characters present themselves — but it is not a publishing tool for your own work.

Phone camera and console capture

The second creative tool is the phone camera. Open the Camera app on the in-game phone and you can take photos and selfies, with poses and facial expressions to choose from, and up to 5x zoom on the rear camera. Photos you take this way land in the phone Gallery, alongside your Photo Studio shots, while screenshots taken with your console's own capture go to the PlayStation Media Gallery or the Xbox capture gallery instead.

For video, use your console's capture: PlayStation's Create button and the Xbox capture system both record clips you can edit and post. One setting to check first is the option that mutes licensed music, sometimes called streamer mode — Leonida's radio is full of licensed tracks, and muting them before you record saves you from copyright claims on the finished video.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I make custom missions in GTA 6?

No. No mission creator or race creator has been announced for GTA 6. The creators that exist belong to GTA Online.

Can I post on Snapmatic?

No. You can browse and follow, but Jason and Lucia do not post, because they are on the run and do not want to leave a digital trail.

Is there a PC mod scene for GTA 6?

No. There is no PC version announced, so there is no PC mod scene. Mod support for consoles has not been announced either.

The bottom line: GTA 6 gives creators Photo Studio, a phone camera, Snapmatic to browse and console capture — and it does not give them a creator mode, mods or a way to post in-game. Build your content on the tools that exist, and do not plan around the ones that were never announced.
