What Is Statutory Guarantee Pay?

When your employer lays you off (sends you home with no work) or puts you on short-time working (cuts your hours), the law says you are still entitled to some pay for days you would normally have worked. This legal minimum is called statutory guarantee pay. Your contract may promise more — but it can never give you less than the statutory amount.

The Guarantee Pay Formula (2026)

Guarantee pay = your normal daily pay (capped at £41) × claimable workless days
Maximum 5 days in any 3-month period — so the most anyone can receive is £205 per 3 months. If you normally earn less than £41 a day, you get your usual daily rate instead of the cap.

Example

You normally earn £95 a day and work 5 days a week. You are laid off for 5 full working days in a 3-month period: 5 × £41 = £205. A colleague earning £30 a day gets 5 × £30 = £150.

Part-Time Workers

Your entitlement is worked out in proportion to your hours. Someone who works 2 days a week can claim for a maximum of 2 days in any 3-month period (not 5). This keeps part-timers on the same footing as full-timers, pro-rata.

Eligibility Checklist

  • You are an employee (not self-employed or a casual worker with no contract).
  • You have worked for the employer for at least 1 month continuously.
  • You were available for work on the day (not on holiday or off sick).
  • You did not refuse reasonable alternative work offered by your employer.
  • The lay-off was not caused by a strike or lockout.

Only Complete Workless Days Count

Guarantee pay is payable only for whole days with no work at all. If you worked even part of the day, that day doesn't count. Days when you were on holiday or off sick don't count either — though you may be able to claim Jobseeker's Allowance for days beyond your guarantee-pay entitlement.

Laid Off for Weeks? You May Be Able to Claim Redundancy Pay

If you are laid off (or on short-time working) for 4 consecutive weeks, or for 6 weeks in total within a 13-week period (with no more than 3 consecutive), you can give your employer written notice that you intend to claim a redundancy payment — provided you have the 2 years' continuous service that statutory redundancy pay requires.

If Your Employer Doesn't Pay

You can take a claim to an employment tribunal within 3 months of the day the payment was due. You can't be dismissed or selected for redundancy for challenging a refusal to pay guarantee pay.

FAQs

Can my employer lay me off without a contract clause?

No. Your employer can only lay you off or cut your hours if your contract allows it, or if you agree to it. Without a contractual right, cutting your pay is a breach of contract — you could claim for lost wages or resign and claim constructive dismissal.

Does guarantee pay apply to zero-hours workers?

Only employees qualify, and the day must be one you would normally have been required to work. If your pattern is genuinely irregular with no normal working days, guarantee pay is unlikely to apply — get advice from Acas.

Is guarantee pay taxed?

Yes — it's paid through payroll like normal wages, so income tax and National Insurance apply as usual.

What happens after the 5 days run out?

Statutory guarantee pay stops. If the lay-off continues, you may be able to claim Jobseeker's Allowance for the remaining workless days, and the redundancy-pay trigger above may apply.

Does the £41 rate change?

The daily limit is reviewed by the government each April. The £41 rate applies from April 2026 — check Acas or GOV.UK for the current figure.
