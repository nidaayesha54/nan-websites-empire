Statutory Guarantee Pay UK: Eligibility, Daily Rate & How to Claim

Laid off or on short-time working? The daily rate, the 5-day cap, eligibility rules, and how to claim within the deadline.

In-depth 2026 guide~6 min readFree

When the work dries up — orders cancelled, a machine broken, a quiet season — your employer may tell you not to come in. If your contract allows lay-offs or short-time working, you won't receive your normal wages for those days. But you are not left with nothing: UK law guarantees a statutory minimum payment for each workless day, and most laid-off employees have never heard of it.

This guide explains statutory guarantee pay in full: the current daily rate, who qualifies, the 5-day cap, how to claim, and the redundancy rights that kick in when lay-offs drag on. Calculate what you're owed with our Statutory Guarantee Pay Calculator.

What is statutory guarantee pay?

Statutory guarantee pay (SGP) is a legal entitlement under the Employment Rights Act 1996. When your employer provides no work on a day you would normally be required to work — a "workless day" — you are entitled to a fixed daily payment, even though you earned no wages that day.

The key word is statutory: this is the legal minimum. Your contract or collective agreement may provide more generous lay-off pay (many unionized workplaces do), but it can never provide less than the statute. If your contract is silent on lay-offs entirely, your employer may not even have the right to lay you off on reduced pay — in that case you may be entitled to your full normal wages, not just the guarantee payment. Check your contract first; it changes everything.

The 2026/27 rate: £41 per day

The daily rate is reviewed every year and normally rises each April in line with inflation. From 6 April 2026, the statutory guarantee payment is £41 per workless day (up from £38). Two important qualifications:

  • If your normal daily earnings are less than £41, you receive your normal daily pay instead — the statute doesn't give you a raise, it guarantees your usual day rate up to the cap.
  • For part-time workers, the payment is pro-rated to your normal working pattern.

The entitlement is capped at 5 days in any rolling 3-month period — so the maximum statutory payout is 5 × £41 = £205 per quarter, regardless of how long the lay-off continues. The 3-month window rolls: each new workless day looks back at the 3 months before it.

Who qualifies?

You are entitled to guarantee pay if, on the workless day:

  • You are an employee (not a casual worker or genuinely self-employed contractor) with at least 1 month's continuous service;
  • You would normally have been required to work that day under your contract;
  • Your employer gave you no work at all that day (even a couple of hours of work disqualifies the day);
  • The reason is a diminution in the employer's need for work of the kind you do (e.g., lost orders, supply problems) — not a reason personal to you.

You are not entitled where the workless day results from a strike, lock-out, or other industrial action affecting your workplace. You also lose the entitlement for any day you unreasonably refuse an offer of suitable alternative work — so if your employer offers you a different shift or site, think carefully before declining.

Short-time working vs. lay-off

The terms are often used interchangeably but they differ. A lay-off means you are given no work at all for the day (or week). Short-time working means your hours are reduced — you work some days or part-days but earn less than a full week's pay.

Guarantee pay applies to whole workless days. A day on which you worked even one hour is not a workless day for SGP purposes (though short-time arrangements have their own rules, and your contract may top up the difference). This is why accurate records of exactly which days you were given no work matter so much.

How to claim (and the deadline)

In practice, most employers pay guarantee pay through payroll automatically once lay-offs are recorded. But if yours doesn't:

1. Put it in writing. Email HR/payroll listing the specific workless days, your normal daily rate, and the statutory £41/day (2026/27) entitlement. Keep it factual and reference the Employment Rights Act 1996.

2. Contact Acas. Acas offers free, confidential advice and runs the Early Conciliation process you must complete before most tribunal claims.

3. Employment tribunal. A claim for unpaid guarantee pay must generally reach the tribunal within 3 months (less one day) of the workless day — the same deadline as most wage claims. Do not let this drift; tribunals rarely extend it.

Separately, if lay-offs continue, bigger rights activate: if you are laid off (or on short-time) for 4 consecutive weeks, or for 6 weeks in any 13-week period, you can give written notice claiming redundancy and request a redundancy payment. Take advice before triggering this — it ends the employment relationship.

Worked example

You earn £130/day and are laid off for 8 working days across May 2026, with no work on any of those days. Your entitlement: the first 5 workless days × £41 = £205. The remaining 3 days attract no statutory payment (the quarterly cap is exhausted), though your contract may provide more. If instead you earned £30/day part-time, you'd receive £30 × 5 = £150 (your normal rate, since it's below the cap).

Common pitfalls

Assuming the contract allows lay-offs. Without an express contractual right (or established custom), a "lay-off" on guarantee pay alone may be an unlawful deduction from wages — you could be owed full pay. This is the highest-value issue in the whole area.

Missing the tribunal deadline. Three months less one day runs fast when you're hoping work picks up. Diarise it from the first workless day.

Not tracking workless days precisely. Partial days don't count; keep a dated log of days you were told not to come in.

Refusing alternative work unreasonably. It can extinguish the entitlement for those days — get advice before refusing.

FAQs

Is guarantee pay taxed?

Yes — it is treated as earnings and subject to income tax and National Insurance like normal wages.

Does it apply to zero-hours workers?

Only if you have employee status and the day in question was one you would normally have been required to work. Pure "as and when" arrangements with no guaranteed hours rarely generate qualifying workless days.

Can my employer lay me off indefinitely?

The statute doesn't set a maximum lay-off duration, but after 4 consecutive weeks (or 6 in 13) you gain the right to claim redundancy. Indefinite lay-offs also risk breaching the implied term of trust and confidence.

What if my employer becomes insolvent owing me guarantee pay?

Unpaid guarantee pay can be claimed from the National Insurance Fund via the Redundancy Payments Service, subject to statutory caps — another reason to keep precise records.

Does guarantee pay affect my Universal Credit?

It counts as earnings for UC purposes and must be reported. A drop in overall income from lay-offs may increase your UC award — report changes promptly.
