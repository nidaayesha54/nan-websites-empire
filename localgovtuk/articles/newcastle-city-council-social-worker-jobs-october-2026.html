The Role: Safeguarding Service

The posts sit in the council’s Safeguarding Service, working on the frontline with children and families alongside partners delivering high-quality, integrated support. Newcastle works through a Families First approach — earlier, relational and restorative practice that strengthens families and improves outcomes across the whole system. The two posts cover both unprogressed and progressed levels, so the council is open to social workers at different career stages who have completed their ASYE and are ready for frontline safeguarding caseloads. The council’s pitch to applicants is direct: “This is your moment. Shape the Future. Feel Supported. Belong in Newcastle.”

Who They Want

  • Qualified social workers who have successfully completed their ASYE (Assessed and Supported Year in Employment) — this is a stated requirement.
  • Practitioners with expertise, confidence and curiosity for meaningful frontline work.
  • People who can lead on cases, collaborate with partners and drive restorative practice with families.

Salary Progression Explained

The banding reflects Newcastle’s social work career structure: unprogressed social workers earn £37,563–£41,117, while progressed social workers — those assessed as practising at the higher level — earn £42,123–£46,579. It is worth applying even if you are early post-ASYE: the progression route is built into the role, not a separate promotion competition.

The Bottom Line

Two permanent safeguarding posts, a clear progression band up to £46,579 and a council selling itself as a supportive employer — but only twelve days on the advert board. Qualified social workers in the North East should move this week.
