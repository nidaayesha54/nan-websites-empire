How to Apply for Local Government Jobs in the UK

Application tips for 2026: person specifications decoded, supporting statements that score, council interview formats and the checks that follow.

Published 13 October 2026 · NAN Jobs Portal editorial team

Council recruitment works differently from both the private sector and the Civil Service. There are no Success Profiles and no national portal — instead, each local authority runs its own application forms, scored against a person specification unique to the role. The good news: once you understand the person-spec system, council applications become highly predictable. This guide shows you how to write applications that get shortlisted in 2026.

Decode the Person Specification

Every council advert includes a person specification dividing requirements into essential and desirable criteria. Shortlisting panels score your application against each essential point — typically on a 0–3 or 0–5 scale — and only the highest scorers are interviewed. Desirable criteria act as tie-breakers.

  • Essential = non-negotiable. If you cannot evidence an essential criterion, do not apply — you will not be shortlisted.
  • Desirable = bonus points. Evidence these where you can; they separate the top candidates.
  • "How assessed" matters. Specs state whether each criterion is assessed at application, interview or test — tailor your evidence to the right stage.
Golden rule: mirror the specification's language. If it asks for "experience of partnership working with health colleagues", use that exact phrase in your example — panels score faster when they can tick criteria off directly.

Writing a Supporting Statement That Scores

Most councils ask for a supporting statement of 1–2 pages (some use online forms with per-criterion boxes). Structure it with one short section per essential criterion:

  1. Head each section with the criterion itself, so the panel can score as they read.
  2. Give one concrete example per criterion — the situation, what you did, and the outcome. Keep each to 100–150 words.
  3. Use "I" throughout. Panels cannot score team achievements; they need your personal contribution.
  4. Quantify outcomes. Caseloads managed, savings delivered, inspections passed, satisfaction scores improved.
  5. Close with motivation. One short paragraph on why this council and this role — generic closings score nothing, specific ones (citing the council's plan or values) score well.

The Council Application Process

Register and find the advert. Create an account on the council's recruitment system, read the full advert, job description and person specification.
Draft your statement offline. Write in a document first, proofread, then paste into the form — council portals can time out and lose unsaved work.
Submit before closing. Council adverts usually run 2–3 weeks. Submit at least 48 hours early; portals slow down on deadline day.
Prepare for panel interview. Expect competency questions mapped to the person spec, scenario questions for frontline roles, and sometimes a presentation or written exercise.
Clear pre-employment checks. References, right-to-work, and DBS checks where required. Keep documents ready to avoid delays.

Eligibility and Checks

Right to work in the UK is required for all posts. Regulated roles need professional registration (Social Work England for social workers) and enhanced DBS checks for work with children or vulnerable adults. Some councils ask about unspent convictions at application stage — answer honestly; councils assess relevance to the role rather than applying blanket bans. Always verify the exact checks on the official advert.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a person specification?

The person specification lists the essential and desirable criteria for the role — qualifications, experience, skills and knowledge. Shortlisters score your application against each point, so address every essential criterion.

How long should a supporting statement be?

Most councils expect 1–2 pages. Address each essential criterion with a brief example, and keep the writing tight — panels shortlist dozens of applications.

What are council interviews like?

Council interviews are usually panel-based with competency questions linked to the person specification, plus scenario questions for frontline roles and sometimes presentations or written exercises.

Do council jobs need DBS checks?

Roles involving children or vulnerable adults require enhanced DBS checks. Many other council roles require standard or basic checks, or none — the advert states the requirement.

How long does council recruitment take?

Typically 6–10 weeks from the closing date, including shortlisting, interviews and pre-employment checks. DBS and reference checks can extend this for regulated roles.
