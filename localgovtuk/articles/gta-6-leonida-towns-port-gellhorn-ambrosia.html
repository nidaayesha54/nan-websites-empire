GTA 6 Towns Beyond Vice City: Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and most of the attention has gone to Vice City — the neon streets, the beaches, the clubs, the skyline that fills every trailer. But Vice City is only one place in a much bigger state. Rockstar has named six regions in Leonida, and each one has its own character, its own people, and its own reasons to visit. This guide maps the towns beyond Vice City: Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia, the Leonida Keys, Grassrivers and Mount Kalaga — and explains what you will actually find in each of them.

Knowing the map matters because the game is not a single city with scenery around it. It is a state, and the story moves through it: Jason starts in the Keys, then the story takes you west to Port Gellhorn, inland to Ambrosia, into the swamps of Grassrivers and up to Mount Kalaga. If you only know Vice City, you know about a sixth of the game.

The Six Regions Rockstar Named

Rockstar's own site for the game names the six regions, and they are the framework for everything below: Vice City, the Leonida Keys, Grassrivers, Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia and Mount Kalaga. Vice City is the metropolis — the beaches, the clubs, the towers, the place the trailers keep returning to. The other five are the rest of Leonida, and they are where the game gets strange, quiet and dangerous in ways a city cannot be.

That six-region structure is also a promise about scale: a coastline, a swamp, a forgotten seaside town, a company town built around a sugar refinery, and a mountain — each one looking different, driving differently, and asking different things of you. The rest of this guide takes them one at a time.

Port Gellhorn: The Forgotten Coast

Port Gellhorn is the place the trailers describe as the forgotten coast, under the tagline "Live Hard" — a faded seaside town on the far west of the map, the kind of place where the motels are empty, the paint is peeling, and the people who live there have nowhere else to go. It is the opposite of Vice City's shine, and that is the point: Leonida is a state with a poor edge, not just a rich centre.

You first go there in Chapter 2, and the town's landmark is Delights Cabaret — a smaller, traditional strip club with more of a bar atmosphere, where Vicki introduces you to her boss. There is also a stranger detail: a car wash in Port Gellhorn, spotted in material from September 2022, carries a washing-machine icon that some players read as a hint at a money-laundering front. That reading is unconfirmed — a hint, not a fact — but it fits the town's character perfectly.

Ambrosia: Keeping Leonida Sweet

Ambrosia is the inland company town, and its tagline is "Keeping Leonida Sweet" — because the town exists for one thing: the Allied Crystal sugar refinery. It is a place built around an industry, the way real Florida has towns built around citrus and sugar, and the refinery dominates it the way a factory dominates any small town. The work is hard, the town is small, and the people in it are the people the refinery employs.

Ambrosia is also biker country: the town is home to a biker gang, which gives it a different kind of danger from Vice City's street crime — less neon, more leather, and a code of its own. If Port Gellhorn is the forgotten coast, Ambrosia is the forgotten interior: a town that makes something the rest of the state consumes, and gets very little back for it.

The Leonida Keys and Grassrivers

The Leonida Keys are where the story begins, and they are the game's home ground. Jason's safehouse is here — a house belonging to Brian Heder, a drug runner from the Keys who lets Jason live at one of his properties without paying rent, so long as he helps with local shakedowns. Heder also runs a boat yard in the Keys, which makes the place a working waterfront: fishing boats, a jetty where your first rod is waiting, and the free fishing boat behind Jason's house. The Keys are quiet, flat and beautiful, and they teach you the game's pace before Vice City teaches you its speed.

Grassrivers is the swamp — the Everglades by another name — and it is where the game gets properly wild. Alligators, airboats, and the "Airgator Airboat 09" at the Grassrivers dock are the landmarks, and the water is a place you travel through, not just look at. The swamp is also where the game's scale shows: a whole region with almost no buildings, where the danger is the wildlife and the water, not the police. Drive carefully, and do not swim where you cannot see the bottom.

Mount Kalaga and the Places Nobody Uses

Mount Kalaga is the mountain — Rockstar's named fishing spot, which points to the game's freshwater side: rivers and lakes away from the coast, in the Leonida countryside. Game Informer's issue 382 named bass fishing specifically, which suggests that different waters hold different fish. Kalaga is the region for players who want to get away from the city entirely: high ground, fresh water, and the kind of quiet that makes the rest of the map feel loud by comparison.

And then there are the places that exist but are not used: players have found two large casinos, a zoo and a big cemetery on the map — built, detailed, and apparently unused by the story. Nobody outside Rockstar knows why they are there: cut content, a future update, or simply scenery built for a world bigger than its plot. They are worth visiting, if only to see how much of Leonida exists for its own sake.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many regions are in GTA 6?

Six, named by Rockstar: Vice City, the Leonida Keys, Grassrivers, Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia and Mount Kalaga. Each has its own character, from the neon city to the swamp, the sugar town and the mountain.

What is Port Gellhorn in GTA 6?

A faded seaside town on the far west of the map, described as the forgotten coast. Its landmark is Delights Cabaret, visited in Chapter 2, and its car wash has a washing-machine icon some players read as a hint at a money-laundering front — unconfirmed.

The Bottom Line

Vice City is only one sixth of Leonida. Start in the Keys, where Jason lives rent-free on Brian Heder's property; cross the Grassrivers swamp by airboat; visit Port Gellhorn's forgotten coast and Ambrosia's sugar refinery and biker gang; and climb to Mount Kalaga for freshwater fishing. And keep an eye out for the casinos, the zoo and the cemetery — built, detailed, and waiting for a reason.
