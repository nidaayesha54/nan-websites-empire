GTA 6 Runs at 30 FPS on Every Console — Here's Why

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Here is the fact every GTA 6 player needs to hear first: the game runs at 30 frames per second on every console, and there is no 60 fps performance mode. Not on PS5, not on Xbox Series X, not anywhere. If you came from games that run at 60, the difference is obvious within seconds — and if you are wondering whether something is wrong with your console, the answer is no. This is how the game was made, and Rockstar has confirmed it.

GTA 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and its visual ambition is the reason for the cap: a dense, living Vice City and the wider Leonida state, with night lighting, real traffic, and detailed streets, at a scale no console can push to 60 fps. This guide explains the cap, why the frame rate dips below 30, the settings that help on each console, and what to do if 30 fps makes you feel unwell.

There Is No 60 FPS Mode

Rockstar confirmed to Kotaku on November 18, 2026, that GTA 6 has no 60 fps performance mode. There is no setting to change, no toggle buried in the menus, and no patch that adds one — at least, none that Rockstar has announced. The game targets 30 fps on every console, and that target is the same whether you are on a base PS5, a PS5 Pro, an Xbox Series X, or an Xbox Series S.

That is worth stating plainly, because the usual advice — "switch to performance mode" — does not apply here. If a video or article promises a 60 fps trick for GTA 6, it is wrong. Do not waste time hunting through menus for an option that does not exist, and do not pay for any "fps unlock" tool: there is no legitimate one.

Why It Dips Below 30

Thirty fps is the target, not a guarantee. On base PS5, the frame rate dips below 30 in heavy combat and explosions, when the screen is full of effects, traffic, and physics. The Xbox Series S is the roughest experience of all: it runs in the mid-20s in busy scenes and can drop to around 18 to 20 frames per second in shootouts. The dips are worst exactly where the game is most demanding — big fights, explosions, and dense city traffic at night.

These dips are the game struggling with its own ambition on fixed hardware. They are not a sign that your console is failing, and they are not something a different disc or a reinstall will fix. They are the cost of the scale Rockstar built.

PS5: Two Settings That Help

On PS5, two settings make the dips less painful. First, clear the console's cache — temporary data can accumulate and make performance worse over time, and a cache clear is a quick, safe step. Second, turn OFF HDCP in the system settings. HDCP is the copy-protection handshake for video output, and disabling it has been reported to smooth out frame drops on base PS5. Neither setting raises the frame rate above 30 — nothing does — but both can make the dips shallower and less noticeable.

To turn off HDCP, go to Settings, then System, then HDMI, and switch HDCP off. Note that turning it off can affect capture and streaming apps that require HDCP, so turn it back on if you stream or record through an external device.

Xbox: Turn On VRR at 120Hz

On Xbox, the fix is VRR — variable refresh rate. Go to Settings, then General, then TV & Display Options, and set the display to 120Hz with VRR enabled. VRR lets the TV match its refresh rate to the game's frame rate, so a dip from 30 to 25 feels like a small stutter instead of a juddery drop. It does not raise the frame rate, but it makes the dips far less noticeable — and on the Series S, where the dips are deepest, it makes the biggest difference.

You need a TV or monitor that supports VRR and 120Hz for this to work. If your display does not support it, the setting will not be available, and there is no software substitute — but the game is still fully playable at its target frame rate.

If 30 FPS Makes You Feel Sick

Some players feel nauseous at 30 fps, especially in a fast, detailed game like this. You cannot raise the frame rate, but you can reduce the effect. Turn the camera more slowly instead of whipping it around. Try the Motion Blur Strength slider — set it low, then try it high, and keep whichever feels better. Pull the camera back to the far view, which makes the motion feel slower. Sit further from the TV, keep a light on in the room, and play in shorter sessions with breaks. These are small changes, but together they make 30 fps much easier to live with.

If the nausea is severe, stop playing and rest. No setting is worth feeling ill over, and shorter sessions with breaks are the most reliable fix of all.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a future patch add a 60 fps mode?

Rockstar has not announced one, and there is no sign of it coming. Treat 30 fps as the game's permanent frame rate and judge the game on that basis.

Is 30 fps a bug on my console?

No. GTA 6 runs at 30 fps on every console by design. Dips below 30 in heavy scenes are performance limits of the hardware, not a fault in your console.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6 is a 30 fps game on every console, with no 60 fps mode — Rockstar confirmed it. Base PS5 dips in heavy combat, and the Series S dips hardest. Clear the cache and turn off HDCP on PS5, or enable VRR at 120Hz on Xbox, to make the dips less noticeable. And if 30 fps makes you feel sick, slow the camera, adjust motion blur, pull the view back, and take breaks. The frame rate is not going to change — but how it feels can.
