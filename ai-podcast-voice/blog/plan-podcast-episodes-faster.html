Plan Podcast Episodes in Half the Time: A 5-Step Workflow

Updated 2026-10-12 · AIPodcastVoice

Plan podcast episodes in half the time: a 5-step workflow — topic, outline, timing, script, voice check — with free tools that do the heavy lifting.

Planning used to be the slowest part of podcasting — most creators spend 2–3 hours outlining a single 30-minute episode. Here's a 5-step workflow that cuts it to under 45 minutes, using free tools that do the mechanical work while you focus on ideas.

Step 1: Lock the topic in one sentence (5 min)

If you can't state the episode's promise in one sentence — "By the end, listeners will understand X" — the topic is too vague. Write the sentence first; everything else serves it.

Step 2: Generate a timed outline (10 min)

Plug your topic into our episode planner: pick your runtime, segment count, and tone, and get an intro/segments/outro structure with talking points and per-segment countdown timers. Edit the segment titles to match your angle — the structure is the starting point, not the finish line.

Step 3: Draft the script from the outline (20 min)

Feed the outline into the script writer with your format (interview, solo, or narrative). You'll get timestamps, speaker cues, and production notes. Now do the part only you can do: add your stories, examples, and opinions. Aim for 150 words per minute of runtime.

Step 4: Voice-check the script (5 min)

Paste your intro into the TTS demo and listen. Awkward phrasing, tongue-twisters, and flat hooks reveal themselves instantly in audio. Fix them now — it's 10× cheaper than re-recording later.

Step 5: Batch and template (5 min)

Save your best-performing outline as a template and reuse the structure weekly. Batch-plan 4 episodes in one sitting: steps 1–2 for all four, then write scripts across the week. Batching is the single biggest time saver in podcasting — context-switching is the hidden tax.

The math

Old way: ~150 minutes per episode of planning and scripting. New way: ~45 minutes. Over a year of weekly episodes, that's 90+ hours saved — time you can spend on promotion, guests, or simply not burning out. The tools are free and run in your browser; the only cost is building the habit.
