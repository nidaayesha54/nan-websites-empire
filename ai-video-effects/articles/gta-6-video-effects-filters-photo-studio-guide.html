GTA 6 Video Effects: Every Filter, Light and Cinematic Trick

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

GTA 6 has the most powerful photo tools any Grand Theft Auto game has ever shipped, and they are available from the first hours of the story. The Photo Studio — the game's photo mode — gives you five light sources, seventeen filters, full exposure controls and eight aspect ratios, and the phone camera adds seventeen more filters of its own. If you make clips or screenshots, these tools are the difference between a flat capture and a shot that looks like it came out of a trailer. This guide covers every effect in the game and how to use them.

The tools matter because GTA 6 is a game people want to show off. Its world is the most detailed Rockstar has built, and the studio clearly expects players to photograph it — the photo mode opens during Lucia's prologue, before you have even finished the first mission, and it works in cutscenes too. The effects are not an afterthought. They are part of the game.

Photo Studio: the full toolkit

To open Photo Studio, pause the game and press Square on PlayStation or X on Xbox. It is available from the moment you control Lucia in the prologue, which means you can use it from the very start of the story. The tools are split into four menus, and each one does something different.

The Camera menu controls the shot itself: camera speed, a composition grid, roll, eight aspect ratios, field of view up to 48.5 degrees, depth of field, aperture, focus distance, a focus guide and an option to hide Jason or Lucia from the frame. The Effects menu is where the image gets its look: exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, saturation, sharpness, grain, temperature, tint, gamma, lens flare, bloom and chromatic aberration. The Lighting menu lets you add up to five extra light sources — spotlights, floodlights and softboxes you can place in the scene. And the Overlays menu holds the gameplay effects, seventeen cosmetic filters, a vignette and colour isolation.

The seventeen filters

The filters are the fastest way to change a shot. The seventeen in Photo Studio include stylised looks like Cartoon Universe, Found Footage and Pixel Art alongside more natural colour grades, and they can be combined with a vignette and colour isolation for a finished look. The phone camera has its own set of seventeen filters, applied before you take the shot — open the Filter option in the rear camera, cycle through the looks, and take the photo with the filter in place.

The two sets are separate tools for separate jobs. Photo Studio is for composed shots: you pause the world, place your lights, set your depth of field and build the image. The phone camera is for quick shots in the middle of play: a selfie with poses and expressions, a 5x zoom on something across the street, a flash photo at night. Both save to the phone's Gallery, and both are worth learning, because the best clips mix the two.

Effects that work in cutscenes

One of the most useful details is that Photo Studio still works during cutscenes, with fewer effects available to adjust. That means you can capture the story's biggest moments — the scenes Rockstar staged most carefully — with your own framing and filters, instead of relying on a screenshot of whatever the camera happened to show.

There is one limit worth knowing: you cannot change Jason and Lucia's poses or facial expressions in Photo Studio. The game gives you the camera, the lights and the filters, but the characters do what the scene tells them to do. If you want posed shots, use the phone camera's selfie mode, which does offer poses and expressions — it is the tool for portraits, while Photo Studio is the tool for scenes.

Motion blur and the streamer settings

Two settings outside Photo Studio matter just as much for how your clips look. Motion blur can be adjusted in Settings, under the Display tab, with a Motion Blur Strength slider that sits at 3 out of 10 by default — and setting it to 0 turns it off completely. If your clips look smeary or your screenshots look soft, that slider is the first thing to check.

The second is the licensed-music mute option, built for streamers and creators. GTA 6's radio is full of licensed songs, and playing them on a stream can get a video muted or taken down. The mute option strips the licensed music while keeping the rest of the audio, so your clips stay safe to publish. It is a small toggle with a big consequence, and every creator should turn it on before recording.

How creators use the effects

Put the tools together and the workflow writes itself. Use the phone camera for quick captures while you play — a sunset over the Keys, a dog on the beach, a car you want to remember. Use Photo Studio for the shots you plan: pause during a mission, hide the character, set the aspect ratio to something cinematic, add a floodlight and a filter, and you have a frame that looks like a film still.

The effects also solve the oldest problem in GTA clips: making gameplay look intentional. A raw capture looks like a game. A capture with depth of field, a warm temperature grade and a vignette looks like a photograph. That is what the seventeen filters are for — not decoration, but a way to make the most detailed world Rockstar has built look the way it deserves to look.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I open Photo Studio in GTA 6?

Pause the game and press Square on PlayStation or X on Xbox. It is available from Lucia's prologue, and it works in cutscenes with fewer effects to adjust.

How many filters does GTA 6 have?

Seventeen in Photo Studio and seventeen more in the phone camera, plus a vignette and colour isolation in Photo Studio. The phone camera also has a flash, a 3x3 grid and 5x zoom.

Can I change poses in GTA 6 photo mode?

Not in Photo Studio — you cannot change Jason and Lucia's poses or expressions there. Use the phone camera's selfie mode for posed shots.

The bottom line: GTA 6 gives you a studio, not a screenshot button. Learn the lights, learn the filters, and your clips will look like they were shot, not captured.
