How Overtime Pay Works in Saudi Arabia

Article 107 of Saudi Labor Law requires employers to pay extra for every hour worked beyond normal working time: the hourly wage plus an additional 50% of the basic wage — in practice, 150% of the hourly rate.

The 150% rule in three steps:
1. Daily rate = monthly wage ÷ 30 days.
2. Hourly rate = daily rate ÷ 8 hours (÷ 6 during Ramadan for Muslim employees).
3. Overtime rate = hourly rate × 1.5. Multiply by your overtime hours.

Worked Example

Monthly wage SAR 6,000, standard 8-hour day: hourly rate = 6,000 ÷ 30 ÷ 8 = SAR 25.00. Overtime rate = 25 × 1.5 = SAR 37.50/hour. 14 overtime hours = SAR 525.00 for the month.

When Does Overtime Apply?

  • Normal limits: 8 hours/day and 48 hours/week. Anything beyond counts as overtime.
  • Ramadan: 6 hours/day and 36 hours/week for Muslim employees — an 8-hour day during Ramadan means 2 overtime hours/day.
  • Rest days and official holidays: all hours worked on the weekly rest day or a public holiday count as overtime at the same 150% rate, and a compensatory rest day may also be due.
  • Shift workers: averaging over up to three weeks is allowed, provided the daily average stays within legal limits.

Who Is Exempt?

Article 108 exempts certain senior roles — top executives and employees with genuine managerial authority over others — when their contract reflects that status. Exemption depends on actual responsibilities, not job title alone. If you are not in such a role, every extra hour must be paid.

FAQs

How do I find my hourly rate?

Divide your monthly wage by 30 (days) and then by your contracted daily hours (8 normally, 6 during Ramadan for Muslim employees). So SAR 4,500 ÷ 30 ÷ 8 = SAR 18.75/hour; overtime = 18.75 × 1.5 = SAR 28.13/hour.

Is work on my weekly rest day paid at 150%?

Yes — hours worked on rest days and official holidays are treated as overtime at the same 150% rate, on top of your normal daily wage. You may also be entitled to a compensatory rest day.

Does overtime include my allowances?

The calculation is based on your wage as defined in your contract — generally the basic salary plus fixed regular allowances. One-off payments, bonuses and variable commissions are normally excluded.

How many hours can my employer ask me to work?

Overtime must be recorded correctly and, where required, agreed with the worker. Keep your own record of extra hours — payslip disputes are far easier to win with your own log.

What if my overtime pay is wrong or unpaid?

Raise it with HR in writing first, showing your hours and the 150% calculation. If unresolved, escalate to the Ministry of Human Resources (MHRSD), which handles labor disputes and can impose penalties.
