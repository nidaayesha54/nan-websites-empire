Saudi Arabia Overtime Rules: 150% Pay, Holidays & Your Rights (Article 107)

When overtime applies in Saudi Arabia, how the 150% rate is calculated, and how holidays and Ramadan hours work.

In-depth 2026 guide~6 min readFree

Saudi Arabia's overtime rule is admirably simple: every overtime hour is paid at 150% of your hourly wage. No tiered rates, no exemptions for salaried staff, no "comp time" substitute in the statute — one clear multiplier in Article 107 of the Saudi Labor Law. The complexity lies in everything around it: what counts as an overtime hour, how the hourly rate is derived, and how holidays and Ramadan reshape the working week.

This English-language guide covers the complete overtime picture: the Article 107 formula, standard vs. Ramadan hours, holiday work, night work, and the mistakes that cost workers money. Verify your own pay with our Saudi Overtime Calculator.

The core rule: Article 107 — 150% of the hourly wage

Article 107 of the Saudi Labor Law provides that hours worked beyond the standard working hours are overtime, paid at 150% of the ordinary hourly wage. The formula:

Overtime pay = hourly wage × 1.5 × overtime hours

The hourly wage itself is derived from your monthly wage by the standard Saudi payroll convention:

Hourly wage = monthly wage ÷ 30 ÷ 8

Example: a monthly wage of SAR 9,000 gives an hourly wage of SAR 37.50, so each overtime hour pays SAR 56.25. Twenty overtime hours in a month = SAR 1,125 — a meaningful addition worth verifying.

Note the wage base question again: "monthly wage" here follows the Labor Law's broad definition — basic pay plus regular allowances (housing, transport). Overtime calculated on basic pay alone understates what you're owed, exactly as with the end-of-service award.

What counts as an overtime hour?

The standard working week in Saudi Arabia is 48 hours — 8 hours per day over 6 days. Any work beyond 8 hours in a day, or beyond 48 in a week, is overtime. Key points:

  • Daily threshold: the 9th hour onward each day is overtime, even if the weekly total stays under 48.
  • Friday work: Friday is the weekly rest day. Work performed on Friday is overtime (paid at 150%) unless it falls under the narrow exceptions for shift-based operations.
  • Public holidays: work on official public holidays — Eid Al-Fitr, Eid Al-Adha, Saudi National Day, Founding Day — is treated as overtime at the 150% rate on top of the holiday pay you're already entitled to.
  • "Actual work" vs. presence: time you are required to be at the workplace at the employer's disposal counts, including waiting time between assignments.

Ramadan: the 36-hour week

During the holy month of Ramadan, working hours for Muslim employees are reduced to 6 hours per day / 36 hours per week — with no reduction in pay. This is one of the most practically important overtime triggers in the Kingdom: hours 7 and 8 of a day during Ramadan, which would be ordinary hours in any other month, become overtime hours at 150%.

Many payroll departments handle this correctly, but Ramadan overtime is also where errors cluster — especially for workers whose schedules don't change on paper while actual hours do. If you worked full 8-hour days through Ramadan, those extra 2 hours daily are overtime. Keep your own log; don't rely on memory in a dispute months later.

Maximum hours and rest

The Labor Law caps the working day and mandates rest in ways that bound overtime:

  • Actual working hours may not exceed 8 per day / 48 per week as standard, with the Ramadan reduction above.
  • Workers must receive rest periods during the day (including prayer, meal, and rest breaks) — these are in addition to working hours, not counted within them for the overtime threshold.
  • Each worker is entitled to a weekly rest day (Friday by default), and to paid public holidays.
  • Overtime should remain the exception: the law's structure assumes a 48-hour norm, and systematic 60-hour weeks signal a staffing problem, not just a payroll line.

Night work

The Labor Law doesn't set a separate night premium the way some jurisdictions do — night hours are paid at the normal rate unless they are overtime hours, in which case the 150% rate applies. The practical effect: a night-shift worker's overtime is calculated identically (hourly wage × 1.5), with the overtime threshold being the same 8-hour day. What matters is accurate timekeeping across midnight — hours after midnight belong to the shift's working day for payroll purposes in most company policies, so confirm how your employer attributes them.

Five overtime mistakes to check on your pay slip

1. Hourly rate derived from basic only. If your allowances are regular, they belong in the monthly wage figure. Recalculate: (basic + allowances) ÷ 30 ÷ 8 × 1.5.

2. Ramadan hours paid at straight time. Hours 7–8 during Ramadan are overtime. This single error can cost two hours of 50% premium daily for a month.

3. Friday/holiday work folded into "normal" pay. Rest-day and public-holiday work attracts the overtime rate — verify it appears as a separate premium line.

4. Unrecorded "extra 30 minutes." Overtime accrues in minutes, not just whole hours. Thirty unrecorded minutes daily is 11+ overtime hours a month.

5. Overtime "offset" against lateness. The law doesn't permit netting your overtime against unrelated deductions. Each stands alone.

What to do if overtime is underpaid

Start with a written query to HR showing your calculation: monthly wage figure used, hourly derivation, overtime hours from your log, and the Article 107 rate. Most discrepancies resolve at this stage — payroll errors are common and often innocent.

If not, the escalation path is a complaint to the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD), which handles wage disputes, and ultimately the labor courts. Keep every pay slip and your own time records; in a dispute, the employer's attendance system is evidence, but your contemporaneous log is your backstop. FAQs

Does the 150% rate apply to salaried (monthly-paid) staff?

Yes. The Labor Law's overtime provisions apply regardless of whether you're paid hourly, daily, or monthly — the hourly rate is derived from the monthly wage using the ÷30÷8 convention.

Can my employer require overtime?

Overtime is generally by agreement and operational need, but the law caps standard hours and requires the premium whenever overtime is worked. Systematic compulsory overtime beyond legal limits is a compliance issue.

Are managers entitled to overtime?

Employees in senior managerial positions with genuine authority may be treated differently under implementing regulations. If your "manager" title doesn't match real authority, you may still be covered — job reality matters more than the title.

How is overtime taxed?

Saudi Arabia has no personal income tax, so overtime is paid gross. Social insurance (GOSI) contributions apply per the usual rules.

What if I work for a company with shifts around the clock?

Continuous-operation employers (healthcare, security, industry) use regulated shift schedules. Overtime within those schedules still attracts the 150% rate for hours beyond the statutory standard.
