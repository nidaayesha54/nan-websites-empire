How to Control Dog Shedding: 9 Proven Solutions 🧹

You can't stop shedding — but you can absolutely control where the hair ends up.

Shedding is normal, healthy, and — for double-coated breeds — seasonal and dramatic. But "normal" doesn't mean your black pants have to be beige. These nine strategies, used together, can cut the loose hair in your home by half or more. They work because they attack shedding at the source: the coat, the skin, and the diet that feeds both.

1. Brush on a Schedule (Not When You Notice Hair)

The single biggest shedding lever is consistent brushing — hair caught in a brush never reaches your couch. Short coats need weekly brushing with a rubber curry brush; double coats need 3–4 sessions per week; long and curly coats need near-daily attention. The mistake most owners make is brushing only when the house looks hairy, by which point it's too late. Put brushing on the calendar with our grooming schedule generator and treat it like a habit, not a reaction.

2. Use the Right Tool for the Coat

The wrong brush just moves hair around. Match the tool to the coat:

  • Short/smooth coats: rubber curry brush or bristle brush — lifts loose hair and massages skin.
  • Double coats: undercoat rake or deshedding tool for the dense underlayer, slicker brush to finish. Use deshedding tools gently — overuse can cut healthy topcoat.
  • Long/silky coats: slicker brush followed by a steel comb; if the comb glides through, you're done.
  • All coats: a high-velocity dryer (or a good shake outside after brushing) blasts out loose undercoat better than any brush.

3. Feed the Coat: Diet and Omega Fatty Acids

Hair is protein, and skin health starts in the bowl. Dogs on low-quality food shed more — it's one of the most consistent patterns vets see. Feed a complete diet with named animal proteins, and consider omega-3 and omega-6 fatty acid supplements (fish oil), which support the skin barrier and measurably reduce excessive shedding in many dogs. Ask your vet for a dose appropriate to your dog's weight. Keep fresh water available — dehydrated skin flakes and sheds more.

4. Bathe Strategically, Not Excessively

A proper bath with thorough brushing before and after removes an astonishing amount of loose hair in one session. But over-bathing backfires: stripped skin gets dry, flaky, and sheds more. Stick to every 4–8 weeks for most dogs (see our frequency chart), use a deshedding or oatmeal shampoo, and always rinse completely. The post-bath brush-out while the coat is clean is prime shedding-capture time.

5. Manage the Seasonal "Blowout"

Double-coated breeds (Huskies, Goldens, German Shepherds, Malamutes) shed their entire undercoat twice a year, typically spring and fall. During a blowout, double your brushing to daily, use the undercoat rake, and consider a professional deshedding treatment — groomers with high-velocity dryers can remove weeks of loose coat in one appointment. This is the one time of year when heavy shedding is completely unavoidable, so plan for it instead of fighting it.

6. Control Your Home Environment

Reduce what lands and make cleanup easy: washable furniture covers, a robot vacuum on a daily schedule, lint rollers at every door, and grooming outdoors or in one easy-clean room when possible. Dry winter air worsens shedding — a humidifier helps both human and canine skin. These don't reduce shedding itself, but they radically reduce the experience of shedding.

7. Rule Out Stress and Health Causes

Sudden increases in shedding, bald patches, red or flaky skin, or constant scratching are not normal shedding — they're symptoms. Common culprits include allergies (food or environmental), parasites, thyroid issues, and stress. If shedding changes abruptly or the skin looks unhealthy, see your vet before buying another brush. No grooming routine fixes a medical problem.

8. Know What NOT to Do

  • Don't shave a double-coated dog to "stop shedding." It doesn't stop shedding, it damages temperature regulation, and the coat may grow back patchy.
  • Don't over-bathe. More baths ≠ less hair; often the opposite.
  • Don't skip the vet when shedding comes with skin changes.
  • Don't expect zero shedding. Even "non-shedding" breeds like Poodles lose some hair — they just trap it in curls (which is why they need so much brushing).

9. Build a Sustainable Routine

The best shedding routine is the one you'll actually do: 5–10 minutes of brushing most days beats a monthly hour-long battle. Pair it with something you already do — brush during TV time, or right after the evening walk. Track it with your personalized grooming schedule, and within a month you'll notice the difference on your floors, your furniture, and your clothes.
