Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is the 2026 federal tax estimator?

It is an estimate built on inflation-adjusted 2025 IRS brackets, clearly labeled as 2026 estimates. It excludes state tax, FICA, AMT, and credits. Confirm your real figures with IRS publications or a tax professional.

What is the difference between marginal and effective tax rate?

Your marginal rate is the tax on your last dollar of income; your effective rate is total tax divided by total income. Because the US uses progressive brackets, your effective rate is always lower than your marginal rate.

Should I take the standard deduction or itemize?

Add up your deductible expenses (mortgage interest, state and local taxes capped at $10,000, charitable gifts). If the total beats the standard deduction — $15,500 single / $31,000 married filing jointly for 2026 estimates — itemize. Otherwise take the standard. Try our free comparison calculator.

When are 2026 taxes due?

Tax year 2025 returns are due April 15, 2026. Tax year 2026 returns are due April 15, 2027. File Form 4868 for an automatic six-month extension if you need more time. See our deadline guide.

What is the standard deduction for 2026?

Our 2026 estimates: $15,500 for single filers, $31,000 for married filing jointly, and $23,250 for head of household. The IRS announces official figures each fall.
