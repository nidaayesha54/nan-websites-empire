Tax Credits vs Deductions: Which Saves More?

By NAN Marketing Agency · October 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Credits and deductions both lower your tax, but they work very differently. A $1,000 tax credit cuts your tax bill by the full $1,000. A $1,000 deduction only cuts it by $1,000 times your marginal rate — $220 if you are in the 22% bracket.

The Core Difference

  • Deductions reduce your taxable income. Their value depends on your bracket.
  • Credits reduce your tax bill directly, dollar for dollar.

Worked Example

Say you owe $5,000 in tax and you are in the 22% bracket:

  • A $1,000 deduction saves you $220 → you owe $4,780.
  • A $1,000 credit saves you $1,000 → you owe $4,000.

The credit is worth more than 4x as much in this case.

Refundable vs Nonrefundable Credits

Nonrefundable credits can only reduce your tax to zero — you lose any leftover. Refundable credits (like parts of the Child Tax Credit and the Earned Income Tax Credit) can push your tax below zero and generate a refund. That is free money many filers leave on the table.

Credits Worth Checking

  • Child Tax Credit and Child/Dependent Care Credit
  • American Opportunity and Lifetime Learning education credits
  • Saver's Credit for retirement contributions
  • Residential clean energy credits
