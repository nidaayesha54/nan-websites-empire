Standard Deduction vs Itemizing: Which Wins?

By NAN Marketing Agency · Updated October 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Every taxpayer gets to subtract either a flat standard deduction or their actual itemized deductions from income — whichever is larger. About 90% of filers take the standard deduction, but itemizing can win big if you have a mortgage, high state taxes, or generous giving.

2026 Estimated Standard Deductions

  • Single / married filing separately: $15,500
  • Married filing jointly: $31,000
  • Head of household: $23,250

Filers 65 or older (or blind) get an additional amount on top.

What You Can Itemize

  • Mortgage interest on up to $750,000 of acquisition debt.
  • State and local taxes (SALT) — income or sales tax plus property tax, capped at $10,000.
  • Charitable donations to qualified organizations (cash and non-cash).
  • Medical expenses exceeding 7.5% of your adjusted gross income.

The SALT Cap Changes Everything

Before 2018 there was no cap, so high earners in high-tax states routinely itemized. With the $10,000 cap, many now find the standard deduction beats their itemized total. If your property tax alone exceeds $10,000, that extra tax buys you zero additional deduction.

A Simple Decision Rule

Add up: mortgage interest + min(SALT, $10,000) + charity + qualifying medical. If the total exceeds your standard deduction, itemize; otherwise take the standard. Our standard vs itemized calculator does this math for you in seconds.

Bunching: A Pro Strategy

If your itemized total hovers near the standard amount, consider bunching: make two years of charitable gifts in one year to itemize, then take the standard deduction the next year. Over time you deduct more than taking the standard every year.
