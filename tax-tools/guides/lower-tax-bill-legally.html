How to Lower Your Tax Bill Legally (2026)

By NAN Marketing Agency · Updated October 12, 2026 · 7 min read

Paying less tax is not about loopholes — it is about using the rules Congress wrote. Here are nine fully legal strategies that work for the 2026 tax year.

1. Max Out Your 401(k) or 403(b)

Every pre-tax dollar you contribute lowers your taxable income this year. At a 22% marginal rate, a $10,000 contribution saves $2,200 in federal tax — plus you keep the money.

2. Fund an HSA If Eligible

Health Savings Accounts are triple tax-advantaged: deductible contributions, tax-free growth, tax-free withdrawals for medical costs. If you have a high-deductible health plan, this is the best deal in the tax code.

3. Contribute to a Traditional IRA

Deductible IRA contributions reduce taxable income now (subject to income limits if you have a workplace plan). Even a Roth IRA, while not deductible, builds tax-free wealth for later.

4. Claim Every Credit You Deserve

Credits beat deductions dollar-for-dollar — see our credits vs deductions explainer. Check the Child Tax Credit, education credits (AOTC, Lifetime Learning), and the Saver's Credit.

5. Bunch Deductions

If your itemized deductions are close to the standard amount, group two years of charitable giving or medical procedures into one year to itemize, then take the standard the next year.

6. Harvest Investment Losses

Sell losing investments to offset gains, and up to $3,000 of ordinary income per year. Watch the wash-sale rule: don't rebuy the same security within 30 days.

7. Time Your Income

Freelancers and business owners can defer December invoices to January or accelerate deductible expenses into the current year to shift income into a lower-tax year.

8. Use a Flexible Spending Account

FSA contributions for health or dependent care come out pre-tax, cutting both income and payroll taxes — if your employer offers one.

9. Adjust Withholding Instead of Overpaying

A big refund is an interest-free loan to the IRS. Update your W-4 so you keep more of each paycheck, then invest the difference. Check where you stand with our refund estimator.
