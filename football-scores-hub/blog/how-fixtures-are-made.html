How Football Fixtures Are Really Made

October 12, 2026 · 5 min read

It starts with a computer — and 1,000 rules

Every June, fixture lists drop and fans immediately hunt for the derbies. Behind that list is a scheduling program fed with constraints: clubs that share a city can't both be home on the same weekend (police request), teams in Europe need spacing around midweek games, and broadcasters want marquee matches in prime TV slots.

TV money moves kickoffs

The dates you see in June are provisional. Broadcasters select games in batches through the season, which is why a Saturday 3pm game becomes a Sunday 4:30pm game. That's also why our schedule planner labels its templates honestly — the pattern is predictable, the exact slot often isn't.

Fairness is engineered in

Schedulers balance home/away sequences so no team gets five away games in a row, and they try to mirror the first half of the season in the second. It isn't perfect — someone always complains about the run-in — but it's far from random.

The human touch

After the computer proposes a list, humans review it: checking for clashes with major events, stadium availability, and fan travel.
