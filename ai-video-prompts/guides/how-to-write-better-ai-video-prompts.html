How to Write Better AI Video Prompts

Most AI video prompts fail for one reason: they're written like search queries, not like director's notes. "Dragon video" gives the model nothing to work with. A director's note tells the model what, doing what, shot how, lit how, in what style, and feeling like what. That is the 6-element framework.

The 6 Elements

1. Subject — who or what is in frame

Be specific and visual. Not "a dog" but "a tiny corgi in a red raincoat". Distinctive details survive generation; generic nouns don't.

2. Action — what is moving

AI video is motion. Every prompt needs motion verbs: flying, pouring, spinning, gliding, exploding. No action = a slideshow.

3. Camera move — how we see it

This is the element beginners skip and pros never do. "Slow dolly-in", "aerial orbit", "low tracking shot" — camera language is the fastest quality upgrade you can make.

4. Lighting — the mood-maker

Golden hour, neon glow, moonlight, volumetric rays. Lighting does half the emotional work of your video.

5. Style — the visual language

Cinematic, photorealistic, anime, claymation, vintage film. One style word anchors the entire render.

6. Mood — the feeling

Epic, serene, mysterious, joyful. Mood words guide color, pacing and atmosphere together.

Before / After

Before (score: ~25):

A beach at sunset

After (score: 95):

Bioluminescent waves glowing electric blue on a moonlit beach, stars above, slow shoreline tracking shot, magical glowing footprints in wet sand, otherworldly natural beauty, serene and dreamlike.

Same idea. The second version tells the model the subject (bioluminescent waves), the action (glowing, footprints forming), the camera (slow shoreline tracking shot), the lighting (moonlit), the style (natural beauty) and the mood (serene, dreamlike, otherworldly).

5 Rules That Always Help

  • Front-load the important. Models weight early words more. Subject and action first, adjectives later.
  • One scene per prompt. "Then the dragon lands" confuses the model — generate scenes separately and edit together.
  • Name the lens. "85mm", "macro", "anamorphic", "35mm film" are strong style anchors.
  • Ban the junk. End prompts with "no text, no watermark, no extra limbs" to suppress common artifacts.
  • Iterate on one element. Change only the camera move or only the lighting between tries — you'll learn what each control does.
