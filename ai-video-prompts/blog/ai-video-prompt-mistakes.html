7 Prompt Mistakes Killing Your AI Videos

If your AI generations keep coming out generic, blurry, or just… off — the tool probably isn't the problem. These seven prompt mistakes account for most disappointing results.

1. The one-word prompt

"Dragon." "Beach." The model has to invent everything, so it invents the most average version of everything. Fix: minimum 25 words covering subject, action, camera, lighting, style and mood.

2. No camera direction

Prompts without camera language render like static postcards. Fix: always include one camera move — "slow dolly-in", "aerial orbit", "low tracking shot".

3. Style soup

"Photorealistic anime cinematic vintage" — contradictory styles cancel out and the model picks randomly. Fix: one style anchor per prompt.

4. Multiple scenes in one prompt

"A dragon flies over the city then lands in the park" — models handle one moment well, sequences badly. Fix: one scene per generation; edit clips together after.

5. Forgetting motion verbs

Describing a still scene ("a beautiful mountain") gives you a slideshow. Fix: every prompt needs motion — wind, water, walking, swirling, exploding.

6. Ignoring lighting

Two prompts with identical subjects look completely different under "harsh noon sun" vs "golden hour". Fix: always name the light.

7. Never iterating

One generation, one disappointment, prompt abandoned. Fix: change exactly one element per retry — you'll learn what each control does, and the prompt checklist shows you which element is weak.

The 30-Second Fix Workflow

  1. Grab a proven base from the prompt library.
  2. Swap in your subject, keep the camera and lighting language.
  3. Run it through the checklist — aim for 80+.
  4. Generate, then change one element per retry.
