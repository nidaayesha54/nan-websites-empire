GTA 6 Choppy Audio? The 2-Minute Fix That Works

November 20, 2026 · 4 min read

It happens in the opening flashback chase: the audio starts stuttering and cutting out, and at the same time the controller locks into a vibration loop that will not stop. The scene is supposed to be intense — but this is not atmosphere, it is a glitch, and it makes the chase nearly unplayable. The good news is that the fix is known, it takes about two minutes, and it does not require reinstalling anything.

GTA 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. The choppy-audio-plus-vibration problem is one of the specific launch issues players have documented with a verified workaround. This guide explains what is happening and the three fixes to try, in order.

What Is Actually Happening

The problem is a clash between two features in the same scene: the advanced trigger resistance on the controller and the spatial headphone sound in the audio settings. During the opening flashback chase, the game drives both hard at once — heavy trigger effects for the driving, and positional audio for the chase — and on some setups the two fight each other. The result is choppy, stuttering audio and a vibration loop in the controller that keeps going even when the scene calms down.

It is a settings-level problem, not a broken controller and not a broken game. That is why the fixes are all in the settings menus: turn off the two features that are clashing, and the scene plays normally.

Fix 1: Turn Off Advanced Trigger Resistance

Open the game's settings and find the controller options. Look for the advanced trigger resistance setting — the adaptive trigger effects on the DualSense — and turn it OFF, temporarily. This is the first fix because the trigger effects are the most likely trigger of the loop: the chase scene leans on them heavily, and disabling them removes the vibration loop at its source.

Turning this off does not hurt the game. The trigger effects are immersion, not mechanics — the driving, shooting, and controls all work exactly the same without them. You can turn the setting back on after the chase if you want the effects for the rest of the game, but many players simply leave it off.

Fix 2: Disable Spatial Headphone Sound

Next, open the audio menu in the game's settings and disable spatial headphone sound. Spatial audio processes the game's sound into a 3D field for headphones, and in this scene it is the other half of the clash: the processing load and the trigger effects together overwhelm the audio path, and the sound starts chopping.

Disabling spatial sound returns the audio to standard stereo, which the scene handles without trouble. If you normally play with a headset, this is the setting to check first after the trigger fix — and if the audio clears up immediately, you have found your culprit. You can re-enable spatial sound later and see whether the problem returns; if it does, leave it off for chase-heavy sections.

Fix 3: The 2-Minute Unplug

If the first two fixes do not clear it, do a hard power cycle: turn the console off, unplug it from the wall, and leave it unplugged for two full minutes. Then plug it back in and power it on. Two minutes unplugged clears the hardware cache, the RAM, and the audio and haptics state that a normal restart preserves — it is a deeper reset than turning the console off and on, and it is the fix that works when the settings changes alone do not.

Do not rush the two minutes. The point is to let every capacitor drain and every cached state clear; thirty seconds is not the same as two minutes. Use the time to double-check the two settings above, then power on and replay the chase.

If It Comes Back

If the problem returns in a later chase scene, repeat the same steps: trigger resistance off, spatial sound off, and a two-minute unplug if needed. Also check for a game update — launch-week patches have addressed audio issues, and an update may contain a permanent fix. And if the problem appears in a different scene, not the opening chase, the same fixes still apply: the clash is in the settings, not in one specific mission.

One more check: if the vibration loop continues even in menus, after the fixes, power the controller itself off and on again. A controller that keeps vibrating after the game is closed has held onto the haptics state, and a controller restart clears it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my controller broken?

No. The choppy audio and vibration loop come from a settings clash in the opening chase scene, not from faulty hardware. The settings fixes in this guide resolve it.

Will unplugging the console delete my saves?

No. A power cycle does not delete saves or games. It only clears temporary hardware state — cache, RAM, and the audio and haptics state.

The Bottom Line

Choppy audio and a stuck vibration loop in GTA 6's opening chase come from advanced trigger resistance and spatial headphone sound clashing in one scene. Turn the trigger resistance off, disable spatial sound, and if it persists, unplug the console for two full minutes. Two minutes of fixes, and the chase plays the way it was meant to.
