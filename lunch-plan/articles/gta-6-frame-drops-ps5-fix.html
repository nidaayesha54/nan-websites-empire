GTA 6 Frame Drops on PS5? What You Can Do

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

GTA 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S — thirteen years after GTA 5 — and for many players the first surprise was not Vice City or the dual protagonists, Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos. It was the frame rate: the game stutters in heavy combat, dips during explosions, and on the weakest console feels genuinely rough.

Here is the short version: GTA 6 runs at 30 frames per second on every console, there is no 60 fps performance mode to switch on, and some dips are part of the game as it shipped. But there are real things you can do on both PlayStation and Xbox to smooth out the worst of it — and a few comfort tweaks if 30 fps makes you feel queasy.

The 30 FPS Reality: No 60 FPS Performance Mode

Rockstar confirmed to Kotaku on November 18, 2026, that GTA 6 runs at 30 fps on every console, with no 60 fps performance mode. There is no hidden setting to unlock and no performance-versus-fidelity toggle buried in the menus. If you came to GTA 6 from games that offer a smooth 60 fps mode, the difference is immediately noticeable — camera pans feel heavier, driving feels less fluid, and fast action looks choppier than you may expect from a 2026 blockbuster.

That 30 fps target is the baseline, not a promise. In quiet moments — walking a beach, driving a country road — the game generally holds it. The trouble starts when the screen fills up: shootouts, explosions, police chases, and crowded streets are where the frame rate gives way.

Base PS5 owners have it better, but the dips below 30 fps in heavy combat and explosions are real and widely reported. If your game only stutters during the loudest, busiest moments, that matches the launch behaviour — frustrating, but it is the game, not your hardware failing.

The PS5 Fix: Clear the Cache and Turn Off HDCP

If you play on PS5, two system-level steps are the verified fixes to try first. Neither changes the 30 fps cap — nothing can — but both can reduce stutter and smooth out the dips.

  1. Clear the cache. Power the PS5 off fully — not rest mode — then boot into Safe Mode and choose Clear Cache and Rebuild Database. A full restart clears out temporary data that builds up over long sessions and updates, and rebuilding the database tidies how the system reads game data from the drive.
  2. Turn off HDCP in system settings. Go to your PS5 system settings and turn HDCP off. HDCP is the copy-protection handshake between your console and your TV, and disabling it removes one more piece of processing from the display chain. Note that turning HDCP off can affect playback in some streaming apps, so if you watch video on the same console, you may want to turn it back on afterwards.

Before you play, also make sure your console is fully up to date. GTA 6 requires PS5 system software 26.07-14.23.00, and the game itself is a 212 GB install — so check that the latest game update is installed and that you have the storage headroom for it. An out-of-date console is the simplest explanation for performance that is worse than everyone else's.

The Xbox Fix: Enable VRR and Set 120Hz

Xbox players have a different pair of settings to reach for. In your Xbox display settings, enable Variable Refresh Rate (VRR) and set the refresh rate to 120Hz. VRR lets your TV adjust its refresh rate to match the console's output frame by frame, which smooths out the judder when the game dips below 30 fps — instead of a hard stutter, you get a softer, less jarring slowdown. The 120Hz setting gives the display more room to do that matching.

Two conditions apply. First, your TV or monitor needs to support VRR — most modern gaming TVs do, but older sets may not show the option. Second, fully quit the game before relaunching it after changing settings, so the new display settings apply cleanly.

If 30 FPS Makes You Feel Queasy

For some players, the real problem is not the number on a counter — it is how 30 fps feels. Motion sickness at low frame rates is common, and it tends to hit hardest in exactly the moments GTA 6 struggles: fast camera movement, driving, and combat. These comfort tweaks help:

  • Turn the camera slower. Reduce your camera sensitivity so pans are gentler and less likely to trigger nausea.
  • Experiment with the Motion Blur Strength slider. Try it low first, then high — different players find opposite settings more comfortable, so test both rather than assuming one is right.
  • Use the far camera view. A wider, further-back camera shows more of the world at once and makes motion feel less intense.
  • Sit further from the TV. More distance shrinks the moving image in your field of vision, which reduces the sensory mismatch that causes sickness.
  • Keep a light on in the room. Playing in a dark room makes the screen fill your vision; a lit room gives your eyes a stable reference point.
  • Play in shorter sessions. Take regular breaks, especially during your first few days, and stop as soon as you feel unwell rather than pushing through it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there a 60 fps mode in GTA 6? No. Rockstar confirmed to Kotaku on November 18, 2026, that the game runs at 30 fps on every console, with no 60 fps performance mode.

Can I play GTA 6 on PC instead for a higher frame rate? No — there is no PC version at launch, and Rockstar has not announced a PC date. Any "PC download" link you see is a scam; do not click it.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6 at launch is a 30 fps game on every console, and no setting will turn it into a 60 fps one. Base PS5 players should expect dips below 30 in heavy combat and explosions; Xbox Series S players should expect the roughest ride, down to around 18–20 fps in shootouts. What you can control is the worst of it: on PS5, clear the cache and turn off HDCP; on Xbox, enable VRR and set 120Hz; and if the frame rate makes you queasy, slow the camera, try both ends of the Motion Blur Strength slider, use the far camera view, sit further back, keep a light on, and play in shorter sessions.
