GTA 6 Robberies: From Loot Bag to Bank Balance

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Every robbery in Grand Theft Auto 6 follows the same arc: get in, grab the goods, get out, and somehow turn a bag full of stolen things into money you can spend. The game does not do that last step for you. Loot goes into a duffel bag, the bag goes to a fence, the fence pays cash, and the cash goes to the bank — and at every step, the loot can be lost. This guide walks the whole cycle, from the first smashed display case to the deposit at the ATM, and explains the three money pools, the five robbery tiers, and the buyers who pay different prices for the same bag.

Money is not just a score here: missions stay locked until you raise the cash they ask for, so the robbery cycle is the engine of the story, not a side activity.

Three pools of money

GTA 6 tracks money in three places, not one. There is cash on your person, which can be deposited into a bank or lost; an ATM card; and the digital BuckMe bank balance, which is the safe part of your savings. Cash in your pocket can be lost — when your character dies, for example — while money in the BuckMe account stays safe no matter what happens. The habit to build early is simple: visit a physical ATM after every large robbery, because the old trick of uploading cash over Wi-Fi is gone. Deposits happen at an ATM, in person, and the BuckMe app on the phone lets you check the balance and possibly send money to others.

Jason and Lucia share one wallet, so cash earned by one hero is available to the other. Money from a robbery done as Lucia can be spent on a car that Jason buys later, with no need to move money between characters. They also share their hideouts and their Wanted level, which is why a robbery gone wrong follows both of them.

The duffel bag: where loot lives

Stolen items go into a duffel bag — Rockstar calls them stash bags — and until the bag is sold, the loot inside is not money yet. Bags typically hold narcotics, jewelry and marked bills, and they are picked up automatically when Jason and Lucia rob a drug den, a jewelry store or a business with a lot of cash on hand. Each of them can carry up to two bags, but the second bag limits weapon use, since one of their hands is used to carry it. You can hand one of your two bags to your partner by holding the right d-pad and choosing the option to give them a bag.

Once free of the police, stash bags in the trunk of your vehicle — not a motorcycle. In a personal vehicle the bag should be safe, so long as the vehicle is not destroyed or impounded, so you can run a robbery spree and pile up bags: some trunks hold two, some six. If you are captured or wasted while carrying a bag, you will probably lose it, plus part of your cash reserve — a steeper price than in GTA 5.

Fences and buyers: same bag, different price

When you are ready to offload a stash bag, you need someone who will take it off your hands — and not every buyer pays the same. Various characters throughout Leonida will buy stash bags, and each one offers different prices for different goods. Early on, you drop bags off at Brian's or Donnie's lockups, before moving on to other characters as the story progresses. Place your icon over a buyer on the map and you get a list of what they are paying for each type of item, so sell to the buyer paying the most for what you are carrying.

There are also specific fences for specific stolen items: a car fence, a jewelry fence, and fences for drugs and artwork, unlocked as you meet new contacts. Stolen cars can be registered under your name through a fence for a fee. The rule is the same everywhere: the loot is not money until a fence buys it, and the fence you choose is the difference between a good robbery and a wasted one.

The five tiers of robbery

Robberies scale from low-risk to high-risk, and the reward scales with them. Small stores and gas stations pay little but are easy to solo; jewelry shops pay more and typically need both Jason and Lucia; bank heists sit at the top, with the biggest payouts and the heaviest police response. Security vans and gang compounds fill out the middle. The map displays the potential take, so you can see what a clean job could earn before you start it.

Bank jobs add one choice: explosives open the safe fast but damage part of the loot, while finding the combination takes longer but pays in full. Use the combination when the police are far away; use explosives when speed matters more than the last dollar.

Stashing, storing, and the story tax

Not ready to sell yet? Leave the bag in a storage facility once you unlock those in Chapter 3, or in the trunk of a car parked in a garage, where your vehicles are safe. Either way, the bag is only safe while it is hidden — the safest place of all is the bank, after the fence has paid you.

Remember the story tax, too. Money you earn in free roam directly affects story progression, because the game requires certain cash amounts to unlock specific missions. Rockstar says the game can slow or halt progression until you reach those cash goals, so you must do off-mission robbery and fencing to convert stolen goods into spendable money. Robbing is not optional grinding. It is how the story moves.

FAQ

How do stash bags work in GTA 6? Stolen narcotics, jewelry and marked bills go into a duffel bag automatically. Each character carries up to two bags; the second limits weapon use. Stash bags in your car trunk, sell them to a buyer, and bank the cash at an ATM.

Do all buyers pay the same for loot? No. Different buyers pay different prices for different goods, so check the map for what each one offers and sell to the highest payer. There are also specific fences for cars, jewelry, drugs and artwork.

What happens if I die carrying a stash bag? You will probably lose the bag and part of your cash reserve. Money in the BuckMe bank account is safe, which is why you deposit after every big job.

The bottom line: in GTA 6, loot is not money until a fence buys it, and cash is not safe until it is in the bank. Rob, fence, deposit — and do it before the next mission asks you for the money.
