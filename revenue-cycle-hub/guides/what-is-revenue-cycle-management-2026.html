What Is Revenue Cycle Management? (2026 Guide)

Revenue cycle management (RCM) is the end-to-end process healthcare providers use to track revenue from the moment a patient schedules an appointment until the final payment is collected. A clean revenue cycle means faster payments, fewer denials, and less money left on the table.

The 7 Stages of the Revenue Cycle

  1. Pre-registration & scheduling — collect demographics and insurance details before the visit.
  2. Eligibility verification — confirm coverage is active and the service is covered. This is where most denials are born — or prevented. Try our eligibility checklist generator.
  3. Charge capture & coding — document services accurately and assign correct CPT/ICD-10 codes.
  4. Claim submission — send clean claims electronically, ideally within 24–48 hours of the encounter.
  5. Denial management — work every denial within 48 hours. Log them with our denial tracker to spot patterns.
  6. Payment posting — post payments and contractual adjustments accurately.
  7. Patient collections — bill patients for their responsibility with clear statements and payment options.

Key RCM Metrics to Watch

  • AR days — target under 40. Calculate yours with our AR days calculator.
  • Denial rate — keep initial denials under 5–10%.
  • Clean claim rate — aim for 95%+ of claims accepted on first submission.
  • Net collection rate — the percentage of allowable revenue actually collected; target 95%+.

In-House vs. Outsourced RCM

Small practices often run RCM in-house with a billing manager and clearinghouse software. Larger groups may outsource to billing companies that charge 4–8% of collections. The right choice depends on your denial rate, AR days, and staff bandwidth — the metrics above will tell you.
