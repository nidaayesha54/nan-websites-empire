How to Reduce Claim Denials

Industry data consistently shows that 5–10% of claims are denied on first submission — and the majority are preventable. Here's a practical playbook to cut your denial rate.

1. Fix the Front End First

Up to 90% of denials trace back to front-end errors: wrong demographics, inactive coverage, missing authorizations. Generate a fresh eligibility checklist for every patient type and make it mandatory at check-in.

2. Verify Eligibility in Real Time

Batch eligibility checks the night before are better than nothing, but real-time verification at check-in catches last-minute coverage terminations — the most common eligibility denial.

3. Tighten Coding Accuracy

Coding errors (wrong CPT, missing modifiers, ICD-10 mismatches) are the second-biggest denial driver. Run pre-bill coding audits on high-dollar claims and track coder-specific error rates.

4. Submit Claims Within 48 Hours

Late submission causes timely-filing denials, which are nearly impossible to appeal. Build a daily claim-drop routine so nothing sits longer than two business days.

5. Work Denials Within 48 Hours

Denials age badly — appeal success drops the longer they wait. Log every denial in our denial tracker, sort by dollar value, and work the biggest ones first.

6. Find Your Pattern, Then Fix the Root Cause

One payer denying for prior auth is a process problem; five payers is a training problem. Categorize denials by reason (eligibility, auth, coding, timely filing) and attack the top category — that alone often cuts total denials by 30–50%.

7. Measure What Matters

Track denial rate, clean claim rate, and AR days monthly. What gets measured gets managed.
