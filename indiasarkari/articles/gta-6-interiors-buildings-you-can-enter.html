GTA 6 Interiors: The Buildings You Can Actually Walk Into

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and one of its biggest changes is also one of the easiest to miss: the doors open. In GTA 5, most buildings were scenery — facades with nothing behind them. In GTA 6, hundreds of buildings have real interiors you can walk into, from nightclubs and hotels to pawn shops and police stations. The exact number is where the story gets complicated, because the figure everyone quotes — 700-plus — is not what Rockstar confirmed. This guide separates what is confirmed from what is reported, lists the kinds of buildings that actually open, and explains why the difference matters.

That matters because the interior count is the most exaggerated number in GTA 6's marketing. Sites that print "700+" as a fact are repeating a rumour, not a Rockstar statement — and the honest number, while smaller, is still the biggest change in the series' history. Knowing which is which is the difference between a guide you can trust and one you cannot.

What Rockstar Has Actually Confirmed: Hundreds

The honest answer to "how many buildings can you enter in GTA 6" is a range, and Rockstar's own word for it is "hundreds". When one outlet put the 700 figure to Rockstar during the preview sessions, the figure itself went unconfirmed — but Rockstar did confirm that the enterable count is in the hundreds, and Rob Nelson separately called it a huge number of buildings. The French magazine Trois Couleurs got the same refusal with a different adjective: Nelson declined to give its reporter a number and called the quantity of interiors enormous.

The strongest statement came from Rockstar North co-studio head Aaron Garbut, who told Edge magazine in its October 29, 2026 cover story that GTA 6 contains "an order of magnitude more interiors" than Rockstar's previous games. GTA 5 shipped with a few dozen genuinely enterable buildings, depending on how you count shops, safehouses, and mission-only spaces — so an order of magnitude more means hundreds, and that is the number Rockstar has actually put its name to. No exact total has been published, and no breakdown by region exists.

Where 700+ Comes From — and Why It Is Not Confirmed

The 700-plus figure comes from insider reporting from January 2025, which said the game had 700-plus enterable interiors in total, with roughly 40 percent of large buildings enterable and effectively all smaller shops open. That reporting was picked up, repeated, and eventually printed as fact by sites that never checked it — but it was never confirmed by Rockstar, and it rests on early material rather than the finished game. The community mapping project behind the State of Leonida map has counted about 296 confirmed interiors from every piece of available footage, and other analysts put the range at 300 to 1,000, personally leaning under 1,000. Treat the totals as reporting, not fact: the types are confirmed, the numbers are estimates, and the exact count will only settle once players have walked through the finished game and counted for themselves.

The 70 percent figure that sometimes appears alongside 700 is even weaker — the creator relaying it declines to believe it, and no source supports it. Ignore any guide that prints a percentage. The honest numbers are these: hundreds confirmed, 700-plus reported, about 300 counted from footage so far.

What You Can Actually Walk Into

The confirmed and reported roster spans the whole civic fabric of Leonida. On the social side: bars, restaurants, fast-food joints, nightclubs, and hotels. On the commercial strip: pawn shops, gun stores, supermarkets, convenience stores, gas stations, and laundromats. And the heavyweight civic buildings — banks, hospitals, police stations, and courthouses — are reported enterable too, along with public transit like the Vice City Metro Station and the malls that anchor the shopping districts.

Enterable buildings are marked blue on the map, and the game renders their floor plans as you approach — a small detail that tells you the interior is real before you open the door. Interiors are tied to confirmed systems rather than to scripted sequences alone: store robberies happen inside shops, exploration rewards sit inside buildings, and the safehouse is itself an interior with its own systems. The world is not a backdrop with doors painted on it. It is a place with rooms in it.

What Isn't True About Interiors

Not every building opens. Vice City alone contains far more than 700 buildings, and the claim is about enterable spaces, which are a subset — most towers and offices are still facades. There is no confirmation that every building is enterable, no published percentage of accessible buildings, and no rule that makes every door open in free roam. Some interiors are mission-only, some are gated behind story progress, and some are shops that open only for Ultimate Edition owners. The honest picture is a city with hundreds of real rooms in it, not a city with no locked doors — and that is still ten times what GTA 5 offered.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many interiors does GTA 6 have?

Rockstar has confirmed hundreds, with no exact total published. The 700-plus figure is insider reporting from January 2025, not a confirmed number, and community counts from footage sit around 300 so far.

Can you enter every building in GTA 6?

No. Enterable buildings are marked blue on the map, and they are a subset of the city — most towers and offices are still facades, and some interiors are mission-only or gated behind story progress.

What kinds of buildings can you enter in GTA 6?

Nightclubs, hotels, restaurants, pawn shops, gun stores, supermarkets, convenience stores, gas stations, laundromats, banks, hospitals, police stations, courthouses, metro stations, and malls.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6 has hundreds of buildings you can walk into — the most Rockstar has ever built, an order of magnitude more than GTA 5's few dozen — and the exact count is still being settled by players, not by press releases. Forget the 700-plus headline and the 70 percent claim: what matters is the map, where enterable buildings glow blue, and the door in front of you, which actually opens.
