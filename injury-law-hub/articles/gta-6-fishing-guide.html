GTA 6 Fishing: Where to Catch Fish in Vice City

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and one of its quietest surprises is also one of its most relaxing: you can go fishing. Fishing appears in Rockstar's Extended Look as one of the game's player activities, alongside jet skiing, weight lifting, boating, canoeing and basketball — and in the finished game it turns out to be a full little system of its own, with rods to find, spots to learn, and a side quest built around a single stubborn fish. This guide explains where to fish in GTA 6, how it works, and — just as importantly — what you cannot do with your catch.

The first thing to understand is the rule that shapes everything else: fishing in GTA 6 is catch and release only. You cannot keep your fish, you cannot sell them, and there is no fish market or cooking system waiting at the end of a good day on the water. That will disappoint anyone hoping for a fishing economy, but it also frees the activity up to be what it clearly is: a calm break from the story, a reason to stand somewhere beautiful for a while, and a small challenge with a real reward attached.

Your First Rod: Key Lento Jetty

Your first fishing rod is waiting at the Key Lento jetty, right where Jason's story begins. Post-launch guides report that you pick the rod up on the jetty itself — no purchase, no quest, no unlock chain. Just walk out onto the jetty and take it. From there the basics are simple: cast out, then use Focus to spot fish in the water before you commit to a spot. Focus highlights what is swimming below, so you are not casting blind into an empty stretch of sea.

The first catch most players land here is a Stoplight Parrotfish, and it is a good introduction to how the system feels: the fish fights, the line matters, and the catch itself is the reward. Key Lento is a quiet place to learn — the water is calm, the jetty is easy to reach, and there is nothing else competing for your attention. If you are the kind of player who likes to slow a big game down, this is where that habit starts.

The Cal Side Quest: Catch a Bigger Fish

Fishing gets a proper quest through Cal, in a side mission called "Catch A Bigger Fish". The target is Gambit Bay, and the goal is a stoplight parrotfish over two pounds — bigger than the one you caught at Key Lento. It sounds simple, and it is simple, but it is also the kind of task that teaches you the difference between casting anywhere and casting where the fish actually are. Gambit Bay is the place to be patient: watch the water with Focus, pick your spot, and wait for the right fish.

The reward is worth the trip. Completing the quest increases your Focus — the same ability you use to spot fish in the first place — and earns the "Baited My Tackle" trophy. That makes this one of the rare side activities in GTA 6 that pays you back in a mechanic, not just a line of dialogue. If you care about trophies, or about making Focus stronger for the rest of the game, this is a quest to do early rather than late.

Where Else to Fish: Mount Kalaga and Bass

Rockstar has named Mount Kalaga as a fishing spot, which points to the game's freshwater side — rivers and lakes away from the coast, in the Leonida countryside. And Game Informer, in issue 382, named bass fishing specifically, which suggests that different waters hold different fish. The game has not published a fish list, so nobody can yet say exactly how many species there are or where each one lives. What is clear is that fishing is not a one-spot activity: the coast, the bay and the mountains all have their own water, and the game expects you to explore them.

That missing list is also a warning. Before launch, several "rare fish" guides circulated naming Los Santos, Paleto Bay and the Golden Trout as GTA 6 fishing spots. Those are GTA 5 locations and GTA 5 fish — they have nothing to do with GTA 6, and any guide that lists them is recycling old content. Trust only what has been verified in the finished game: Key Lento, Gambit Bay and Mount Kalaga are real. Everything else is still unconfirmed.

What Fishing Is For

So why fish at all, if you cannot keep or sell anything? Because GTA 6 is a game that rewards slowing down. The story is about escape, and the world around it — the Leonida Keys, the swamps, the mountains — is built to be lived in, not just driven through. Fishing is one of the activities that lets you do that: stand on a jetty, watch the water, and let the game's most beautiful places do their work for a while.

It also connects to the rest of the game in small, practical ways. Focus is a real mechanic with real uses beyond fishing, and the Cal quest makes it stronger. The boats you use to reach the best spots — including the free fishing boat behind Jason's Key Lento house — are part of the same water world. Fishing is not a separate mini-game bolted on the side; it is one thread in a game that takes its water seriously.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I sell the fish I catch in GTA 6?

No. Fishing is catch and release only. Fish cannot be kept or sold, and there is no fish market in the game. The rewards are the experience itself, the Focus increase from Cal's quest, and the trophy.

Where is the best fishing spot in GTA 6?

Key Lento jetty is the best place to start — your first rod is there, and the Stoplight Parrotfish is an easy first catch. Gambit Bay is the place for Cal's side quest and bigger fish. Mount Kalaga is Rockstar's named freshwater spot. No complete fish list has been published yet, so the full map of spots is still being worked out by players.

The Bottom Line

Fishing in GTA 6 is simple, calm and honest about what it is: catch and release, no economy, no fish list yet. Pick up your first rod at the Key Lento jetty, use Focus to spot your fish, then head to Gambit Bay for Cal's "Catch A Bigger Fish" quest — a bigger parrotfish, a stronger Focus, and a trophy. And ignore any guide that sends you to Los Santos for a Golden Trout: that is GTA 5, not GTA 6.
