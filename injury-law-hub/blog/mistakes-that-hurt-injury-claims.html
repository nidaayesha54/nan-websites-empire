7 Mistakes That Hurt Injury Claims

Strong claims get weakened every day by avoidable missteps. Here are the seven most common — and what to do instead.

1. Posting on social media

A smiling vacation photo can be used to argue you weren't really hurt, even if you were in pain the whole trip. Insurers routinely check claimants' social media. Best practice: stay quiet online about the accident, your injuries, and your activities until the claim resolves.

2. Gaps in medical treatment

Skipping appointments or waiting weeks to see a doctor signals — fairly or not — that the injury wasn't serious. Follow your treatment plan and keep every appointment, or document why you couldn't.

3. Giving a recorded statement to the other insurer

You generally aren't required to give one, and casual remarks ("I'm feeling okay") can be used to minimize your injuries. It's reasonable to decline and seek advice first.

4. Accepting the first settlement offer

Early offers often come before the full extent of injuries — and future medical needs — is known. Once you sign a release, you typically can't ask for more later.

5. Not documenting losses

Undocumented losses are unrecoverable losses. Keep every bill, receipt, pay stub showing missed work, and a daily journal of pain and limitations.

6. Waiting too long to act

Evidence fades: witnesses forget, footage is deleted, and — critically — statutes of limitations expire. Early action protects your options.

7. Exaggerating injuries

Credibility is everything. Inconsistencies between what you claim and what records or surveillance show can destroy an otherwise valid claim. Be honest and consistent.

The takeaway

Protect your health, document everything, stay quiet publicly, and get professional guidance early — most personal injury attorneys offer free consultations. Small discipline in the first weeks can mean a large difference in the final outcome.
