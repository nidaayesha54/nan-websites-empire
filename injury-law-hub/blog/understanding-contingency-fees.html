Understanding Contingency Fees: How "No Win, No Fee" Really Works

Most personal injury attorneys work on contingency: they get paid a percentage of your recovery, and nothing if you lose. That's the famous "no win, no fee" promise — but the details matter.

Typical percentages

Contingency fees commonly range from 25% to 40% of the recovery. Many agreements use a sliding scale — for example, 33% if the case settles before a lawsuit is filed, rising to 40% if it goes to trial, reflecting the extra work involved. Percentages vary by state, firm, and case complexity.

Fees vs. costs

The attorney's fee is separate from case costs: filing fees, expert witness fees, medical record charges, deposition transcripts, and investigation expenses. In most agreements, the firm advances these costs and is reimbursed from the settlement — but the terms differ:

  • Costs deducted before the fee is calculated — you keep more.
  • Costs deducted after the fee — the fee applies to the gross recovery first.

On a $100,000 settlement at 33%, the difference between these two methods can be thousands of dollars. Always ask which method your agreement uses.

What if you lose?

"No win, no fee" usually means no attorney fee if there's no recovery. However, some agreements still hold you responsible for advanced costs even if the case loses. Others forgive costs entirely. Read that clause carefully before signing.

Questions to ask before signing

  • What is the exact percentage at each stage (pre-suit, litigation, trial, appeal)?
  • Are costs deducted before or after the fee is calculated?
  • Am I responsible for costs if we lose?
  • Who pays for experts, and do I approve major expenses?
  • How and when will I receive settlement statements?
