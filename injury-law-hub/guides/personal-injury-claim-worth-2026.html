How Much Is My Personal Injury Claim Worth? (2026 Guide)

There is no fixed price list for personal injury claims. Every case is valued on its own facts — but attorneys, insurers, and courts generally look at the same building blocks. Understanding them helps you see why one claim settles for $15,000 and a similar-looking one for $150,000.

1. Economic damages: the documented losses

These are the bills and receipts you can add up:

  • Medical expenses — past and reasonably expected future treatment, therapy, medication, and equipment.
  • Lost wages and earning capacity — income lost during recovery, plus reduced future earning ability for lasting injuries.
  • Out-of-pocket costs — travel to appointments, home care, property damage.

Economic damages are the foundation. Without documentation, losses are hard to recover — which is why keeping every bill and pay stub matters.

2. Non-economic damages: pain and suffering

These compensate for physical pain, emotional distress, loss of enjoyment of life, and scarring or disfigurement. They are inherently subjective, so the common multiplier method is used as a starting point: multiply economic damages by a factor — often 1.5× for minor injuries up to 4–5× for severe or permanent ones. 3. Fault: who caused the accident?

Most states reduce your recovery by your percentage of fault (comparative negligence). A few states bar recovery entirely if you were even slightly at fault. Disputed fault is one of the biggest reasons similar injuries settle for different amounts.

4. Insurance limits and collectability

A claim's practical value is capped by available insurance coverage and the defendant's ability to pay. A $500,000 injury against a $25,000 minimum policy may recover only the policy limit unless other coverage exists.

5. What reduces a payout

  • Gaps in medical treatment or missed appointments
  • Pre-existing injuries to the same body part
  • Social media posts that contradict claimed limitations
  • Delayed reporting of the accident
  • Weak or missing evidence of fault

The bottom line

Claim value = documented losses × severity, adjusted for fault and limited by available coverage. Online estimates are rough starting points — a licensed attorney who reviews your medical records and evidence can give a far more grounded evaluation, usually in a free consultation.
