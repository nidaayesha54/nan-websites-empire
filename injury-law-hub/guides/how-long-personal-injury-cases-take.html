How Long Do Personal Injury Cases Take?

Most personal injury claims settle in 6 to 18 months. But the range is wide: a straightforward soft-tissue case can resolve in a few months, while a complex case that goes to trial can take 2–4 years. Here's where the time goes.

Stage 1: Treatment and maximum medical improvement (3–12 months)

Claims are usually valued after your condition stabilizes — settling before doctors know the full prognosis risks leaving money on the table. Serious injuries simply take longer to reach this point.

Stage 2: Demand and negotiation (2–6 months)

Your attorney sends a demand package to the insurer with evidence and a settlement figure. Insurers typically respond in 30–90 days, then negotiation begins. Clear liability and complete documentation speed this up.

Stage 3: Filing a lawsuit (if needed)

If negotiation stalls, a lawsuit is filed — but it must be filed before your state's statute of limitations expires. Filing doesn't mean a trial is coming; it often restarts serious settlement talks.

Stage 4: Discovery and mediation (6–18 months)

Both sides exchange evidence, take depositions, and hire experts. Courts often order mediation, where a neutral third party helps negotiate. The large majority of filed cases settle here — only about 3–5% of personal injury cases ever reach trial.

Stage 5: Trial (1–2 weeks in court, but months to schedule)

Trials themselves are short, but getting a court date can take a year or more in busy jurisdictions.

What makes cases settle faster

  • Clear liability (e.g., rear-end collision with a police report)
  • Complete, well-organized medical records
  • Reasonable, well-documented demand
  • Adequate insurance coverage

What slows cases down

  • Disputed fault or multiple parties
  • Severe or evolving injuries needing long treatment
  • Low policy limits with high damages (coverage fights)
  • Court backlogs in your county

The bottom line

Plan for months, not weeks — and be wary of quick lowball offers before treatment is complete. Track where you are with our claim timeline planner, and remember: only a licensed attorney can advise on the timeline for your specific case.
