Do I Need a Personal Injury Lawyer? 7 Signs (2026)

Not every scrape needs a lawyer — but some situations almost always do. Here are the signs that legal help is worth getting, the cases where you may be fine on your own, and the biggest cost myths.

7 signs you should talk to a lawyer

  1. Serious or long-term injuries — hospitalization, surgery, or injuries that may never fully heal. High stakes mean high-value negotiations.
  2. Fault is disputed — the other side (or their insurer) blames you, or multiple parties are involved.
  3. The insurer denies, delays, or lowballs — a common pattern: deny liability, stall, then offer a fraction of the claim's value.
  4. Government involvement — injuries on public property or by a public employee trigger short notice deadlines that are easy to miss.
  5. Medical malpractice or product liability — these cases require expert witnesses and complex proof; they are almost never DIY.
  6. You missed work or lost income — calculating lost wages and reduced earning capacity correctly takes documentation strategy.
  7. The deadline is approaching — if the statute of limitations is near, you need a lawyer immediately to file in time.

When you may not need a lawyer

  • Minor injuries with full recovery and small medical bills
  • Clear liability and a cooperative insurer
  • Total damages within small-claims court limits

Even then, a free consultation costs nothing and can confirm you're not leaving money on the table.

The cost myth: "I can't afford a lawyer"

Most personal injury lawyers work on contingency: they take a percentage of your settlement (typically 33–40%) and charge nothing upfront — and nothing at all if you lose. Read our explainer on how contingency fees really work.

What to ask in a free consultation

  • Have you handled cases like mine? What were the outcomes?
  • What is your fee percentage, and what costs am I responsible for if we lose?
  • Who will actually work on my case — you or an associate?
  • What is the realistic timeline and value range?

The bottom line

Serious injuries, disputed fault, bad-faith insurers, or approaching deadlines are strong reasons to hire a lawyer. Since consultations are free and fees are contingency-based, the real question is usually not "can I afford one?" but "can I afford not to ask?"
