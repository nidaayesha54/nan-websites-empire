What to Do After a Car Accident: A Step-by-Step Checklist

The minutes and days after a crash matter — for your health and for any future claim. Follow these steps in order.

At the scene

  1. Check for injuries and call 911. Move to safety if you can. Never leave the scene.
  2. Call the police. A police report creates an official record. Get the report number.
  3. Exchange information. Names, phone numbers, insurance details, license plates, and vehicle descriptions.
  4. Document everything. Photograph all vehicles, damage, skid marks, road conditions, traffic signs, and visible injuries.
  5. Find witnesses. Ask for names and phone numbers — independent witnesses are powerful evidence.
  6. Don't admit fault. Stick to facts when speaking to police and others. Fault is for investigators and courts to determine.

In the first 24–72 hours

  1. See a doctor — even if you feel fine. Adrenaline masks injuries; whiplash and concussions often appear later. Medical records link injuries to the crash.
  2. Notify your insurer. Report promptly; most policies require timely notice.
  3. Start a file. Keep the police report number, medical records, receipts, tow bills, and a daily pain/symptom journal.
  4. Be careful with the other insurer. You are not obligated to give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurance company. Consider speaking with an attorney first.

In the following weeks

  1. Follow all medical advice. Attend every appointment. Gaps in treatment are used to argue you weren't really hurt.
  2. Track all losses. Medical bills, prescriptions, mileage to appointments, missed work days, and help you needed at home.
  3. Stay off social media about the crash. Photos and posts can be taken out of context and used against your claim.
  4. Know your deadline. Use our statute of limitations checker — but confirm the real deadline with a licensed attorney early.
  5. Consider a free consultation. Most personal injury attorneys offer free case reviews and work on contingency, so there is no upfront cost to get professional advice.

What NOT to do

  • Don't sign a release or accept a quick settlement before you know the full extent of your injuries.
  • Don't give recorded statements to the opposing insurer without advice.
  • Don't post about the accident, your injuries, or activities online.
  • Don't skip medical appointments.
