GTA 6 Driving: Series-Best Handling, Explained

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Driving is the activity you will spend the most time doing in GTA 6 — more than shooting, more than missions — and it is the thing the reviews praise most consistently. IGN calls the car handling the strongest it has ever been in the series. Digital Foundry says the racing and car simulation are in many ways on par with dedicated games like Gran Turismo or Forza. And Kotaku notes that narrower streets and harsher crashes make driving more exciting than ever. The game launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and this guide explains what actually changed about the driving, how the new car-stealing rules work, and how to drive well in a city that punishes bad drivers.

The Verdict: Series-Best Handling

The praise is unusually specific. IGN's Luke Reilly wrote that he was “completely enamoured with its series-best car handling and weaving through the realistically dense traffic,” and described the handling as “the strongest it's ever been in the series” and “a refined version of GTA 4 than GTA 5.” That comparison is the key: GTA 4's cars were heavy and demanded skill; GTA 5's were light and arcade-like. GTA 6 takes the weight of GTA 4 and the accessibility of GTA 5, and lands in between — cars with a sense of weight and suspension that GTA 5 lacked, without the wobble that made GTA 4 feel like driving a boat.

Digital Foundry goes further, with John Linneman saying the racing and car simulation are “in many ways on par with dedicated games like Gran Turismo or Forza” — not a claim anyone made about GTA 5. The Thrillbilly Mud Club backs it up: dirt bike and off-road circuit racing with rich surface deformation and tactile physics. Driving in GTA 6 is not a way to get to the missions. It is a system in its own right.

What Changed: Weight, Sliding, and Narrow Streets

The first thing you feel is the weight. Each car's handling differs by drivetrain, tyres, suspension and power — and the game shows it, with animated drivetrains visible when a car flips over. The cars feel like they have mass now, and that mass is what makes the sliding work. IGN's line is the one to remember: “You can really slide cars properly here, and throwing them into 90-degree corners and perching them on the throttle is now total Hollywood highlight reel stuff.” The slide is controlled, not scripted — you steer into it, hold it, and come out the other side.

The streets changed too: narrower and more realistic than in GTA 5, with denser traffic and real roadworks. That combination forces quick decisions — a truck blocking your lane, a patrol car in the next one, a turn you have to take now or not at all. Rob Nelson told IGN before launch that the narrower streets would take a bit of skill, and he was right. Driving in Vice City is a negotiation with the traffic, and the drivers who read the road win it.

Stealing Cars Is Harder Now

The biggest change is not the handling — it is the stealing. For the first time in more than 25 years, GTA makes stealing cars hard: some cars are easy to pop the lock on and hotwire, but the best ones cannot be hotwired for a large chunk of the game. Rob Nelson gave the reason before launch — those cars need a key cloner. You can smash the window and get in, but you cannot start the car without it. Some cars carry trackers too, which help the police find you later.

The rule, as Nelson explained it, is stratification: you can carjack any driver you see, but parked cars are different. Some are off limits until the story hands you the tools, and the tools arrive as you progress. The cars you can steal early are the ordinary ones; the cars worth stealing come later, with the tools to take them — and the knowledge of which ones carry trackers.

The Details That Make It Feel Real

The small things are what sell the driving: turn signals on the D-pad, windows that wind down, a special menu for more in-car actions than ever before, animated drivetrains, harsher crashes and spectacular destruction. IGN highlights the improved gunplay and the destruction, and the world reacts to the driving — traffic, roadworks, pedestrians and the wanted system all respond to what you do behind the wheel.

One thing the reviewers are less enthusiastic about is the motorcycles: IGN found them upright and stiff, the one clear weakness in the driving system. If you have a choice between a car and a bike, take the car — especially at night, especially in a chase. The bikes are usable, but they are not where the handling shines.

Racing: The Thrillbilly Mud Club and Beyond

Racing is a full system, not a side activity. The Thrillbilly Mud Club is the headline venue: dirt bike and off-road circuit racing with surface deformation, tactile physics and rival combat. The mud matters — tyres sink, spin and fling thick mud, and tracks persist before washing away in heavy rain. Street racing exists too, and the dense traffic and narrow streets make it a different challenge from the open highways of GTA 5. Racing rewards the same skills as driving: reading the road, managing the slide, knowing when to brake.

The advice for racing is the same as for driving: learn one car before you chase the next. Each car handles differently — find one you like, learn how it slides and brakes, then take it racing. The game rewards the driver who knows their car, not the one with the fastest car on the street.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is GTA 6 driving better than GTA 5?

Yes, according to every major review. IGN calls it the strongest handling in the series — a refined GTA 4, with real weight and suspension — and Digital Foundry compares the racing simulation to Gran Turismo and Forza. GTA 5's cars were often criticised as too light; GTA 6's are not.

Why can't I steal some cars?

The best cars need a key cloner, which you get as the story progresses. You can smash the window and get in, but you cannot start the car without it. Some cars also carry trackers that help the police find you. Carjacking a driver still works — it is parked cars that are stratified.

Are motorcycles good in GTA 6?

No — they are the weak point. IGN found motorcycle handling upright and stiff compared with the cars. Take the car when you have the choice, especially in a chase or at night.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6's driving is the best the series has ever had: GTA 4's weight, GTA 5's access, and a slide that feels like a Hollywood highlight reel. Learn the weight, respect the narrow streets, steal the cars you can and wait for the tools for the ones you cannot — and take the car, not the bike. The city rewards the driver who reads the road.
