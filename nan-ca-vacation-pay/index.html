Example: a worker in Ontario earning $60,000 with 6 years of service accrues 6% = $3,600 in vacation pay (3 weeks off). The same worker in their 3rd year accrues 4% = $2,400. In Saskatchewan, a $60,000 earner with 3 years of service accrues 5.77% = $3,462.

How Vacation Pay Is Calculated

  • Vacation time vs vacation pay are two separate rights: the weeks off, and the percentage of wages that funds them.
  • Vacation pay is usually calculated on your gross wages (salary plus most overtime, commissions, and bonuses). The vacation pay itself is generally not re-counted.
  • It must be paid before your vacation starts in most provinces — though several allow it on each paycheque if you agree in writing.
  • Unused vacation pay must be paid out when your employment ends, in every jurisdiction.
  • Quebec uses a May 1 – April 30 reference year for calculating vacation entitlements.
  • Your employer can schedule vacations with reasonable notice, and cannot pay you less than the statutory rate — but your contract may pay more.

FAQs

Is vacation pay paid on overtime and bonuses?

In most provinces, vacation pay is calculated on your total wages, which includes overtime, commissions, and non-discretionary bonuses. Exact inclusions vary by jurisdiction — check your province's employment standards guide.

When should I receive my vacation pay?

Usually at least one day before your vacation begins. Some provinces allow vacation pay to be added to each regular paycheque if you and your employer agree in writing.

What happens to unused vacation pay if I quit or am fired?

Every Canadian jurisdiction requires accrued but unused vacation pay to be paid out on termination, usually with your final pay.

Why is Saskatchewan's rate 5.77% instead of 4%?

Saskatchewan doesn't use the 4%/6% model. It grants 3 weeks of vacation (3/52 of annual wages ≈ 5.77%) after 1 year, and 4 weeks (4/52 ≈ 7.69%) after 10 years.

Can my employer refuse my vacation request?

Employers can generally schedule vacations to suit business needs with reasonable notice, but they cannot deny your statutory minimum vacation time or pay.

I'm federally regulated — which rules apply?

If you work for a bank, airline, telecom, or interprovincial transport company, the Canada Labour Code applies (4%/6%/8%), not your province's rules.
