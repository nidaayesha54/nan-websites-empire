7 Costly Travel Insurance Mistakes to Avoid

October 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Travel insurance only feels like a waste of money until the day it saves you $40,000. But plenty of travelers pay for policies that would never have paid them — because of avoidable mistakes at purchase time. Here are the seven costliest ones, and exactly how to dodge each.

Mistake 1: Buying too late to get the pre-existing condition waiver

The waiver that covers flare-ups of known conditions is usually free — but only if you buy within 14–21 days of your first trip payment. Buy on day 30 and that heart condition, asthma, or diabetes complication may be excluded entirely. Fix: buy your policy the same week you book the first non-refundable piece of the trip.

Mistake 2: Insuring refundable costs

If your $2,000 hotel booking is fully refundable, insuring it buys you nothing — the hotel already refunds you. Yet travelers routinely insure the full sticker price of the trip. Fix: add up only non-refundable costs and insure that number. Use our quote estimator to see the premium difference.

Mistake 3: Choosing medical limits that are too low

A $50,000 emergency medical limit sounds like a lot — until a week in a foreign hospital plus an air ambulance totals $120,000. This is the single most dangerous place to economize. Fix: $100,000 minimum for international travel, $250,000+ for remote destinations, and $250,000+ evacuation coverage. Read the full breakdown in our coverage guide.

Mistake 4: Assuming "cancellation" means "any reason"

Standard trip cancellation covers a specific list of reasons — illness, injury, weather, family emergencies. "I changed my mind," "work got busy," and "I found a cheaper flight" are not covered reasons. Only CFAR (Cancel For Any Reason) covers those, and it costs ~40–50% extra for 50–75% reimbursement. Fix: read the covered-reasons list before you buy; add CFAR only if your cancel risk is genuinely "cold feet."

Mistake 5: Forgetting the adventure activities exclusion

Standard policies exclude skiing, scuba diving, paragliding, high-altitude hiking, and similar activities. Travelers discover this after the injury, not before. Fix: if your itinerary includes any adventure sport, buy the adventure rider or a plan that explicitly covers your activities.

Mistake 6: No documentation when things go wrong

More claims die from missing paperwork than from uncovered events. No receipt for the stolen camera, no police report, no proof the flight was delayed — no payment. Fix: before you leave, create a "trip docs" folder on your phone; photograph luggage contents, save every booking confirmation, and file police reports immediately for any theft.

Mistake 7: Never reading the policy during the free-look period

Nearly every plan gives you 10–15 days after purchase to cancel for a full refund. Almost nobody uses this window to actually read the policy — the one document that determines whether your claim gets paid. Fix: buy early, spend 20 minutes with the certificate of insurance during the free look, and cancel if the exclusions surprise you.
