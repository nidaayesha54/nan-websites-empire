11 Travel Insurance Buying Tips for 2026

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Most travelers buy travel insurance the way they buy extended warranties: quickly, anxiously, and without reading anything. Insurers count on it. These 11 tips — drawn from claims data, policy fine print, and traveler mistakes — will help you buy the right coverage at the right price.

1. Buy within 14–21 days of your first trip payment

This is the single highest-value tip on this list. Buying early (usually within 14–21 days of your first deposit) unlocks the pre-existing condition waiver for free and makes you eligible for CFAR upgrades. It costs nothing extra to buy early — it only unlocks more.

2. Insure non-refundable costs only

Your premium is based on your insured trip cost. If your $6,000 trip includes $2,500 in refundable hotels, insure $3,500 — not $6,000. Travelers who insure the sticker price instead of the at-risk price routinely overpay by 30–50%. Our quote estimator shows how trip cost drives your premium.

3. Match medical limits to your destination

$50,000 in emergency medical coverage is fine for a domestic trip. For international travel, get $100,000 minimum, $250,000+ for remote destinations. And evacuation coverage should be $250,000+ — one air ambulance can cost $100,000. Medical limits are the worst place to economize.

4. Check what you already have first

Before buying, audit your existing coverage: premium travel credit cards often include trip delay, baggage delay, and even cancellation benefits. Your health insurance may cover domestic travel fully. Your homeowners/renters policy may cover stolen luggage. Buy only the gaps.

5. Read the "covered reasons" list, not the marketing page

Two plans can both advertise "trip cancellation" with totally different covered-reason lists. The marketing page says "peace of mind"; the policy document lists exactly which 15–25 reasons qualify. Open the certificate of insurance and search "covered reasons" before you pay.

6. Skip CFAR unless you genuinely need flexibility

Cancel For Any Reason sounds amazing — until you see the price (~40–50% more) and the fine print (only 50–75% reimbursement, must buy within 14–21 days). Standard cancellation already covers illness, injury, weather, and family emergencies. CFAR is for people who might cancel because of work drama or cold feet — be honest about whether that's you.

7. Compare at least three quotes

Identical coverage can vary 2× in price between insurers. Use comparison marketplaces, then sanity-check the winner's claim reviews. A cheap plan from an insurer famous for denying claims is the most expensive plan of all.

8. Mind the age brackets

Premiums jump at age milestones — 50, 65, 70, 75. If a traveler in your group crosses a bracket before the trip, the premium changes. And if you're 65+, verify there's no upper age limit on medical coverage — some budget plans quietly cap it.

9. For frequent travelers, price an annual plan

Three or more trips a year? An annual multi-trip plan is often 30–50% cheaper than separate single-trip policies, and it covers spontaneous weekend trips you wouldn't bother insuring individually. Just check the per-trip day limit (usually 30–45 days).

10. Document everything from day one

Claims are denied more often for missing paperwork than for uncovered events. From the moment you book: save every receipt, screenshot booking confirmations, photograph your packed luggage, and get a police report for any theft. Create a "trip docs" folder on your phone before you leave.

11. Know the free-look period

Almost every plan includes a 10–15 day free-look period after purchase: you can cancel for a full refund if you haven't departed or filed a claim. Use it. Buy early, read the real policy during the free look, and cancel if the fine print disappoints. This is your risk-free review window — treat it like one.

12. Watch for these red flags before you pay

Not all plans deserve your money. Walk away if you see any of these: the insurer hides the actual policy document behind a purchase wall; the "covered reasons" list is vague or missing; claim reviews repeatedly mention denied payouts for documented events; the medical evacuation limit is under $100,000 for international trips; or the plan excludes your specific destination in the fine print. A legitimate insurer shows you the certificate of insurance before checkout and answers coverage questions in writing. If a deal looks dramatically cheaper than three comparable quotes, the savings are coming from somewhere — usually exclusions you haven't found yet.

Quick-start checklist: buy within 14 days of first payment → insure only non-refundable costs → get $100k+ medical / $250k+ evacuation for international trips → compare 3 quotes → use the free-look period to read the real policy. Do those five things and you're ahead of 90% of travelers.
