How Much Does Travel Insurance Cost in 2026?

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer: most US travelers pay 4–10% of their total trip cost for a comprehensive travel insurance plan in 2026. A $5,000 international trip typically costs $200–$500 to insure; a $10,000 luxury cruise can run $500–$1,000. But that range hides a lot — your age, destination, and trip length can move your premium dramatically.

This guide breaks down real 2026 pricing, the six factors that set your premium, and seven proven ways to save 20–40% without losing meaningful coverage.

The 6 Factors That Set Your Premium

1. Trip cost (the biggest factor). Insurers base pricing primarily on your non-refundable trip cost. Double the trip cost, roughly double the premium. Booking refundable hotels and flights you can cancel free can genuinely lower your insurable trip cost — and your premium.

2. Age. This is the second-biggest lever. Travelers under 30 often pay below the average rate; premiums climb steadily from age 50, and travelers 65–74 commonly pay 25–70% more than a 40-year-old for the same trip. At 75+, expect double or more. Medical risk is the driver — insurers know emergency claims rise sharply with age.

3. Destination. Domestic US trips are cheapest to insure. Europe and the Caribbean add a modest bump; Asia, Africa, and the Middle East cost more because medical care and evacuation are expensive and logistically complex. Cruises carry their own surcharge — if you miss the ship, "catching up" at the next port is costly.

4. Trip length. A 7-day trip and a 21-day trip to the same place are priced differently. Most plans scale with days of coverage, and trips over 30 days often face surcharges. Annual multi-trip plans can be cheaper if you travel 3+ times a year.

5. Coverage level and limits. Higher medical limits ($250k vs $50k), higher evacuation limits, and add-ons like CFAR or rental-car coverage raise the price. CFAR alone typically adds 40–50% to a base plan — and only reimburses 50–75% of trip cost.

6. Pre-existing conditions and add-ons. A pre-existing condition waiver (free if you buy within 14–21 days of your first trip payment) doesn't raise the premium — but buying late and needing medical underwriting can. Adventure sports riders, business equipment coverage, and pet coverage each add a little.

Real Cost Examples for Popular 2026 Trips

  • Family of four, $8,000 Orlando trip: a comprehensive plan runs roughly $400–$650. Kids are often included free or cheap on family plans — always check.
  • Couple, $6,000 Europe trip (ages 35 and 38): expect $300–$550 comprehensive. International medical coverage is the main reason to buy here — most US health plans don't cover Europe.
  • Solo senior (68), $4,000 Caribbean cruise: expect $350–$600. Age plus cruise surcharge pushes this above the 5% average.
  • Backpacker (24), $2,500 Southeast Asia, 6 weeks: $150–$250. Youth keeps the rate low, but the long duration and remote destinations push it up.
Rule of thumb: if your quote is under 4% of trip cost, check the medical limits — they're probably too low. Over 12%? You're likely over-insured or buying expensive add-ons you don't need.

7 Ways to Save 20–40% on Travel Insurance

1. Buy within 14–21 days of your first trip payment. This unlocks the pre-existing condition waiver for free and the earliest eligibility for CFAR. Buying early doesn't cost more — it just unlocks more.

2. Only insure non-refundable costs. Don't insure the full $5,000 if $2,000 of it is refundable. Lower insurable trip cost = lower premium.

3. Consider an annual plan. If you take 3+ trips a year, an annual multi-trip plan often costs less than three single-trip policies — sometimes 30–50% less.

4. Raise your tolerance for small losses. Skip baggage-delay riders and low-limit add-ons if your credit card already covers them. Many premium travel credit cards include baggage and delay benefits — check before you duplicate coverage.

5. Compare at least 3 quotes. Pricing for identical coverage varies wildly between insurers. Comparison marketplaces exist for exactly this reason.

6. Skip CFAR unless you need it. CFAR adds ~40–50% to your premium and only refunds 50–75%. Standard cancellation covers illness, injury, weather, and many other reasons — read the covered-reasons list first.

7. Travel with a group or family plan. Family plans often cover kids at little or no extra cost, and group rates can beat individual policies.

Is Travel Insurance Worth the Cost?

Do the math on your worst case, not your premium. An emergency medical evacuation from abroad can cost $50,000–$100,000+. A week in a foreign hospital can exceed $20,000.
