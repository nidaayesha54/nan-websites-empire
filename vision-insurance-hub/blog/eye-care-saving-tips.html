10 Eye-Care Saving Tips That Actually Work

October 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Vision care is one of the easiest household expenses to cut — most people overpay simply because they never comparison-shop. These ten tips work whether you have vision insurance or not, and together they can save a typical glasses-wearing household $200–$500 per year.

1. Buy glasses online

Complete prescription glasses cost $30–$100 online (Zenni, EyeBuyDirect) versus $200–$400 at a mall optical shop. Quality is fine for standard single-vision prescriptions — just have your pupillary distance measured. This single tip saves most people $150+ per pair.

2. Use FSA/HSA dollars

Eye exams, glasses, contacts, contact solution, and even LASIK are all FSA/HSA-eligible. Paying with pre-tax dollars effectively cuts 20–30% off everything, depending on your tax bracket. If your employer offers an FSA, contribute what you'd spend on vision anyway.

3. Try warehouse optical departments

Costco and Sam's Club optical centers charge roughly $70–$100 for exams and sell glasses far below mall prices. You don't need vision insurance, and non-members can often still use the optical department (call ahead to confirm).

4. Ask for your prescription — it's the law

US federal law requires your eye doctor to give you your prescription after the exam, free. With it in hand, you're free to shop anywhere instead of buying from the office that examined you.

5. Time purchases to your plan's allowance cycle

Most vision plans reset allowances every 12 or 24 months. Buy new frames in the first month of a new cycle, not the last — missing the window by a week can cost you a full year's allowance.

6. Skip lens upgrades you don't need

Anti-glare coating, blue-light filters, and ultra-thin lenses add $35–$150 each. Anti-glare is genuinely useful; blue-light filtering has thin evidence. For mild prescriptions, standard lenses are perfectly fine.

7. Use your plan's LASIK discount before surgery

Most vision plans offer 15–25% off LASIK at participating surgeons. On a $4,500 procedure, that's up to $1,100 — more than five years of premiums. Ask your plan before booking consultations.

8. Compare contacts prices per box, not per lens

Contact lens pricing is deliberately confusing. Always compare the annual supply cost across retailers (including rebate offers — manufacturers run $50–$100 rebates regularly). Subscription services are convenient but rarely cheapest.

9. Get a second quote for big purchases

Progressive lenses and specialty contacts vary enormously in price between providers. For anything over $200, get two quotes — a 10-minute call commonly saves $50–$150.

10. Run the numbers before renewing your plan

Vision plans change allowances and premiums every year, and your usage changes too. Spend 60 seconds with our Eye Care Cost Estimator before open enrollment — switching plans (or dropping to self-pay) is the highest-leverage saving move most people never make.
