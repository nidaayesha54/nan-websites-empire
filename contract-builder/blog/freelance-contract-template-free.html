What a Good Template Includes

A template worth using covers these essentials — if any are missing, find a better one:

  1. Party details — full legal names and contact info for you and the client.
  2. Scope of work — specific deliverables, not vague descriptions.
  3. Timeline — start date, milestones, and final deadline.
  4. Payment — total fee, deposit, invoice schedule, due dates.
  5. Late fees — typically 1.5% per month on overdue invoices.
  6. Revisions — how many rounds are included before extra billing kicks in.
  7. IP ownership — ideally transfers to the client upon final payment.
  8. Termination — notice period and payment for work completed.
  9. Confidentiality — mutual protection of sensitive information.
  10. Signatures — dated signatures from both parties.

How to Customize It for Your Niche

Designers & developers

Add clauses about source files (do you hand over working files?), browser/device testing scope, and post-launch support periods. Specify that portfolio display rights are retained.

Writers & marketers

Define word counts or deliverable counts precisely, kill fees for cancelled assignments (typically 25–50%), and usage rights — is the client buying full ownership or a license?

Video & photo

Cover raw footage ownership, number of shooting days, travel expenses, and delivery formats. Model releases are a separate document — don't skip them.

5 Mistakes That Make Templates Useless

  1. Leaving blanks. A template with "[INSERT HERE]" everywhere isn't a contract. Fill every field.
  2. Skipping the deposit. No deposit = no commitment. 25–50% upfront is standard.
  3. Vague scope. "Website design" means nothing. "5-page responsive site with copy deck provided by client" means everything.
  4. No late fee. Without one, "net-30" becomes "whenever I feel like it."
  5. Never sending it. The best template is the one you actually use. Send it with every proposal — it takes two minutes with our generator.

Is a Free Template Enough?

For standard projects under ~$10,000 with straightforward scope, a good template signed by both parties covers you well. Consider an attorney when: the deal exceeds $25,000, involves equity or royalties, crosses international borders with complex tax issues, or the client sends you a 20-page contract to sign (get it reviewed before you do).
