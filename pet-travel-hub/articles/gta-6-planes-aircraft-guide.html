GTA 6 Planes: Every Aircraft and Where to Fly

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

The skies over Leonida are busy. GTA 6's trailers and screenshots show everything from a tiny seaplane on the Keys to a jumbo jet over the Vice City sign, and the game's own reviews confirm that Jason and Lucia can fly them. This guide lists every aircraft spotted so far, explains where the airport is, and separates what Rockstar has shown from what nobody has confirmed yet — because flying in GTA 6 is one of the most asked-about features, and also one of the least documented.

The first thing to know is the airport. Vice City International Airport — VCIA — is the game's major civilian aviation hub, and it replaces Escobar International from the original Vice City. Its radar and control towers are visible behind the Vice City sign in Trailer 1, and a black Buckingham Luxor is shown landing there in the Extended Look, with Nimbus jets parked nearby. If you want to fly, that is where the runways are.

The planes spotted so far

The light aircraft come first, because they are the ones beginners will fly. The Dodo seaplane returns — it rests on large floats, so it takes off from and lands on water, and it is the island hopper of the class, perfect for the Leonida Keys. The Mammatus is a small single-engine plane for short private flights, and the Duster is a small propeller biplane that flies over the towns of Leonida — a pink one in AudaCity livery appears in the launch trailer. These planes spawn at small airfields and offer the easiest flight handling for beginners.

The private jets are the fast way across the map. The Shamal is a slim twin-engine business jet that needs a runway to take off and land — one flies low over the giant Vice City sign at the airport in a released image. The Luxor is another business jet, and the Nimbus combines Gulfstream and Cessna Citation styling with a top speed of 600 km/h. The Jet is the huge four-engine airliner of the class, a Boeing 747 with a raised hump over the front of its body: it rules the skies over Leonida International, and it is the kind of aircraft you are far more likely to watch land than ever get behind the controls of.

Helicopters, blimps and the military

Helicopters cross the city and the wild country. The Maverick is a light civilian helicopter and a quick way to travel across Leonida by air. The Police Maverick watches chases from above and follows suspects where patrol cars struggle to reach — it hovers over Jason and Lucia as they jump out of a window in Trailer 2. The Buzzard is a light attack helicopter, shown chasing an airboat across the Grassrivers wetlands with its rotor downdraft creating waves and kicking up particles. The Sea Sparrow is a small, simple helicopter for short hops. And a blimp — a Goodyear-style airship — drifts over the city at 80 km/h, the slowest aircraft in the game and the best seat for sightseeing.

The military aircraft live at Mile Zero Air Station on Goose Key, in the far southwest of the map. The P-996 Lazer fighter jet and the Botticher 015A plane sit on its flight line, and the base is heavily guarded: ramming the gate gives you four stars, and climbing into the jet raises it to five. A fan-mapping analysis also places a radar blimp over the Keys, mirroring the real surveillance blimp that watches for smugglers over the Florida Keys — treat that as a fan reading, not a confirmed feature.

Can you actually fly them?

Here is the honest answer: Rockstar has not said how you get a plane or a helicopter. No steal method, no buy method and no mission method has been announced, and no flight school has been announced either. What exists is smaller, but it is real. Kotaku's review of the released game says Jason and Lucia can drive boats, planes, helicopters, dirt bikes, jet skis and more. IGN's review says you can still land a jumbo jet on the beach — though you now have to make your own way to the cockpit rather than appearing in it. And IGN reports that Jason pilots a helicopter over the Grassrivers swamps at the end of Trailer 2.

Trailer 2 adds one more scene: Jason helping other men load suitcases onto a small plane at what appears to be a private airfield. The plane's model has not been named, but the moment is a clear nod to smuggling storylines and small-airfield gameplay — the kind of flying that happens away from VCIA's runways, on grass strips and private fields across Leonida.

Where to fly, and where not to

The best flying in GTA 6 is the sightseeing: the Keys from the air, the Grassrivers from above, the Vice City skyline at sunset with the neon coming on. The Dodo is the plane for that — water take-offs from the Keys, island hopping down the chain, and landings on any stretch of calm water. The Maverick is the helicopter for it: quick, light, and able to set down almost anywhere.

The place not to fly is Mile Zero. In GTA 5, flying into Fort Zancudo's restricted airspace triggered wanted levels and surface-to-air missiles, and the same logic should apply to Leonida's military base — give it a wide berth unless you are ready for the consequences. The base's jets and missiles are not scenery. They are the answer to an unauthorised flyover.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you fly planes in GTA 6?

Yes, according to reviews of the released game — Kotaku lists planes and helicopters among the vehicles Jason and Lucia can drive, and IGN reports Jason piloting a helicopter in Trailer 2. But Rockstar has not announced how you get a plane, whether you can steal or buy one, or where they are found.

Where is the airport in GTA 6?

Vice City International Airport (VCIA) is the major civilian hub, visible in Trailer 1 behind the Vice City sign. It replaces Escobar International from the original Vice City, matching the real Miami International Airport naming convention.

What planes are in GTA 6?

Spotted so far: the Dodo seaplane, Mammatus, Shamal, Luxor, Nimbus, the jumbo Jet, the Duster biplane, the Maverick and Police Maverick helicopters, the Buzzard attack chopper, the Sea Sparrow and a blimp. Rockstar has not published a full aircraft roster.

The bottom line: Leonida's skies are full, and the airport is waiting. The Dodo will take you down the Keys, the jets will take you across the map, and the helicopters will take you anywhere at all — just do not fly over Mile Zero unless you are ready for what answers back.
