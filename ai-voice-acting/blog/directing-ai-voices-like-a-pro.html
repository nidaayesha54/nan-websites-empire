Directing AI Voices Like a Pro

October 12, 2026 · 5 min read

AI voice tools don't come with a director — you are the director. These seven techniques separate robotic output from performances players actually feel.

1. Tag every line with an emotion

"I can't believe you did that." means nothing. "I can't believe you did that. [betrayed, voice breaking]" gets a performance. Emotion tags are the single highest-leverage habit — our Dialogue Writer bakes them into your script format.

2. Use punctuation as stage direction

Commas force breaths. Ellipses create dread. Em dashes cut thoughts off mid-stream. A line written "No. No, no, no — don't you dare." will always outperform "No no no don't you dare."

3. Regenerate with intent, not hope

Set a rule: maximum three regenerations per line, and change something each time — a word, a tag, the punctuation. Mindless re-rolling burns budget and time.

4. Lock voice seeds per character

The same character across 300 lines needs one voice ID, one seed, one style setting. Log these in your cast plan before you generate a single line.

5. Direct the silences

Pauses between lines are acting. In-engine, a 400ms delay before a villain's reply reads as menace; the same delay before a sidekick's reply reads as a bug. Time your silences deliberately.

6. Generate efforts separately

Grunt, gasp, laugh, scream — these "effort" sounds sell combat and cutscenes. Generate them as their own batch with exaggerated emotion tags; they'll be the most-used files in your game.

7. Always do a listening pass

Listen to every line once, in scene order, before locking. You'll catch mispronounced names, wrong emotions, and the one line where the hero sounds like the villain. Fix them now — patching voice after launch is ten times the work.
