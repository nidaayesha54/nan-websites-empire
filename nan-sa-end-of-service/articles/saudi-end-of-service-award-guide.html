Saudi End of Service Award: Complete Guide to Calculation & Payment

How Saudi Labor Law calculates your end-of-service gratuity: the half-month/full-month formula, resignation vs termination, payout rules.

In-depth 2026 guide~6 min readFree

The end-of-service award (EOSB) is the largest single payment most employees in Saudi Arabia will ever receive from an employer — often worth several months' salary. It is also one of the most miscalculated: employers quietly use the wrong wage base, ignore the rate doubling after five years, or misapply the resignation reductions. The rules sit in Articles 84–88 of the Saudi Labor Law, and they are precise.

This English-language guide walks through the complete EOSB rules: the Article 84 formula, the Article 85 resignation fractions, which wage figure counts, partial-year pro-rating, and the payout deadline. Estimate your own award with our Saudi End of Service Calculator.

The legal framework: Articles 84–88

The end-of-service award is governed by Chapter Four of the Saudi Labor Law (issued under Royal Decree No. M/51), administered by the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (MHRSD). It applies to Saudi nationals and expatriate workers alike in the private sector. (Domestic workers fall under separate regulations.)

The award is a statutory gratuity — it is owed when the work relationship ends, regardless of the reason for separation. It is entirely separate from Article 77 compensation for termination without valid cause; in some separations, a worker may be entitled to both.

The Article 84 formula

On expiration of the work relationship, the employer must pay an end-of-service award calculated as:

  • Half a month's wage for each of the first five years of service; plus
  • One full month's wage for each year after the fifth year.

Partial years are pro-rated — 7 years and 6 months earns the 7-year figure plus half of the 8th year's rate. The calculation uses your last wage, so a final-year raise lifts the entire award.

Worked example — last monthly wage SAR 10,000, 8 years of service:

  • First 5 years: 5 × SAR 5,000 = SAR 25,000
  • Years 6–8: 3 × SAR 10,000 = SAR 30,000
  • Total: SAR 55,000 (contract expiry or employer-initiated end)

Which wage counts? More than basic pay

This is where most underpayments happen. Article 84 says the award is calculated on the last wage — and Article 2 of the Labor Law defines "wage" (where unqualified) as the actual wage: the basic wage plus all regular components, including housing and transport allowances and other regular cash allowances.

In practice, that means basic pay plus regular allowances — a wider base than many employers voluntarily use. Commissions and percentage-based pay count too, unless your contract expressly excludes them (Article 86 permits this exclusion by agreement). One-off bonuses and genuinely irregular payments do not count.

If your pay slip shows basic SAR 6,000 plus housing SAR 1,500 and transport SAR 1,000, your EOSB wage base is SAR 8,500 — not SAR 6,000. That 40%+ difference compounds across every year of service. Qiwa's official calculator asks for this same "total salary" figure.

Resignation: the Article 85 fractions

If you resign, the award is reduced according to length of service:

Service when you resignShare of the award
Less than 2 yearsNothing
2 to 5 yearsOne third
More than 5, less than 10 yearsTwo thirds
10 years or moreThe full award

Two important exceptions under Article 87 override these fractions and grant the full award regardless of service length: where the worker leaves due to force majeure beyond their control, and where a female worker ends the contract within six months of her marriage or three months of giving birth.

No reduction applies where the employer ends the contract or where a fixed-term contract simply expires — the full Article 84 award is due. Note that under current rules, expatriate contracts are fixed-term by law, and the 2025–2026 amendments refined resignation procedures without changing Articles 84–88 themselves.

When must it be paid?

Article 88 sets a hard deadline: the employer must settle all wages and entitlements — including the end-of-service award — within one week of the end of service, or within two weeks where the worker ended the contract. Keep dated written requests and acknowledgments of receipt; they are your evidence if payment slips.

If payment doesn't arrive, the escalation path runs through MHRSD's dispute channels and ultimately the labor courts. Cross-check your figure first with both the MHRSD EOS calculator and the Qiwa EOS Reward Calculator — the two official tools — and save PDFs of both outputs to attach to your claim.

Five mistakes that shrink the award

1. Basing it on basic pay only. Regular allowances are part of the wage base. Recalculate with the full figure.

2. Forgetting the rate doubles after year 5. Years 6+ earn a full month each, not half. Long-service awards are dramatically larger than the "half month per year" shorthand suggests.

3. Rounding down partial years. Seven years and four months is not seven years — it's 7⅓. Pro-rating is a statutory right.

4. Using an old salary. The award is based on the last wage. If you were promoted in your final year, the whole award moves up.

5. Accepting Article 85 reductions when they don't apply. If the employer ended the contract or your fixed term expired, no resignation fraction applies — demand the full award.

How to verify your award before you sign anything

Never accept a final-settlement figure without checking the components yourself. Ask your employer (or former employer) for a written breakup showing: (1) the monthly wage figure used as the base, with each allowance listed; (2) your exact service period with start and end dates; (3) the Article 84 calculation year by year; and (4) whether any Article 85 resignation fraction was applied, and why. Run the same inputs through the calculator and through Qiwa's official EOS calculator — if all three agree, you can sign with confidence. If the employer's figure is lower, the discrepancy is almost always in the wage base (allowances excluded) or a missed pro-rated partial year. Get any correction in writing before signing the final settlement, because reopening a signed settlement is far harder than fixing it beforehand.

FAQs

Do I get EOSB if I'm terminated for cause?

The award is generally still due — it accrues with service and is separate from the reason for separation, though specific misconduct dismissals can affect entitlements. The resignation fractions apply only to voluntary resignation.

Is the end-of-service award taxed?

Saudi Arabia levies no personal income tax, so the award is received gross. If you are tax-resident elsewhere, your home country may tax it — check local rules.

Can my contract give me more than the statute?

Yes. The Labor Law sets minimums; contracts and company policies frequently provide enhanced EOSB (for example, a full month from year one). The statute is the floor.

What if I worked for multiple employers?

Each employment relationship generates its own award based on service with that employer. There is no combining service across employers.

Does unused annual leave get paid too?

Yes — accrued but unused annual leave (21 days, rising to 30 after 5 years) is payable on separation, calculated on the wage base, in addition to the EOSB.
