GTA 6 Chop: How Old Is Franklin's Dog Now?

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, thirteen years after GTA 5 — and one question has followed the game through the whole wait: where is Chop? Franklin Clinton's Rottweiler was one of the most loved characters in GTA 5. Players walked him, played fetch with him, taught him tricks, and used his scent-tracking in missions. He felt like a real dog, and fans have spent thirteen years wondering whether he could possibly still be alive.

The answer is a piece of sad maths, and this guide does it honestly: how old Chop would be now, what a Rottweiler's lifespan really is, what the GTA 6 trailers actually show — and what a launch-day clip claims that nobody has confirmed.

The maths: Chop would be about sixteen

Chop's exact age was never stated in GTA 5, but fans estimate he was about three years old when the game launched in 2013. Count forward thirteen years to the GTA 6 launch in November 2026, and Chop would be roughly sixteen or seventeen years old today. That is the number that started the whole debate, because a Rottweiler's typical lifespan is eight to ten years, with some sources stretching it to eleven or twelve.

By that measure, Chop would be well past the breed's usual lifespan — a grandfather many times over, in the words of one fan wiki. Some dogs do live past twelve, so the maths is not a death certificate. But it is the reason so many fans concluded, months before launch, that Chop could not realistically appear in GTA 6 as the same dog. The thirteen-year gap between games is longer than a Rottweiler's whole life.

What the trailers show — and what they do not

Dogs appear in both GTA 6 trailers, in street scenes, yards and residential areas around Vice City and Leonida. That is confirmed: the world has dogs in it, and they are part of the scenery of the state. What no trailer shows is Lucia or Jason petting a dog, adopting a dog, feeding a dog, or taking a dog on a mission. No announcement, interview or Rockstar statement has described a pet companion system, a dog mechanic, or any way for players to interact with animals.

That is the honest position, and it matters: until Rockstar says so, a companion dog in GTA 6 is a hope, not a fact. The trailers prove dogs exist in Leonida. They do not prove you can own one, and they do not prove Chop is among them. Anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing, and the guess is usually built on a screenshot of a dog that looks like a Rottweiler — which, in a state full of dogs, proves very little.

The launch-day clip: a claim, not a confirmation

On launch day, a social media clip showed a player character petting a black Rottweiler on a sunny street, with a voiceover celebrating that Chop is in GTA 6. The clip spread fast, and the comments split between excitement and disbelief — many viewers argued the dog in the clip is not the same character, because of the size and the breed details, and others pointed out that a petting animation is not the same as Chop himself.

Treat that clip the way you should treat any single social post: as unverified. A dog being petted in a game does not prove it is Franklin's dog, and a fan clip does not carry the weight of a Rockstar statement or a review from a reputable outlet. If Chop is in the game, the confirmation will come from the game itself, played by millions — not from one clip on launch day.

What fans actually want

The hope is easy to understand, because GTA 5 gave players something rare: a dog with a personality. Chop was not scenery. He followed Franklin, he reacted, and he felt like a companion. Fans hope GTA 6 gives Lucia and Jason a similar dog — one that lives with them, follows them, and becomes part of the story. Some fans have even asked for a Chop Jr, a puppy that carries the name forward without pretending a sixteen-year-old Rottweiler is still chasing cars.

Whether that hope is answered is still unknown. The game's story is more personal than previous GTA games: it follows a couple, their home life, their neighbourhood, and their daily routines. A dog would fit that story perfectly. But fitting the story and being in the game are two different things, and until the game is played through, the answer stays where it has been all along: we do not know.
