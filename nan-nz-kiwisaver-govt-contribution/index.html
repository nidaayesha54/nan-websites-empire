How the KiwiSaver government contribution works (2026)

Every KiwiSaver year (1 July – 30 June), the government matches 25 cents for each dollar of your own contributions, up to a maximum of $260.72. The match was halved from $521.43 in Budget 2025, but the qualifying contribution stayed at $1,042.86 — roughly $20 a week. If you earn more than $180,000 a year, you get nothing.

What counts as "your own contributions"

Employee (salary) contributions and voluntary top-ups count. Employer contributions, past government contributions, and funds transferred from Australian super schemes do not count.

Who is eligible

  • Aged 16–64 (16- and 17-year-olds became eligible for the government contribution from 1 July 2025)
  • Living in New Zealand
  • Annual taxable income of $180,000 or less
  • A KiwiSaver member who has contributed during the year

Key date: 30 June

Contributions must be processed by your provider by 30 June to count for that year's top-up. Don't leave a top-up to the last week — processing delays can push it into the next KiwiSaver year.

Worked example

Someone earning $50,000 who contributes the default 3.5% ($1,750/year) and puts in nothing extra:

Frequently asked questions

When is the government contribution paid?

It's paid directly into your KiwiSaver account each July for the KiwiSaver year just ended (1 July – 30 June).

Do my employer's contributions count toward the $1,042.86?

No. Only your own money counts: employee salary deductions and voluntary contributions. Employer contributions are excluded.

What if I earn over $180,000?

You are not eligible for the government contribution at all (assessed on income in one of the last two tax years after your return is finalised).

Can I still get something if I can't reach $1,042.86?

Yes — it's a 25% match, not all-or-nothing. Every dollar you put in earns 25 cents of government money up to the $260.72 cap.

Why did the amount drop from $521.43?

Budget 2025 halved the match rate from 50 cents to 25 cents per dollar (fiscally sustainable targeting), while keeping the $1,042.86 qualifying contribution the same.

I'm 17 — do I qualify?

Yes. Since 1 July 2025, 16- and 17-year-olds are eligible for the government contribution, and working 16–17s also get mandatory employer contributions from 1 April 2026.
