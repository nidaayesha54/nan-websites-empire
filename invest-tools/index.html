Frequently Asked Questions

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is earning returns on both your original money and the returns it has already earned. Over decades, compounding turns modest monthly contributions into serious wealth — which our compound interest calculator shows year by year.

What is a good CAGR for investments?

The US stock market has returned roughly 10% per year (about 7% after inflation) over long periods. A 7–10% CAGR is a reasonable long-term planning assumption; anything promising far more deserves skepticism.

How should I allocate stocks vs bonds by age?

A common rule of thumb is 110 minus your age in stocks, the rest in bonds. A 30-year-old would hold about 80% stocks. Adjust for your risk tolerance with our portfolio allocator.

Are index funds better than individual stocks?

For most investors, yes. Index funds give instant diversification, low fees, and long-term returns that beat most professional stock pickers. Read our full comparison.

How much should I invest per month?

Invest as much as you can while keeping an emergency fund of 3–6 months of expenses. Even $100–$200 a month compounds powerfully over 30+ years. Consistency matters more than the starting amount.
