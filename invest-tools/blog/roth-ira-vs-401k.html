Roth IRA vs 401(k): Which Should You Fund First?

By NAN Marketing Agency · October 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Both accounts build retirement wealth, but they tax you at opposite ends. The right order depends on one simple question: will your tax rate be higher now or in retirement?

The Core Difference

  • Traditional 401(k): contributions are pre-tax (you save tax now), withdrawals in retirement are taxed.
  • Roth IRA: contributions are after-tax (no deduction now), but qualified withdrawals in retirement are completely tax-free.

The Priority Order Most Experts Recommend

  1. 401(k) up to the employer match. A 50–100% instant return beats any tax optimization. Never leave match money behind.
  2. Roth IRA up to the annual limit. Tax-free growth, more investment choices, and no required withdrawals later.
  3. Back to the 401(k) up to the max if you can save more.
  4. Taxable brokerage for anything beyond that.

When Roth Wins

Early in your career (lower tax bracket now than later), or if you expect higher income in retirement. Young investors get decades of compounding on money that will never be taxed again.

When Traditional Wins

Peak earning years in a high bracket, especially if you expect lower income in retirement. The upfront deduction at 24–32% is hard to beat.

Income Limits and Gotchas

  • Roth IRA contributions phase out above certain incomes — high earners may need the "backdoor Roth" strategy.
  • Traditional 401(k) has no income limit for contributions, but very high earners should check deduction rules.
  • Check current IRS contribution limits each year; they adjust for inflation.
