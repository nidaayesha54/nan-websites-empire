Index Funds vs Stocks: Which Should You Buy?

By NAN Marketing Agency · Updated October 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Should you buy one index fund or pick individual stocks? For most investors, the answer is clear — but it helps to understand exactly why.

What Each One Is

  • Index fund: one purchase that holds hundreds or thousands of companies (e.g., a total US market fund). You own a slice of the whole market.
  • Individual stocks: shares of single companies. Your return depends entirely on each company's fate.

Diversification: The Decisive Factor

Owning 500+ companies means one bankruptcy barely dents you. Owning 5 stocks means one bad earnings report can wreck your year. Studies consistently show most professional stock pickers underperform simple index funds over 10+ years — and they do this full-time.

Fees and Effort

  • Index funds charge tiny expense ratios (often 0.03–0.15% per year) and need no research.
  • Individual stocks demand hours of research per company, plus the discipline to hold through volatility.
  • Frequent trading adds taxes and commissions that quietly drag returns.

Realistic Returns

Broad market index funds have delivered roughly 10% annualized before inflation over long stretches. Individual stocks can beat that — and can also go to zero. Measure any strategy honestly with our CAGR calculator.

When Individual Stocks Make Sense

  • You have a genuine informational edge in an industry.
  • You enjoy the research and accept the risk.
  • It is a small "explore" slice (5–10%) around an index-fund core.

The Bottom Line
