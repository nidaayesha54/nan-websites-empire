Compound Interest Explained: The 8th Wonder

By NAN Marketing Agency · Updated October 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Compound interest means earning returns on your returns. Year one, your money grows. Year two, the growth itself grows. Over decades, this snowball effect does most of the heavy lifting in wealth building.

A Simple Example

Invest $10,000 at 7% annual return:

  • After 10 years: ~$19,672
  • After 20 years: ~$38,697
  • After 30 years: ~$76,123

Notice the second decade adds far more than the first — that acceleration is compounding.

Why Starting Early Beats Investing More

Sarah invests $200/month from age 25 to 35 ($24,000 total) then stops. James invests $200/month from 35 to 65 ($72,000 total). At 7% returns, Sarah ends up with more — roughly $368,000 vs $303,000 — because her money compounded for 10 extra years. Time is the most powerful input.

The Formula

Future value = PV(1+r)n + PMT × (((1+r)n−1)/r), where PV is your starting amount, PMT your monthly contribution, r the monthly rate, and n the number of months. Our compound interest calculator runs this for you with a year-by-year table and chart.

Three Rules to Maximize Compounding

  1. Start now. Every year you wait costs you the most valuable compounding years.
  2. Reinvest everything. Dividends and interest must stay invested to compound.
  3. Keep fees low. A 1% annual fee quietly eats about a quarter of your 30-year gains.

Estimate Your Doubling Time

Use the Rule of 72: divide 72 by your annual return to estimate years to double your money. At 7%, money doubles roughly every 10 years.
