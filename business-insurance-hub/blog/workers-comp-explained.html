Workers' Comp Explained: Rates, Rules & Exemptions

February 2026 · 6 min read

What it is

Workers' compensation pays medical bills and lost wages when employees get hurt on the job — and in exchange, employees generally can't sue you over the injury. It's the grand bargain of workplace injury law.

Who must carry it

Nearly everyone with employees. Every US state except Texas requires it once you hire (thresholds vary: some states kick in at 1 employee, others at 3–5). Sole proprietors with no employees are usually exempt — but can opt in.

How rates work

Premiums = payroll ÷ 100 × class rate × experience modifier. Class rates reflect job danger:

  • Clerical/office work: ~$0.30–$0.60 per $100 of payroll
  • Retail: ~$1.50–$2.50 per $100
  • Construction: ~$5–$15+ per $100

Your experience modifier (1.0 = average) rises with claims and falls with clean years — a 0.8 mod means 20% off.

Owner & officer exemptions

Many states let sole proprietors, partners, and corporate officers exclude themselves from coverage — cutting premiums. Rules differ wildly by state, so check yours before assuming.

How to lower your premium

  • Classify correctly — misclassified employees in high-risk codes overpay for years.
  • Keep payroll records clean — auditors estimate upward when records are messy.
  • Run a safety program — documented training earns credits with many carriers.
  • Pay-as-you-go billing — ties premiums to actual payroll, avoiding year-end audit surprises.
  • Shop annually — rates for identical risk vary 30%+ between carriers.
