7 Business Insurance Mistakes That Cost Owners Thousands

February 2026 · 7 min read

1. Buying general liability and assuming you're "covered"

GL is the foundation, not the house. It doesn't cover professional mistakes, employee injuries, cyberattacks, or your own property. Audit your actual risks with our coverage quiz.

2. Skipping professional liability (E&O)

If clients pay for your expertise, one "your advice cost me money" claim can run six figures. E&O typically costs $500–$2,000/year — the best ROI in insurance.

3. Underinsuring to save a few hundred dollars

A $500K limit sounds fine until a $750K judgment arrives. The jump from $1M to $2M in GL limits often costs only 15–25% more. Don't starve the coverage that matters most.

4. Forgetting to update policies as you grow

Hired staff? Added a location? Doubled revenue? Every change alters your risk profile. Review coverage annually — stale policies are the #1 cause of denied claims.

5. Assuming home insurance covers a home business

It doesn't. Standard homeowner's policies exclude business activity. A home-based endorsement ($150–$500/yr) or small BOP closes the gap.

6. Not reading the exclusions

Flood, earthquake, professional services, intentional acts — policies are defined as much by what they exclude as what they cover. Read the exclusions page; it's usually only two pages.

7. Buying on price alone

The cheapest quote often has the narrowest wording. Compare coverage terms first, price second — a $200 saving means nothing if the claim is denied.
