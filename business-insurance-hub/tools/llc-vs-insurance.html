LLC vs Business Insurance: What Protects What?

Updated for 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer: An LLC protects your personal assets from business debts. Insurance pays for lawsuits, injuries, and damage. Most businesses need both.

What an LLC actually does

A Limited Liability Company creates a legal wall between you and your business. If the business owes money or gets sued, creditors generally can't touch your house, car, or savings — only business assets.

  • Protects against: business debts, contract defaults, some lawsuits
  • Does NOT protect against: your own negligence, personal guarantees you signed, professional mistakes, or injuries on your premises
  • Cost: $50–$500 state filing fee + annual reports

What business insurance actually does

Insurance pays the bills when things go wrong: a customer slips in your store, a client sues over a missed deadline, a fire destroys your inventory. The insurer hires lawyers and pays settlements — up to your policy limits.

  • Protects against: lawsuits, injuries, property damage, professional errors, data breaches
  • Does NOT do: shield personal assets from business debts (that's the LLC's job)
  • Cost: typically $500–$2,500/year for small businesses

The bottom line

Think of it this way: the LLC is a wall between your personal and business life. Insurance is a wallet that pays when the wall isn't enough. One filing fee plus one annual premium buys you both layers — for most small businesses, that's the cheapest peace of mind available.
