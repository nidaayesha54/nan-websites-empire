Types of Business Insurance Explained

1. General liability

The foundation policy for almost every business. Covers third-party bodily injury, property damage, and advertising injury (libel/slander in your marketing). Example: a customer slips in your store, or you damage a client's wall during an install. Typical cost: $400–$1,500/year for small businesses.

2. Business Owner's Policy (BOP)

A BOP bundles general liability + commercial property (and often business-interruption coverage) into one package — usually cheaper than buying the pieces separately. Typical cost: $500–$2,500/year. Best for small offices, retail shops, and restaurants. Eligibility usually requires fewer than ~100 employees and modest premises.

3. Professional liability / errors & omissions (E&O)

Covers claims that your professional advice or service caused a client financial loss — the classic "you messed up my project" lawsuit. Consultants, agencies, IT firms, accountants, and real estate professionals need it. Typical cost: $500–$2,000/year (healthcare and legal fields run much higher). Note: general liability does not cover this.

4. Workers' compensation

Pays medical costs and lost wages for employees injured on the job — and in most states it's legally required once you hire. Pricing is per $100 of payroll by job class: office roles run ~$0.30–$0.60 while field/construction classes can exceed $5. Try our workers' comp estimator, and see the requirements by state.

5. Commercial property

Covers your building, equipment, inventory, and furniture against fire, theft, vandalism, and weather. Even home-based businesses should check this — homeowners policies typically exclude business property. Often cheapest inside a BOP. Typical standalone cost: $500–$3,000/year.

6. Commercial auto

Required for vehicles owned by the business, and usually needed if employees drive for work (even personal cars — "hired and non-owned auto" coverage). Typical cost: $600–$2,400/year per vehicle.

7. Cyber liability

Covers data breaches, ransomware, and the cost of notifying customers after an incident. If you store customer data, take online payments, or hold client credentials, this is increasingly non-optional. Typical cost: $1,000–$7,500/year for small businesses — and one breach averages far more than the premium.

8. Commercial umbrella

An extra layer of liability that kicks in when your underlying policies hit their limits — e.g., an extra $1M on top of your $1M general liability. Relatively cheap (often $500–$1,500/year per million) and popular with contractors and landlords.

Which ones do YOU need?

Short version:

  • Every business: general liability (or a BOP)
  • Any employees: workers' comp (legally required in nearly every state)
  • Advice/services for clients: professional liability
  • Physical location or equipment: commercial property
  • Business vehicles: commercial auto
  • Handle client data: cyber liability
