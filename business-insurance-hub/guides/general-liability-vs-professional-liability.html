General Liability vs Professional Liability: Which Do You Need?

Updated January 2026 · 6 min read

One-line version: General liability covers physical mishaps (injuries, damage). Professional liability covers financial harm from your work (bad advice, errors, missed deadlines).

General liability: the "oops, someone got hurt" policy

Covers third-party claims of:

  • Bodily injury — a customer slips in your store
  • Property damage — your employee dents a client's wall
  • Advertising injury — a competitor claims you copied their ad

Typical cost: $400–$1,500/year for small businesses. Almost every business should carry it — landlords and clients frequently require it.

Professional liability (E&O): the "oops, my work cost you money" policy

Covers claims that your professional service, advice, or deliverable caused a client financial loss:

  • A consultant's strategy flops and the client sues for lost revenue
  • A designer's error forces a costly reprint
  • An accountant misses a deadline triggering penalties

Typical cost: $500–$2,000/year (much higher for medical/legal). Essential for consultants, agencies, IT, real estate, and healthcare.

Do you need both?

Most service businesses: yes. A marketing agency can both have a client trip in the office (GL) and get sued over a failed campaign (E&O). The two policies cover non-overlapping risks — one doesn't substitute for the other.

Retail/restaurant with no advice component: general liability (often via a BOP) is usually enough.
