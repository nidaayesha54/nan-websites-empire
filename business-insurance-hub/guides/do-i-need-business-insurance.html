Do I Need Business Insurance? (Honest 2026 Answer)

Updated January 2026 · 7 min read

When it's legally required

  • Workers' compensation — required in nearly every US state the moment you hire your first employee (rules and exemptions vary; Texas is the notable exception).
  • Commercial auto — required in every state if the business owns vehicles, with state-minimum liability limits.
  • Professional liability — required by law for certain professions in many states (doctors, lawyers, real estate agents).
  • Unemployment & disability insurance — state payroll taxes, not optional.

General liability is rarely required by law — but read on, because "not required" doesn't mean "not needed."

When contracts require it (even if the law doesn't)

  • Commercial leases almost always require general liability ($1M is the common minimum).
  • Client contracts — especially enterprise and government clients — routinely demand GL + E&O with certificates of insurance.
  • Lenders require property insurance on financed equipment and buildings.
  • Marketplaces & platforms (Amazon, event venues, farmers markets) often set their own minimums.

The honest risk math of going uninsured

A basic general liability policy costs roughly $40–$125/month for a small business. Compare that to:

  • Average slip-and-fall settlement: $30,000–$50,000
  • Average cost to defend a lawsuit (even one you win): $75,000+
  • A single data breach for a small business: $25,000–$100,000+

One incident can exceed decades of premiums. Insurance isn't about the likely — it's about the survivable.

"But I'm just a freelancer / side hustle"

Freelancers get sued too — missed deadlines, alleged plagiarism, a client claiming your work cost them revenue. A basic professional liability policy ($500–$800/yr) is the cheapest sleep-aid in business. And if clients visit your home office, your homeowner's policy almost certainly excludes business activity.
