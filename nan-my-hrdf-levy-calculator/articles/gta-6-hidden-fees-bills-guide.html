GTA 6 Hidden Fees: The Bills Leonida Sends You

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and from your very first drive, Leonida starts billing you for things GTA V gave away free. In GTA V's story, you could steal a car, keep it, drive it forever without stopping for gas, then wreck it, and it cost you nothing. In GTA 6, almost every one of those things costs money: a fee to get your car back, dents off a stolen car's price, picked-up guns that can be gone forever, fuel, a registration fee for every stolen car you keep, and a cash goal before the next story mission. This guide is the full bill — every hidden fee in the game, what it costs, and how to dodge it.

Start with the car, because that is where the bills pile up. If the police impound your personal vehicle — and they will, if you do crimes in it and leave it in the search area — you have two ways to get it back. You can drive to the police impound lot and take it yourself, which costs nothing except the wanted level it triggers, or you can pay a fee through the Scooter Bros app on your phone and have it delivered instantly. The app's exact single-retrieval fee has not been published in the sources checked, so treat any number you see for it as unconfirmed. What is confirmed is the premium option: Scooter Bros Premium costs $12,500 and includes three free uses per in-game day plus free pickup from the impound. A single repair has been shown at $430. In Chapter 1, Joey fixes your car and drops it off at no cost, and he also respawns cars that get wrecked — after that, Scooter Bros takes over, and the free ride ends.

Registration, fuel and the price of a wreck

Keeping a stolen car is the second bill. Scan it with the WAiNK app and read the Cost to Register line: about $1,400 for a cheap old car, $11,250 for a Vapid Riata. The fee is several times the car's sell value — a pickup that sells for $1,800 costs $11,250 to register — and you need a free garage space before you can keep it. Then there is fuel. GTA V never charged for it; GTA 6 does, and running dry mid-chase is a real way to lose a getaway. Damage is a bill too: wrecking a stolen car lowers its sale price, and a destroyed registered car comes back through Scooter Bros for a fee instead of free. Guns follow the same logic. Picked-up guns can be gone forever when your car blows up or after a bust, so the weapon you looted for free can cost you the next fight.

Fuel, guns and the mission cash goals

Fuel is the fee that catches people by surprise. GTA V never charged for it; GTA 6 does, and the pump is not a decoration — run the tank dry mid-chase and the getaway ends on the hard shoulder. The same logic applies to the guns. Picked-up guns can be gone forever when your car blows up or after a bust, so the weapon you looted for free can cost you the next fight, and the weapon locker in your garage is where the guns you care about live. Damage is a bill too: wrecking a stolen car lowers its sale price, and a car that went into the water or was wrecked comes back repaired through Joey's delivery in Chapter 1, but only while the free ride lasts. And then there are the mission cash goals. The story itself charges you: Chapter 2's "Prepare for Vice City" needs $15,000 before it opens, and the cash goal before the next story mission is a bill like any other — the game will not let you through the door until you can pay it.

Put it all together and the bill is long. A fee to get your car back. Dents off a stolen car's price. Picked-up guns that can be gone forever. Fuel. A registration fee for every stolen car you keep. A cash goal before the next story mission. Every fact in that list has been stamped by the launch guides: seen in Rockstar's video, reported with the outlet's name, series history, or not announced. No prices have been announced for some of these, so every unknown price is marked as unconfirmed rather than guessed. The point is not the size of any single fee. It is that GTA 6 charges for the things GTA V gave away free, and the players who learn the numbers early — the ones who bank their cash, register their cars and keep their tanks full — are the ones who stop paying them.

Dying, and the cash you cannot protect

The biggest bill is the one you pay when you die. In GTA V, getting wasted cost you 5% of your cash. In GTA 6, you lose all the cash you are carrying — every dollar in your pocket, gone. The only protection is the bank: deposit your money at an ATM or through the BuckMe app, because the wallet and the bank are separate, and the bank balance survives. Carry up to $20,000 and the rest has to go somewhere safe. That is the real lesson of Leonida's billing system. The game does not hide its fees; it puts them in apps on your phone, in the impound lot on your map, and in the price tag on every stolen car. Learn the numbers once — $12,500 for Premium, $1,400 to $11,250 to register, $430 for a repair, all of your pocket cash if you die — and the bills stop being surprises. They become the cost of doing business.
