What Is the HRD Corp Levy?

The HRD Corp levy (still commonly called HRDF) is a mandatory training levy collected from registered employers under the PSMB Act 2001 to fund workforce upskilling. Employers with 10 or more Malaysian employees must register and contribute 1% of monthly wages; employers with 5–9 Malaysian employees may register voluntarily at 0.5%.

The Formula

Monthly levy = (Total basic salary + Fixed allowances) × 1%
For voluntary registrants (5–9 staff), multiply by 0.5% instead.
Only Malaysian employees count. Overtime, bonuses, commissions and reimbursements are excluded; fixed allowances (fixed transport, housing, meal, shift) are included.

Worked Example

15 Malaysian staff: total basic RM 45,000 + fixed allowances RM 5,000 = base RM 50,000; levy = RM 50,000 × 1% = RM 500/month — RM 6,000/year, claimable for approved training (SBL, SBL-Khas, SLB, OJT schemes).

Key Rules

  • The levy is an employer-only cost — it must never be deducted from employees' salaries.
  • Payable to HRD Corp by the 15th of the following month.
  • Registered employers can claim back levy paid for approved training grants — use it or risk losing unused balances (a 15% deduction policy on unused balances applies).
  • Covers specified industries under the PSMB Act 2001 (manufacturing, services, mining, construction and gazetted sectors).

FAQs

Is the HRDF levy deducted from my salary?

No. The levy is paid entirely by the employer. Deducting it from staff wages is not permitted.

Do foreign workers count toward the levy?

No. Only Malaysian employees are subject to the HRD Corp levy; foreign workers are excluded from both the headcount and the wage base.

Does overtime or bonus count in the levy base?

No. The base covers basic salary plus fixed allowances only. Overtime pay, performance bonuses, commissions and reimbursements are excluded.

What can my company claim with the levy?

Registered employers claim training costs through HRD Corp schemes such as SBL, SBL-Khas, SLB and OJT — covering course fees, trainer fees, materials and travel for approved programmes.

What happens if we miss the payment deadline?

Levy is due by the 15th of the following month. Late payment can attract penalties and interest under the PSMB Act — keep a monthly payment routine.
