The first rule: bank your cash

Before you earn a single dollar, learn the rule that protects it. You can only carry $20,000 in your pocket. Everything above that has to go into the bank, and there are 454 ATMs across Leonida to do it at. Open the BuckMe bank account — it unlocks with the mission "Buck Me" — and deposit at an ATM whenever your pocket gets heavy. Pocket cash is lost when you die or get arrested, and it does not carry into Chapter 2. The other safe spot is under the floorboards in Jason's bedroom, where you can stash spare money at home. The habit is simple: earn, bank, repeat. Every method below assumes you are doing that.

Jewelry stores: the fastest early cash

The fastest verified early-game money is jewelry store robberies, at around $20,000 each. The two named targets are Victoria Lee Jeweler in Capri and Blanc and Giroux in the Decor District. From Chapter 3 you can use the snap gun to break in silently, which keeps the job quiet and the heat low. Two stores at $20,000 each is $40,000 for one afternoon's work — enough to open the next story mission and still have cash left for a car upgrade. Plan the escape before you walk in: know which street you are leaving on, and have a fast car parked close by, because the wanted system only reacts to what the police actually see.

Treasure dives: slow money, almost no risk

If robberies are not your style, Kenton's treasure dives are the calmest money in the game. After the mission "Island Dreams", Kenton runs wreck dives that pay around $1,400 per wreck, and there are 33 underwater discoveries in total to find. It is slow money next to a jewelry store, but it is almost no risk — no cops, no shootout, no lost cash. Take the Arras Bullhead if you plan to dive deep, and treat the dives as a steady income between story missions rather than a get-rich-quick scheme. The dives also take you through the Leonida Keys, which is one of the most beautiful parts of the map, so the trip pays twice.

Side jobs that pay

Leonida pays for honest work too, and the side jobs add up fast. Towing and deliveries are the reliable earners, and Pilot Pete pays $20,000 for a completed job — one of the best single payouts outside the story. Hunting pays as well: the first hunt pays around $500, and the hunting grounds are Mount Kalaga National Park and the Grassrivers swamp. Another steady trick is stealing cars right next to a Pay 'N' Spray and selling them — the spray shop is right there when you need it, and the sale is clean cash. None of these will make you rich in an hour, but together they keep your pocket full between the big jobs, and they never put your save at risk.

The big score waits until Chapter 4

The biggest payout in the game is the bank vaults, at up to $205,000 — but only at the end of Chapter 4. Do not plan your early game around them, because you cannot reach them yet. The right way to think about GTA 6 money is in chapters: early on, jewelry stores and side jobs; mid-game, treasure dives and hunting; late game, the vaults. When the vault money does land, you will want somewhere to put it — there are 8 garages across Leonida costing around $160,000 in total, and only 3 vehicle slots to start with, so spend on storage and cars you actually keep. Cars are the one purchase that pays you back every time you drive them.

There are 70 clothing stores in Leonida and the cheapest full outfit costs as little as $9 at Bargain World in Port Gellhorn, so looking good is cheap — going broke for a jacket is not.
