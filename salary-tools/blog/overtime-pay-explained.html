Overtime Pay Explained: Who Gets 1.5×?

By NAN Marketing Agency · October 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, non-exempt employees must be paid 1.5× their regular rate ("time and a half") for hours over 40 in a workweek. But not everyone qualifies — here's how it actually works.

Exempt vs Non-Exempt: The Key Distinction

  • Non-exempt: entitled to overtime. Most hourly workers fall here.
  • Exempt: no overtime required. Typically salaried workers in executive, administrative, professional, or outside-sales roles earning above the salary threshold.

Your job title doesn't decide this — your duties and pay structure do. Misclassification is common; if you're hourly and working 50-hour weeks without overtime pay, that's worth questioning.

How Overtime Math Works

Overtime rate = regular hourly rate × 1.5. At $25/hr, overtime pays $37.50/hr. Five overtime hours a week = $187.50/week = $9,750/year before taxes. Calculate yours with our overtime calculator.

What Counts Toward the 40 Hours?

Only hours actually worked — federal law doesn't require overtime for working on weekends or holidays per se, just for exceeding 40 hours in the week. (Many employers and states are more generous; some states require daily overtime after 8 hours.)

Salaried Non-Exempt Workers

Yes, this exists: you can be salaried and non-exempt, earning overtime on your salary's hourly equivalent. If your salary assumes 40 hours, your regular rate = salary ÷ 2,080.

The Bottom Line

Know your classification, track your hours, and do the math — overtime is often the highest-paid work you'll do all week. Compare salary vs hourly offers with overtime included before you sign.
