Cost-of-Living Raise vs Merit Raise

By NAN Marketing Agency · October 12, 2026 · 4 min read

"Everyone's getting 3% this year" sounds like a raise. It might just be treading water. Here's the difference between the two kinds of pay increases — and why it matters.

Cost-of-Living Adjustment (COLA)

A COLA is meant to offset inflation — keeping your purchasing power flat as prices rise. If inflation is 3% and you get 3%, you can buy exactly what you could last year: zero real raise. If inflation is 4% and you get 3%, you took a 1% pay cut in real terms.

Merit Raise

A merit raise rewards your performance — skills, results, expanded responsibility. This is the raise that actually grows your real income and compounds over your career. Typical merit increases run 3–5%, on top of any COLA at companies that separate the two.

Why the Confusion Hurts You

Many employers blend them into one number and call it a raise. A "4% raise" in a 3%-inflation year is really a 3% COLA plus a 1% merit bump. Knowing the split tells you whether your performance is being rewarded — or just inflation being acknowledged.

What to Actually Ask For

When negotiating, anchor on real growth above inflation. Use our raise calculator to see any offer's real raise after inflation, then read how to negotiate a raise in 2026 for the conversation itself.

The Bottom Line

COLA keeps you even; merit moves you up. Accept the COLA gladly — then negotiate the merit raise separately.
