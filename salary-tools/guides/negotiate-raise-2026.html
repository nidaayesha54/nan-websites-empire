How to Negotiate a Raise in 2026

By NAN Marketing Agency · Updated October 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Most people who ask for a raise get one — and most people never ask. The gap between those groups is worth tens of thousands over a career. Here's how to do it well.

When to Ask

  • After a win: completed project, exceeded targets, great review — strike while the evidence is fresh.
  • At review time or budget season: ask 2–3 months before annual reviews, when budgets are being set.
  • When your role expanded but your pay didn't — new responsibilities are the strongest case there is.

What Data to Bring

Three things: (1) your accomplishments with numbers (revenue influenced, costs cut, projects shipped), (2) market salary data for your role and city, and (3) your target number — a specific figure, not a range. Ranges get you the bottom of the range.

Scripts That Work

The opener: "I'd like to discuss my compensation. Over the past year I've [2–3 concrete wins], and market data shows this role pays $X–$Y. Based on my contributions, I'm asking for $Z."

If they say budgets are tight: "I understand. What would need to be true for us to get to $Z in the next 6 months? Can we put a plan and a date in writing?"

If they lowball: "I appreciate the offer. Given [win #1] and [market data], I was expecting something closer to $Z. Is there flexibility?" Then stop talking — silence is a negotiation tool.

Negotiate More Than Salary

If base pay won't move: signing bonus, extra PTO, remote days, title change (which raises your next salary), professional development budget, or an earlier review date. Total compensation is the real number.

Know Your Number First

Before the conversation, use our raise calculator to see what different percentages mean monthly — and what counts as a real raise after inflation. Walking in with math beats walking in with hope.

The Bottom Line

Ask with evidence, name a specific number, and have a plan for "no." The worst realistic outcome of a professional ask is information — and the best is a life-changing raise.
