How Paychecks Work: Taxes Explained

By NAN Marketing Agency · Updated October 12, 2026 · 6 min read

Your offer letter says $75,000. Your bank account says something much smaller. Here's exactly where the difference goes.

1. Federal Income Tax (Progressive Brackets)

The US taxes income in brackets — only the dollars inside each bracket are taxed at that bracket's rate. For a single filer (2025 brackets, used as estimates): 10% up to $11,925, 12% to $48,475, 22% to $103,350, and so on. Earning "into the next bracket" never taxes your whole income at the higher rate — a myth that refuses to die.

The standard deduction ($15,000 single / $30,000 married filing jointly in 2025) is subtracted first, so that chunk is taxed at 0%.

2. FICA: Social Security + Medicare (7.65%)

Unlike income tax, FICA is flat from dollar one: 6.2% Social Security (on wages up to the annual cap, ~$176,100) plus 1.45% Medicare (no cap). Your employer matches it — self-employed workers pay both halves (15.3%).

3. State and Local Taxes

These vary wildly: nine states have no income tax; others take up to 13%+. Some cities add their own. Our paycheck estimator shows the federal picture; treat state tax as an additional haircut.

4. Pre-Tax Deductions (These Actually Help)

401(k) contributions, HSA contributions, and health premiums come out before income tax is calculated — shrinking your taxable income. A $200/month 401(k) contribution might only reduce take-home by ~$155 because of the tax savings.

5. Withholding: The Estimate Game

Your employer withholds based on your W-4. Too much withheld = big refund (an interest-free loan to the IRS); too little = a bill in April. Big life changes (marriage, kids, second job) are good times to update it.

A Worked Example

$75,000 salary, single, biweekly: taxable income ≈ $60,000 → federal tax ≈ $8,114 → FICA ≈ $5,738 → take-home ≈ $61,149/year or ~$2,352 per paycheck (before state tax and deductions). Run your own numbers in our free paycheck estimator.

The Bottom Line

Gross pay is a headline; net pay is your real budget number. Understand the five layers above and you'll never be surprised by a paycheck again.
