Salary vs Hourly: Pros, Cons & How to Compare Offers

By NAN Marketing Agency · Updated October 12, 2026 · 6 min read

A $65,000 salary and $31/hr sound equivalent — until overtime, benefits, and unpaid hours enter the picture. Here's how to compare them honestly.

Salary Jobs: Pros and Cons

Pros: predictable paychecks, usually better benefits (health insurance, PTO, retirement match), paid holidays and sick days, and a certain professional status.

Cons: most salaried roles are exempt — no overtime pay. A "40-hour" salary job that regularly demands 50 hours quietly cuts your effective hourly rate by 20%.

Hourly Jobs: Pros and Cons

Pros: every hour is paid, and overtime (over 40/week for non-exempt workers) pays 1.5× by federal law. Fewer unpaid expectations.

Cons: income fluctuates with scheduled hours, benefits are often thinner, and hours can be cut in slow periods.

How to Compare Two Offers Fairly

  1. Convert to the same units with our salary-to-hourly converter — but use realistic hours, not 40. Ask salaried employers about typical weekly hours.
  2. Add overtime value. 5 hrs/week overtime at $25/hr = ~$9,750/year extra. Our overtime calculator does the math.
  3. Price the benefits. Employer health insurance can be worth $6,000–$20,000/year; a 401(k) match is literal free money. A salary offer with great benefits often beats a higher hourly rate without them.
  4. Compare take-home, not gross. Run both through our paycheck estimator.

When Each Wins

Salary usually wins for career roles with strong benefits and true ~40-hour weeks. Hourly usually wins in trades, healthcare shifts, and any role with regular overtime — 10 overtime hours a week can add 25%+ to annual pay.

The Bottom Line

Never compare the headline numbers — compare effective hourly rate × realistic hours + benefits value. Do that math, and the better offer usually becomes obvious.
