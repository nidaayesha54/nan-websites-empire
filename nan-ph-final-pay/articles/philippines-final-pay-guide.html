Philippines Final Pay: What You Are Owed and the 30-Day Release Rule

When employment ends in the Philippines, your employer owes you a final settlement — and the law gives them 30 calendar days to release it. This guide lists everything that should be in it.

Key takeaways:
  • Final pay = everything owed at separation: salary, 13th month, leave, refunds.
  • DOLE requires release within 30 calendar days of separation.
  • Separation pay applies only for authorized causes — redundancy, retrenchment, closure.

Your last day at work isn't the end of the money story — it's the start of the settlement. In the Philippines, everything your employer still owes you when employment ends is called final pay (often "last pay" or "back pay" in everyday speech). And the Department of Labor and Employment gives employers a clear deadline: release it within 30 calendar days of separation.

This guide lists every component that can appear in final pay, explains the 30-day rule and its limits, covers separation pay triggers, and shows what to do when the money doesn't arrive.

Final Pay vs Separation Pay vs Backwages

Three terms, three different things:

  • Final pay = the total of all wages and benefits already due when employment ends. Almost every separated employee is owed some final pay.
  • Separation pay = an additional amount owed only in specific situations (redundancy, retrenchment, closure, disease). It's one possible component of final pay — not every resignation includes it.
  • Backwages = compensation awarded by a court for illegal dismissal. Not part of an ordinary final-pay computation.

Worked Example

Monthly salary ₱25,000, resigned after 8 months, 5 unused SIL days, ₱12,500 unpaid salary for the last half-month:

  • Unpaid salary: ₱12,500
  • 13th-month pro-rata: 25,000 × 8 ÷ 12 = ₱16,666.67
  • Unused SIL: (25,000 ÷ 26) × 5 = ₱4,807.69
  • Gross final pay ≈ ₱33,974.36 (before deductions and tax adjustments)

The 30-Day Release Rule

DOLE Labor Advisory No. 06, Series of 2020 — reaffirmed by DOLE in January 2026 — says final pay should be released within 30 calendar days from the date of separation, unless a company policy, individual agreement, or CBA gives a more favorable (earlier) period. "Days" means calendar days, including weekends and holidays.

The 30 days run from the date employment actually ended — not from when HR finishes its paperwork. DOLE has clarified that while employers may run a legitimate clearance process, clearance should be completed within the 30-day window and cannot be used to restart the clock or delay payment indefinitely.

When Separation Pay Applies

Separation pay is owed for authorized causes under the Labor Code:

  • Redundancy: at least 1 month's salary per year of service (minimum 1 month).
  • Retrenchment: at least ½ month's salary per year of service (minimum 1 month).
  • Closure (not due to serious losses) and disease: statutory formulas apply.
  • Resignation or just-cause dismissal: generally no separation pay — but final pay's other components still apply.

A fraction of 6 months or more typically counts as a full year in these computations.

Deductions Must Be Lawful

Employers may deduct genuine accountabilities — unreturned equipment, cash advances, salary loans — but deductions need a lawful basis. They can't invent penalties, and they can't hold your entire final pay hostage over a disputed amount while the undisputed portion sits unpaid. If part of the computation is contested, the undisputed part should still be released.

What to Do If Final Pay Doesn't Arrive

1

Get the computation

Request a written breakdown. Complete your clearance promptly and keep proof.

2

Send a written demand

A dated demand letter starts your paper trail and often resolves it.

3

File for SEnA

File a Request for Assistance under DOLE's Single Entry Approach — a ~30-day conciliation process before any full case.

The Clearance Process, Done Right

Clearance exists so the employer can verify returned property and settle accountabilities — laptops, IDs, advances, tools. DOLE accepts legitimate clearance, but its current guidance is explicit: clearance should start immediately upon separation and finish within the 30-day final-pay period. Practical advice:

  • Return everything on or before your last day and get signed receipts.
  • Ask HR for the clearance checklist and routing slip on day one — don't wait for them to chase you.
  • If a signatory is "on leave," escalate in writing; one absent approver doesn't pause your statutory timeline.
  • Keep copies of every clearance document. If the dispute reaches SEnA, your paper trail is your case.

Your Certificate of Employment

Separate from money: on separation you have the right to a Certificate of Employment stating your dates of service and position — the employer must issue it upon request and may not add negative remarks beyond factual information. Request it alongside your final pay; you'll need it for your next job application anyway.

Government Workers Follow Different Rules

The 30-day DOLE rule covers the private sector. Government personnel follow civil service clearance, budgeting, and Commission on Audit procedures, which run on their own (often longer) timelines. Overseas Filipino workers, seafarers, and kasambahay have sector-specific procedures too — though DOLE's SEnA assistance remains available as a starting point for disputes.

Final Pay at a Glance

What: the full settlement of everything owed when employment ends — unpaid salary, pro-rated 13th-month pay, convertible leave, earned incentives, refunds, minus lawful deductions. Deadline: within 30 calendar days of separation (DOLE Labor Advisory No. 06-2020). Separation pay: only for authorized causes — redundancy (≥1 month/year), retrenchment (≥½ month/year), closure, disease. Clearance: legitimate but must finish inside the 30-day window. If unpaid: written demand, then a SEnA Request for Assistance with DOLE.

Frequently Asked Questions

It's been 45 days and my clearance "isn't done." Is that legal?

The 30-day period runs from separation, and DOLE guidance says clearance should be finished within that window — not used to extend it. Follow up in writing citing Labor Advisory No. 06-2020.

I was dismissed for cause. Do I still get final pay?

Yes — unpaid salary, pro-rated 13th month, and convertible leave remain due. What's typically not due on just-cause dismissal is separation pay.

My employer deducted a "training bond." Can they?

Only if you actually agreed to a valid training bond in writing and its conditions were met. Challenge vague or undocumented deductions in your written demand.

Does the 30-day rule apply to project and contractual workers?

Yes — it covers private-sector employees generally, including probationary, project, seasonal, and fixed-term workers, once an employer-employee relationship existed.

What's the difference between "final pay" and my last salary?

Your last salary is one component. Final pay is the whole settlement: salary owed + 13th month + leave conversion + any separation/retirement pay + refunds, minus lawful deductions.

Can I still claim final pay if I left without rendering 30 days’ notice?

Yes — skipping the 30-day resignation notice doesn’t erase wages already earned. The employer may have a separate claim for damages caused by abrupt resignation, but your unpaid salary, 13th-month pay, and convertible leave remain due.
