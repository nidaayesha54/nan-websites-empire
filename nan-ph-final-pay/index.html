What Is Included in Final Pay

  • Unpaid salary and wages — earned from the last cut-off to your last day, plus any earned overtime, premiums and incentives.
  • Pro-rated 13th month pay = total basic salary earned in the calendar year ÷ 12 (Presidential Decree 851). You keep it even if you resign in November.
  • Leave conversion — unused service incentive leave (5 days/year) plus any company vacation or sick leave that your policy allows to convert, multiplied by your daily rate.
  • Separation pay — only for authorized causes: at least 1 month or 1 month's pay per year of service (whichever is higher) for redundancy or installation of labor-saving devices; 1 month or ½ month's pay per year for retrenchment, closure without serious losses, or disease. No separation pay for resignation or just-cause dismissal unless policy provides it.
  • Returnables — cash bonds/deposits and any tax refund (over-withheld tax via BIR Form 2316).
  • Minus only lawful deductions: tax, statutory contributions, and documented loans/advances.

The 30-Day Release Rule

DOLE Labor Advisory No. 06-2020 requires employers to release final pay within 30 calendar days from separation. "Back pay" strictly means wages already earned but not yet released — it is not a bonus. If release is delayed, you may file a Request for Assistance (SEnA) at the nearest DOLE regional office; money claims prescribe within 3 years.

Worked example

₱20,000 salary, resigned after 10 months, ₱20,000 unpaid salary, 5 unused leave days: pro-rated 13th = (20,000 × 10) ÷ 12 = ₱16,667; leave conversion = (20,000 ÷ 26) × 5 = ₱3,846; gross final pay = 20,000 + 16,667 + 3,846 = ₱40,513 before deductions.

FAQs

Do I lose my 13th month pay if I resign before December?

No. You are entitled to pro-rated 13th month pay for the basic salary you earned during the year, payable as part of final pay — usually within 30 days of separation, not year-end.

Is separation pay automatic when I resign?

No. Resignation does not legally require separation pay. It is owed only for authorized causes (redundancy, retrenchment, closure, disease) or if your company policy or CBA grants it.

What is "back pay" exactly?

DOLE distinguishes the terms: final pay is the full package of entitlements on separation; back pay strictly refers to unpaid wages or overtime already earned but not yet released.

Can my employer deduct cash advances and loans?

Yes, if they are documented and authorized in writing (loans, advances, accountabilities). Unauthorized fees or arbitrary penalties are prohibited by Article 113 of the Labor Code.

What should I do if my final pay is delayed past 30 days?

Follow up in writing, then file a Request for Assistance through DOLE's SEnA portal or the nearest regional office. Keep your payslips, contract, resignation/termination letter and computation sheet as evidence.
