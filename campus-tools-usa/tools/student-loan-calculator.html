How Student Loan Payments Work

Monthly payment uses standard amortization: M = P × r(1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1) — P is principal, r monthly rate, n number of payments. Early payments are mostly interest; later ones mostly principal.

Power move: an extra $50/month on a $30,000 loan at 6.5% saves roughly $2,700 in interest and cuts ~2 years off a 10-year term. Extra payments attack principal directly.

FAQs

How is the monthly payment calculated?

Standard loan amortization on principal, annual rate, and term.

Do extra payments save money?

Yes — they go straight to principal, cutting interest and time.

Should I refinance?

Compare APRs and check for origination fees. Refinancing federal loans into private ones loses federal protections — weigh carefully.
