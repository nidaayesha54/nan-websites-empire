Travel Nurse Requirements in 2026: Everything You Need to Start

Travel nursing lets RNs take 8–26 week contracts across the country, earning $95,000–$150,000+ per year in total packages. But agencies are selective. Here's what you actually need.

1. Active RN License

You need an unencumbered RN license. The fastest route is the Nurse Licensure Compact (NLC): if your home state participates (40+ states do), one multistate license covers all compact states. For non-compact states like California, you'll apply for licensure by endorsement — start 6–8 weeks before your contract.

2. Experience: 1–2 Years Minimum

Almost all agencies require at least 1 year of recent experience in your specialty, and 2 years is preferred for ICU, ED, and OR. New grads are generally not eligible — facilities expect travelers to hit the ground running with minimal orientation.

3. Certifications

  • BLS — required everywhere (American Heart Association preferred).
  • ACLS — required for ICU, ED, telemetry, and most acute care.
  • Specialty certs (CCRN, CEN, TNCC, NRP) — make you far more competitive and unlock higher-paying contracts.

4. Clean Record & Health Requirements

Expect: background check, drug screening, up-to-date immunizations (flu, COVID, Hep B, MMR, varicella), TB test, and a physical. Keep digital copies of everything — you'll submit this packet for every contract.

How Travel Nurse Pay Works

Your package has two parts:

  • Taxable hourly wage — typically $30–$50/hour.
  • Tax-free stipends — housing (~$1,000–$1,400/week) and meals & incidentals (~$400–$500/week), tax-free if you maintain a tax home and the assignment is under a year.

Plus completion bonuses ($500–$2,000) and travel reimbursement. The effective rate usually lands at $55–$75/hour — far above staff pay. Run any offer through our travel nurse pay analyzer before signing.

Choosing an Agency

Compare at least 3 agencies. Ask about: stipend vs. provided housing, cancellation policies, guaranteed hours, health insurance start date, 401(k) match, and whether they cover licensing costs for new states. The biggest agencies aren't always the best payers — smaller ones sometimes offer higher stipends.

Is Travel Nursing Right for You?

It's ideal if you: adapt quickly, enjoy new cities, and want maximum pay flexibility. It's harder if you: need stability, have young kids in school, or struggle with first-day nerves. Many nurses travel for 2–3 years, bank the premium pay, then settle into a staff role.

Note: Tax rules for stipends are complex — consult a tax professional familiar with travel healthcare workers. Pay figures are 2026 estimates.
