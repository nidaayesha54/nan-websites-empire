15 Highest Paying Nursing Specialties in 2026 (Ranked by Salary)

Not all nursing jobs pay the same. While the average RN earns about $101,000, the right specialty — or advanced degree — can double or triple that. Here are the top-paying nursing roles in 2026, based on BLS data and published salary surveys.

The Top 5

1. Certified Registered Nurse Anesthetist (CRNA) — ~$248,000

CRNAs administer anesthesia and are the highest-paid nurses by a wide margin. Requires a BSN, 1+ years of ICU experience, then a 3-year doctoral (DNP/DNAP) program. The investment is steep (~$150,000 in tuition), but the payoff is unmatched.

2. Nurse Practitioner (NP) — ~$132,000

NPs diagnose, prescribe, and manage patient care — in many states practicing independently. Requires an MSN or DNP (6–7 years total). Specialties like psychiatric-mental health NP and acute care NP pay toward the top of the range.

3. Nurse Midwife (CNM) — ~$128,000

Certified nurse-midwives deliver babies and provide women's health care. Requires an MSN in midwifery. Deeply rewarding work with excellent pay.

4. Clinical Nurse Specialist (CNS) — ~$120,000

CNSs are expert clinicians who also improve systems and mentor staff. Requires an MSN or DNP.

5. Nurse Manager / Director — ~$115,000

Leadership pays. Nurse managers oversee units; directors oversee departments. Usually requires a BSN plus experience, with MSN preferred.

How to Maximize Your Nursing Pay

  • Get certified — specialty certifications (CCRN, CEN, OCN) typically add 5–10%.
  • Work nights/weekends — differentials of $4–$10/hour are common.
  • Consider travel nursing — packages worth $95,000–$150,000+/year. Analyze offers with our travel pay analyzer.
  • Move states — California RNs earn roughly double South Dakota RNs. Check your state with our salary calculator.
