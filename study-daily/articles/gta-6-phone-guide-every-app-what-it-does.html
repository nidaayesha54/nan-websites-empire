GTA 6 Phone Guide: Every App and What It Does

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and the most-used object in the game is not a gun or a car — it is the phone. Jason and Lucia carry an iFruit, the series' long-running parody of a smartphone, and in this game it is a physical device they pull out of their pockets and use in person, taking over the whole screen. It holds your messages, your money, your rides, your photos and your car scanner, and new apps join it as the story moves on. This guide explains every app, when each one arrives, and what the phone cannot do.

How to open the phone

Press Up on the D-pad to take the phone out, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S alike. The home screen shows around ten apps at the start, with widgets, a custom wallpaper and ringtones, according to Frandroid, a French site that reviews real smartphones and tested the in-game handset on launch day. Nine apps are there from the first time you open it: WhatUp for messages, Contacts for calls, Camera for photos, Gallery for the photos you have taken, Snapmatic for the social feed, Fitness for workouts and sleep, plus News, Weather and Settings.

The rest arrive with the story. BuckMe, the banking app, comes from the Chapter 1 mission Buck Me, late in the chapter, when Jason and Lucia end up with the app instead of a normal bank account. WAiNK, the car scanner, is in use by the Chapter 2 mission Moses. Scooter Bros, the car delivery service, arrives in Chapter 2 once Moses starts registering stolen cars for you. Vice City Real Estate joins later too, and RydeMe, the ride-hailing app, arrives as the story reaches it. If an app you have read about is not on your phone yet, the answer is almost always the same: keep playing the story.

WhatUp and Contacts: messages and calls

WhatUp is the messaging app, a parody of WhatsApp, and it is where the story reaches you. Story contacts and side characters text you here, and the old texts already on the phone when you first open it are worth a read — they fill in backstory the cutscenes skip. Contacts is the only way to make a call: pick a name and call them. There is no keypad, so there is no 911, no calling the police or an ambulance to a crash, and no ringing random numbers. If you want to talk to someone, they have to be in your contacts first.

A few contacts are worth calling on purpose: Joey at Coastal Auto Care has someone deliver Jason's car to you, repaired, at no charge, after the Just a Scratch mission, and Paradise Taxi sends a cab to a destination you pick. The contacts list fills up as the story introduces people, so check it after every chapter.

BuckMe, RydeMe and Scooter Bros: money and movement

BuckMe is your bank account, and it is the most important app on the phone. Money in BuckMe is safe when you die or get busted — cash on your person is not — so depositing after every job is the single best habit in the game. Shops take pocket cash only up to $999, and the rest of a purchase comes out of BuckMe, and the app has an ATM finder to locate a nearby deposit point. The $20,000 pocket cap means you cannot carry a fortune anyway; the bank is where the fortune lives.

RydeMe is the ride-hailing app, a parody of Uber and Lyft, and it is the game's fast travel: book a ride to your waypoint, pay the fare, and extra cash skips the drive. Scooter Bros is the courier service: for a small fee, it has one of your registered cars delivered to you, which is the answer to the oldest GTA problem — your good car is three miles away and you are on foot.

WAiNK, Camera, Snapmatic and Fitness

WAiNK is the scanner: point the phone camera at a parked car and it reports the locks, the alarm, the tracker, the condition, the resale value and the cost to register it. It reports what a car is worth before you steal it. The Camera app takes quick photos and selfies — 5x zoom, a composition grid, filters and posed selfies — and saves them to the Gallery. For careful shots, the separate Photo Studio in the pause menu is the better tool.

Snapmatic is the social feed: a vertical feed of other people's posts, videos and comments, which you can browse, like, and mark on your map. What you cannot do is post — Jason and Lucia are passive on social media, and Rockstar has said they do not upload photos, because of their digital footprint. Fitness tracks food, workouts, sleep and weight, and warns you when you are about to gain weight — it feeds the same body system as the gyms and the Strength, Cardio and Focus stats. Headphones and earbuds let the radio and on-demand podcasts play on foot too.

What the phone cannot do

Three limits are worth knowing. First, there is no web browser on the phone — the home screen has no browser app, so the internet in GTA 6 is the social feed and the messages, not the web. Second, there is no keypad and no 911, so you cannot call for help in an emergency the way you could in older games. Third, you cannot post on Snapmatic, so the social feed is something you watch, not something you feed. None of those are bugs. They are the game's design: Jason and Lucia are two people trying not to be found, and a phone that broadcasts their life would be the worst tool they could carry.

FAQ

How do you open the phone in GTA 6? Press Up on the D-pad. The home screen shows around ten apps at the start, with more — BuckMe, WAiNK, Scooter Bros, Vice City Real Estate and RydeMe — arriving as the story reaches them.

Can you call 911 in GTA 6? No. The phone has no keypad, and Contacts is the only way to place a call, so there is no 911 and no calling the police or an ambulance.

Can you post on Snapmatic in GTA 6? No. You can browse the feed, like posts and mark locations, but Jason and Lucia do not post photos.

The bottom line: the GTA 6 phone is your bank, your taxi, your car scanner and your camera in one device. Open it with Up on the D-pad, bank your cash in BuckMe after every job, and keep playing the story — the best apps are the ones it gives you later.
