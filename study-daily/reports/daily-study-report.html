🧠 Study Technique of the Day: The Spacing Effect

One of the most replicated findings in learning science: spacing your study sessions beats cramming — often by a wide margin. In a landmark UCLA analysis, students who spaced practice over days retained up to twice as much as those who massed the same study time into one session.

How to apply it today

  1. Split, don't stack. Take the 3 hours you'd spend cramming Sunday night and split them into three 1-hour sessions across Friday, Saturday and Sunday.
  2. Use expanding intervals. Review new material after 1 day, then 3 days, then 7 days. Each successful recall strengthens the memory more than the last.
  3. Pair it with active recall. Spacing works best when each session forces retrieval — close the book and write what you remember before re-reading.

✅ Exam Season Tips

Midterm season is approaching on most US/UK academic calendars. Three high-leverage moves this week:

  • Build your "one-pager" now. Condense each subject to a single page of the hardest concepts. The act of condensing is the studying.
  • Do past papers under timed conditions. Familiarity with format reduces exam-day anxiety more than extra content review.
  • Protect your sleep. An extra hour of sleep beats an extra hour of midnight review — memory consolidation happens while you sleep.
