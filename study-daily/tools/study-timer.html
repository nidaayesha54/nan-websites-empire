Why the Pomodoro Technique works

Developed by Francesco Cirillo in the 1980s, the Pomodoro Technique breaks study into focused 25-minute sprints separated by short breaks. Research on attention shows that focused work in bounded intervals reduces procrastination and mental fatigue compared to open-ended study marathons.

Tips for best results

  • One task per sprint. Decide what you'll work on before the timer starts.
  • Protect the sprint. Silence notifications; a single interruption can cost 15+ minutes of refocus time.
  • Use breaks properly. Stand up, stretch, get water — don't scroll social media, which drains the same attention you just built.
