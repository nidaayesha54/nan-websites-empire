Ultimate Exam Preparation Guide — The 4-Week Study System

Study Daily Reports · Updated October 2026 · 10 min read

Top students don't study more — they study on a system. This is the complete 4-week exam preparation framework: what to do each week, which techniques to use when, and how to walk into the exam hall calm and ready. It works for high school finals, university midterms and professional certifications alike.

Week 1–2: Close the Gaps

Goal: learn what you don't know yet. Most students waste this phase re-reading what they already understand.

  1. Audit every subject. List all topics; mark each red (weak), yellow (shaky) or green (solid). Spend 70% of time on reds.
  2. Learn with active recall. For each red topic: read once, close the book, write everything you remember. Check, repeat. See our study techniques guide for the full method.
  3. Build "one-pagers." Condense each subject's hardest concepts onto a single page. The condensing is the learning.
  4. Space it out. Hit each subject 3–4 times across the two weeks, not in one block. Spacing doubles long-term retention.

Week 3: Practice Under Pressure

Goal: convert knowledge into exam performance.

  1. Past papers, timed, no notes. Do at least 3 full past papers per subject under real time limits. This is the highest-value activity of the entire month.
  2. Mark harshly. Use official mark schemes. Every lost mark reveals a gap — log them and drill those topics.
  3. Build an error log. A running list of every mistake and its correction. Review it — not your notes — in the final days.
  4. Interleave subjects. Alternate subjects within a day to train your brain to switch gears, exactly like the exam timetable demands.

Week 4: Consolidate and Sharpen

Goal: peak at the right time, not burn out.

  1. Review one-pagers and error logs only. No new textbooks. If it's not on your one-pager by now, it won't stick.
  2. One final timed paper per subject early in the week — then stop. Confidence matters more than one more drill.
  3. Taper study hours. Reduce volume by ~30% in the last 3 days. Your brain consolidates during rest.
  4. Fix logistics. Know the venue, pack your bag, set two alarms, plan food and water.

The Night Before & Exam Day

  • Night before: light review of one-pagers only (max 1 hour), then stop. Sleep 7–9 hours — an hour of sleep beats an hour of midnight review.
  • Morning: normal breakfast, arrive early, avoid last-minute quizzing with anxious classmates.
  • In the exam: read all instructions first; budget time per mark; answer easy questions first to bank confidence; if stuck for 2 minutes, move on and return later.

Know your numbers

Use our free grade tracker to see exactly what score you need on each exam to hit your target grade — then allocate study hours proportionally. A 40%-weighted final deserves twice the prep of a 20% one.

The golden rule: weeks 1–2 = learn gaps · week 3 = timed practice · week 4 = consolidate. Never invert the order — practice without knowledge is guessing, and knowledge without practice is fragile.

FAQ

How far in advance should I start studying for exams?
Four weeks is ideal for major exams: weeks 1–2 for learning gaps, week 3 for practice papers, week 4 for review and consolidation. Spaced study beats cramming every time.
How many hours a day should I study for exams?
4–6 focused hours beats 10 distracted ones. Use 25–50 minute Pomodoro sprints with real breaks — try our free study timer.
What should I do the night before an exam?
Light review only, then sleep. Pack your bag, set two alarms, and avoid learning new material — memory consolidation happens during sleep.
