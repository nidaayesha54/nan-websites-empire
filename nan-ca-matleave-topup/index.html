How EI Maternity & Parental Benefits Work (2026)

Outside Quebec, parental benefits are paid through federal Employment Insurance (EI). The basic rate is 55% of your average insurable weekly earnings, capped at $729/week for claims starting in 2026. If you choose the extended option, the rate is 33% capped at $437/week — spread over more weeks, for a similar total payout.

2026 key numbers: Maximum insurable earnings $68,900 · Weekly max $729 (standard) / $437 (extended) · Maternity: up to 15 weeks (birth parent only) · Standard parental: 35 weeks per parent (40 shared) · Extended parental: 61 weeks per parent (69 shared) · Eligibility: 600 insurable hours in the last 52 weeks. Quebec residents use the QPIP system instead, with different rates and durations.

What Is an Employer Top-Up (SUB Plan)?

Many employers offer a Supplemental Unemployment Benefit (SUB) plan — a "top-up" that raises your weekly income to a percentage of your normal salary, typically 80–100% for 17–26 weeks. Common structures:

  • Federal government & Ontario public sector: ~93% of salary, up to 52 weeks (federal) or 17–52 weeks (Ontario public).
  • Large banks: often 100% of salary for 17–26 weeks.
  • Tech companies: typically 80–100% for 12–26 weeks.
  • Small/medium businesses: varies widely; many offer no top-up.

A properly structured top-up is not deducted from your EI benefits — but the combined EI + top-up cannot exceed 100% of your normal weekly earnings. Ask your employer: (1) how the top-up is calculated, (2) how many weeks it runs, and (3) whether it's tied to the 55% standard rate (some plans pay less — or nothing — if you choose the extended 33% option). Get any top-up agreement in writing before your leave begins.

Standard vs Extended: Which Pays More?

Over the full leave, both options pay a similar total amount — the extended option just spreads it over more weeks at a lower weekly rate. Example at a $60,000 salary (birth parent, full claim): standard pays about $31,115 total (~$2,593/month), extended about $32,131 total (~$1,691/month) — figures are gross, before tax. Choose standard if you need higher monthly income or your top-up only covers 12 months; choose extended if you have savings to bridge the gap and want more time at home. Once a payment is made, you cannot switch options.

FAQs

Do employer top-ups reduce my EI payments?

No — a properly registered SUB top-up is not deducted from your EI benefits, as long as EI + top-up together don't exceed 100% of your normal weekly earnings.

What is the EI waiting period?

Normally a 1-week unpaid waiting period applies at the start of a claim — it delays your first payment but doesn't reduce your total benefit weeks. Temporary waivers have applied during 2025–2026; check the current Service Canada rules when you apply.

Who qualifies for EI maternity and parental benefits?

Employees who accumulated at least 600 insurable hours in the last 52 weeks (or since their last claim) and whose weekly earnings dropped by more than 40% for at least one week. Self-employed workers can qualify if they opted into EI at least 12 months before applying.

Does this apply in Quebec?

No — Quebec parents are covered by the Quebec Parental Insurance Plan (QPIP), which has its own rates, plans, and maximum insurable earnings ($103,000 for 2026). Use the official QPIP benefit simulator for Quebec estimates.

Are EI maternity and parental benefits taxable?

Yes — EI benefits are taxable income, and tax is usually deducted at source. The figures in this calculator are gross (before tax).

Can both parents receive benefits?

Yes. Parental benefits can be shared. Sharing unlocks 5 extra weeks (standard) or 8 extra weeks (extended) that are lost if only one parent claims. Maternity benefits (15 weeks) are for the birth parent only and cannot be shared.
