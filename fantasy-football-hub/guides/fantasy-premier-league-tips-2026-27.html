Fantasy Premier League Tips 2026/27: The Complete Playbook

Last updated: October 2026. Whether you're playing your first season or chasing your tenth top-10k finish, these Fantasy Premier League tips for 2026/27 cover everything: how to structure your £100m squad, when to trust the template and when to go differential, how to plan transfers, and how to time your chips. Bookmark this page and revisit it before every gameweek deadline.

1. Get your squad structure right first

Before you pick a single player, decide how your £100m is split. The proven structures in FPL are: 3-5-2 or 3-4-3 with premium-heavy midfields (midfielders score bonus points more reliably), or a balanced 4-4-2 when clean sheets look predictable. Avoid spending more than £4.5m on a second goalkeeper who will never play — that's dead money sitting on your bench. Read our full breakdown in Fantasy Football Squad Structure Explained, and test structures in our free team planner.

2. Template picks vs. differentials

Template picks are the highly-owned players everyone has. Early in the season, own them — going against the crowd is how you drop 100k ranks in a single gameweek. Differentials (players under ~10% ownership) are for chasing: use them when you need to gain ground in the final third of the season or to break a mini-league tie. The rule of thumb: build a template core of 8–10 players, then add 2–3 differentials with great fixtures. Compare any two candidates with our player comparison tool.

3. Captaincy is half the game

The captain scores double points, which makes it the single highest-leverage decision each gameweek. Prioritise: an attacking player, playing at home, against a weak defence, who takes penalties and plays the full 90. The detailed 5-rule framework is in our guide on how to pick a fantasy football captain.

4. Plan transfers, don't chase points

  • Roll transfers when you can. You get one free transfer per gameweek and can bank up to five — having two banked gives you flexibility for injuries and fixture swings.
  • Never take a points hit for a sideways move. A −4 hit needs roughly a 4-point swing to break even after price changes. Save hits for genuine emergencies (injuries, suspensions, double gameweeks).
  • Buy before the price rise, sell before the drop. Price changes reward early movers — but never sacrifice your planned transfer strategy just to chase £0.1m.

5. Use the fixture calendar as your map

Fantasy is won in the fixtures. Target teams with a run of 3+ easy games (home vs. bottom-half sides) and rotate out players facing a brutal stretch. Our fixture difficulty tracker lets you rate fixtures 1–5 stars and instantly highlights the easiest upcoming runs for your rotation planner.

6. Time your chips for the big weeks

You get one Wildcard (squad rebuild), one Free Hit (one-week squad), one Bench Boost (all 15 players score) and one Triple Captain (captain scores 3×). The golden rule: save Bench Boost and Triple Captain for double gameweeks, and use the Wildcard right before one to set up. The full timing breakdown is in our fantasy football chip strategy guide.

7. Mini-league tactics

In head-to-head or mini-league play, block your rivals: own their captains so their hauls can't hurt you, then differentiate with one or two unique picks in weeks you need to gain ground. If you're leading late in the season, mirror the chaser's team — safety first.

2026/27 pre-season checklist

  • ✅ £100m squad built with a sensible structure (use the planner)
  • ✅ Captain and vice-captain set every single gameweek
  • ✅ One free transfer banked before the first international break
  • ✅ All four chips held for double gameweeks
  • ✅ Fixture runs mapped for the next 6–8 weeks (track them here)
