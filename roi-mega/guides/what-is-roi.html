What Is ROI and How to Calculate It

The complete beginner-friendly guide to return on investment — with real examples.

The ROI Formula

ROI % = (Gain − Investment Cost) ÷ Investment Cost × 100

ROI (Return on Investment) answers one question: for every dollar you put in, how much did you get back? If you invest $1,000 and receive $1,250, your profit is $250 and your ROI is 25%.

Worked Example

You buy stock for $5,000, sell it later for $6,200, and paid $30 in fees:

  • Gain = $6,200 − $30 = $6,170
  • Cost = $5,000 + $30 = $5,030
  • ROI = ($6,170 − $5,030) ÷ $5,030 × 100 = 22.7%

Annualized ROI: Comparing Different Time Periods

Annualized ROI = (1 + ROI)^(1 ÷ Years) − 1

A 50% return over 5 years (~8.4%/year) is very different from 50% in 6 months. Annualizing lets you compare investments fairly. Our General ROI Calculator does this automatically.

Common ROI Mistakes

  • Forgetting hidden costs — fees, taxes, holding costs and selling commissions all reduce ROI.
  • Ignoring time — 20% in one year beats 30% in three years. Always annualize.
  • Confusing revenue with profit — ROAS of 3x sounds great until product costs leave you with nothing.
  • Not counting your time — a flip that "made" $20,000 over 400 hours of your labor paid $50/hour.
