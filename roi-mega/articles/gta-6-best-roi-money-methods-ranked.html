GTA 6 Best ROI: The Money Methods That Pay Back Fastest

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and within a day the question every player was asking was the same: what pays best? The story keeps putting a price on progress. Missions stay locked until you have the cash, garages cost money, and the good gear is never cheap. So the return on your time matters more than the thrill of any single job, and not every money method in Leonida is worth the same hour.

This guide ranks the money methods by reported payout, from the biggest vault jobs down to the steady early earners. Every figure here comes from launch-week reporting by PowerPyx, quoted through EGamers, and none of it has been measured by us — treat the numbers as reported, not guaranteed. What matters is the order: which methods pay the most, when each one unlocks, and what each one risks.

1. Large bank vaults: the biggest reported payout

The top of the list is the large bank vault job, reported to pay up to $205,000 for roughly five minutes of work. It only opens at the end of Chapter 4, through an optional mission called That's Where The Money Is, and it starts with tailing a security van until it parks at a bank. A guard carries a briefcase into the vault, the door stays open briefly, and that is your window. Inside you loot the vault, the cash drawer and the safe, and you walk out with a four-star wanted level straight away.

That payout is the headline, but the return is not free money. The job needs the whole story behind you, a plan, and a clean getaway under heavy police pressure. It is the best return in the game by the numbers, and it is also the one most players will reach last. If you are still in Chapter 2, it is a goal to work toward, not a method you can use today.

2. Supercars and small banks: the mid-game earners

Second on the reported list is selling supercars to a buyer called Randy, at $30,000 or more per car in perfect condition. It opens around the middle of Chapter 3, after a mission called Key Cloner, and the catch is the condition: a damaged supercar pays far less, and advanced trackers alert the police once you slow down. Drive it clean, deliver it whole, and the payout is real money for a single delivery.

Small banks sit just below that, reported at roughly $5,000 to $45,000 each, with fourteen of them across the map. They open in Chapter 3 after a mission called Credit Check, and the rule is simple: loot both the cash drawer and the safe, or the job does not count. Jewelry stores pay a similar range, $10,000 to $25,000, once Yasmani's jewelry jobs open in Chapter 3 — disable the security system, crack the back-room safe, and leave before the police arrive.

3. Deliveries and payday loans: steady, repeatable cash

Pilot Pete deliveries pay a reported $20,000 per run, seven packages on a timer, and they open in Chapter 4 as a Strangers and Freaks activity for Jason only. They are not the biggest payout, but they are repeatable, low-drama work: fly the route, drop the packages, get paid. For a player who wants steady income without a four-star chase every time, deliveries are the honest middle of the list.

Payday loan stores pay a reported $2,000 to $25,000 depending on the store, with seven of them to hit, opening in Chapter 3 after Credit Check. Smash 'n' grabs — pawn shops and jewelers, thirty-five of them — pay about $1,000 to $25,000 depending on the shop, but the same rule applies as the banks: take the safe as well, or the location may not count toward your progress.

4. Stolen cars: the early-game engine

Before any of that opens, stolen cars are the engine. Selling a stolen car to Moses typically pays $1,000 or more per car from Chapter 2, after the Moses mission, with a cooldown of about two minutes between sales — and no trackers accepted, so only clean cars qualify. Selling a good car at Pay 'n' Spray pays around $4,000 or more, but only from early Chapter 3, and only cars with standard trackers.

The return here is lower per job, but the jobs are available from the start and repeat without limit. Early on, the best return on your time is not a single big score — it is a loop: steal, sell, deposit, repeat. Bank the cash at an ATM after every run, because cash on your person can be lost when you die or get caught, and the bank balance is the only money that is truly yours.

What never pays back

Gang operations — thirty-nine of them — pay in narcotics sold to a fence, and the payout is not sourced in the reporting we have, so do not plan around a number nobody has published. The same goes for the lists promising forty buyable properties with daily income: nothing Rockstar has published backs those numbers, and only garages are buyable in story mode. A method that pays you back is one you can measure. Chase the measured ones first, and let the rumours wait until someone proves them.

FAQ

What is the best way to make money in GTA 6? By reported payout, large bank vaults at up to $205,000, then supercars to Randy at $30,000 or more, then small banks and jewelry stores. All of those open late — early on, stolen cars to Moses are the steady earner.

When do the big payouts unlock? Small banks, jewelry stores and payday loans open in Chapter 3. Large vaults wait until the end of Chapter 4. Pilot Pete deliveries are a Chapter 4 Strangers and Freaks activity.

The bottom line: the best return in GTA 6 is the big vault job at the end of Chapter 4, but the best return on your time right now is whatever is open to you — steal, sell, deposit, repeat, and let the big scores come when the story does.
