How Much Life Insurance Do I Need in 2026?

Ask ten agents and you'll get ten answers. The most trusted formula among financial planners is the DIME method: add up your Debts, Income replacement, Mortgage, and Education costs — then subtract liquid assets and existing coverage.

The DIME Method, Step by Step

D — Debts: Everything except the mortgage: credit cards, car loans, student loans, personal loans. Add the full balances.

I — Income replacement: Your annual income × the number of years your family would need support (often 10–15 years for young families, fewer near retirement).

M — Mortgage: The remaining balance, so your family keeps the house.

E — Education: Future college costs per child (a public 4-year degree averaged ~$100K+ in 2026).

Then subtract: savings, investments, and any life insurance you already own.

A Real Example

Sarah, 34, earns $80,000/year with a $280,000 mortgage, $20,000 in debts, two young kids, and $60,000 in savings:

  • Debts: $20,000
  • Income: $80,000 × 15 years = $1,200,000
  • Mortgage: $280,000
  • Education: $200,000
  • Minus savings: −$60,000
  • Total need: ~$1,640,000

The old "10× income" rule would have suggested $800,000 — barely half. This is why DIME matters.

Term vs. Whole Life?

For most families, a 20–30 year term policy is the right tool: cheap, simple, and covers the years your family depends on your income. A healthy 35-year-old can often get $1M of 20-year term for roughly $40–60/month in 2026.
