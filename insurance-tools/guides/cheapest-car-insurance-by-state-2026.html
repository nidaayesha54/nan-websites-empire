Cheapest Car Insurance by State (2026)

The average American pays roughly $2,300/year for full coverage in 2026 — but where you live changes everything. Drivers in Michigan can pay more than double what drivers in Vermont pay for the same coverage.

Cheapest States for Car Insurance

States with the lowest average full-coverage premiums include Vermont, Maine, Iowa, Ohio, and Virginia — typically $1,200–$1,700/year. Common traits: lower population density, fewer uninsured drivers, and milder weather claims.

Most Expensive States

Michigan, Florida, Louisiana, New York, and Nevada top the expensive list, often $3,000–$4,500+/year. Michigan's historically unlimited no-fault medical benefits, Florida's fraud and storm claims, and Louisiana's litigation rates drive costs up.

7 Proven Ways to Lower Your Premium

  1. Shop 3–5 quotes every year. Insurers price the same driver hundreds of dollars apart. Loyalty rarely pays.
  2. Raise your deductible from $250 to $1,000 — often saves 15–25%.
  3. Bundle auto + home/renters with one insurer (typically 10–20% off).
  4. Keep a clean record. One at-fault accident can raise rates ~45% for 3 years.
  5. Ask about discounts: good student, defensive driving course, low mileage, telematics/usage-based programs.
  6. Improve your credit tier where allowed — most states let insurers use credit-based scores.
  7. Drop full coverage on old cars. If the car's value is under ~$4,000, collision/comprehensive may cost more than they're worth.
