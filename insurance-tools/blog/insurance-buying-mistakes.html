7 Mistakes People Make When Buying Insurance

Insurance is one of the biggest household expenses — and most people overpay or mis-buy. Here are the seven costliest errors.

1. Insuring small risks, skipping big ones

People buy phone insurance ($15/month to protect a $800 phone) but skip disability insurance — even though a 30-year-old is far more likely to become disabled than to die before retirement. Insure catastrophes, self-insure annoyances.

2. Never re-shopping quotes

Insurers raise rates on loyal customers ("price optimization"). Shopping 3–5 quotes every 1–2 years routinely saves $300–600/year on auto insurance alone.

3. Choosing deductibles emotionally

A $250 deductible feels safe but can cost 25% more in premiums than a $1,000 deductible. If you have an emergency fund, take the higher deductible.

4. Buying whole life when term would do

Paying 15× more for permanent coverage you don't need is the most expensive mistake in life insurance. (See our term vs whole life guide.)

5. Underinsuring liability

State-minimum auto liability ($25K in many states) won't cover a serious accident. One lawsuit can wipe out savings. $100K/$300K is the sensible floor for most drivers.

6. Forgetting the "break-even" question

Extended warranties, pet insurance, travel insurance — run the expected-value math first with our break-even analyzer.

7. Not reading exclusions

Flood isn't in standard homeowners. Earthquakes usually aren't either. The cheapest policy is worthless if it excludes your actual risk.
