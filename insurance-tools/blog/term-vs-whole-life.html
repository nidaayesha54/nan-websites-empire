Term vs Whole Life Insurance: Which Is Right for You?

This is the most argued-over question in life insurance — and agents have a conflict of interest (whole life pays them far bigger commissions). Here's the straight answer.

Term Life: Simple and Cheap

Term covers you for a set period (10, 20, 30 years). If you die during the term, it pays. If not, it expires worthless — like car insurance. A healthy 35-year-old pays roughly $40–60/month for $1M of 20-year term in 2026.

Whole Life: Expensive and Permanent

Whole life covers you forever and builds "cash value." The same $1M policy might cost $800–1,200/month — 15–20× more. The cash value grows slowly and you often borrow against your own money to access it.

Who Is Each For?

  • Term: ~95% of families. You need big coverage during the years people depend on your income — then you don't.
  • Whole life: rare cases — estate planning for very large estates, or special-needs dependents requiring lifelong support.

The "Buy Term and Invest the Difference" Argument

Take the $750+/month you'd save with term and invest it. Over 30 years at 7% returns, that's potentially $900,000+ — usually far more than a whole life policy's cash value.
