How to Make AI Product Videos in 2026: The Complete Workflow

10 min read · Last updated October 2026

AI product videos — demos, unboxings, and ads built with AI avatars, voiceovers, and generative footage — have gone from novelty to standard marketing practice. In this guide, you'll learn the exact workflow brands use to go from idea to finished AI product video in a single day, without a camera crew.

Step 1: Write a conversion-focused script

Every high-performing product video follows the same skeleton: Hook → Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA. The hook (first 3 seconds) decides everything — 65%+ of viewers who pass the 3-second mark watch to the end.

  • Hook (0–5s): Name the problem or tease the transformation. Pattern-interrupt visuals work best.
  • Problem/Agitate (5–20s): Show the pain, then the cost of ignoring it.
  • Solution (20–45s): Introduce the product and demo 2–3 features — no more.
  • Proof (45–55s): Reviews, before/after, or a guarantee.
  • CTA (55–60s): One clear action. Never two.

Step 2: Plan your shots

A shot list is what separates amateur AI videos from professional ones. For a 60-second video you need roughly 8–12 shots. Mix framing: extreme close-ups for emotion, macro shots for product detail, and wide shots for context.

For each shot, note the framing, camera movement, duration, on-screen text, and — critically for AI generation — a text prompt you can paste into your video tool. Our shot list generator does all of this from your script automatically.

Step 3: Generate the footage with AI

Here's where 2026 tools shine. Match the tool to the shot:

  • Presenter / talking head: AI avatar tools (HeyGen, Synthesia) — upload your script, pick an avatar, render.
  • Product b-roll: Text-to-video tools (Runway, Kling, Veo) — paste your shot-list AI prompts.
  • UGC-style clips: CapCut Commerce Pro and similar ad tools generate creator-style footage from product links.
  • Product-on-white / packshots: Image-to-video: generate a clean product image first, then animate it.

Pro tip: generate each shot individually (5–10s clips) rather than one long video. You'll get far better consistency and control in editing.

Step 4: Add voiceover & captions

Good audio matters more than good visuals. Options ranked by quality:

  1. Record yourself reading the voiceover transcript — always the most authentic for UGC-style ads.
  2. AI voiceover (ElevenLabs, PlayHT, or the voices built into avatar tools) — pick one voice and use it across all your brand's videos for consistency.
  3. On-screen text only — works for ASMR/unboxing formats where talking would break the vibe.

Always burn in captions: 80%+ of social video is watched muted, and captioned videos hold attention measurably longer. CapCut's auto-captions + manual corrections is the fastest path.

Step 5: Edit, brand & publish

Assemble in CapCut, Premiere, or Descript. Keep cuts every 2–4 seconds, add your logo watermark, and export in the right aspect ratio: 9:16 for TikTok/Reels/Shorts, 16:9 for YouTube and landing pages, 1:1 for feed ads.

Publishing checklist:

  • Hook in the first frame (don't bury it under a logo intro)
  • Captions on, music at −20dB under voice
  • One CTA, on-screen, for the last 5 seconds
  • Post natively to each platform — never repost with another platform's watermark

7 mistakes to avoid

  1. Skipping the script. AI footage can't save a weak message. Script first, always.
  2. Too many features. One video = one core message. Demo 2–3 features max.
  3. Generic AI voice on every video. Pick one brand voice and stick to it.
  4. Ignoring the first 3 seconds. No logo intros. Start mid-action or mid-sentence.
  5. No captions. Muted viewers scroll past silent-looking videos.
  6. Uncanny avatar faces. If an AI avatar looks "off," switch to UGC-style or product-only shots.
  7. Publishing once and stopping. Make 3–5 hook variants of every winner — hooks are 80% of ad performance.
