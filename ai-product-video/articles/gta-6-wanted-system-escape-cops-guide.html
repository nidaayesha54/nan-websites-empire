GTA 6 Wanted System: How to Escape the Cops

November 25, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, thirteen years after GTA 5, and IGN's review calls the game a masterpiece. One of the biggest changes hiding inside that praise is easy to miss: the wanted system has been rebuilt. In GTA 6, the police act only on what they actually witness. There is no magic GPS, no instant knowledge of where you are. If no cop — and no witness — sees what you did, the law does not magically find you. That single change rewrites how chases work in Vice City, and this guide explains exactly what it means for the way you play.

How the new wanted system works

What IGN's review showed is a system built on line of sight. Police respond to crimes they witness, or crimes that witnesses report — they do not simply know where you are the moment you break the law. If you commit a crime where no one can see you, the response is different from committing the same crime in front of a patrol car. The game is simulating observation: the police have eyes, and they have limits, just like real police do. A quiet street at night is a different place from a crowded boulevard at noon, and the game treats it that way.

Witnesses are the real danger

The most dangerous person in the new system is not always the cop — it is the bystander. A civilian who watches you commit a crime can report it, and that report brings the police to where you were, not where you are. The game world includes an in-game phone and social media layer, so word travels fast once someone sees something. That means the seconds after a crime matter: a witness standing there with a phone is a problem that a fast getaway alone does not solve. The practical lesson is to think about witnesses before the crime, not after it. A quiet approach, a less crowded route, and a quick exit all reduce the number of people who can describe what you did — and fewer witnesses means a slower, weaker response.

Breaking line of sight

When the police are after you, the core of the escape is breaking line of sight. Get out of view, and stay out of view — the search is local, not omniscient. Ducking down a side street, slipping into an alley, or putting a building between you and the patrol changes the situation in a way it never quite did in older games. The police are searching an area because they saw you there, not because a marker on the map is telling them exactly where you are. And distance matters: the further you get from where you were seen, the weaker the search becomes. The old advice of "just drive fast" is less useful now than the new advice: "stop being seen."

Change vehicles, change streets

Changing vehicles helps for the same reason breaking line of sight does. The police are looking for what they saw — the car, the clothes, the direction you were heading. A different car, a different street, and a few blocks of distance can turn a hot pursuit into a cold search. Vice City rewards this: its grid of side streets, alleys, and parking garages gives you options that a straight highway never will. If the chase is on a main road, take the first turn that gets you out of view; if you can swap cars while you are out of sight, do it, and drive away calmly instead of speeding. A calm car on a quiet street is invisible in a way a speeding car on a boulevard never is.

Time of day and crowd change everything

That is why the time of day and the crowd around you matter more than they used to. A busy Vice City street has dozens of potential witnesses; an empty road in the Leonida countryside has none. Before a risky job, it is worth looking around: who is watching, where are the patrols, and is there a quieter way to do the same thing? The players who treat the world as full of eyes will stay out of trouble far more often than the ones who treat it as empty. The same job done at 3 AM on a quiet street and at noon on Ocean Drive are two different jobs, with two different consequences.

What not to do

Do not assume you are safe just because no police car is on screen. A witness may have seen you, and the response may be on its way. Do not hang around the scene after a crime — the longer you stay where you were seen, the easier you are to find. Do not assume the chase is over the moment the sirens fade: if you stay in the same area, a new witness can start the whole thing again. And above all, do not play the new system with old habits. Charging through Vice City in daylight, in front of everyone, is the fastest way to learn how the system works — from inside a police chase, with your pocket cash at risk.

Frequently asked questions

Is there still a wanted level in GTA 6? Yes — the police still respond to crime, and the response still escalates. No full breakdown of the levels has been published. What IGN's review confirmed is the principle: the police act on what they witness, with no magic GPS.

Can I lose the cops just by hiding? Breaking line of sight and leaving the area is the core of it. The police search where they saw you, not where you are — so hiding, changing vehicles, and putting distance between you and the scene all help. Staying in the same area, even hidden, is the mistake: the search is local, but it is thorough.

Do witnesses call the police? Yes — a civilian who sees a crime can report it, and the game's in-game phone and social media layer means word travels. If you are spotted, break line of sight and leave the area quickly, before the report turns into a response.
