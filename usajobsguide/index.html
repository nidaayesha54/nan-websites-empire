How to Apply

Five steps to land your government job.

Create Your USAJOBS Profile

Register at USAJOBS.gov and complete your profile — personal information, work experience, education and hiring-path eligibility (veteran, student, military spouse).

Use the Resume Builder

The built-in Resume Builder formats your resume to federal standards automatically, so you never miss required details like hours per week or supervisor contact info.

Filter by GS Grade and Hiring Path

Use search filters for pay grade, location and who may apply. Announcements open to 'the public' accept all U.S. citizens; others are restricted to current feds or special groups.

Upload Required Documents

Most announcements need transcripts, a federal resume and proof of eligibility (DD-214, SF-50, Schedule A letter). Missing documents are the #1 reason applications are rejected.

Track Your Application Status

USAJOBS shows statuses like 'Received', 'Referred' and 'Selected'. Referred means your resume reached the hiring manager — the hardest step is behind you.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about government hiring.

What is USAJOBS.gov?

USAJOBS.gov is the official U.S. government job board where every federal agency posts openings. It is the only legitimate place to apply for federal jobs — never pay anyone to apply for you.

How is a federal resume different?

Federal resumes include details private resumes skip: hours worked per week, exact start and end dates, supervisor names and phone numbers, and salary for each role. They often run 3 to 5 pages.

What do USAJOBS application statuses mean?

'Received' means your application was submitted. 'Referred' means you met qualifications and your resume went to the hiring manager. 'Selected' or 'Not Selected' is the final decision.

What are hiring paths?

Hiring paths define who may apply — such as 'public' (all U.S. citizens), 'federal employees', 'veterans', 'students' or 'military spouses'. Read the 'Who may apply' section before applying.

How do I qualify for a GS grade?

Each announcement lists specialized experience requirements per grade — typically one year of experience at the next lower grade, or qualifying education such as a master's degree for GS-9.

Is USAJOBS free to use?

Yes — creating a profile, building a resume and applying on USAJOBS.gov is completely free. Any site charging fees to apply for federal jobs is a scam.
