Veterans' Preference in Federal Hiring 2026: 5-Point, 10-Point & CPS/CP/XP Guide

Veterans' preference can put you at the top of a federal hiring certificate — but only if you claim it correctly with the right documents. Here is how 5-point, 10-point, CPS, CP, and XP preference work in 2026.

If you served in the U.S. Armed Forces, federal hiring law gives you a real edge — veterans' preference, a system of advantages that can move your application to the top of the certificate sent to the selecting official. But the categories, codes, and document requirements confuse even seasoned applicants.

The core split: 5-point preference for veterans who served during a war, campaign, or expedition, and 10-point preference for veterans with service-connected disabilities — plus eligible spouses, widows, widowers, and mothers.

This guide explains each category, the CPS/CP/XP codes, the documents that prove your claim, and — critically — where preference does not apply.

5-Point vs. 10-Point: The Two Tiers

5-point (TP) preference covers veterans who served on active duty during a war, in a campaign or expedition for which a campaign badge was authorized, or during qualifying periods — discharged under honorable conditions. These candidates get 5 points added to a passing score (or equivalent advantage in category rating).

10-point preference is the stronger tier: CPS (compensable service-connected disability), CP (disability preference eligible), and XP (disabled veterans). In many examinations, CPS and XP candidates go to the very top of the certificate, ahead of all other eligibles.

Derived Preference: When Family Members Qualify

Preference is not only for the veteran. Derived preference extends 10-point preference to spouses of disabled veterans (when the disability prevents employment), unremarried widows and widowers, and mothers of deceased or permanently and totally disabled veterans — each under specific statutory conditions.

Derived claims need the SF-15 (Application for 10-Point Veteran Preference) plus supporting documents: VA rating decision, marriage or birth certificates, and proof of the veteran's service and disability.

Proving It — and Where Preference Does Not Apply

Claim it with paperwork: DD-214 (member copy showing character of service), SF-15 for 10-point claims, and your VA disability rating letter. Missing documents mean HR cannot adjudicate your claim — you will be considered without preference.

Know where preference does not help: Direct Hire Authority (qualified veterans still get full consideration), most excepted-service hiring such as FEMA CORE, and Senior Executive Service appointments. There, compete purely on qualifications.

How to Claim Veterans' Preference on USAJOBS

  1. Determine your category. 5-point (TP) for wartime/campaign service; 10-point (CPS/CP/XP) for service-connected disabilities; derived preference for qualifying family members.
  2. Complete the SF-15 for 10-point claims. The Application for 10-Point Veteran Preference is mandatory — fill it out fully and accurately.
  3. Gather proof documents. DD-214, VA disability rating letter, and for derived claims, marriage/birth certificates and the veteran's service records.
  4. Claim preference in the application. Answer the preference questions in the questionnaire — HR can only adjudicate what you claim.
  5. Check the hiring authority. Under Direct Hire or excepted-service announcements, preference points do not apply — compete on qualifications instead.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between 5-point and 10-point preference?

5-point goes to veterans with honorable discharge who served during a war or campaign. 10-point goes to veterans with a service-connected disability (plus eligible family members), with CPS/CP/XP subcategories.

What do CPS, CP, and XP mean?

CPS: compensably disabled preference eligible. CP: disability preference eligible. XP: disabled veterans entitled to 10-point preference. CPS and XP candidates often top the certificate.

What documents prove veterans' preference?

Your DD-214 (honorable discharge), SF-15 (for 10-point claims), and VA disability rating letter — upload all of them.

Does veterans' preference apply to every federal job?

No. It does not apply under Direct Hire Authority, in most excepted-service hiring (like FEMA CORE), or for Senior Executive Service roles.

Can military spouses get veterans' preference?

In limited cases — derived preference can extend to spouses of disabled veterans, unremarried widows/widowers, and mothers of deceased or disabled veterans who meet the criteria.
