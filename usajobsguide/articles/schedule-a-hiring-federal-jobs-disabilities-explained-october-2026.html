Schedule A Hiring: Federal Jobs for People with Disabilities

Schedule A is a federal hiring authority that lets agencies appoint people with disabilities without the competitive line. Here is who qualifies, what the required letter must say, the two ways to apply, and the 2-year path to permanent federal status.

Most people know USAJOBS as a resume-and-wait portal. But there is a second door into federal service: Schedule A, the hiring authority that lets agencies appoint people with disabilities without the competitive process. If you have a severe physical disability, a psychiatric disability, or an intellectual disability — and the documentation to prove it — Schedule A puts your application on a separate certificate and can get you hired faster. Here is exactly how it works in 2026.

What Schedule A Actually Is

Schedule A (5 CFR 213.3102(u)) is an excepted-service appointing authority created by OPM. "Excepted" means positions are excepted from the usual competitive hiring procedures: instead of thousands of applicants racing through one USAJOBS announcement, an agency can appoint an eligible candidate directly — at any grade level, and for any job the person qualifies for, whether permanent, time-limited, or temporary. It covers three categories: intellectual disability, severe physical disability, and psychiatric disability. Severe physical disabilities include blindness, deafness, paralysis, missing limbs, epilepsy, and dwarfism. It is not a preference point or a bonus — it is a separate, noncompetitive hiring lane.

The Schedule A Letter: Your Required Proof

You cannot be hired under Schedule A without proof, and that proof is a short certification called a Schedule A letter. Three sources can write it:

  • A licensed medical professional — a physician or other professional certified by a state, DC, or a U.S. territory to practice medicine.
  • A licensed vocational rehabilitation specialist — state or private.
  • A disability-benefits agency — any federal, state, DC, or territorial agency that issues or provides disability benefits.

The letter goes on letterhead, carries a signature, and must state that you are eligible for employment under Schedule A, 5 CFR 213.3102(u). It does not need to name your diagnosis, describe treatment, or attach medical records — eligibility is the only required content. Vocational rehabilitation counselors write these routinely, so if a doctor's office drags its feet, that route is often the fastest. You must submit the documentation before the agency can appoint you.

Two Ways to Apply Under Schedule A

Eligible applicants can use either the competitive or the noncompetitive process. Competitive: apply on USAJOBS to announcements open to persons with disabilities, attaching your Schedule A letter with the required documents; HR verifies eligibility and qualifications after the announcement closes. Noncompetitive: this is the part most people miss — you can apply even when no announcement exists. Many agencies run special hiring authority mailboxes or talent portals where you submit your resume and Schedule A letter directly; HR then reviews eligible candidates and refers them on a separate noncompetitive certificate to hiring managers. You can also compete through the traditional process like any other applicant — the lanes are not mutually exclusive.

The 2-Year Trial Period and Conversion

Schedule A appointments begin on a trial (probationary) basis. After two years of satisfactory service, the agency may convert you — without competition — to a permanent career or career-conditional appointment in the competitive service. Time on a temporary appointment does not count toward the requirement, but time-limited appointments of more than one year do. This conversion is the real payoff: the same permanent federal career status as everyone else, earned through performance instead of a competitive gauntlet. Federal agencies are also required by law to provide reasonable accommodations to qualified applicants and employees with disabilities — contact the agency's Disability Program Manager or HR specialist to request them. For the official OPM breakdown of the authority, documentation, and accommodations, see OPM's federal hiring information.

Apply Under Schedule A in 5 Steps

  1. Get the letter. Ask a licensed medical professional, a vocational rehabilitation counselor, or your disability-benefits agency for a signed Schedule A letter on letterhead stating your eligibility.
  2. Save your documents in USAJOBS. Upload the letter and your resume to your USAJOBS documents section so every application has them ready.
  3. Find both lanes. Search USAJOBS for announcements open to persons with disabilities, and look up agency special hiring authority mailboxes for noncompetitive submissions.
  4. Attach the letter to every application. Schedule A eligibility is only recognized when the documentation is in the package HR reviews.
  5. Request accommodations early. Ask the agency's Disability Program Manager about accommodations during the application process and after hire.

Bottom line: Schedule A does not lower the qualifications bar — you must still meet the job's requirements. What it removes is the competitive line. If you are eligible, the letter costs one appointment and opens a hiring lane most applicants never see.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who qualifies for Schedule A federal hiring?

People with an intellectual disability, a severe physical disability, or a psychiatric disability that substantially limits one or more major life activities. A qualifying letter must document eligibility before appointment.

Who can write a Schedule A letter?

A licensed medical professional (state/DC/territory certified), a licensed vocational rehabilitation specialist (state or private), or a federal, state, DC, or territorial agency that issues disability benefits. It must be on letterhead, signed, and state your Schedule A eligibility.

Can I apply under Schedule A when no job announcement exists?

Yes. The noncompetitive route lets eligible applicants submit a resume and Schedule A letter directly to an agency's special hiring authority mailbox or talent portal, even without a posted announcement.

Does Schedule A mean I skip the qualifications?

No. You must still meet the position's qualifications and be likely to perform the duties. Schedule A bypasses the competitive procedures, not the qualification standard.

What happens after the 2-year trial period?

After two years of satisfactory service, the agency may convert you noncompetitively to a permanent career or career-conditional appointment. Time-limited appointments over one year count toward the requirement.
