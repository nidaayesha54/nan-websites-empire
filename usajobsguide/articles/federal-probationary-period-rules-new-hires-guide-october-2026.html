Federal Probationary Period Rules: What New Feds Must Know

Every new federal hire serves a probationary period — 1 year in the competitive service, 2 in the excepted service. Here's what it means, your rights, and the 2026 certification rule.

Landing a federal job is only half the battle — your first year (or two) on the job is a probationary period, and the rules governing it changed significantly in 2025–2026. Whether you are entering the competitive service or the excepted service, understanding probation protects your new career. Rules below reflect federal regulation and a 2025 executive order; confirm how your specific agency implements them with your HR office.

What the Probationary Period Actually Is

Probation is an extension of the hiring process, not a formality. Under 5 CFR Part 315, agencies use the first year to assess whether you can perform the job and fit the organization. Your supervisor evaluates your performance and conduct, and the agency decides whether finalizing your appointment serves its needs. Prior federal civilian service in the same agency and line of work can count toward probation if the break was under 30 days — but for most new hires, the clock starts at day one.

The 2026 Certification Rule: Probation Is No Longer Automatic

The biggest recent change comes from Executive Order 14284 (2025), which created Civil Service Rule XI. Under it, completing probation is not automatic when you hit your anniversary date:

  • Your agency must provide written certification — within the 30 days before your anniversary — affirming that your continued employment advances the public interest.
  • Without that certification, your employment automatically terminates the day before the anniversary date.
  • The burden is on you to demonstrate why your continued service is in the public interest.
  • Termination grounds were broadened beyond performance and conduct to include the agency's needs and interests.

Probationary Period vs. Trial Period

OPM uses the terms precisely. A probationary period applies to the competitive service (most USAJOBS postings) and lasts one year. A trial period applies to the excepted service (agencies like TSA, intelligence community, and many direct-hire roles) and lasts two years for non-preference eligibles. If you move from an excepted appointment into a competitive career appointment, you generally serve a new one-year probationary period.

Your Rights During Probation

Probationers have limited appeal rights — generally no MSPB appeal of a termination except narrow cases like discrimination claims. The honest bottom line: during probation, strong performance and a clean conduct record are your real job security.

Transfers and promotions: if you transfer agencies, or are promoted, demoted, or reassigned before finishing probation, you must complete the probationary period in the new position — the clock does not simply reset in your favor.

How to Survive and Clear Probation

  1. Clarify expectations in week one. Ask your supervisor for written performance standards and critical job elements.
  2. Document your wins. Keep a running file of accomplishments, positive feedback, and completed assignments — this is your certification evidence.
  3. Request mid-year feedback. Do not wait for the annual review; ask explicitly how you are tracking at six and nine months.
  4. Keep conduct spotless. Attendance, ethics, and workplace behavior weigh as heavily as output during probation.
  5. Ask about certification at month ten. Confirm who issues your written certification and what they need from you.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is the federal probationary period?

One year of continuous service for competitive-service appointments (5 CFR 315.802). Excepted-service appointees generally serve a two-year trial period instead.

Can a federal probationary period be extended?

No. The regulation states the one-year probationary period may not be extended — though certain nonpay absences can shift the end date.

What is the 2026 certification rule for probationers?

Under Civil Service Rule XI (Executive Order 14284), your agency must issue written certification within 30 days before your anniversary that your continued employment is in the public interest. Without it, employment automatically ends.

Can I be fired during the probationary period?

Yes, and with limited appeal rights. Agencies may terminate probationers for performance, conduct, or broader agency needs, with far fewer MSPB protections than tenured employees have.

Do I serve probation again if I transfer agencies?

If you transfer, or are promoted, demoted, or reassigned before completing probation, you must complete the probationary period in the new position.

Does prior federal service count toward probation?

Prior civilian service in the same agency and same line of work can count if the break in service did not exceed 30 calendar days.
