TSA Transportation Security Officer Jobs 2026: Pay, Requirements and How to Apply

The Transportation Security Administration is hiring Transportation Security Officers at airports nationwide — including a Portland announcement open through October 21, 2026. No college degree required, federal benefits included, and starting pay that beats most entry-level private-sector work.

The Transportation Security Officer (TSO) role is one of the most accessible doors into federal service — no college degree required. TSA hires TSOs at airports nationwide, and a Portland announcement is open through October 21, 2026, though it may close early. Here is the full picture: pay, duties, requirements, and how to apply.

What TSOs Actually Do

TSOs protect air travelers, airports, and aircraft. Duties include operating screening equipment, performing searches and pat-downs, lifting bags up to 50 pounds, controlling terminal entry points, and assisting the public. It is a frontline job demanding focus in a noisy, high-pressure environment.

Requirements and Physical Demands

The job is open to all U.S. citizens and nationals, and no college degree is required. But it is physically demanding, and applicants should be honest with themselves about the requirements:

  • Standing and walking: You may walk up to three miles during a shift and stand for as long as four hours at a stretch.
  • Lifting: You must be able to lift and carry bags and bins weighing up to 50 pounds.
  • Schedule flexibility: Part-time shifts generally run 16–32 hours per week, and work can include days, nights, weekends, holidays, overtime, and split shifts.
  • Training travel: New-hire training may require three to six weeks of travel in full-time duty status away from your home airport, with authorized travel expenses reimbursed.
  • Certification: You must complete required training, certification, recertification, and ongoing fitness-for-duty requirements.

Federal Benefits

Federal benefits may include health, dental, vision, life, and long-term care insurance; federal retirement; the Thrift Savings Plan; paid leave and federal holidays; tuition reimbursement; and a uniform allowance. Full-time TSOs get the full federal package.

How to Apply

  1. Search USAJOBS: Find the TSO announcement for your airport on USAJOBS.gov. Each airport has its own announcement with its own salary range.
  2. Read the whole announcement: Note the closing date — the Portland announcement is open through October 21, 2026, but TSA may close it early.
  3. Write your own essays: TSA now requires applicants to certify that optional essay responses are their own words and were not produced by a consultant or AI tool. Write them yourself.
  4. Complete the assessment: TSO applicants typically complete an online assessment and, if selected, a medical evaluation and background check.
  5. Apply before the cap: Like many TSA postings, the announcement can close once enough qualified applications arrive. Apply early in the window.
Apply through USAJOBS only: TSA hires exclusively through official USAJOBS announcements. Anyone asking you to pay a fee or email a resume for a TSA job is running a scam.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a college degree to become a TSA officer?

No. Transportation Security Officer positions are open to U.S. citizens and nationals without a degree requirement, though you must meet physical and background standards.

How much do TSA officers make in 2026?

Recent announcements show starting ranges around $40,332 to $57,182 per year depending on the airport's locality pay — roughly $19 to $27 per hour.

Is the Portland TSO announcement still open?

The Portland announcement is scheduled to remain open through October 21, 2026, but TSA may close it early if enough applications are received. Check USAJOBS for current status.

What are the physical requirements for TSOs?

Officers may walk up to three miles per shift, stand up to four hours, and lift up to 50 pounds. New-hire training can require three to six weeks of travel.

How do I apply for TSA jobs?

Apply through the official announcement on USAJOBS.gov. TSA requires applicants to certify that essay responses are their own words, not AI-generated.
