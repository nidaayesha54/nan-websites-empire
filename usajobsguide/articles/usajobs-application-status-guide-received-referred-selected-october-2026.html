USAJOBS Application Status Guide 2026: What 'Referred' Really Means

You applied on USAJOBS and now your dashboard says "Received" — or maybe "Referred" — and nothing is happening. This guide decodes every federal application status, shows where to track your real progress, and tells you what to do when your application stalls.

After you submit a federal application, your USAJOBS dashboard shows a status — Received, Reviewed, Referred, Selected, Not Selected — and weeks can pass with no change. These statuses follow a fixed federal pipeline. Here is what each one means.

What Each Status Means

Received. Your package reached the agency — submission confirmed, nothing reviewed yet. Agencies evaluate applications after the announcement closes, so weeks at "Received" is normal.

Reviewed / Eligible / Ineligible. An HR specialist checked your package against the announcement's requirements. "Ineligible" usually means a missing document or a resume that did not explicitly state hours per week, salary, or specialized experience — agencies cannot infer qualifications.

Referred. HR found you eligible and qualified and put your name on a referral certificate for the hiring manager. You cleared the competitive hurdle — but it is not a job offer. The manager may interview several referred candidates, select someone else, or cancel the announcement.

Selected / Not Selected. The final disposition: "Selected" means a tentative offer; "Not Selected" closes the loop. All referred applicants receive a final notification once a selection is made.

How to Check Your Real Status in 2026

Do not wait for email — several agencies now state USAJOBS no longer sends status alert emails. Check status manually:

  • USAJOBS Applicant Dashboard: log in, open Applications, select Application Status, then the position's details link — it shows your status, processed documents, and agency correspondence.
  • Agency Talent Acquisition System (TAS): some announcements link to the agency's own TAS for finer-grained tracking.
  • The agency contact: if the TAS shows no detail, email the listed point of contact — the official channel.

Many agencies still email at three key stages — received, referral decision, final disposition — but with 2026 hiring freezes and backlogs, plan on months, not days.

What to Do When Your Application Stalls

First, verify document completeness: uploads take hours to clear the virus scan, and incomplete documents are the top preventable cause of "Ineligible." Second, keep applying — a long "Received" usually means a big pool or a slow cert, not a verdict on you.

Third, prioritize direct-hire authority announcements — shortage occupations move fastest in 2026. Finally, a polite status inquiry to the listed agency contact beats refreshing the dashboard.

Check Your Application Status in 4 Steps

  1. Log in to USAJOBS and open the Applicant Dashboard.
  2. Select Application Status, then the details link for the position.
  3. Confirm document processing: every required document should show as received and processed (allow hours for the virus scan).
  4. Follow the agency link: use the Talent Acquisition System (TAS) if provided; otherwise contact the agency POC on the announcement.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does 'Referred' mean on a USAJOBS application?

'Referred' means HR found you eligible and qualified and placed you on a referral certificate sent to the hiring manager. A major milestone — but not a job offer.

How do I check my USAJOBS application status?

Log in to USAJOBS, open Applications, select Application Status, then the position's details link. It shows your status, processed documents, and agency correspondence. Some agencies route you to their Talent Acquisition System (TAS).

My status has been 'Received' for weeks. Is something wrong?

Usually not. Agencies review only after the announcement closes; large ones take weeks or months. With 2026 freezes and backlogs, longer waits are common. Verify your documents processed, then keep applying.

Will USAJOBS email me when my status changes?

Many agencies send fewer status emails; some state USAJOBS no longer sends status alerts. Do not rely on email — check your USAJOBS dashboard or the agency's TAS regularly.

What does 'Not Referred' or 'Ineligible' mean?

'Ineligible' means you did not meet minimum requirements — often a missing document or a resume that omitted hours per week, salary, or specialized experience. Fix the documents and reapply.

How long does the federal hiring process take in 2026?

OPM's long-standing target is about 80 days from close to offer, but 2026's freezes and reductions stretch many pipelines. Direct-hire announcements usually move fastest.
