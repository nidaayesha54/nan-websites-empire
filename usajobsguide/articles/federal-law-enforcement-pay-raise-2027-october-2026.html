Federal Law Enforcement Pay Raise 2027

While most civilian federal employees face a proposed 0% pay freeze in 2027, eligible federal law enforcement personnel are slated for a 3.8% increase. Here is who qualifies and what happens next.

Buried inside the White House's August 26, 2026 alternative pay plan is a major carve-out: while most civilian federal employees are on track for a 0% pay raise in 2027, eligible federal law enforcement personnel would receive a 3.8% increase. For officers, agents, and investigators across agencies like the FBI, DEA, CBP, and the Secret Service, that exception could mean thousands of dollars. Here is exactly what the proposal says, who it covers, and what could still change.

The 3.8% Law Enforcement Exception

Under the Federal Employees Pay Comparability Act, the President must submit an alternative pay plan to Congress by September 1 each year or let the statutory formula take effect. The August 26 letter set both base and locality adjustments to zero for most civilian employees in 2027, citing fiscal responsibility — but it separately directed the Office of Personnel Management (OPM) to provide a 3.8% compensation increase for certain federal law enforcement personnel under separate statutory authority.

This is not a general raise with a law enforcement flavor. It is a distinct pay action for a defined group, and OPM — not individual agencies — will determine which positions qualify. If you are in federal law enforcement, your agency's HR office and OPM's forthcoming guidance are the two sources that matter.

Which Law Enforcement Roles Could Qualify?

OPM has not yet published the final eligibility list, but the 3.8% authority is aimed at federal law enforcement officers — a category that generally includes 1811 criminal investigators (FBI special agents, DEA agents, ATF agents, Secret Service agents, HSI investigators), CBP officers and Border Patrol agents, Federal Protective Service officers, and federal police forces. The precise position coverage will come from OPM's determination.

The Fight Over the Freeze

The 0% proposal for most civilians has triggered a political battle. A group of 110 lawmakers sent a letter to congressional leadership opposing the freeze and followed up with a Capitol Hill press conference alongside federal employee union leaders. They are targeting December's government funding deadline as the best remaining opportunity to change the outcome.

What Federal Law Enforcement Officers Should Do Now

  1. Confirm your position's coverage. Ask your HR office whether your series and position fall under the law enforcement pay authority OPM is implementing.
  2. Watch for OPM guidance. OPM will define exactly which positions receive the 3.8% — check OPM's pay administration pages for updates.
  3. Track the legislative fight. Follow the FAIR Act and December appropriations on Congress.gov; either could change the outcome.
  4. Check your January LES. Verify the increase appears on your Leave and Earnings Statement with the first 2027 pay period.
  5. Plan around the freeze if you are civilian non-LE. With base and locality flat, focus on within-grade increases, TSP, and career moves that change your grade.
Not final yet: The 3.8% law enforcement increase and the 0% civilian freeze are proposals in the President's alternative pay plan. Final 2027 pay tables are normally established by executive order in December — monitor OPM announcements for confirmation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will federal law enforcement get a raise in 2027?

Under the August 26, 2026 alternative pay plan, eligible federal law enforcement personnel are slated for a 3.8% compensation increase in 2027, while most civilian feds face a 0% freeze. OPM will determine which positions qualify.

Who decides which law enforcement positions get the 3.8%?

The Office of Personnel Management (OPM). The alternative pay plan directed OPM to implement the 3.8% increase for qualifying federal law enforcement personnel under separate statutory authority.

Will most federal employees get a raise in 2027?

As proposed, no. Most civilian federal employees are on track for a 0% raise — both base and locality pay frozen at 2026 levels — unless Congress overrides the plan through appropriations.

What is the FAIR Act?

The Federal Adjustment of Income Rates (FAIR) Act, reintroduced by some lawmakers, proposes a 4.1% average raise for civilian federal employees instead of the freeze. It has not been enacted.

When will 2027 federal pay be final?

Final 2027 pay tables are normally established through a presidential executive order in December. Until that order is signed, treat the proposed figures as likely but not certain.
