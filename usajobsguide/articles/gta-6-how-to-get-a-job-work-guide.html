How to Get a Job in GTA 6: Every Way to Work in Leonida

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

There is no job application in GTA 6. You cannot walk into an office, hand in a resume and wait for a call back. Work in Leonida comes the way work comes in every Grand Theft Auto game: you meet the right person, you do the right favour, and the next job follows from the last one. The trick is knowing where the work is, because the game never hands you a job board — and the first jobs you find pay almost nothing.

That is by design. The early jobs in GTA 6 exist to teach you the systems, not to make you rich. The story introduces them one at a time, each one unlocking a tool or a contact you will need later, and the pay stays low until you have earned the right to bigger work. This guide covers every way to get hired in Leonida, in the order you will find them, and what each one actually pays.

Start with the story: the first jobs find you

Your first job is a tow truck. In the mission called Past Due, Jason repossesses a car for a man who has stopped paying, and the job teaches you the basics: drive the truck, hook the car, bring it back to the yard. There is even a laundromat on the way where you can leave the owner's things for him — a small kindness the game lets you choose, and a hint that the work in this game is never just about the money.

The pattern repeats through Chapter 1. The story hands you small jobs — a delivery here, a collection there — and each one introduces a person or a tool. The pay is deliberately low, because the story is using these jobs to set up better equipment and bigger scores later. Do not judge the job system by Chapter 1. It is the tutorial, not the career.

Taxi, delivery and bodyguard work

Once the world opens up, the steady jobs appear. Taxi missions pay low to medium money, are very low risk and are repeatable — the most reliable early cash flow in the game. Delivery driving pays a little more and is a good way to learn the map, since the routes take you across Vice City and out into the counties. Bodyguard work pays for standing next to someone important and looking dangerous, and playing pool at bars can earn money too if your aim is good.

None of these jobs will make you rich on their own, and that is the point. They are the honest work of Leonida: repeatable, low risk, always there when you need cash. Use them between story missions, when your pockets are empty and the next big job has not appeared yet. They are also the safest way to earn in the early game, when a robbery gone wrong can cost you more than it pays.

Hunting and diving: the jobs that pay real money

The first job that pays properly is hunting, which unlocks in Chapter 2. You track target animals across Lummox County, and each trip can earn anywhere from a few hundred to several thousand dollars, with rare animals worth even more. Hunting is more profitable in GTA 6 than it was in Red Dead Redemption 2, and it is the first work in the game that feels like a career rather than a chore.

Diving pays even better. A diving tour near the Keys pays $400 for the trip itself, and Jason can also take underwater treasure-hunting jobs from a contact named Kenton. Find all the treasures at a diving location and a single site can be worth tens of thousands of dollars — hand the gold and valuables over to Kenton and he turns them into cash. It is the best-paying honest work in the early game, and it is worth finding as soon as the story lets you near the water.

The other side of work: cars, ATMs and robberies

Not all work in Leonida is legal, and the game does not pretend otherwise. You can steal cars and sell them at almost any Pay 'n' Spray, which can also repair a car, register a stolen one under your name and take unwanted vehicles off your hands. A single car can bring in several thousand dollars — but the selling option only unlocks later in the story, and you can only sell a limited number of cars each day.

Leonida has 454 ATMs, and 168 of them can be hooked up to a tow truck and ripped out of the wall. It pays, and it brings the police — so does robbing a convenience store, which pays $500 to $2,000 per hit, and mugging pedestrians, which pays almost nothing and can get you shot by your own victim. Armored trucks spawn randomly across the map, and blowing their doors open is one of the best-paying crimes in the game. The rule is the same for all of it: the money is real, the heat is real, and the game makes you choose.

How the money gates work

The most important thing to understand about jobs in GTA 6 is that the story waits for your money. Some missions will not start until you have reached a cash target, which means grinding small jobs is not optional — it is how the game paces itself. The story can wait, but only until you can afford the next step.

That is why the smartest early strategy is boring: do the story missions, take the taxi and delivery jobs between them, bank your cash at every opportunity, and save the risky work for when you can afford the consequences. The jobs that pay the most — hunting, diving, car sales, heists — all unlock after you have put the early hours in. Leonida rewards patience more than daring, at least at first.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I get a job in GTA 6?

There is no application process. Jobs come from the story and from contacts you meet — taxi and delivery work opens up early, hunting unlocks in Chapter 2, and diving work comes from Kenton near Key Lento.

What is the best-paying job in GTA 6?

Early on, diving treasure hunting for Kenton pays the most — tens of thousands per site. Hunting is the best repeatable work, and car sales pay well once they unlock later in the story.

Do jobs in GTA 6 pay every day?

Some do. Taxi, delivery and bodyguard work are repeatable whenever you want them, and the game has daily-pay jobs that reward showing up regularly. The big payouts — heists, treasure sites, rare animals — are one-offs or limited.

The bottom line: nobody hands you a job in Leonida — you earn it by doing the last one well. Start with the tow truck, learn the map with the taxi, and let the hunting and diving pay for the rest.
