For candidates drawn to high-profile protective work in the nation's capital, this is one of the most significant federal police hiring pushes of the year.

What Secret Service Police Officers Do

Uniformed Division officers provide protection for the White House and its grounds, the Vice President's residence at the Naval Observatory, the Treasury Department building, and foreign diplomatic missions in the Washington, D.C. area. Duties include fixed-post security, patrol, canine and emergency-response assignments, and support to protective details during National Special Security Events. It is visible, mission-critical police work at the center of American government.

The $50,000 Recruitment Incentive

The Secret Service has offered a $50,000 recruitment incentive for Police Officer hires - among the largest law-enforcement signing incentives in the federal government. Key points to understand:

  • Incentives are typically paid in installments tied to a service agreement (often several years).
  • The incentive is on top of base salary, locality pay, and law-enforcement availability pay.
  • Exact terms - payout schedule, eligibility, and service commitment - are set in the vacancy announcement, not in general recruiting materials. Read the announcement line by line.

Requirements: No Experience Needed for Some Postings

Entry-level Uniformed Division announcements have historically been open to candidates without prior police experience. Baseline requirements typically include:

  • U.S. citizenship and a valid driver's license.
  • Age requirements - federal law enforcement positions carry minimum and maximum entry ages; check the announcement.
  • Education or experience - a high school diploma plus general experience, or qualifying education, depending on the grade.
  • Vision, hearing, and fitness standards - protective-duty medical standards apply.
  • Top Secret clearance eligibility - an extensive background investigation is mandatory.

How to Apply on USAJOBS

  1. Find the announcement on usajobs.gov - search "Secret Service Police Officer" or "Uniformed Division".
  2. Confirm eligibility - check the "Who May Apply" and age-requirement sections before investing time.
  3. Build a federal resume - include exact dates, hours per week, and detailed duties. Use the USAJOBS Resume Builder if unsure of the format.
  4. Complete the questionnaire honestly - overclaiming is caught during verification.
  5. Submit before 11:59 PM ET on the closing date with all required documents attached.
Do not submit photographs with your application, and do not upload Adobe portfolio-style documents when a standard federal resume is requested. Incomplete document packages are scored as incomplete.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the $50,000 Secret Service incentive still available in 2026?

The Secret Service has offered a $50,000 recruitment incentive for Uniformed Division Police Officer hires. Availability and terms are set per vacancy announcement, so confirm the current offer on USAJOBS before applying.

Do I need police experience to apply?

Not for entry-level postings - some Secret Service Police Officer announcements require no prior law enforcement experience. The agency provides full training. Higher-grade postings may require experience.

Where would I be stationed?

Uniformed Division officers serve primarily in the Washington, D.C. metropolitan area - the White House complex, the Vice President's residence, and diplomatic missions.

What clearance is required?

Top Secret clearance eligibility is required, which means an extensive background investigation and polygraph. Start gathering ten years of residence, employment, and reference history early.

How long is the hiring process?

Federal protective-service hiring commonly takes many months, with the background investigation the longest stage. Apply early and keep your contact information current.
