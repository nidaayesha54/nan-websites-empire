Veterans Preference Guide: 5-Point vs 10-Point Explained

Veterans preference can move you to the top of a federal hiring list — but only if you claim it correctly. Here is how 5-point and 10-point preference work, and the documents you must submit.

Veterans preference is one of the most valuable advantages in federal hiring — and one of the most misunderstood. In competitive examinations that use numerical rating, qualified preference-eligible veterans receive 5 or 10 extra points. Under category rating, preference eligibles with a compensable service-connected disability of 10 percent or more are placed at the top of the highest category. Preference is not a guarantee of a job, and preference you are entitled to but do not document does not exist in the hiring system.

Who Gets 5-Point Preference

Five-point preference goes to honorably separated veterans who served on active duty (not active duty for training) during a qualifying period. OPM groups these veterans as TP — preference eligibles with no disability rating. Qualifying service includes service during a war declared by Congress, the Gulf War period, or service in a campaign or expedition for which a campaign medal was authorized. Veterans who enlisted after September 7, 1980, or entered active duty on or after October 14, 1982, without prior continuous service generally must have served 24 months or the full period called to active duty.

Claim it with your DD-214, showing an honorable or general discharge.

Who Gets 10-Point Preference

Ten-point preference covers more people, divided into groups that matter for ranking:

  • CPS — disability rating of 30 percent or more (10 points).
  • CP — disability rating of at least 10 percent but less than 30 percent (10 points).
  • XP — disability rating less than 10 percent, including a 0 percent rating, plus Purple Heart recipients and certain derived preference cases (10 points).
  • SSP — sole survivorship preference (0 points, but still a preference category).

Note the XP trap: a veteran with a 0 percent service-connected rating is still a 10-point preference eligible, not a 5-point one. Any service-connected rating at all moves you from TP to the 10-point groups. Certain spouses, widows, widowers, and parents of veterans may also be entitled to 10-point derived preference.

How Preference Works in Practice

Under a numerical rating system, 5 or 10 points are added to the passing score of a qualified preference eligible. Under category rating — the system most agencies use today — CPS and CP veterans are placed at the top of the highest quality category while XP and TP veterans are placed above non-preference eligibles within their assigned category. For excepted-service positions, agencies follow 5 CFR part 302 procedures unless the position is exempt.

The most common failure: claiming 10-point preference without the SF-15. You must submit a completed SF-15 plus your DD-214 and a VA letter stating your disability percentage, or preference is not applied.

Preference Is Not a Job Guarantee

Veterans preference improves your standing; it does not compel an agency to hire you. Agencies still select from the referral list based on qualifications. Pair preference with a strong federal resume proving you meet the grade's specialized experience.

How to Claim Veterans Preference Correctly

  1. Identify your group. Match service and disability rating to TP, XP, CP, CPS, or SSP.
  2. Gather documents. DD-214 for 5-point; DD-214, SF-15, and VA letter for 10-point.
  3. Claim it on every application. Answer the preference questions and upload documents with each application.
  4. Check derived preference. Spouses, widows, widowers, and parents may also be eligible.
  5. Verify before submitting. Without the right documents, preference is not applied.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between 5-point and 10-point veterans preference?

5-point (TP): honorably separated veterans with qualifying service, no disability rating. 10-point: disabled veterans, Purple Heart recipients, certain family members (CPS, CP, XP).

Does a 0 percent disability rating count for 10-point preference?

Yes. Any service-connected rating, including 0 percent, puts you in XP at 10 points — not 5.

What documents do I need to claim 10-point preference?

A DD-214, completed SF-15, and VA letter stating your disability percentage. Purple Heart recipients must show it on the DD-214.

Does veterans preference guarantee me a federal job?

No. Preference improves ranking, but agencies select on qualifications — undocumented preference is not applied.

Do spouses or parents of veterans get preference?

Certain spouses, widows, widowers, and parents may qualify for derived preference, such as the spouse of a veteran unemployable due to service-connected disability.
