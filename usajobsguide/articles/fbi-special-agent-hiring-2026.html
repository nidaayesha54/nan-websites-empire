FBI Special Agent Hiring 2026: 700 New Agents Wanted, No Law Enforcement Experience Required

The FBI is running a nationwide recruitment drive with a goal of adding 700 special agents by the end of 2026. Prior law enforcement experience is not required — the bureau is actively seeking teachers, lawyers, accountants, and cybersecurity professionals.

The Federal Bureau of Investigation is in the middle of one of its biggest hiring pushes in years. Following a wave of retirements, the bureau is running a nationwide recruitment initiative focused on hiring special agents, intelligence analysts, and professional staff in 2026 — with the bureau aiming to add 700 special agents by the end of 2026. And here is the part most people get wrong: you do not need prior law enforcement experience to become an FBI special agent.

Who the FBI Is Recruiting Right Now

The bureau employs nearly 13,000 special agents drawn from remarkably diverse careers, and current recruiting emphasizes backgrounds that are not traditionally associated with law enforcement. FBI recruiters have specifically said they are seeking:

  • Cyber and technology professionals — computer science and cybersecurity skills are among the most valued right now, translating directly into both special agent and professional staff roles.
  • Lawyers — law degrees translate well into investigative work, and the FBI actively recruits attorneys.
  • Finance and accounting professionals — financial backgrounds are prized for white-collar, fraud, and money-laundering investigations.
  • Teachers and educators — recruiters cite teachers becoming successful special agents, valuing their communication and people skills.
  • Military veterans — military experience, including military intelligence, remains a strong foundation for bureau service.

The FBI's Detroit field office highlighted the nationwide effort in 2026, noting the bureau seeks diverse backgrounds and specialized skills in technology, intelligence, finance, and law enforcement to address evolving threats. Careers are available across all 56 FBI field offices.

The Hiring Process

Becoming a special agent is a multi-stage process, and candidates should expect it to take many months:

  1. Online application: Everything starts at fbijobs.gov — review the special agent requirements and employment disqualifiers first.
  2. Written examination and panel interview: Candidates are assessed on core competencies including leadership, communication, initiative, adaptability, and decision-making.
  3. Physical fitness test: The test is strenuous — start training well before you apply.
  4. Background investigation: A thorough investigation including a polygraph examination and drug screening.
  5. Academy training: Selected candidates attend the FBI Academy at Quantico, Virginia.
Why the urgency is real: The retirement wave was driven in part by a federal deferred resignation program that pushed many experienced agents out. That creates genuine opportunity — but also means the bureau is hiring at volume now, and competitive candidates should apply while the push is on.

Intelligence Analyst and Professional Staff Roles

Not everyone needs to carry a badge. The same recruitment drive covers intelligence analysts and professional staff — the behind-the-scenes experts in data analysis, linguistics, forensics, IT, and administration who make investigations possible. These roles often have different qualification paths and can be an excellent entry point into the bureau for candidates whose strengths are analytical or technical rather than operational.

Tips for Standing Out

Recruiters emphasize service, integrity, and strong judgment. Candidates with cyber skills, language abilities, or specialized professional credentials should highlight them prominently. Attend an FBI information session — field offices regularly host them — to hear directly from agents what the bureau is looking for and to get your questions answered before you apply.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need law enforcement experience to join the FBI as a special agent?

No. The FBI has confirmed that prior law enforcement experience is not required. The bureau actively recruits teachers, lawyers, accountants, tech professionals, and military veterans.

How many special agents is the FBI hiring in 2026?

Reporting indicates the bureau aims to add 700 special agents by the end of 2026, following a wave of retirements.

What are the basic requirements to become an FBI special agent?

You must be a U.S. citizen between 23 and 36, hold a bachelor's degree, have two to three years of professional work experience, hold a valid driver's license, and pass a background investigation, polygraph, and physical fitness test.

Where do I apply for FBI jobs?

All applications start at fbijobs.gov. Careers are available across all 56 FBI field offices nationwide.

Does the FBI hire intelligence analysts and support staff too?

Yes. The 2026 recruitment drive also covers intelligence analysts and professional staff in areas like IT, linguistics, forensics, and administration.
