Army Posted 17,225 Job Ads During the Hiring Freeze: What the Data Means for Applicants

New analysis of USAJOBS data shows the Army posted 17,225 civilian job advertisements while its hiring freeze was in effect — and nearly three-quarters were labeled permanent. Here is what the numbers reveal, and what they mean for your 2026 application strategy.

An analysis by Federal Hiring Data of USAJOBS postings found that the Army posted 17,225 civilian job advertisements during its hiring freeze — the freeze the Army lifted on September 25, 2026. The finding surprises many applicants who assumed federal hiring had simply stopped. It had not: agencies kept advertising positions, building pipelines, and in many cases hiring under exceptions, even while the freeze was technically in place.

The headline numbers deserve a careful read. Of the 17,225 Army ads, 12,853 (74.6%) carried a "permanent" appointment label — but "permanent" describes the advertised appointment type, not a guarantee that anyone entered on duty. And 8,698 ads (50.5%) were open to "the public," meaning half were restricted to internal candidates, veterans, military spouses, or other specific hiring paths.

Where the Ads Came From

Two organizations dominated the Army's advertising. U.S. Army Installation Management Command posted 5,158 ads and Army National Guard units posted 5,059 — together 59.3% of all Army ads during the freeze. The Army Corps of Engineers followed with 2,024 ads. These are hiring-agency labels from the USAJOBS data, not counts of unique people or authorized positions.

The mix of appointment types differed sharply by organization. Installation Management's postings included 1,221 labeled "intermittent" appointments, while National Guard units had 636 labeled "temporary." Applicants should not read the Army-wide total as a uniform wave of permanent vacancies — the label on the specific announcement you are reading is what matters.

The Public-Access Split Applicants Must Understand

The most important number for outside applicants is the hiring-path tag. Across the Army, only about half of freeze-era ads were open to the public — but the split varied enormously. Installation Management listed the public on 4,569 of 5,158 ads (88.6%), while National Guard units listed the public on only 749 of 5,059 (14.8%). Army Medical Command was the most open of all at 415 of 437 (95.0%), though its volume was small.

The lesson: always filter USAJOBS by "open to the public" if you are not a current federal employee, veteran, or military spouse — and target Installation Management and Medical Command announcements, which historically welcome outside applicants at far higher rates than Guard units.

What "Posted During the Freeze" Actually Means

A job ad posted during a hiring freeze is not the same as a job filled during a hiring freeze. Agencies advertise for many reasons: to build candidate pipelines, to meet legal posting requirements, to hire under freeze exceptions for mission-critical roles, or simply because the announcement was already in motion. The USAJOBS data documents advertising activity, not hiring outcomes.

Still, the data is encouraging for one reason: it shows the Army's hiring apparatus never fully stopped. Organizations that posted thousands of ads during the freeze have active recruitment pipelines, established announcement templates, and hiring managers accustomed to moving candidates — all of which should translate into faster movement now that the freeze is lifted.

Your Application Strategy Now That the Freeze Is Lifted

First, search USAJOBS for Army announcements open to the public and sort by closing date — the freshest announcements represent the post-freeze hiring wave. Second, set up saved searches with email alerts for your target occupational series at Installation Management Command, the Corps of Engineers, and Army Medical Command.

Third, read every announcement's "who may apply" section before investing hours in an application — with roughly half of Army ads historically closed to the general public, targeting is everything. And fourth, remember that direct-hire authority announcements for shortage occupations move fastest; they skip some of the competitive procedures that slow standard announcements.

How to Use This Data in Your Army Job Search

  1. Filter for public announcements. On USAJOBS, use the hiring-path filter for "open to the public" so you only see announcements you are actually eligible for.
  2. Target the high-volume, high-openness organizations. Installation Management Command (88.6% public) and Army Medical Command (95.0% public) historically welcome outside applicants.
  3. Read the appointment type. "Permanent," "temporary," and "intermittent" are different commitments — confirm the label on your specific announcement.
  4. Set saved-search alerts. Create USAJOBS saved searches for your occupational series at Army organizations so new post-freeze announcements reach your inbox.
  5. Apply to direct-hire announcements first. Shortage-occupation direct-hire postings typically move through the process fastest.
