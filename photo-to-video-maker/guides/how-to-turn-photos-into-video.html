How to Turn Photos Into a Video: The Complete Beginner Guide

Updated October 2026.

Turning photos into a video is simple: pick a free slideshow maker, arrange your photos, add music and transitions, export. The difference between an amateur and a memorable slideshow is in the planning. This guide covers the full workflow.

Step 1: Choose your free photo to video maker

For most projects, Canva Video (templates), CapCut (mobile, beat-sync) or Adobe Express (design quality) are the best free options. See our ranked comparison of all 10 free tools before you commit.

Step 2: Collect and order your photos

  • Tell a story: opening establishing shot → highlights in the middle → emotional closer.
  • Cull ruthlessly: 40–80 photos for a 3–5 minute video. Near-duplicates kill pacing.
  • Mix it up: alternate close-ups, group shots and detail shots so no two consecutive photos feel the same.
  • Resize first: crop to 16:9 (1920×1080) so no photo shows ugly black bars.

Step 3: Plan your timeline

Don't guess — calculate. Our slideshow planner does the math, but the rules of thumb are:

  • Birthdays & weddings: 3–5 seconds per photo.
  • TikTok / Reels-style: 2–3 seconds per photo.
  • Memorials: 5–7 seconds per photo.
  • A 5-minute slideshow ≈ 75 photos at 4 seconds each.

Step 4: Add transitions (one style only)

The #1 beginner mistake is using every transition in the menu. Pick one — a soft fade or a clean slide — and use it throughout. Consistency looks professional; variety looks chaotic.

Step 5: Add music

  • Use royalty-free music from your tool's library (Canva, CapCut and Adobe Express all include one).
  • Match track length to your slideshow runtime — our music guide covers matching, looping and fading.
  • Lower the volume under any voiceover; fade out the last 3 seconds.

Step 6: Titles and captions

Add an intro title card (name + occasion + date), caption key photos with context ("Graduation, 2024"), and close with a thank-you card. Stuck for words? Our caption generator writes titles, captions and hashtags in seconds.

Step 7: Export and share

  • Export 1080p MP4 (H.264) — plays everywhere, reasonable file size.
  • Watch the full export once before sharing: check timing, typos and audio sync.
  • Share via YouTube (unlisted for privacy), Google Drive, or loop it on a TV at the event.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Too many photos — tighter is better. Cut 20% after your first draft.
  • Random transitions — one style, every time.
  • Music that cuts off abruptly — always fade.
  • Tiny text on photos — keep captions short and high-contrast.
