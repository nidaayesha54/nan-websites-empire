GTA 6 Photo Mode: How to Take the Perfect Shot

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, and one of its best features has nothing to do with driving, shooting or heists: it is a full photo mode, available from the start of the game. Rockstar gave GTA 6 a Photo Studio in the pause menu and a working camera on the in-game phone, and between them they turn Vice City into a photography set. This guide explains how to open both, what every setting does, and where your shots actually end up.

The two cameras do different jobs. The Photo Studio is for the perfect shot — full control over the camera, the lighting and the look. The phone camera is for the quick shot — pull it out, frame it, take it, and it lands in the phone gallery. Learn both and you will never miss a moment in Leonida.

How to open Photo Studio

Photo Studio becomes available once you take control of Lucia during the prologue. Open the pause menu with the Start button and select Photo Mode, or press Square on PlayStation and X on Xbox. You can even activate it during cutscenes, though the functions are limited there. Once it is open, the game freezes and the camera is yours.

The Camera tab is where the shot is built. Speed controls how fast the camera moves; grid shows guide lines for positioning; roll tilts the camera to any angle. There are eight aspect ratios to choose from, field of view up to 48.5 degrees, depth of field with a bokeh effect, aperture, focus distance and a focus guide that shows what is in focus and what is not. You can also hide your character entirely — useful when the scene, not Jason or Lucia, is the subject.

Effects, lighting and overlays

The Effects tab is the darkroom: exposure, contrast, highlights, shadows, saturation and sharpness, all adjustable on the spot. The Lighting tab is the most powerful tool in the mode — you can place up to five light sources in the scene, spotlights, floodlights and softboxes, to brighten a dark alley or put a rim of light on a car at dusk. The Vehicle tab toggles your vehicle's lights, and the Overlay tab handles the vignette, filter intensity and gameplay effects.

There are 17 cosmetic filters in Photo Studio, and a colour isolation option that highlights one colour while the rest of the scene fades out. When the shot is set, press Triangle on PlayStation or Y on Xbox to hide the interface and take a clean picture with nothing on screen. That hide-UI step is the one most players forget — and it is the difference between a screenshot and a photograph.

The phone camera: quick shots and selfies

The phone camera is the faster tool, and it arrives after the prologue, once you take control of Jason. Press Up on the D-pad to bring out the smartphone, open the Camera app — the third icon in the first row — and the rear camera opens by default. R2 takes the photo, L1 toggles the camera light, L3 toggles the grid, and R3 switches to the front camera for selfies.

The phone camera has its own tricks: up to 5x zoom, 17 visual filters you cycle with the D-pad, and selfie controls for poses and facial expressions. It is designed for quick photographs while you explore — you do not need to open Photo Studio every time something interesting happens. The two cameras share the same filter count, but the phone is for moments and the Studio is for masterpieces.

Where your photos are saved

Photos taken in Photo Studio and with the phone camera are saved in the game, and you can find them in Jason and Lucia's phone gallery — the same place the phone camera's pictures live. That is the in-game save location, and it matters: screenshots taken with the console's own capture feature do not appear in the game at all.

System captures go elsewhere. On PS5, screenshots taken with the console's built-in feature land in the Media Gallery. On Xbox Series X|S, they go to the console's capture gallery. If you want a shot on your phone or your computer, use the console capture — or share from the gallery through the console's own sharing tools. Know which save location you used before you go hunting for a photo that is not there.

Five shots worth taking first

Start with the easy wins. One: the Vice City skyline at dusk from the beach, with the Studio's depth of field on the skyline and a softbox on the foreground. Two: a night drive on the coastal highway, vehicle lights on, neon signs in the background. Three: a selfie with a dog — the pet interaction makes a better portrait than any posed shot. Four: the Grassrivers at golden hour, with an alligator in frame if you are brave enough. Five: a rain shot on a wet street, with the Studio's lighting bringing out the reflections. Each one teaches a different tab, and together they teach the whole mode.

FAQ

How do I open photo mode in GTA 6? Pause the game and select Photo Mode, or press Square on PS5 / X on Xbox. It unlocks during Lucia's prologue. The phone camera opens with D-pad Up, then the Camera app, once you control Jason.

How many filters does GTA 6 photo mode have? 17 cosmetic filters in Photo Studio and 17 in the phone camera, plus vignette, colour isolation and full exposure and lighting controls in the Studio.

Where are my GTA 6 photos saved? In-game photos live in the phone gallery. Console captures go to the PS5 Media Gallery or the Xbox capture gallery — they do not appear in the game.

The bottom line: Photo Studio is for the shot you plan, the phone camera is for the shot you stumble on, and the hide-UI button is the one press that turns either into a photograph worth keeping.
