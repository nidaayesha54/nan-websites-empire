GTA 6 Editions: Standard vs Ultimate — Is the $20 Worth It?

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and it comes in two editions: Standard at $79.99 and Ultimate at $99.99 in the United States. The $20 gap is the one decision every buyer has to make — and it is a decision that is easier than it looks, because the two editions contain the same game. The Ultimate Edition does not buy a better story, an earlier start, or extra missions. It buys sixteen extras, woven through the story, that you can also buy later for the same $20. This guide lists everything in each edition, what the extras actually are, and who should pay the extra money.

That matters because the edition question is where buyers waste money. The Ultimate Edition looks like the better deal — more content for $20 more — but the content is cosmetic and convenience, not story. Knowing what the $20 buys, and that you can buy it later for the same price, is the difference between a smart purchase and a rushed one.

Standard Edition: $79.99 — The Whole Game

The Standard Edition is the complete game: the full Jason and Lucia story in Leonida, the whole map, the whole cast, and nothing held back. Rockstar describes it simply as the standard edition of Grand Theft Auto VI, and that is exactly what it is — the game, with no part of the story, the map, or the main cast locked behind a more expensive version. It comes digitally, or as a download code in a box — there is no disc in the box, and the game downloads either way, all 212 GB of it.

Standard buyers get the same pre-order bonus as everyone else: digital purchases made before November 20, 2026, include the Vintage Vice City Pack — the '55 Vapid Stanier Sedan and garage, outfits and hairstyles, and an exclusive weapon pattern — plus one month of GTA+ membership. The GTA+ month has to be claimed by March 31, 2027, and cannot be redeemed on child accounts with age-restricted content settings. The bonus is tied to when you buy, not which edition you buy.

Ultimate Edition: $99.99 — Sixteen Extras

The Ultimate Edition is everything in Standard, plus what Rockstar calls an exclusive collection of items threaded across all aspects of Jason and Lucia's story. Its own edition comparison lists sixteen of them, and they unlock chapter by chapter as the story progresses — not all at once, and not at the start.

The vehicles are the headline: the '95 Grotti Cheetah, a white mid-1990s supercar; the Shitzu Squalo, a pink and blue speedboat docked at Washington Beach, with a weapons crate; the '67 Vapid Dominator Buggy, which comes with the Paradise Garage in Watson Bay; a Dinka Enduro bike and Crest Kayak; and a Ganado Retro Build modkit. The weapons are the Hawk & Little Morgan revolvers and engraved versions of Jason's and Lucia's pistols. The rest is clothing and tattoos, and five shops that only open for Ultimate owners: Rideout Customs, Sara's Unisex Salon, Stock 305 clothing, the Electric Fang Tattoo Parlor, and One-Eyed Willie's mod shop. The Ultimate Edition is digital only — there is no boxed version of it at all.

What Ultimate Does NOT Give You

Here is the part that decides the purchase: the Ultimate Edition gives you no early access, no extra missions, no separate storyline, and no different ending. Neither edition lets you play early, and both get the same pre-order bonus. Reports mention two exclusive side missions in the Ultimate Edition, but Rockstar has not said which entries those are, so treat that as reported rather than confirmed. The extras are things, not story — cars, a boat, weapons, outfits, and shops — and they are woven into the game you already own, not added on top of it.

The upgrade math is what makes the decision easy. The Ultimate Edition Upgrade is sold separately on the Xbox Store for $20 — the same as the gap between editions — and Rockstar says you can upgrade at any point, so you can start with Standard and add the rest later for the same total. Standard plus the upgrade costs $99.99, exactly what Ultimate costs up front. There is no penalty for waiting, and no discount for buying early. If you are unsure, buy Standard, play the game, and decide when you know which extras you actually want.

The Collector's Bundle — and the $399 Question

There is one more product on the shelf, and it is the easiest one to misunderstand: the Collector's bundle, at $399.99, is a physical box of merchandise — and it does not include the game. It is a collector's item for fans who already own GTA 6, not a third edition. Do not buy it expecting a copy of the game inside, because there is not one.

The prices, for reference: $79.99 and $99.99 in the US, £69.99 and £89.99 in the UK, €79.99 and €99.99 in the eurozone, with the upgrade at $20, £20, or €20. Both editions pre-load digitally on November 10 and launched on November 19, 2026.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which GTA 6 edition should I buy?

Standard, unless you know you want the sixteen extras. The game is identical in both editions, and the upgrade costs the same $20 later — so there is no reason to pay for Ultimate before you have played.

Can I upgrade from Standard to Ultimate later?

Yes. The Ultimate Edition Upgrade is sold separately for $20 on the Xbox Store and can be bought at any time, once your game code has been redeemed. Standard plus the upgrade costs the same as Ultimate up front.

Is the Ultimate Edition digital only?

Yes. There is no physical Ultimate Edition. A boxed copy from a retailer is always the Standard Edition, and it holds a download code, not a disc.

The Bottom Line

The $20 between Standard and Ultimate buys sixteen story extras — cars, a boat, weapons, outfits, and five shops — and nothing else: no early access, no extra missions, no different story. Buy Standard for $79.99, play the game, and add the $20 upgrade later if the extras appeal to you. The game is the same either way, and the upgrade will still be there when you decide.
