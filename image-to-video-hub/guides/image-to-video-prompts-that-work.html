Image to Video Prompts That Work — Proven Formulas

After testing hundreds of image-to-video generations, one formula wins consistently:

Subject + Specific Motion + Camera Move + Mood/Lighting + Style

Vague prompts ("make it move") produce mush. Specific prompts produce cinema. Below are battle-tested formulas per category — or skip the writing entirely with our Motion Prompt Generator.

🙂 Portraits & people

"Subject: young woman smiling softly. Motion: slow natural blink, subtle head turn to the left, hair swaying gently. Camera: slow push-in. Lighting: warm window light. Style: photorealistic."

Rules: keep motion subtle — big movements warp faces. One action at a time.

🏔 Landscapes & nature

"Subject: mountain lake at sunrise. Motion: clouds drifting slowly, gentle ripples on water, birds crossing the sky. Camera: slow pan right. Lighting: golden hour glow. Style: cinematic."

Rules: layer 2–3 ambient motions; nature is forgiving, so go bigger here.

📦 Products

"Subject: red perfume bottle on marble. Motion: slow 360 rotation, light glint sweeping across glass, mist rising. Camera: orbit around subject. Lighting: studio softbox. Style: commercial photography."

Rules: orbit/rotation moves sell products; add a light sweep for premium feel.

🐶 Animals

"Subject: corgi puppy in a garden. Motion: head tilt, ears perking, tail wagging, tongue out panting. Camera: static. Lighting: bright daylight. Style: photorealistic."

Rules: animals animate well — stack 2–3 small behaviors; keep the camera static for cuteness.

5 prompt killers to avoid

  1. "Make it move" — too vague; the AI guesses randomly.
  2. Five actions at once — motion budget is limited; pick 1–3.
  3. Camera + subject moving together — choose one hero of motion.
  4. Contradicting physics — "frozen waterfall flowing" confuses the model.
  5. Over 500 characters — most free tools truncate; stay concise.
