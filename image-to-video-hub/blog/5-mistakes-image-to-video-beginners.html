5 Mistakes Image-to-Video Beginners Make (and How to Fix Them)

Your first AI-animated photos probably looked... off. Everyone's do. Here are the five mistakes that cause 90% of bad results — and the fixes that make clips look professional.

1. Prompting "make it move"

Vague prompts give the model no direction, so it invents random motion — usually mush. Fix: name one specific motion ("head turns slowly left, hair swaying"). Use the Motion Prompt Generator to build precise prompts in clicks.

2. Animating low-res or cluttered photos

Blurry source images produce blurry motion, and text overlays warp into garbage. Fix: start at 1080p+, center the subject, remove text first. Full prep list in How to Animate Photos with AI.

3. Too much motion in one prompt

Asking for five actions burns the model's motion budget and everything slides unnaturally. Fix: one hero motion + one ambient motion max. See the formulas in Prompts That Work.

4. Using the wrong tool for the subject

Animating a portrait with a tool tuned for landscapes gives you face soup. Fix: portraits → Kling 1.6; products → Runway; landscapes → Luma. Compare free plans in our Tool Comparator.

5. Forgetting the AI disclosure

Platforms increasingly require AI-generated labels, and skipping them risks takedowns. Fix: enable the AI label at upload on every platform — every time.

Bonus: verify your clip

Run your finished video through the AI Video Detector to see how strongly it reads as AI-generated — great for calibrating how natural your results look.
