Indonesia THR 2026: The Complete Guide to the Tunjangan Hari Raya Holiday Allowance

THR (Tunjangan Hari Raya) is Indonesia's mandatory religious holiday allowance — one of the most searched employee rights in the country. This guide explains exactly who gets it, how much, and when.

Every year, in the weeks before the big religious holidays, Indonesian workers ask the same question: how much THR will I get? The Tunjangan Hari Raya — literally the "religious holiday allowance" — is one of the strongest worker protections in Indonesian labour law. It is not a bonus. It is not a gift from a generous employer. It is a mandatory payment that every employer must make to qualifying workers, on time and in full.

This guide explains the whole scheme: the legal basis, who qualifies, the exact formula, what counts as your wage, when the money must land, what happens to employers who don't pay, and how THR is taxed. At the end you'll also find a checklist for what to do if your THR never arrives.

The Legal Basis: Two Regulations to Know

THR is governed by Minister of Manpower Regulation No. 6 of 2016 on Religious Holiday Allowances for Workers in Companies. The regulation sits inside the broader Manpower framework, and its enforcement teeth come from Government Regulation No. 36 of 2021 on wages. Together they say three things that matter:

1. Employers must pay THR to every worker who has worked continuously for at least one month. 2. The amount follows a fixed formula based on length of service. 3. It must be paid no later than seven days before the religious holiday.

Note the word "religious" — THR is not only for Eid al-Fitr (Lebaran), although that is when most Indonesians receive it. The regulation covers each worker's own religious holiday: Christmas for Christians, Nyepi for Hindus, Vesak for Buddhists, and others. In practice, Lebaran dominates because it affects the largest share of the workforce, and most public discussion of THR happens in the Ramadan run-up.

Who Qualifies for THR?

The qualifying bar is deliberately low. You are entitled to THR if:

  • You have an employment relationship with the company — permanent (PKWTT), fixed-term contract (PKWT), part-time, or casual daily work all count.
  • You have worked continuously for at least one month before the holiday.

That's it. There is no minimum salary, no probation exclusion, and no requirement to be a permanent employee. Contract workers who meet the one-month minimum get THR under the same formula. Part-time employees are entitled to THR calculated proportionally to their working arrangement.

The THR Formula

The formula has two branches, based on length of continuous service immediately before the holiday:

12 months or more of continuous service1 full month's wage
1 month to under 12 months(months of service ÷ 12) × 1 month's wage
Less than 1 monthNo entitlement

Worked Example

A worker earns IDR 7,000,000 per month (basic salary plus fixed allowances) and has worked for 9 months before Eid:

THR = (9 ÷ 12) × IDR 7,000,000 = IDR 5,250,000

The same worker with 12 or more months of service receives the full IDR 7,000,000. And a colleague who started only three weeks before the holiday receives nothing — the one-month minimum is a hard floor.

What Counts as "One Month's Wage"?

This is where most confusion — and most disputes — happen. The regulation defines one month's wage as the basic salary plus fixed allowances: payments the company makes regularly with a fixed nominal amount. Typical fixed allowances include child allowances, housing allowances, and regional allowances.

What is excluded: variable or irregular payments such as transport allowances tied to attendance, performance bonuses, commissions, and overtime pay. If your contract uses an all-in "clean wage" (gaji bersih) with no separate allowances, that full figure is the basis.

Practical tip: check your payslip's fixed components. If HR calculates your THR on basic salary alone while you receive a fixed monthly housing allowance, the THR is understated — politely ask for a breakdown.

Casual Daily Workers

For workers on daily agreements (harian lepas), the "one month's wage" is the average monthly earnings over the last 12 months — or the monthly average over the actual shorter working period. The same 12-month threshold and proportional formula apply.

When and How THR Must Be Paid

Three hard rules govern payment:

  • Deadline: no later than 7 days before the employee's religious holiday.
  • Form: in money, in Indonesian Rupiah — parcels, hampers, or goods do not count as THR.
  • Lump sum: it must be paid in full, not in instalments.

Many companies pay earlier — two to four weeks before Lebaran is common — because workers need the money for travel and preparations. Early payment is fine; late payment is not.

Penalties for Not Paying

Employers who fail to pay THR, or pay it late, face real consequences under the enforcement framework:

  • A fine of 5% of the unpaid THR amount.
  • Administrative sanctions escalating from written warnings to temporary or even full suspension of business activities.

Workers can report non-payment or late payment to the local Manpower office (Disnaker). In practice, most disputes are resolved after a Disnaker summons — few employers want the sanctions process to run its course.

Is THR Taxed?

Yes. THR is taxable income in Indonesia and is included in the PPh 21 withholding calculation. It is taxed as irregular income on top of your regular salary, which is why the net THR hitting your account is slightly less than the gross figure. Your employer's payroll should handle the withholding; if you file your own annual return, the THR appears as part of your total employment income.

What to Do If Your THR Doesn't Arrive

If the 7-day deadline passes without payment, work through these steps:

1

Ask HR in writing

Request a written explanation and payment date. Keep copies of everything.

2

Check with colleagues

If the whole workforce is unpaid, a joint complaint carries more weight.

3

Report to Disnaker

File a complaint with your local Manpower office, which can summon the employer.

THR and Your Other Rights

THR is a separate entitlement from your salary, your 13th-month-style company bonuses, and your severance rights. However, the same "one month's wage" concept (basic + fixed allowances) is used in both THR and severance (UP/UPMK) calculations under the Job Creation Law — so understanding your wage components pays off twice.

One more useful fact: if your employment agreement, company regulation, or collective labour agreement sets a higher holiday payment than the statutory formula, the company must pay the higher amount. The regulation is a floor, not a ceiling — many large employers pay above it.

Why THR Moves the Whole Economy

THR is more than a worker right — it's a macroeconomic event. Every year, the Lebaran THR payout injects trillions of rupiah into household spending within a few weeks: mudik travel home, new clothes, food, and gifts. Economists and business groups watch the THR cycle the way retailers watch holiday seasons elsewhere, because for millions of families it is the largest single cash inflow of the year.

That scale is exactly why enforcement tightens around Ramadan. The Manpower Ministry typically opens THR complaint posts and publicly reminds employers of the 7-day deadline each year, and late payers face the 5% fine plus sanctions. For workers, the practical lesson is timing: if your THR hasn't arrived a week before the holiday, don't wait — start the written inquiry immediately, because Disnaker complaint channels get busiest in the final days.

THR Across Different Religious Holidays

While Lebaran dominates the conversation, the regulation entitles each worker to THR for their own religious holiday. In workplaces with mixed workforces, HR departments stagger payments: Muslim employees before Eid al-Fitr, Christian employees before Christmas, Hindu employees before Nyepi, Buddhist employees before Vesak. The formula and deadline are identical — only the date shifts.

If your company pays everyone on the Lebaran schedule regardless of religion, that is administratively convenient but not what the regulation literally requires. In practice few workers complain about early payment, but the entitlement technically attaches to your holiday, and the 7-day deadline is measured from it.

Frequently Asked Questions

My company pays a Lebaran bonus bigger than the THR formula. Which applies?

The higher amount. The statutory formula is the minimum; any agreement, company regulation or collective agreement that sets a bigger holiday payment overrides it.

I resigned two months before Eid. Do I still get THR?

THR is for workers employed at the holiday. If your employment ended before the holiday period, you are generally not entitled — but check your separation settlement, because accrued rights are sometimes negotiated in.

Can my employer pay THR in two instalments?

No. The regulation requires payment in full, in money, no later than 7 days before the holiday. Instalments do not satisfy the rule.

I'm a part-time worker. Is my THR reduced?

Part-time workers are entitled to THR calculated proportionally to their working hours relative to full-time, using the same service-length formula.

Does THR apply to company directors?

THR applies to workers in an employment relationship. Commissioners and directors without an employment relationship fall outside the scheme; those employed as workers are covered like anyone else.
