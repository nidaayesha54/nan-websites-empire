How the math works

First, your annual spending is grown by inflation to your retirement year: E × (1 + i)n. Then we compute the lump sum needed at retirement to fund that spending through age 95 — the present value of an annuity: Nest egg = Eret × [1 − (1+r)−m] / r, where m is years in retirement. Finally, the monthly savings figure is the payment that grows to the nest egg at your expected return.

Nest egg = E·(1+i)n · [1 − (1+r)−m] / r  ·  Monthly = Nest egg · (r/12) / ((1+r/12)12n − 1)

Tips

  • Social Security will cover part of your spending — subtract your expected benefit from expenses for a lower target.
  • Healthcare is the wildcard: Fidelity estimates a 65-year-old couple needs roughly $330,000 for medical costs in retirement.
  • Re-run the numbers yearly; small changes in return assumptions swing the target a lot.
