How the math works

Claiming before your full retirement age of 67 shrinks your check: 5/9 of 1% per month for the first 36 months early, then 5/12 of 1% per month. That is why age 62 pays ~70%. Each year you delay past 67 adds 8% delayed retirement credits until 70 (~124%). Lifetime totals multiply the monthly benefit by the years from claiming age to life expectancy.

Tips

  • If you are married, coordinate: often the higher earner delays to 70 to maximize the survivor benefit.
  • Claiming early while still working can trigger the earnings test, temporarily reducing benefits.
  • Break-even between 62 and 70 is typically around age 78–80 — live longer and waiting wins.
