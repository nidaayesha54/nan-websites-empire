When Should You Take Social Security: 62, 67, or 70?

Updated 2026-10-12

When should you take Social Security? Compare claiming at 62 vs 67 vs 70, break-even ages, and who should wait.

The three numbers that matter

With a full retirement age of 67: claim at 62 and you get ~70% of your full benefit for life; at 67 you get 100%; wait until 70 and you get ~124%. There is no benefit to waiting past 70. Try the slider in our Social Security timing analyzer to see your own numbers.

The break-even math

Claiming at 62 vs 70 is a bet on longevity. With a $2,000 FRA benefit: 62 pays $1,400/mo, 70 pays $2,480/mo. The 70-claimer catches up around age 78–80 and pulls ahead every year after. Live to 90 and waiting was worth roughly $200,000+ in extra lifetime benefits.

When claiming early makes sense

  • Health issues or family history suggest a shorter lifespan.
  • You need the income and have no other bridge funds.
  • You are the lower-earning spouse and the higher earner is delaying (maximizing the survivor benefit).

When waiting wins

  • You are healthy with longevity in the family.
  • You can fund ages 62–70 from savings or part-time work.
  • You are the higher earner — your delayed benefit becomes your spouse's survivor benefit.

Watch the earnings test

Claim before FRA while earning above ~$23,400 (2026) and Social Security withholds $1 for every $2 over the limit. It is not lost forever — benefits are recalculated at FRA — but it can surprise early claimers.

Bottom line

Single and average health? The math favors waiting if you can afford the bridge years. Compare your scenarios in the timing analyzer before you file — the decision is largely irreversible after 12 months.
