401(k) vs IRA: Which Is Better in 2026?

Updated 2026-10-12

401(k) vs IRA in 2026: contribution limits, taxes, employer match, and which account to fund first — explained in plain English.

The one-sentence answer

Fund the 401(k) up to the employer match first, then an IRA, then back to the 401(k). That order captures free money, then the IRA's flexibility, then the 401(k)'s high limits.

2026 contribution limits

  • 401(k): $24,500 elective deferrals ($31,000 if 50+); $70,000 total with employer contributions.
  • Traditional/Roth IRA: $7,500 ($8,600 if 50+), but deductibility and Roth eligibility phase out at higher incomes.

Where each account wins

401(k) advantages

  • Employer match — an instant 50–100% return you cannot get in an IRA.
  • Much higher contribution limits for big savers.
  • Automatic payroll deduction (you never see the money).

IRA advantages

  • Unlimited investment choice — any stock, bond, or fund, vs your plan's menu.
  • Usually lower fees than 401(k) plans.
  • Roth IRA: tax-free withdrawals and penalty-free access to contributions anytime.

The funding order that works for most people

  1. 401(k) to the full match — see what compounding does in our 401(k) projector.
  2. Max a Roth IRA (if eligible) for tax diversification.
  3. Back to the 401(k) up to the max.
  4. Taxable brokerage for the rest.

Roth vs traditional within each

This is a separate decision from 401(k)-vs-IRA: it is about when you pay tax. Our guide to Roth vs traditional 401(k) walks through the decision rule.

Bottom line

Do not leave match money on the table, use the IRA for flexibility and fund choice, and let the 401(k)'s big limits do the heavy lifting after that.
