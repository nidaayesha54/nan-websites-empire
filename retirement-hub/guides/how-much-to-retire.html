How Much Do You Need to Retire Comfortably in 2026?

Updated 2026-10-12

How much do you need to retire comfortably in 2026? Learn the 25x rule, the 4% rule, and how inflation changes your number.

The short answer: 25x your annual spending

The most-used rule of thumb in retirement planning is simple: save 25 times what you plan to spend in one year. Spend $60,000 a year in retirement? Your target is about $1.5 million in today's dollars. This is the inverse of the famous 4% rule — withdraw 4% in year one, adjust for inflation after, and history says the money lasts 30 years.

Why "today's dollars" matters

$1.5 million sounds big until inflation shrinks it. At 3% inflation, $1.5 million of purchasing power today requires about $2.43 million in 30 years. That is why our retirement calculator grows your spending by inflation first, then computes the nest egg — most people underestimate their number by ignoring this step.

Adjust the rule to your life

  • Retiring early? Use 30–33x spending (a 3–3.3% withdrawal rate) for a 40+ year retirement.
  • Big Social Security check? Subtract expected benefits from your spending before multiplying. A $24,000/year benefit cuts a $1.5M target by $600,000.
  • High-cost city? Housing dominates retirement budgets; relocating can cut your number by six figures.

The savings rate that gets you there

Rules of thumb for targets are useless without a savings plan. Financial planners often suggest saving 15% of income (including employer match) starting in your 20s or 30s. Starting at 35 with a 7% return, 15% of a $90,000 salary plus a typical match grows to roughly $1.5M by 65 — run your own numbers in our 401(k) projector.

Don't forget healthcare

Fidelity estimates a 65-year-old couple retiring in 2026 needs around $330,000 for healthcare alone. An HSA — triple tax-advantaged — is the best tool for this bucket if you have a high-deductible plan.

Bottom line

Start with 25x spending, adjust for inflation, subtract Social Security, and save 15% along the way. Then personalize it: calculate your exact number and revisit it every year.
