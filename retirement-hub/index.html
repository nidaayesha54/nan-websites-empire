Frequently asked questions

How much do I need to retire?

A common rule of thumb is 25 times your annual retirement spending (the inverse of the 4% rule). If you will spend $60,000 per year, target roughly $1.5 million in today's dollars — then adjust for inflation to your retirement year with our retirement calculator.

What is the 4% rule?

The 4% rule suggests you can withdraw 4% of your portfolio in the first year of retirement, then adjust for inflation each year, with low odds of running out over 30 years. It is a planning guideline, not a guarantee.

How much should I contribute to my 401(k)?

At minimum, contribute enough to capture the full employer match — it is an instant 50–100% return. Many planners suggest 15% of income (including the match) as a baseline savings rate.

Is it better to claim Social Security at 62 or 70?

Claiming at 62 permanently reduces your benefit to about 70% of your full amount; waiting until 70 raises it to about 124%. If you expect an average or longer lifespan, waiting usually pays more over your lifetime.

Roth or traditional 401(k)?

Choose Roth if you expect to be in a higher tax bracket in retirement than today; choose traditional if you expect a lower bracket. When in doubt, splitting contributions hedges your tax bet.
