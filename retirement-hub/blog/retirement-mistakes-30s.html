5 Retirement Mistakes to Avoid in Your 30s

Updated 2026-10-12

The 5 biggest retirement mistakes people make in their 30s — and how to fix each one before it costs you six figures.

Your 30s are the highest-leverage decade in retirement planning: every dollar invested now has 30+ years to compound. Here are the five mistakes that cost thirty-somethings six figures.

1. Leaving the employer match on the table

A 50% match on 6% of salary is a 3% raise you throw away every year you skip it. Over 30 years at 7%, that neglected match alone compounds to hundreds of thousands. Contribute at least to the match — today.

2. Keeping everything in the default fund

Target-date funds are fine, but many 401(k)s default to ultra-conservative options. At 35, you can weather volatility — check that your allocation matches a 30-year horizon, not a 3-year one.

3. Ignoring fees

A 1% annual fee vs 0.1% sounds trivial. On $500,000 over 25 years at 7%, it is the difference of roughly $200,000. Favor low-cost index funds; your plan's fee disclosure is worth 10 minutes.

4. No Roth diversification

All-traditional savings is a tax time bomb if rates rise. Even small Roth contributions in your 30s — when earnings (and brackets) are often lower — buy valuable tax-free income later. See Roth vs traditional.

5. Lifestyle inflation eating every raise

The fix that requires zero sacrifice: save half of every raise. A 3% raise becomes 1.5% more spending and 1.5% more saving — your lifestyle still grows, and your savings rate climbs on autopilot.

The fix in one afternoon

Raise contributions to the match, check your fund allocation and fees, open a Roth IRA, and automate half-raise savings. Then calculate your number so you know exactly what you are aiming at.
