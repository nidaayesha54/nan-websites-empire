Roth vs Traditional 401(k): How to Choose in 2026

Updated 2026-10-12

Roth vs traditional 401(k): how to choose in 2026 based on your tax bracket now vs in retirement, with a simple decision rule.

The decision rule

Pay tax now (Roth) or later (traditional)? Choose Roth if your tax rate in retirement will be higher than today; traditional if it will be lower. Young earners early in their careers usually win with Roth; peak earners usually win with traditional.

How they differ

  • Traditional: pre-tax contributions cut today's tax bill; withdrawals taxed as income in retirement. Required minimum distributions start at 75.
  • Roth 401(k): after-tax contributions; qualified withdrawals (59½ + 5-year rule) are tax-free, including all growth. No RMDs for the original owner.

When Roth wins

  • You are in the 12% bracket now and expect 22%+ later.
  • You want tax-free income to manage Medicare IRMAA brackets in retirement.
  • You expect a pension or large traditional balance already pushing up future taxable income.

When traditional wins

  • You are in the 32%+ bracket now and expect lower income in retirement.
  • You need the deduction today to afford maxing contributions.
  • You plan early retirement with low-income Roth-conversion years.

The hedge: split it

Uncertain? Split contributions — say 50/50. Tax diversification is the only free lunch here: it lets future-you withdraw strategically from whichever bucket is cheapest each year.

Bottom line

Match your choice to your lifetime tax curve, not this year's refund. And whichever you pick, the 401(k) projector shows what decades of compounding do with it.
