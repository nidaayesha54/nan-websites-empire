GTA 6: What to Buy First (Tools Before Cosmetics)

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and money matters more in this game than in any GTA before it. Story missions can ask you to earn a set amount before they will start, cash in your pocket is lost if you die, and the opening hours punish waste in a way the series never has. So the question every new player asks — what should I buy first? — has a real answer, and it is not the one the game's shops want you to hear. This guide gives you a spending order that works: tools before cosmetics, storage before style, and a cash reserve before anything fun.

The order matters because the game tests it. Chapter 2 has a mission called "Prepare for Vice City" that asks you to make at least 15,000 dollars. If you spend your whole bankroll on a paint job and a designer jacket in Chapter 1, you will be grinding for that gate instead of walking through it. Spend like the next job depends on it, because it does.

Rule One: Tools Before Cosmetics

The principle behind every purchase in this guide is simple: buy equipment that removes a current obstacle. A tool that lets you take a better job is worth its price; a cosmetic that only looks good is not, at least not yet. The game's own reporting backs this up. Preview coverage has shown that money gates the story, that fences pay for stolen goods, and that the right equipment opens up targets you cannot touch without it. A key cloner, for example, is an access purchase — it does not print money by itself, but it gets you into cars that do.

So the order is: required tools first, suitable weapons and armour second, a cash reserve third, and luxury purchases last — funded from money left over after your earning setup is covered. If buying a luxury item leaves you unable to fund the work that replaces its cost, postpone it. That is the whole discipline, and the difference between a player who is always broke and one who is always ready.

The Spare Outfit: The Cheapest Life-Saver

The first purchase on the list sounds ridiculous, and it might save you more trouble than almost anything else: a spare outfit, kept in the trunk of your car. Not designer clothes — just a different outfit or two. The reason is the wanted system. In GTA 6, the police act on what they witness, and they build a picture of the suspect: the clothes, the car, the direction you were heading. Change your clothes after a crime and the police no longer match the description they were given.

It is cheap, it is easy, and it pairs with the other identity tricks the game rewards: changing your hairstyle, putting a mask on or taking one off, swapping cars, and splitting up Jason and Lucia. A spare outfit in the trunk is the foundation of all of it, and it costs almost nothing. Buy it before you buy anything that shoots.

Weapons: Loot First, Buy Second

Do not buy every gun. Preview coverage has shown that defeated enemies drop their weapons, and you can loot them for free — including ammunition — instead of buying every gun and ammo box at the store. The game gives you gear through the story too, so a big early shopping trip mostly buys you duplicates of things you were about to get for nothing.

When you do spend on weapons, spend narrowly: weapon slots are limited, so one good gun beats many mediocre ones. Pick a favourite, upgrade it, and consider a suppressor — a quieter shot is a smaller witness problem. And buy body armour: even ordinary robberies can turn into shootouts. Cover the demands of the job you intend to attempt, and nothing more, until you know what the next job needs.

Cars: Steal, Fence, Repeat

Do not buy a car early. Steal one instead. You can hijack actively driven vehicles without any tools, and fences pay around 21,000 dollars for stolen vehicles — though repairs and damage affect the payout, so weigh the costs before you deliver a wreck. The loop is the early game's economy in miniature: steal, deliver, get paid, repeat. And it is also why storage matters: without a garage, the cars you want to keep are harder to hold on to.

That is where the first real investment comes in: garages and storage, including gun lockers, are valuable early purchases, because they support car theft and gun management — the two things the early game is actually about. A dependable getaway vehicle matters too, and it does not need to be expensive: it needs to be yours, repaired, and ready. A car you own, with a spare outfit in its trunk, is a plan.

The 15,000-Dollar Gate and the Cash Rules

Three money rules govern everything else, and they are worth learning before you spend a dollar. First, cash in your pocket can be lost. Post-launch guides report that you can carry up to 20,000 dollars, and that physical cash is lost if you die while carrying it — so deposit it at an ATM, where banked money is safe. Chapter 1 walks you through this in the "Buck Me" mission, and the habit should stick for the whole game.

Second, loot is a different problem from cash: duffel-bag loot cannot be deposited and has to go to a fence, and some fences unlock only with story progress — so early loot may be unsellable until you meet the right contact. Do not plan a purchase around loot you cannot sell yet. And third, the gate: Chapter 2's "Prepare for Vice City" asks you to make at least 15,000 dollars, so do not spend your whole bankroll right before it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the first thing to buy in GTA 6?

A spare outfit to keep in your trunk. It is cheap, and changing clothes after a crime stops the police matching you to the suspect description. After that, body armour, a garage and a gun locker — tools and storage before cosmetics.

Should I buy weapons and cars straight away?

No. Loot weapons from defeated enemies for free, steal cars instead of buying them, and save your money for the 15,000 dollars Chapter 2 asks for. The story hands you gear, so big early purchases mostly duplicate what you were about to get anyway.

The Bottom Line

Buy in this order: a spare outfit for the trunk, body armour, a garage and a gun locker, then the tools your next job actually needs — and keep a cash reserve for the 15,000-dollar gate in Chapter 2. Loot your guns, steal your cars, bank your cash at an ATM, and let the paint jobs and designer clothes wait until your earning setup is paid for. Tools earn money back. Cosmetics do not.
