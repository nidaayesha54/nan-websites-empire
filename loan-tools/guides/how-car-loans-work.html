How Car Loans Work: A Complete Guide

By NAN Marketing Agency · Updated October 12, 2026 · 7 min read

A car loan is simple on the surface — borrow, pay back with interest — but the details decide whether you overpay by thousands. Here is everything that matters.

The Basics

You borrow the vehicle price minus your down payment and trade-in value, then repay over 36–72 months at a fixed APR. The car itself is collateral: miss payments and the lender can repossess it.

Down Payment: 20% Is the Gold Standard

New cars lose ~20% of value in the first year. Put down at least 20% (10% for used) so you never owe more than the car is worth — being "underwater" traps you when you need to sell or the car is totaled.

Term Length: Shorter Saves Thousands

A $25,000 loan at 6.5% costs about $2,600 in interest over 48 months but $4,300 over 72 months — and the longer you stretch, the longer you are underwater. Choose the shortest term whose payment fits your budget. Model it with our auto loan calculator.

Dealer Financing vs Bank/Credit Union

  • Credit unions consistently offer the lowest auto rates — get preapproved before you shop.
  • Banks are competitive and convenient if you already bank there.
  • Dealer financing can match or beat both — but dealers may mark up the rate they quote you. A preapproval gives you a rate to beat.

Add-Ons to Refuse (or Negotiate Hard)

  • Extended warranties priced at 2–3x their value.
  • Paint protection, VIN etching, and "nitrogen" tires at absurd markups.
  • GAP insurance at dealer prices — your own insurer sells it far cheaper.

Anything rolled into the loan accrues interest for years. Say no, then buy what you actually want elsewhere.
