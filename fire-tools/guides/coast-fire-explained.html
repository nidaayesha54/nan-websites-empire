Coast FIRE Explained

By FIRETools · October 2026 · 6 min read

Coast FIRE is the most underrated milestone in personal finance: the day your retirement is fully funded and you don't have to save another dollar for it. Work becomes a choice, not a requirement.

The definition

You've hit Coast FIRE when your current invested savings, compounding untouched until traditional retirement age, will reach 25× your annual expenses. From that day, every paycheck only needs to cover today's bills.

A concrete example

Age 35 with $200,000 invested, retiring at 60, 7% real returns: $200,000 × 1.0725 ≈ $1,085,000. If you spend $40,000/year in retirement, your FI number is $1,000,000 — congratulations, you've coasted. Run your own numbers in the Coast FIRE calculator.

Why Coast FIRE changes everything

  • Career freedom: take the lower-paying meaningful job; the pay cut doesn't touch retirement.
  • Risk tolerance: a sabbatical or startup no longer threatens old-age security.
  • Psychology: 'I never need to save for retirement again' is a profound stress reliever.

What Coast FIRE is not

  • It's not early retirement — you still work to cover living costs.
  • It's not guaranteed — it assumes long-run market returns cooperate.
  • It's not an excuse to stop all saving — emergency funds and short-term goals still need cash.

How to get there faster

Front-load investing in your 20s and 30s: a dollar invested at 25 is worth ~$10 at 60 (7% real). Every aggressive saving year now buys years of coasting later — model it with the savings-rate calculator.
