How to Calculate Your FIRE Number

By FIRETools · October 2026 · 7 min read

Your FIRE number is simple to compute and easy to get subtly wrong. Follow these five steps and you'll land on a number you can actually trust.

Step 1: find your real annual spending

Pull 12 months of bank and card statements and total everything — the subscriptions, the car repairs, the gifts. Most people underestimate by 15–20%. Say it comes to $60,000.

Step 2: adjust for retirement changes

  • Add: health insurance (huge pre-Medicare), travel, hobbies.
  • Subtract: mortgage if it'll be paid off, commuting costs, retirement-account contributions.

Suppose adjustments net to $65,000/year of retirement spending.

Step 3: multiply by 25

$65,000 × 25 = $1,625,000. That's the classic 4% rule number: withdraw 4% ($65,000) in year one, adjust for inflation after. Get your instant answer in the FIRE number calculator.

Step 4: pad for what the rule misses

  • Taxes on withdrawals (Roth vs traditional mix matters).
  • One-off lumpy costs: roof, car, college.
  • Sequence-of-returns risk: retiring into a bear market hurts.

Many planners add a 10–20% buffer or use 3.5% instead of 4% for retirements over 40 years — that's Fat FIRE territory.

Step 5: convert to a date

A number without a date is a dream. Plug your income, savings rate, and current investments into the savings-rate calculator to see your FI year — then watch it move closer every raise you don't spend.
