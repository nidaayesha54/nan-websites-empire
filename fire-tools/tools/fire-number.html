Where the 4% rule comes from

The Trinity study tested withdrawal rates against historical U.S. market data and found a 4%-ish withdrawal survived 30-year retirements in most scenarios. Multiply expenses by 25 and you get the classic FIRE number. Our FIRE number guide walks through the math and its limits.

Lean vs Fat FIRE

Lean FIRE (×20 expenses) means retiring on minimal spending with a higher ~5% withdrawal rate — faster, but less margin. Fat FIRE (×33) means a cushier lifestyle at ~3% — slower, but far safer. Which fits you? Compare timelines with the savings-rate calculator.

What the number doesn't include

Taxes in retirement, health insurance before Medicare, and one-off big costs (new roof, college) sit outside the basic formula — pad your number 10–20% if those worry you.
