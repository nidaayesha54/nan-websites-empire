How ROI Is Calculated

The formula is simple:

ROI = (Net Profit ÷ Cost of Investment) × 100

Net profit is the final value minus everything you put in — including the additional costs field above. Many people overstate ROI by forgetting fees, taxes and maintenance; this calculator keeps you honest.

Worked Example

You buy $10,000 of stock, pay $200 in fees, and sell two years later for $13,500:

  • Total cost = $10,000 + $200 = $10,200
  • Net profit = $13,500 − $10,200 = $3,300
  • ROI = ($3,300 ÷ $10,200) × 100 = 32.35%
  • Annualized = (13,500 ÷ 10,200)1/2 − 1 = 15.05%/year

That annualized figure is what lets you compare this trade against, say, a rental property held for 8 years. Try the CAGR calculator for deeper comparisons.

FAQ

How do I calculate ROI?

Subtract the total cost from the final value, divide by the total cost, multiply by 100. Example: ($12,000 − $10,000) ÷ $10,000 × 100 = 20% ROI.

Should additional costs be included?

Yes — true ROI includes every dollar spent: purchase price, fees, taxes, repairs and even your time if you value it. Use the "Additional Costs" field above.

Can ROI be negative?

Yes. If you get back less than you put in, ROI is negative — the calculator will show it and flag the loss in the verdict.
