CAGR smooths volatility into one steady yearly rate — useful for comparison, but remember real investments bounce around. It also ignores cash flows in and out during the period.

What Is CAGR?

CAGR — Compound Annual Growth Rate — answers one question: at what constant yearly rate would my money have needed to grow to get from the start value to the end value?

CAGR = (Ending Value ÷ Starting Value)1 ÷ Years − 1

Why It Beats Simple ROI

Simple ROI ignores time, which makes comparisons misleading:

  • Investment A: 50% return over 10 years → CAGR ≈ 4.1%/year
  • Investment B: 30% return over 2 years → CAGR ≈ 14.0%/year

Investment B is the far better performer per year, even though its headline ROI is lower. Whenever holding periods differ, use CAGR — or the main ROI calculator, which shows both figures side by side.

FAQ

What is CAGR in simple terms?

It's the steady yearly growth rate that would take an investment from its starting value to its ending value. A $10,000 investment growing to $16,105 over 5 years has a CAGR of 10%.

Why use CAGR instead of simple ROI?

Simple ROI ignores time. A 50% return over 10 years (≈4.1%/yr) is very different from 50% over 1 year (50%/yr). CAGR puts every investment on the same per-year footing.

Does CAGR account for volatility?

No — it smooths everything into one constant rate. Two investments with the same CAGR can have wildly different ride quality. Use it for comparison, not as the whole story.
