Frequently Asked Questions

What is ROI?

ROI (Return on Investment) measures how much profit an investment generated relative to its cost. The formula is: (Net Profit ÷ Cost of Investment) × 100. A $10,000 investment that returns $12,000 has an ROI of 20%.

What is a good ROI?

It depends on the asset class and risk. The S&P 500 has averaged roughly 10% per year over long periods. Real estate investors often target 8–12% cash-on-cash returns, while venture-style bets need far higher returns to justify the risk.

What is the difference between ROI and annualized ROI?

ROI is the total return over the entire holding period. Annualized ROI (also called CAGR) converts that into an equivalent yearly rate, letting you fairly compare a 6-month investment with a 5-year one.
