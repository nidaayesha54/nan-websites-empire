ROI vs ROAS: What's the Difference? (With Examples)

Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

Marketers throw around ROI and ROAS like they're interchangeable. They're not — and confusing them can make a losing campaign look like a winner. Here's the difference, plainly explained.

The Definitions

MetricStands ForFormulaWhat It Measures
ROASReturn on Ad SpendRevenue ÷ Ad SpendRevenue per $1 of ads (ignores all other costs)
ROIReturn on Investment(Net Profit ÷ Total Cost) × 100True profitability after all costs

Worked Example: The Same Campaign, Two Stories

An online store spends $5,000 on Meta ads. The campaign generates $20,000 in revenue. Product costs, shipping and payment fees total $12,000.

  • ROAS = $20,000 ÷ $5,000 = 4:1 🎉 ("Amazing!")
  • ROI = [($20,000 − $12,000 − $5,000) ÷ ($12,000 + $5,000)] × 100 = 17.6% 😐 ("Okay, actually…")

Same campaign. ROAS says "scale it!" ROI says "it's mildly profitable." Both are true — they just answer different questions.

When to Use Each

Use ROAS when…

  • Comparing ad platforms or creatives against each other (apples to apples)
  • Optimizing campaigns day-to-day inside an ad account
  • Your margins are stable, so revenue is a reliable proxy for profit

Use ROI when…

  • Deciding whether the marketing program is actually profitable
  • Reporting to a founder, CFO or client who cares about profit, not revenue
  • Comparing marketing against other investments (hiring, inventory, equipment)

Why ROAS Can Mislead

  1. It ignores cost of goods sold. A 4:1 ROAS on a 25%-margin product barely breaks even.
  2. It ignores attribution inflation. Platforms happily take credit for sales that would have happened anyway.
  3. It ignores everything else — returns, support costs, discounts used to close the sale.

A useful guardrail: compute your break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ profit margin. With 40% margins, you break even at 2.5:1 ROAS — anything below that loses money no matter how good the dashboard looks.

The Bottom Line

ROAS is a campaign-optimization metric. ROI is a business-decision metric. Great marketers track both: ROAS to tune the machine daily, ROI to prove the machine is worth running. For the full picture on ROI itself, read What Is ROI?

FAQ

What is the difference between ROI and ROAS?

ROAS = Revenue ÷ Ad Spend (revenue only, ad cost only). ROI = Net Profit ÷ Total Cost × 100 (profit after ALL costs). ROAS is always the bigger, flattering number.

What is a good ROAS?

4:1 is the classic benchmark, but "good" depends on margins. Break-even ROAS = 1 ÷ profit margin: at 25% margins you need 4:1 just to break even; at 80% margins, 1.25:1 suffices.

Can ROAS be high while ROI is negative?

Absolutely — and it happens often. A 5:1 ROAS with 15% product margins and high return rates can easily be unprofitable once all costs are counted.
