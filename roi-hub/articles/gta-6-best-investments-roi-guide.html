GTA 6 Best Investments: What Actually Pays You Back

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Money matters more in Grand Theft Auto 6 than in any GTA before it. Cash gates the story itself — missions stay locked until you raise the money they ask for — and the early game gives you very little of it, so wasting starting cash on the wrong items can make the opening hours much harder. This guide ranks the purchases that actually pay you back, from the garage that holds your cars to the spare outfit that gets you out of a police chase, and names the spending that never earns its price.

The ranking is by return, not by price. The best investment in GTA 6 is not the most expensive thing — it is the cheap thing that saves you the most trouble.

1. Garages: the only property you can buy

Buying multiple garages is one of the best investments you can make with your money in GTA 6. You can purchase garages after speaking with Moses, using the Vice City Real Estate app, which arrives on your phone in Chapter 2. Having more garages lets you store more stolen cars and increases the number of your owned car slots — you start with room for three personal vehicles, and when every space is full you have to sell a car before another can become yours.

Garages also act as a place to change your loadout, manage your weapons, improve focus, and store the illegal contraband that you later want to sell for cash. You do not have to buy every garage available in Leonida, but having a few of them spread across the map makes life much easier — and a car parked in a garage is safe, which makes a garage the best storage for stash bags you are not ready to sell yet. The return is not money in the bank. It is capacity, safety and options, which is what money is for in this game.

2. BuckMe savings: the story pays you back

Depositing money in the shared BuckMe savings account has a direct effect on the main story missions. Reaching specific BuckMe goals also brings better interest rates, so start saving from the moment you set up your account in the early game. Cash in your pocket can be lost — when your character dies, for example — while money in the BuckMe account stays safe no matter what happens, and deposits happen at a physical ATM.

The return here is twofold: safety, because banked money cannot be lost, and progress, because the story asks you to raise specific amounts before missions unlock. A player who banks after every robbery moves the story faster than a player who carries everything in their pocket and loses half of it to a bad chase.

3. Spare outfits: the cheapest getaway

The first purchase on many early-game lists sounds ridiculous, and it might save you more trouble than almost anything else: a spare outfit or two, kept in the trunk of your car. Police in GTA 6 can track you based on your description from the crime scene, and that includes the clothes you were wearing while committing the crime. Change clothes after a crime and the police no longer match your appearance to the suspect description, which makes it much easier to lose the cops and escape pursuit.

The cost is almost nothing and the return is a clean getaway without a chase, a shootout or a hospital bill. It is the best return on investment in the game by a wide margin, and it works from the first hour.

4. A silenced weapon and body armour

Weapon mods play a huge part in the game. Starting a crime with a loud weapon is one of the easiest ways of attracting the attention of police, so an early investment in a silenced pistol or a silenced assault rifle makes combat and robberies much smoother. Pair it with body armour, because even ordinary robberies can turn into shootouts, and armour is the difference between walking away and waking up in a hospital.

Buy one favourite gun and upgrade it, possibly with a suppressor, rather than owning many mediocre weapons — weapon slots are limited, and one good gun beats a shelf of average ones. And loot weapons from defeated enemies for free instead of buying every gun and ammo box at the store: the enemies you beat are a free armoury.

5. Gym membership, food, and registering your cars

A gym membership and food and drinks provide stamina and health benefits that matter for later missions — gym work raises health and regeneration speed, and food heals, with a beer restoring focus and an apple restoring health. Spend on registering a vehicle as your own, too: Moses will help you swap plates and register the vehicle in your name, which is what turns a stolen car into a car you own. Fences pay around $21,000 for a stolen vehicle, according to early reports, so a registered car is both transport and an asset, and repairs and damage affect the payout if you sell it.

Tracker removal services are worth it for the same reason: a car with a tracker is a car the law can follow. Remove the tracker, register the car, and it is yours.

What never pays you back

Do not buy every gun immediately, do not spend early cash on designer clothes, and do not buy an expensive car before you have garage space to keep it in. None of those earn their price, and the early game punishes waste. And ignore the lists claiming forty buyable properties with daily income — apartments, nightclubs, warehouses and cocaine lockups with set payouts. Those numbers are not backed by anything Rockstar has published: only garages are buyable, safehouses come with the story, businesses belong to characters, and no passive income has been announced for story mode. A purchase that pays you back is one you can measure. A list that invents the measurement is selling you a different game.

FAQ

What should I buy first in GTA 6? A spare outfit for the trunk, body armour, a silenced pistol, and a garage once the Vice City Real Estate app opens in Chapter 2. Bank the rest in BuckMe.

Can you buy businesses in GTA 6? No — nothing Rockstar has published shows a business you can buy, run or earn from in story mode. The only property on sale is garages.

How do I make money to invest? Rob stores, gangs, banks and security vans, put the loot in a duffel bag, sell it to the right fence, and deposit the cash at an ATM. Fences pay around $21,000 for a stolen car.

The bottom line: in GTA 6, the best investments are a garage, a bank balance, a spare outfit and a quiet gun. Buy those first, and let the flashy stuff wait until the story has paid for it.
