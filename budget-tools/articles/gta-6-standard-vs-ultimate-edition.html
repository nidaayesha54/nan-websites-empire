GTA 6 Standard vs Ultimate: Which Should You Buy?

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 comes in two editions, and the internet has spent months arguing about which one to buy. The Standard Edition is $79.99. The Ultimate Edition is $99.99. The $20 gap buys a long list of extra cars, weapons, shops and hideouts — but none of it is a new story chapter, and you can upgrade later anyway. The game launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and this guide settles the question with the facts from Rockstar's own edition comparison and the official store pages: what each edition includes, what the bonuses are, and the verdict that ends the debate.

The Prices, and What Is the Same

Standard is $79.99 in the US and it is the complete game. Ultimate is $99.99 and it is the same complete game plus the Ultimate Edition Upgrade. Both editions include the full story of Jason and Lucia, the whole map of Leonida, all 91 main missions, and every system in the game. Nothing in the story, the map or the main cast is held back for the more expensive version, and neither edition lets you play early — there is no early access at any price.

Both editions also come with the same two pre-order bonuses, if you bought before November 20, 2026. The Vintage Vice City Pack is a two-tone '55 Vapid Stanier sedan with its own garage near Ocean Beach, plus outfits and hairstyles for Jason and Lucia and a weapon pattern. And a free month of GTA+ — Rockstar's subscription, which includes a monthly GTA$500,000 deposit for your GTA Online character, discounted and free GTA Online vehicles, and a library of older Rockstar games — comes with digital purchases. The boxed copy, meanwhile, is a download code in a box: there is no disc in the box, and there is no boxed Ultimate Edition at all.

What the Ultimate Edition Adds

Ultimate adds what Rockstar calls an exclusive collection of 16 items, threaded across all aspects of Jason and Lucia's story, with new items unlocked chapter by chapter. The vehicles are the '95 Grotti Cheetah, a mid-90s sports car with a retro-futuristic livery; the Shitzu Squalo, a pink and blue speedboat docked at Washington Beach with a weapons crate; the '67 Vapid Dominator Buggy and Garage; and a Classic Car Collection. The weapons are the Hawk and Little Morgan Revolvers and personalised weapon variants. The hideouts are Jason's Safehouse Vehicles, the Ganado Retro Build and the PTT Youngin$ Compound. And the businesses are five shops: Rideout Customs Mod Shop, Sara's Unisex Salon, Stock 305 Clothing Store, Electric Fang Tattoo Parlor and One-Eyed Willie's Mod Shop.

Three of those shops — Rideout Customs, One-Eyed Willie's and Stock 305 — open only with the Ultimate Edition, and the PTT Youngin$ Illegal Goods Store is the one exclusive playable mission in the package: a raid on a Southside Vice City gang compound that yields special items and distinct contraband. Everything else is an item, a vehicle, a weapon or a cosmetic: useful, fun, and none of it required to finish the story. The Ultimate extras are threaded through the game, not gated behind it — they turn up as you play, chapter by chapter, rather than arriving all at once.

The Upgrade Path: The Fact That Ends the Debate

Here is the fact that makes the whole argument unnecessary. The Ultimate content is sold separately as a $20 upgrade on the Xbox Store, and Rockstar says you can upgrade at any point — so you can start with Standard and add the rest later for the same total. Standard at $79.99 plus the upgrade at $20 is $99.99, exactly the price of Ultimate. There is no penalty for waiting, no discount for buying upfront, and no exclusive that disappears if you do not buy it on day one.

That means the decision is not really a decision. Buy Standard now. Play the game. If the Ultimate extras call to you — if you want the Cheetah, the Squalo, the shops, the mission — buy the upgrade later, when you know which of the sixteen items you actually want. If they do not call to you, you have saved $20 and lost nothing. The only wrong move is buying Ultimate for the fear of missing out, because there is nothing to miss: the upgrade will still be there next month, at the same price, for the same items.

The One Real Deadline

The only deadline that matters is the pre-order bonus cutoff, and it has already passed for most readers: purchases before November 20, 2026, include the Vintage Vice City Pack and a month of GTA+. If you bought before the cutoff, you have both bonuses regardless of which edition you chose. If you are buying after launch day, the bonuses are gone — and that is the one real cost of waiting. It is not a reason to panic-buy Ultimate. It is a reason to buy the game when you are ready, and to accept that the launch-day bonuses were for launch-day buyers.

There is one more thing to check before you buy: the format. The boxed copy is the Standard Edition as a download code — no disc, no boxed Ultimate. If you want a physical object on your shelf, the box is a code in a box. If you want Ultimate, it is digital only. Buy the format that matches how you actually play, not the one that looks best on a shelf.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Ultimate Edition worth $20 more?

For most players, no — not upfront. The story is identical, and you can buy the same $20 upgrade later for the same total. Buy Standard, play the game, and upgrade later only if the extras appeal to you once you have seen them in context.

Does either edition include early access?

No. Neither edition lets you play early, and there is no early access at any price. Both editions unlock at midnight local time on launch day, and the preload does not change that.

Is there a physical disc version of GTA 6?

No. The boxed copy contains a download code, not a disc, and there is no boxed Ultimate Edition. The game is 212 GB, and it downloads either way — the box just holds the code.

The Bottom Line

Buy Standard for $79.99. It is the whole game, and the Ultimate extras are a $20 upgrade you can buy later for the same price if you want them. The story, the map and the missions are identical either way — and the only thing Ultimate buys you upfront is impatience.
