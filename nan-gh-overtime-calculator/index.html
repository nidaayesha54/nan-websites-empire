Ghana Overtime Rules at a Glance

  • Normal working hours: 8 hours per day and 40 hours per week (§33–38, Labour Act 2003, Act 651). Anything beyond is overtime.
  • The Act does not fix a statutory rate. Your employer must fix the overtime rate and make it known to you before you work overtime — no one can compel you to work overtime where no rate has been fixed.
  • Customary practice: 150% (1.5×) of the normal hourly rate for weekday overtime; rest-day and public-holiday work is commonly paid at 150%–200%, with 200% standard for casual/temporary workers on public holidays (§77).
  • Hourly rate: monthly basic salary ÷ 26 working days ÷ 8 hours — the common Ghana payroll convention.
  • Pregnant women and mothers of children under 8 months may not be asked to work overtime without their express consent.
  • Emergencies (accidents threatening lives or the survival of the business) are the only case where overtime may legally be required without extra pay.
  • Tax: overtime up to 50% of your monthly basic salary is taxed at a flat 5%; anything above that falls under normal progressive PAYE.
  • Unpaid overtime? File a complaint with the National Labour Commission (NLC), which can order your employer to pay.

FAQs

Does Ghana's Labour Act set the overtime rate?

No. The Act (§33–38) sets 8 hours/day and 40 hours/week as normal working hours, but leaves the overtime rate to be fixed by the employer — it must be set and communicated before the overtime is worked. 150% of the hourly rate is the widespread customary practice.

How is my hourly overtime rate calculated?

The common Ghana payroll convention is: monthly basic salary ÷ 26 working days ÷ 8 hours. Your employer may use a slightly different divisor — check your payslip.

What is the rate for rest days and public holidays?

There is no statutory rate for permanent workers — it is negotiated, with 150%–200% common. For casual and temporary workers, work on a public holiday is legally set at 200% of the normal rate (§77).

Is overtime taxed in Ghana?

Yes. Overtime pay up to 50% of your monthly basic salary is taxed at a flat 5%; amounts above that are taxed under the normal progressive PAYE bands.

Can my employer force me to work overtime?

No — you cannot be compelled to work overtime except where the viability of the business requires it, or in emergencies threatening life or property. And if no overtime rate has been fixed and disclosed, overtime cannot be required at all.

What if my employer refuses to pay overtime?

Report to the National Labour Commission (NLC). Labour inspectors can also visit workplaces to enforce hours-of-work and wage provisions under the Act.
