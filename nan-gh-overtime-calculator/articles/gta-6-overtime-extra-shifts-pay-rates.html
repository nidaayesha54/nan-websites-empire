GTA 6 Overtime: The Extra Shifts That Pay After Hours

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and it starts you broke. Jason and Lucia begin the story with almost nothing, and the story missions alone do not pay enough to cover what the game asks you to buy — garages, cars, weapons, clothes. The money in GTA 6 comes from the extra shifts: the repeatable work you do between missions, the jobs that pay by the run, by the car and by the delivery, and the grind that turns an empty wallet into a bank balance.

That matters because the overtime in GTA 6 is not one job — it is a ladder, and every rung pays differently. A hunting trip pays $500. A car pays $1,000 or more. A delivery run pays $20,000. A bank vault pays up to $205,000. Knowing which shift to work, and when each one unlocks, is the difference between grinding for hours and getting paid properly for your time.

The Small Shifts: Hunting, Taxis and Pawn Shops

The smallest paid work in GTA 6 is also the safest. Hunting with Bret unlocks in Chapter 2, and each hunting trip pays $500 through BuckMe — it matters more for the 100% completion checklist than for your balance, but it is steady, legal and repeatable, and you can track target animals across Lummox County. Taxi missions pay low to medium money with very low risk, and they are repeatable, which makes them the most reliable early income for a player who wants cash without a wanted level. Delivery driving pays a medium amount with low risk, and it is a good way to learn the map while you earn.

Pawn shops are the next step up. The fastest early money in the game, according to launch-era guides, is fencing stolen luxury jewellery and electronics at Vice Beach pawn shops, at $800 to $2,500 per run — looting high-value personal valuables from parked vehicles and affluent tourists, then selling them on. Convenience store robberies pay $500 to $1,200 per store, but they come with a medium-to-high risk of a police chase, which is why they sit above pawn shops on the risk ladder and below them on the safety one. These figures come from launch-era guides and may change with patches, so treat them as reported rather than guaranteed.

The Middle Shifts: Cars and Deliveries

Car flipping is where the overtime starts paying properly. The ladder starts in Chapter 2 with Moses Watkins, who buys any untracked car you bring him for $1,000 or more, with a cooldown of about two minutes after a sale. From Chapter 3, Pay 'n' Spray buys cars with a Standard Tracker for around $4,000 each, with a limit of three cars per day. And partway through Chapter 3, after the Clonin' Keys, Makin' GS mission, Randy gives you his Wish List of 18 high-end sports cars — deliver one in perfect condition and it can pay $30,000 or more.

Deliveries are the other middle shift. Pilot Pete's delivery missions pay $20,000 per run when all seven packages land on time, and they appear in Chapter 4 as part of the Strangers and Freaks quest line, for Jason only. The missions only appear as the quest line progresses, so you cannot farm them endlessly — but $20,000 for one timed run is the best legal-ish pay in the game before the big robberies open up, and it is the shift to work when it is available.

The Big Shifts: Vaults and the Story Payday

The top of the ladder is the bank vaults. Large bank vault robberies pay up to $205,000 apiece for roughly five minutes of work, according to PowerPyx, but they only open up at the end of Chapter 4, through the optional mission That's Where The Money Is, and they come with a four-star wanted level the moment you start. Small banks pay around $40,000 to $50,000 at most, and there are 22 bank robbery locations in total — 14 small banks and 8 large ones.

Between the shifts, the story itself pays. According to PowerPyx, the story gives you $120,000 when Chapter 3 ends, which is the single biggest automatic payout in the early game. The lesson of the whole ladder is the same one the game's money guides keep repeating: a mission that pays $80,000 in eight minutes beats a mission that pays $150,000 in thirty minutes, because raw payout means nothing without time. Work the shift that pays best per hour, bank the cash at an ATM before you do anything dangerous, and remember that most of these figures come from one outlet's launch-era guides — if a payout feels too good, do not build your whole account around it, because Rockstar can change it in the next patch.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best-paying extra shift in GTA 6?

Large bank vaults, at up to $205,000 for about five minutes of work — but they only open at the end of Chapter 4. Before that, Pilot Pete deliveries at $20,000 a run and Randy's Wish List cars at $30,000 or more are the best repeatable pay.

How much does hunting pay in GTA 6?

$500 per trip through BuckMe, from Chapter 2. It matters more for the 100% completion checklist than for your balance, but it is steady and safe.

Do story missions pay enough in GTA 6?

Not on their own. The story pays $120,000 when Chapter 3 ends, but the early missions pay very little, and the game's economy is built around the extra shifts — cars, deliveries, pawn shops and, eventually, vaults.

The Bottom Line

Overtime in GTA 6 is a ladder: $500 hunting trips and taxi fares at the bottom, $1,000 to $4,000 cars in the middle, $20,000 delivery runs above them, and $205,000 vaults at the top. Work the rung you have unlocked, bank the cash before you risk it, and measure every job by what it pays per hour — because in Leonida, the shift you choose is worth more than the shift you finish.
