ICE Deportation Officer Jobs 2026: Veteran Recruitment Drive & How to Apply

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement is actively recruiting military veterans for deportation officer positions nationwide — offering priority access to the hiring process. Here is what the roles involve and how veterans apply.

On October 7, 2026, U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement took recruiting directly to veterans: the agency posted a call on its official X account urging military veterans to apply for deportation officer positions nationwide — promising priority access to the hiring process, bypassing the standard waiting period other applicants face.

The pitch was framed as continued service: “if you still hear the call to serve this country, ICE needs you,” the agency told veterans, arguing military backgrounds suit the work of removing individuals it describes as violent criminals and dangerous terrorists.

For veterans eyeing federal law enforcement, this is a genuine fast lane: an agency explicitly courting veteran applicants with expedited processing. Here is what the role involves and how to position a military background.

What Deportation Officers Do

Deportation officers serve in ICE's Enforcement and Removal Operations (ERO) — identifying, apprehending, detaining, and removing immigration-law violators; conducting investigations and interviews; preparing cases for proceedings; and coordinating with other law enforcement agencies.

It is frontline federal law enforcement: officers carry firearms, make arrests, and work cases from routine checks to fugitive operations. The veteran pitch leans on this profile — discipline, firearms familiarity, operational planning, and chain-of-command experience translate directly.

The Veteran Fast Lane

The headline benefit is priority hiring access — meaningful in federal law enforcement, where background investigations, medical exams, and fitness testing stretch timelines for months. Veterans also carry veterans' preference points on competitive announcements (with DD-214/SF-15 documentation).

Resume strategy: lead with military police, security forces, investigations, or leadership experience; quantify team sizes and operations; make any security clearance history prominent — it can accelerate suitability processing.

Pay, Locations, and Career Path

Deportation officers typically enter at GL-7/9 with promotion potential (often to GS-12), plus Law Enforcement Availability Pay (LEAP) — a 25% premium for unscheduled-duty demands — and locality pay. With LEAP, total compensation is highly competitive for entry-level federal law enforcement.

Duty stations are nationwide, and ERO careers ladder into supervisory roles, special response teams, and headquarters policy positions — with well-established veteran cohorts and mentorship networks across DHS.

How Veterans Apply for ICE Deportation Officer Jobs

  1. Find announcements on USAJOBS.gov. Search “deportation officer” filtered to Immigration and Customs Enforcement.
  2. Claim veterans' preference. Upload DD-214 (and SF-15 plus VA rating letter for 10-point claims); answer the preference questions.
  3. Translate military experience. Lead with MP, security forces, investigations, or leadership — quantify teams, operations, outcomes.
  4. Highlight clearances and fitness. Prior clearances can speed suitability; stay ready for the physical and medical requirements.
  5. Engage ICE recruiters. The October 7 call signals active veteran hiring — meet recruiters at veteran hiring events to get noticed.
