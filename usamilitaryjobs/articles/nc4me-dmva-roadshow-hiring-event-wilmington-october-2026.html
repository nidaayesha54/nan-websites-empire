NC4ME — North Carolina for Military Employment — runs hiring events built around a single idea: skip the booth-walking and go straight to a real interview. Its event on Wednesday, October 28, 2026, from 10:00 AM to 2:00 PM at the Warwick Center on the UNC Wilmington campus (601 College Road, Wilmington, NC) is the closing stop of the North Carolina Department of Military and Veteran Affairs' 2026 "Unlock the Veteran & MilSpouse Advantage" roadshow — the statewide series the DMVA confirms on its official site with Wilmington as the October stop.

This matters for one reason: NC4ME does not do resume-drop fairs. Registered candidates are pre-matched with participating employers for formal, scheduled one-on-one interviews with hiring managers who are actively looking for military-connected talent. If you are a veteran, a transitioning service member, or a military spouse in eastern North Carolina, this is one of the highest-signal events on the October calendar.

How the Pre-Matched Format Works

Most job fairs hand you a floor plan and hope for the best. NC4ME's model works the other way around: you register for the event and send your resume to info@nc4me.org, and the organization matches your background against participating employers in advance, building you a prearranged interview schedule. You walk in knowing who you are meeting, and employers walk in knowing who you are — which is why NC4ME positions its events as "high-efficiency hiring pipelines" with rapid hiring timelines.

The numbers NC4ME publishes are worth noting: the organization says more than 24,600 service members and spouses have engaged with its programs, and that over 52% of its "Career Connections" result in a job offer or second interview. Those are the organization's own figures, not an independent audit — but they explain why the registration mechanics are stricter than a walk-in fair. This is a curated interview day, not a browsing session.

The DMVA Roadshow Context

The event sits inside the NC Department of Military and Veteran Affairs' 2026 roadshow, a statewide push built on a simple statistic: more than 681,000 veterans call North Carolina home, with thousands of service members transitioning out of bases like Fort Bragg, Camp Lejeune and Seymour Johnson every year. The series has already stopped in Asheville, Winston-Salem, Charlotte and Greenville; Wilmington in October is the finale.

The roadshow's employer-facing sessions cover how military experience maps to civilian roles, federal incentives available for eligible veteran hires, onboarding and retention programs, and employer success stories — including major employers like Pfizer, Novo Nordisk, Amgen and GE Aerospace that have participated in earlier stops. The through-line for job seekers: the employers in the room are already sold on military talent, so your conversation starts from "which role" rather than "why veterans."

Timing note — check before you travel: NC4ME’s event listing asked candidates who want a prematched interview schedule to email their resume to info@nc4me.org no later than October 16, 2026. That date has now passed. If you did not register by then, contact the organizer first to ask whether late registration, standby interviews, or walk-in attendance are still possible — do not assume a spot is waiting.

Who Should Attend

The event is built exclusively for veterans, transitioning service members and military spouses. There is no single stated qualification — NC4ME events historically span skilled trades, logistics, healthcare, IT, management and operations roles, depending on which employers sign on. What matters most is that your resume is ready to be pre-matched: translate your MOS/AFSC/rating into civilian terms, lead with clearances, certifications (CDL, PMP, technical licenses) and leadership scope, and keep the format clean enough for a recruiter to match in under a minute.

Military spouses should pay particular attention: North Carolina has one of the largest active-duty military populations in the country, and the roadshow's "MilSpouse Advantage" branding means spouse-friendly employers — portable careers, remote-friendly roles, flexible scheduling — are deliberately part of the mix. For spouses who PCS frequently, landing with an employer that already understands military life can make the difference between a stopgap job and a career that survives the next move.

How to Prepare for the NC4ME Wilmington Event

  1. Confirm the event status first. The prematched-schedule resume deadline (Oct 16) has passed — check NC4ME’s event listing or email info@nc4me.org about late or standby options before making travel plans.
  2. Get your resume match-ready. One page is fine if it translates your military experience into civilian role titles, certifications and quantified achievements.
  3. Verify details on the official roadshow page. The NC DMVA confirms the Wilmington October stop at milvets.nc.gov/roadshow — use it, not third-party reposts, for the latest updates.
  4. Prepare for real interviews, not booth chat. Bring printed resumes, dress for interviews, and research likely employers — NC4ME formats skew heavily toward biotech, aerospace, logistics and healthcare employers in NC.
  5. Follow up the same week. With prearranged interviews, same-week thank-you emails to each hiring manager convert at a meaningfully higher rate than post-fair blasts.
