The Department of Veterans Affairs is recruiting Veterans Service Representatives for its Veterans Benefits Administration at regional offices across the country. Issued under Direct Hire Authority and open to U.S. citizens, this GS-7 to GS-10 career ladder has a first cutoff of November 9, 2026.

The Veterans Service Representative (VSR) is the backbone of the VA's disability and benefits pipeline: these are the federal civilians who develop, evaluate and decide veterans' benefit claims. Announced on USAJOBS this month under Direct Hire Authority, the nationwide openings are open to all U.S. citizens — not only veterans — which makes this one of the broadest VA civilian hiring pushes of the year and an especially strong fit for separating service members, veterans, and military-connected civilians who want to keep serving the veteran community.

The career ladder runs from GS-7 to GS-10 (series GS-0996), with non-competitive promotion potential up to the GS-10 full performance level once regulatory, qualification and performance requirements are met. Because applications are reviewed against cutoff dates — the first one is November 9, 2026 — this is a "apply early, not just on time" announcement.

What a Veterans Service Representative Does

VSRs handle the claims lifecycle end to end. Per the official announcements, the position:

  • Makes determinations on eligibility for the benefit sought, including questions of military service adequacy, medical evidence, income, employability, dependence and relationship
  • Requests medical examinations, re-examinations and medical opinions to evaluate disabilities resulting from diseases or injuries
  • Reviews claims files to make sure all issues have been addressed, applying and interpreting VA laws, veterans' case law, statutes, procedures and precedent rulings
  • Conducts interviews in person or by phone with veterans, representatives and advocates to explain the full range of VA benefits
  • Prepares and releases correspondence used by other federal, state and local agencies, and works daily in electronic data processing systems

The announcements also flag practical realities: selectees must attend a mandatory 8-week consecutive training session, which can require government travel to a different city or state; the position carries Public Trust — Low Risk designation with a background investigation after selection; and there may be a probationary period.

Direct Hire Authority: Why It Matters

These announcements are issued under Direct Hire Authority, which changes the calculus for applicants. Traditional competitive federal hiring ranks applicants with veterans' preference and limits who can be considered; direct hire lets the agency appoint qualified U.S. citizens more directly, without the preference ranking competition. For veterans, the advantage is different but real: mission knowledge, familiarity with service culture, and understanding of what claimants are going through are exactly what the job demands, even though eligibility itself is not restricted to veterans.

Pay, Grades, Schedules and Telework

The GS-0996 career ladder covers grades 7, 9 and 10. The announced salary band of $50,460–$82,007 reflects the 2026 "Rest of US" pay table, with actual pay set by the locality table for the duty station — so positions in high-cost areas pay meaningfully more. Work schedule is full-time, typically Monday through Friday, 8:00 AM to 4:30 PM, with compressed or flexible schedules potentially available at management's discretion depending on the office.

One point to be clear on: the announcements state this is not a remote or virtual position. Telework is limited to situational (ad hoc) approval on a case-by-case basis with supervisory sign-off; routine or recurring telework is not authorized. If you need fully remote work, this posting is not it.

Who Should Apply

The GS-07 entry level is deliberately accessible: one year of specialized experience (data entry and case-file work, reviewing documents for completeness and accuracy, communicating with diverse customers) at the GS-05 level, or one full year of graduate-level education, or superior academic achievement at the bachelor's level. That opens the door to recent graduates, administrative and customer-service professionals, paralegal and casework staff, and of course veterans and military spouses whose service included administrative, personnel or medical-record work. Higher starting grades require correspondingly more specialized experience, so read the Qualifications section of the specific announcement for the duty station you want.

Honesty check: the public announcements do not publish a single number of openings — treat this as an ongoing nationwide hiring effort rather than a fixed headcount. Duty-station details, telework flexibility and training logistics vary by location announcement, so verify specifics in the USAJOBS posting for the city you apply to.

How to Apply for the VSR Position

  1. Search USAJOBS for "Veterans Service Representative." Filter by the duty station(s) you want; there are location-specific announcements under this posting.
  2. Read the Qualifications section carefully. It spells out exactly what counts as specialized experience at each grade — match it point for point in your resume.
  3. Build a federal-style resume. USAJOBS resumes need hours worked, supervisor names and detailed duty descriptions, not a one-page private-sector version.
  4. Upload transcripts if using education to qualify. Transcripts are required when you claim education substitution or superior academic achievement.
  5. Apply before the first cutoff: November 9, 2026. Applications are reviewed in cutoff batches; waiting until the December 21 close means competing with everyone who applied earlier.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Veterans Service Representative (VSR)?

A VSR is a federal civilian in the Veterans Benefits Administration who develops and decides veterans' benefit claims — determining eligibility, requesting medical examinations and opinions, reviewing claims files, and explaining the full range of VA benefits to veterans and their advocates.

What is the deadline for the VA Veterans Service Representative hiring?

The announcement closes December 21, 2026, but the first application cutoff is November 9, 2026. Applications are reviewed as cutoffs pass, so applying before the first cutoff is strongly advised.

Do I have to be a veteran to apply for the VSR position?

No. The announcements are issued under Direct Hire Authority and are open to all U.S. citizens. Veterans are natural fits for this mission, but non-veterans with the right experience or education can apply.

What grades and salary does the VSR role offer?

VSR is a career ladder from GS-7 to GS-10 (series GS-0996), with non-competitive promotion potential to the GS-10 full performance level. The announced salary band is $50,460 to $82,007 depending on location and grade, adjusted for 2026 locality pay.

Is the Veterans Service Representative job remote?

No. The announcements state this is not a remote or virtual position, and telework is limited to situational, case-by-case approval with a supervisor. You report to the official duty station each scheduled workday.
