DoW SkillBridge Program Guide 2026: How Transitioning Service Members Land Civilian Jobs

The Department of War's SkillBridge program lets you spend your last 180 days in uniform training with a civilian employer — while still collecting military pay. Here is how eligibility, authorization, and applications work in 2026.

The hardest part of leaving the military is often the first civilian job — employers want experience, but your experience is in uniform. The Department of War's SkillBridge program bridges that gap by letting active-duty service members train, intern, or apprentice with civilian employers during their last 180 days of service.

Here is the key detail: while you train with the company, you keep receiving your military pay and benefits. The employer provides the training opportunity — real projects, real teams, real industry experience — and many participants convert their SkillBridge placement into a full-time offer.

Major defense and technology employers are authorized SkillBridge organizations through the Department of War. BAE Systems, for example, publicly participates in SkillBridge alongside its Warrior Integration Program for post-9/11 service members with service-connected injuries — and attends 45+ military outreach and recruitment events a year looking for transitioning talent.

How SkillBridge Works

SkillBridge connects service members approaching separation with civilian employers offering industry training, apprenticeships, or internships. Placements typically run during the final 180 days of active-duty service and must be approved through your chain of command and installation transition office.

The training is full-time and immersive: you show up at the company, join a team, and do real work — building the civilian resume bullets and professional references that make post-service hiring dramatically easier. Because the program is authorized at the Department level, participation is a recognized, legitimate part of your transition plan, not time off.

Eligibility and Authorization

Eligibility centers on active-duty status within 180 days of separation, with specific requirements varying by service branch — honorable service standing and command approval are standard prerequisites. Start the conversation with your chain of command and transition assistance office well before your 180-day window opens, since approvals and placement matching take time.

On the employer side, companies must be authorized SkillBridge organizations. Look for explicit SkillBridge mentions on employers' military and veterans career pages — authorized participants advertise it, because the program is a proven veteran-hiring pipeline for them too.

Beyond SkillBridge: Related Transition Programs

Some employers layer additional support on top of SkillBridge. BAE Systems' Warrior Integration Program (WIP), for instance, is a specialized integration and training program designed for post-9/11 service members with service-connected injuries transitioning into company careers. When evaluating employers, ask what wraparound support — mentorship, veteran employee resource groups, benefits navigation — comes with the placement.

How to Apply for SkillBridge

  1. Start 9–12 months before separation. Talk to your installation's transition assistance office about SkillBridge timelines for your branch — approvals take time, so do not wait for the 180-day mark.
  2. Get command buy-in early. SkillBridge requires chain-of-command approval; bring a concrete plan showing how the training supports your transition.
  3. Identify authorized SkillBridge organizations. Target employers in your desired field and confirm their authorized status on their military/veterans career pages.
  4. Apply to the employer's SkillBridge openings. Treat it like a job application: tailored resume translating your military experience, plus any required assessments or interviews.
  5. Complete the authorization paperwork. Work with your transition office to finalize approvals, training plans, and any service-specific agreements before your start date.
  6. Perform like it is a 180-day interview. Because it is — many placements convert to offers for participants who deliver.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the DoW SkillBridge program?

SkillBridge is a Department of War–authorized program that lets active-duty service members train, intern, or apprentice with civilian employers during their last 180 days of service — building civilian work experience before separation.

Who is eligible for SkillBridge?

Generally, active-duty service members within 180 days of separation with command approval. Each service branch has specific eligibility rules, so confirm details with your installation's transition office.

Do SkillBridge participants get paid?

Yes — participants continue receiving military pay and benefits while training with the civilian host organization, which provides the training opportunity.

How do I find authorized SkillBridge organizations?

Employers must be authorized SkillBridge organizations through the Department of War. Major defense employers — including companies like BAE Systems — publicly list their SkillBridge participation on their military and veterans career pages.

Can SkillBridge lead to a full-time job?

Often, yes. Many host organizations use SkillBridge as a pipeline to full-time offers — it is effectively an extended working interview where both sides evaluate fit.
