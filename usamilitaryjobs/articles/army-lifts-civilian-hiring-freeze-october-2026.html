Army Civilian Jobs Are Back: Hiring Freeze Lifted After 20 Months

The Army has resumed civilian hiring after a nearly 20-month freeze — authorized to staff up to 96% of positions after losing 43,000 civilians. Here is what changed and what it means for veterans and applicants.

The U.S. Army has resumed civilian hiring, ending a freeze that lasted nearly 20 months. In a September 25, 2026 memo, Army Undersecretary Michael Obadal authorized hiring up to 96% of the service's direct-funded full-time equivalent positions — with hiring focused on priority missions and kept within budget limits.

The restart follows staggering losses: about 43,000 civilians left the Army since January 20, 2025 against only ~10,000 hires — including 6,000+ at the Army Corps of Engineers and 4,300 at Installation Management Command.

For veterans and military-connected applicants, this is the biggest federal hiring door to reopen in two years. Here's what the memo means — and how to walk through it.

What the Memo Actually Authorized

The September 25 memo directs the Army's assistant secretary for manpower and reserve affairs to issue implementation guidance — the detailed rules commands will follow. The headline authorization: hire to 96% of direct-funded FTE, prioritizing mission-critical roles within budget.

One caveat: the governmentwide hiring freeze remains in effect, and across DoD, strategic hiring committees must still approve civilian hiring actions. The Army's restart is an authorized exception — real, but managed. The Air Force and Space Force are also planning to add civilian positions, suggesting the thaw is spreading.

The Scale of the Rebuild

Roughly 43,000 departures against 10,000 hires is a net loss of over 30,000 civilians — engineers, logisticians, HR specialists, installation staff. The Corps of Engineers' 6,000+ departures hit civil works; IMC's 4,300 hit base operations.

That gap is your opportunity. Rebuilding at this scale means sustained announcements across grades and series — not a one-time surge. Veterans, with preference points and military familiarity, are the natural first wave of hires.

What This Means for Your Application

Watch USAJOBS now. Army postings will flow as implementation guidance lands — set saved searches for Department of the Army announcements in your series and grade, with email alerts on. Priority-mission roles (engineering, logistics, cybersecurity, medical) should lead.

Claim every preference you're owed. Veterans' preference, military spouse preference, and Schedule A where applicable — bring DD-214s, SF-15s, and documentation ready. And tailor ruthlessly: with thousands of applicants expected, resumes that mirror the announcement's specialized-experience language survive the first cut.

How to Apply for Army Civilian Jobs Now

  1. Set USAJOBS saved searches today. Filter to Department of the Army, your series and grade — turn on email alerts for new postings.
  2. Target priority missions. Engineering, logistics, cybersecurity, and medical roles lead the rebuild; match your background to them.
  3. Prepare preference documents now. DD-214, SF-15, VA rating letters, spouse PCS orders — don't scramble when a posting drops.
