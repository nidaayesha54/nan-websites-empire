Army's New 40D Space Operations MOS: Apply for Space Jobs in 2026

The Army just stood up its first new enlisted branch in years: on October 1, 2026, more than 600 soldiers reclassified into the brand-new 40D Tactical Space Operations Specialist MOS. Here is what the Space Operations Branch is and how to get into it.

The Army has codified decades of space expertise into a brand-new enlisted branch. On October 1, 2026, more than 600 soldiers reclassified into the new 40D Tactical Space Operations Specialist MOS — becoming the first enlisted members of the Army Space Operations Branch, which was formally established in June 2026. It is one of the most significant new career fields the Army has created in years, and it is open to soldiers from any background.

The branch got its official insignia — a globe, sword, arrow, and orbital lightning bolt — in a digital unveiling the same day the first soldiers joined. The Army plans to keep growing: leadership told the Association of the United States Army (AUSA) conference this month it aims for roughly 1,700 space soldiers by the end of next fiscal year.

What Is the Space Operations Branch?

For years, enlisted soldiers with specialized space training rotated through critical space assignments and then returned to their basic branches. The Army decided that rotational system could not meet future demands, so it created a permanent career path for enlisted space professionals — MOS 40D — alongside the officer-side 40A designation. This transition lets the Army build a professional, sustainable workforce with real training pipelines, career progression, and leader development.

The move also relieves pressure on the donor branches. Soldiers for the first 40D cohort were drawn mainly from Military Intelligence, Air Defense Artillery, and the Signal Corps, and establishing the new branch allows those branches to redistribute soldiers to critical vacancies instead of lending them to temporary space billets.

Who Was Selected — and What It Takes

Competition for the first cohort was steep: nearly 3,000 soldiers applied and just over 600 were selected — roughly a one-in-five acceptance rate. Command Sgt. Maj. Rickey Jackson of the U.S. Army Space and Missile Defense Command (SMDC) said the branch drew the "most capable, professional soldiers in the Army" from MI, ADA, and Signal — but stressed that the 40D MOS is open for anyone to apply.

The formal path is a permanent career field for active-duty, National Guard, and Reserve soldiers in the ranks of specialist through sergeant major. The ideal candidate profile from the first selection: a soldier who has mastered their current job, stands apart from peers, and brings the kind of technical aptitude that translates to satellites, communications, and orbital operations.

What 40D Soldiers Actually Do

Tactical Space Operations Specialists are the Army’s hands-on space warfighters. Their mission includes:

  • Satellite communications: helping troops access and stay connected through satellite networks in the field
  • Navigation and positioning: delivering satellite-based global navigation support to ground forces
  • Orbital awareness: monitoring potential adversary activities in orbit
  • Space interdiction: supporting operations designed to disrupt an adversary’s space capabilities

Assignments run through the Army’s space brigades, multidomain task forces, and theater strike effects groups — and this is not a rear-echelon job. Lt. Gen. John Rafferty, commander of the Army Space and Missile Defense Command, told the AUSA conference that about 2,500 soldiers from his command were deployed across the U.S. Central Command area of operations, underlining that space support is an active part of ongoing military operations.

Space Force vs. Army Space — No, It Hasn’t Folded

You may have seen speculation that the Space Force is being folded into the Army as a "Space Corps." That has not happened. The U.S. Space Force remains a separate military service — and it is growing, too. On October 7, 2026, the Space Force activated SPACEFOR-SOC, a new service component command under U.S. Special Operations Command that will train Space Special Warfare Teams for special operations missions.

The two organizations are complementary, not redundant. The Space Force’s Guardians manage military space operations for the wider joint force, while the Army’s Space Operations Branch builds the space professionals who deliver satellite services — communications, navigation, coordination — directly to ground forces. If you want space as a career, both doors are real: 40D in the Army, or service as a Space Force Guardian.

Honesty check: the Army has not published a public, step-by-step 40D application checklist with dates the way some programs do — selection runs through Army career-management channels and the Space and Missile Defense Command. Confirm current eligibility, application windows, and training requirements with your career counselor, chain of command, or an Army recruiter before starting a packet.

How to Pursue the Army Space Operations Field

  1. Confirm the current application route. Ask your career counselor or chain of command about the latest 40D reclassification or accession process through the Space and Missile Defense Command.
  2. Master your current job first. The first cohort’s selectors explicitly looked for soldiers who excelled in their current MOS — performance is the filter.
  3. Build relevant technical credentials. Backgrounds in intelligence, signal/communications, air defense, or cyber/IT carry over best; technical schooling and clearances help.
  4. Check the official Army announcement. The Army’s article on the 40D stand-up lays out the branch’s mission and leadership vision — use it to brief your command on why your transfer matters.
  5. Explore the Space Force option too. Reported Space Force interservice-transfer applications ran through October 2, 2026; confirm with a recruiter whether a current window exists.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Army's 40D MOS?

40D is the Tactical Space Operations Specialist MOS, the new enlisted career field of the Army's Space Operations Branch, effective October 1, 2026. 40D soldiers operate satellite communications, navigation, and space-support systems for ground forces and may support counterspace (space interdiction) missions.

Who can apply for the Army Space Operations Branch?

The 40D MOS is a permanent career path for active-duty, National Guard, and Reserve soldiers from specialist through sergeant major. The Army says the field is open to soldiers from any background, though the first cohort came mostly from military intelligence, air defense artillery, and signal specialties.

How selective was the first 40D class?

Nearly 3,000 soldiers applied and just over 600 were selected for the October 1, 2026, reclassification — roughly a one-in-five acceptance rate. The Army plans to grow to around 1,700 space soldiers by the end of the next fiscal year.

Has the Space Force been folded into the Army?

No. The U.S. Space Force remains a separate military service — it even stood up a new Space Force special operations component (SPACEFOR-SOC) under U.S. Special Operations Command on October 7, 2026. The Army's Space Operations Branch is the Army's own career field for space professionals who deliver satellite support to ground forces.

What does a 40D soldier do day to day?

Tactical Space Operations Specialists help troops access satellite communications and navigation systems, monitor potential adversary activity in orbit, and support operations that disrupt an adversary's space capabilities (space interdiction). Assignments include space brigades, multidomain task forces, and theater strike effects groups.
