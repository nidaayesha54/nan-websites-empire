Army Civilian Hiring Freeze Lifted 2026

The Army officially lifted its civilian hiring freeze on September 29, 2026, and resumed civilian hiring — reopening doors for veterans, military spouses, and former Army civilians.

The Army has officially lifted its civilian hiring freeze and resumed civilian hiring, according to a September 29, 2026 memo reported by Federal News Network. For veterans, military spouses, and former Army civilians watching USAJOBS for months, this is the green light to re-engage. But returning to — or joining — the Army civilian workforce comes with rules worth understanding: rehire rights, the Reemployment Priority List (RPL), separation pay repayment, and pay-setting protections. Here is what changed and what to do now.

What Happened on September 29, 2026

Per reporting on the Army memo, the service formally ended its civilian hiring freeze and resumed civilian hiring across the force. The move reopens one of the largest civilian employers in the federal government to new announcements on USAJOBS. Note that hiring discipline remains: DoD civilian hiring vetting boards still review hires, and the broader federal hiring accountability framework — including Executive Order 14356 on continued accountability in federal hiring — still applies.

Rehire Rights and the Reemployment Priority List

If you were separated during workforce reshaping actions, the Reemployment Priority List (RPL) under 5 CFR part 330, subpart B may give you priority consideration for rehire. The RPL is the federal system's formal mechanism for returning displaced career employees to vacancies for which they qualify. Registration, eligibility windows, and priority order are all defined by regulation — so if you believe you qualify, contact the Army civilian HR office that handled your separation and confirm your RPL status in writing.

Separation Pay: The Repayment Trap

Here is the rule that catches returning employees off guard: if you accepted separation pay and then accept federal employment anywhere within 5 years of that separation, you generally must repay the entire separation payment to DoD unless a waiver is granted on an only-qualified-applicant finding — by the Secretary for DoD jobs or the OPM Director for other executive agencies. The DoD ceiling on that payment is $25,000. Before you accept a tentative offer, do the math on repayment versus the new salary, and ask HR about waiver criteria in writing.

What to Do Now That Hiring Has Resumed

  1. Set USAJOBS alerts for Army civilian announcements in your occupational series, grade range, and preferred locations.
  2. Confirm your RPL status if you were displaced — contact Army civilian HR and get your registration in writing.
  3. Assemble your documents now: DD-214, SF-50s, transcripts, and any veterans' preference paperwork (SF-15, VA letter).
  4. Do the separation-pay math before accepting any offer if you took separation pay within the last 5 years.
  5. Invoke the maximum payable rate rule in writing if you are returning at a lower grade and want your previous salary matched.
Verify the memo's scope: The September 29 memo itself was not publicly posted. Confirm current Army civilian vacancy availability on USAJOBS.gov and verify rehire, RPL, and pay-setting details with the servicing Army civilian personnel office before making career decisions.

Frequently Asked Questions

When did the Army lift its civilian hiring freeze?

According to Federal News Network reporting, the Army officially lifted its civilian hiring freeze and resumed civilian hiring via a memo dated September 29, 2026.

What is the Reemployment Priority List (RPL)?

The RPL, governed by 5 CFR part 330, subpart B, gives displaced federal career employees priority consideration for rehire into vacancies for which they qualify. Contact Army civilian HR to confirm your registration.

Do I have to repay separation pay if I return to federal service?

Generally yes: accepting federal employment within 5 years of separation typically requires repaying the full separation payment to DoD (ceiling $25,000), unless a waiver is granted on an only-qualified-applicant finding.

Can the Army match my old salary if I return at a lower grade?

It can try. Under 5 CFR 531.221, the agency may apply the maximum payable rate rule using your highest previous rate. It is discretionary, so raise it in writing before accepting the offer.

Do DoD hiring vetting boards still apply?

Yes. Even with the freeze lifted, DoD civilian hiring vetting boards continue to review hires, and federal hiring accountability rules remain in effect.
