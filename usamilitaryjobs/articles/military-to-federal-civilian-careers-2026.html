Military to Federal Civilian Careers 2026: Veterans Preference on USAJOBS Explained

Veterans hold a powerful advantage in federal hiring - but only if they claim it correctly. This 2026 guide explains veterans preference on USAJOBS, the difference between 5-point and 10-point preference, CTAP/ICTAP career-transition programs, and how to translate military service into a federal civilian career.

Every year, tens of thousands of service members trade their uniforms for federal civilian careers - and the system is designed to reward them for it. Veterans' preference gives eligible veterans a measurable edge in competitive federal hiring, and special hiring authorities open doors that do not exist for the general public. But preference is not automatic: you must understand it, claim it correctly on USAJOBS, and document it precisely. This guide covers everything transitioning service members need to know in 2026.

What Is Veterans' Preference?

Veterans' preference is a hiring advantage granted by law to eligible veterans in competitive federal employment. In practice, it works like this: when applicants take a numerical rating process, 5 or 10 points are added to the passing scores of preference-eligible veterans. In category-rating systems (the most common today), preference can move you into a higher quality category - often the difference between being referred to the hiring manager or not.

Crucially, preference applies to competitive service positions. It does not guarantee a job, and it does not apply to every hiring process - but in a close competition, it is frequently decisive.

5-Point vs. 10-Point Preference

Preference TypeWho QualifiesKey Documentation
5-point (TP)Veterans with qualifying active-duty service, generally including during designated periods or campaignsDD-214 showing character of service and dates
10-point (CP/CPS/XP)Veterans with service-connected disabilities, including disabled veterans and certain spouses/widows/widowers/mothersDD-214 plus SF-15 plus VA disability documentation
Derived preferenceCertain spouses, widows/widowers, and mothers of veteransSF-15 with supporting documentation

The SF-15 (Application for 10-Point Veteran Preference) is the form that trips up the most applicants. If you are claiming 10-point preference, you must submit the SF-15 with every application - not just once. Missing or incomplete SF-15s are one of the top reasons veterans lose preference they were entitled to.

How to Claim Preference Correctly on USAJOBS

  1. Answer the eligibility questions accurately in your USAJOBS profile and each application - the questionnaire determines whether you are coded as preference-eligible.
  2. Upload your DD-214 (Member 4 copy or equivalent) showing dates of service and character of discharge.
  3. Attach the SF-15 for 10-point claims, with VA letters or other supporting documents.
  4. Check the announcement's Required Documents section - some postings specify exactly which version of documents they accept.
  5. Submit before 11:59 PM ET on the closing date - preference cannot rescue a late application.
Common mistake: Claiming preference in the questionnaire but forgetting to upload the DD-214 or SF-15. HR adjudicates preference based on documents, not on what you clicked. No documents, no preference.

CTAP and ICTAP: Career Transition Programs

Beyond veterans' preference, displaced federal employees have their own safety net. The Career Transition Assistance Plan (CTAP) and Interagency Career Transition Assistance Plan (ICTAP) give selection priority to eligible employees displaced by downsizing, restructuring, or base closures:

  • CTAP applies within your own agency - you get priority for vacancies at your agency for which you are well-qualified.
  • ICTAP extends that priority across agencies - eligible displaced employees receive selection priority government-wide.
  • To use either, you must apply, meet the "well-qualified" standard defined in the announcement, and submit proof of eligibility (such as a RIF notice).

If you are a veteran who also served as a federal civilian and were displaced, these programs can stack with your veterans' status - a powerful combination in a competitive announcement.

Translating Military Experience to Federal Grades

The biggest practical challenge in the military-to-civilian transition is translation. Federal HR cannot infer your qualifications from a military job title - your resume must spell out duties, scope, and scale in civilian terms:

  • Convert rank to responsibility: describe team size, budget authority, and equipment value, not just your paygrade.
  • Use the announcement's keywords: mirror the specialized-experience language in the posting - reviewers match your resume against it.
  • Include hours per week and exact dates for every assignment - federal resumes require these fields.
  • Claim the right grade: senior NCOs and officers often qualify for GS-9 through GS-12+ roles; junior enlisted typically target GS-5 through GS-7. The announcement's Qualifications section is the final word.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does veterans' preference guarantee me a federal job?

No. Preference adds points or improves your category ranking in competitive hiring, but you must still meet minimum qualifications and compete. It is a significant advantage, not a guarantee.

What is the difference between 5-point and 10-point preference?

5-point preference generally applies to veterans with qualifying service; 10-point preference applies to veterans with service-connected disabilities (and certain family members), requires the SF-15 form, and carries stronger protections including priority in reductions in force.

What are CTAP and ICTAP?

Career-transition programs giving selection priority to eligible displaced federal employees - CTAP within their own agency, ICTAP across agencies. You must be found "well-qualified" and submit proof of displacement eligibility.

Do I need to submit the SF-15 with every application?

Yes, if you are claiming 10-point preference. Attach the completed SF-15 plus supporting VA documentation to each application - HR evaluates preference per announcement.

Can military spouses get federal hiring preference?

Yes. Eligible military spouses have their own hiring authorities and, in some cases, derived preference. Check the announcement's "Who May Apply" section and the military-spouse hiring path on USAJOBS.
