IRS Veteran Hiring 2026: Non-Competitive Hiring for Veterans After 30% Workforce Cut

The IRS lost about 30% of its workforce between January 2025 and January 2026 — and it missed its 14% veteran hiring goal. Now the agency is leaning on non-competitive hiring authorities for veterans and military spouses to rebuild. Here is what that means for you.

The Internal Revenue Service is rebuilding from historic staffing losses — and veterans are central to the plan. Between January 2025 and January 2026, 31,273 employees left the IRS through voluntary separation, incentive programs, and layoffs, cutting the workforce by about 30%. Hiring fell far short: the IRS was authorized to bring on 1,900 submission-processing employees but onboarded only 800 — a 58% shortfall — while account management hired 2,300 of a 3,500 target, a 34% shortfall, according to Treasury Inspector General for Tax Administration report 2026-400-031. Against that backdrop, the IRS also failed to meet its 14% veteran hiring goal — and leadership now has a plan to fix it.

The Veterans Hiring Plan

To rebuild veteran hiring, the plan on the table includes several concrete actions:

  • Non-competitive special hiring authorities for veterans and military spouses — meaning eligible candidates can be appointed without going through the standard competitive rating and ranking process.
  • Partnerships with state and local veterans' workforce programs to source candidates directly from the veteran community.
  • USAJOBS resume mining — using the USAJOBS resume database to proactively identify available veteran applicants rather than waiting for applications.
  • Maintained lists of qualified veterans and military spouses so hiring managers can reach eligible candidates quickly when openings post.

The IRS has also been holding seasonal hiring events around the country for filing-season positions — the kind of high-volume hiring where veterans' preference and direct-hire approaches move fastest.

Why This Is Good News for Veteran Applicants

A 30% smaller workforce doing the same national mission means the IRS needs people urgently — and urgency favors candidates with hiring advantages. Veterans' preference (5 or 10 points), Veterans Recruitment Appointment authority, and the 30%-or-more disabled veteran authority all give eligible veterans paths that bypass the toughest parts of competitive federal hiring. Military spouses get their own non-competitive appointment authority. When an agency is authorized to hire thousands and struggling to fill seats, these authorities turn from bureaucratic fine print into genuine fast lanes.

How Veterans Should Apply

  1. Claim every authority you qualify for: On USAJOBS, indicate veterans' preference status, VRA eligibility, and any other special hiring authority. Upload your DD-214 and VA disability documentation.
  2. Watch jobs.irs.gov and USAJOBS: IRS seasonal and filing-season announcements post in waves — set up saved searches with veteran filters.
  3. Attend IRS hiring events: The agency holds in-person hiring events around the country; these are prime venues for direct conversations with hiring managers.
  4. Use resume mining to your advantage: Keep your USAJOBS resume current and searchable, since the IRS is actively mining the database for veteran candidates.
  5. Consider the mission fit: IRS roles span tax examination, criminal investigation (IRS-CI), IT, customer service, and enforcement — military backgrounds in finance, logistics, analysis, and investigations all translate.
File early for seasonal roles: Filing-season hiring moves in waves ahead of each tax season. Getting your USAJOBS profile, documents, and veteran paperwork ready now means you can apply the day announcements post instead of scrambling.

Frequently Asked Questions

Did the IRS meet its veteran hiring goal?
