How to Apply

Five steps to land your government job.

Talk to a Recruiter

Each branch has recruiters who explain options, bonuses and enlistment terms. There is no obligation — ask about the jobs that match your ASVAB strengths.

Take the ASVAB

The Armed Services Vocational Aptitude Battery determines which military jobs (MOS/AFSC/rates) you qualify for. Higher scores unlock technical fields like cyber and intelligence.

Pass MEPS Screening

The Military Entrance Processing Station conducts your medical exam, physical fitness check and background screening before you can sign a contract.

Choose Your Job and Sign

Qualified applicants select a guaranteed job in their contract when available. Ask about enlistment bonuses (up to $50,000 in high-demand fields) and the GI Bill.

Complete Basic Training

After signing, you ship to basic training (8–13 weeks depending on branch), then to technical school for your specific career field before your first duty station.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about government hiring.

What are the age requirements to join the military?

You must be 17 to 42 depending on the branch — Army and Navy accept up to 35-42, the Air Force up to 42, Marines up to 28. A parent's consent is required at 17.

What is the ASVAB?

The ASVAB is a multi-aptitude test covering math, science, reading and technical skills. Your scores determine which of hundreds of military jobs you qualify for.

Do military members get free college?

Yes — the GI Bill covers tuition at approved colleges, plus tuition assistance while serving and credentialing programs that convert military training into civilian certifications.

What is military pay based on?

Pay is set by rank (E-1 through E-9 for enlisted, O-1 through O-10 for officers) and years of service, plus tax-free housing (BAH) and food (BAS) allowances and special-duty pays.

Can I choose my military job?

In most cases yes — recruiters can guarantee specific jobs in your enlistment contract based on ASVAB qualification and availability, especially in high-demand technical fields.

What benefits do military families receive?

Free or low-cost healthcare through TRICARE, housing allowances, commissary and exchange shopping privileges, and education benefits for spouses and children.
