Pay Off Debt or Save First?

Updated 2026-10-12

Pay off debt or save first? A simple framework: emergency fund, high-interest debt, then investing — in order.

The order of operations

Personal finance has a correct sequence, and skipping steps is expensive:

  1. $1,000 starter emergency fund — so one surprise does not become new debt.
  2. Kill high-interest debt (roughly 7%+ APR) — credit cards first. A 22% APR costs more than any safe investment earns.
  3. Full 3–6 month emergency fund.
  4. Invest while paying low-interest debt — a 4% student loan vs 8–10% market returns favors investing (with risk tolerance).

The math behind it

Paying off a 22% APR card is a guaranteed, tax-free 22% return — unbeatable. But holding zero savings while doing it means the next emergency goes right back on the card at 22%. The starter fund breaks that cycle.

The exceptions

  • 401(k) match: contribute to the match while paying debt — a 50–100% instant return beats any APR.
  • 0% promo debt: pay minimums, park cash in high-yield savings, kill it before the promo ends.
  • Tax-advantaged deadlines: an IRA contribution you skip this year is gone forever; debt can wait weeks.

Bottom line

Starter fund → high-interest debt → full fund → invest. In that order, no skipping, and the match always gets fed first.
