5 Credit Utilization Myths Debunked

Updated 2026-10-12

5 credit utilization myths debunked: 0% vs 1%, per-card vs overall, and what actually moves your score.

Myth 1: "0% utilization is best"

Slightly false. Scoring models slightly prefer 1–9% over 0% — a tiny reported balance shows active, responsible use. The difference is small (a few points), but 0% across all cards is not the optimum people claim.

Myth 2: "Only overall utilization matters"

Both matter. Maxing out one card at 95% while others sit at 0% still hurts, even if overall utilization is 20%. Keep each card under 30%, not just the total.

Myth 3: "Paying in full means low utilization"

Only if you pay before the statement closes. Issuers report the statement balance — charge $3,000, pay in full after the statement, and the bureaus still saw 60% utilization that month. Test the effect in our simulator.

Myth 4: "High utilization damage is permanent"

Utilization has no memory in current scoring models. Last month's 90% stops mattering the moment a low balance is reported. It is the fastest-healing factor in your score.

Myth 5: "Closing a card helps utilization"

The opposite — closing a card deletes its limit from your available credit, instantly raising utilization. Keep old cards open with a small recurring charge.

The real rule

Under 30% everywhere, under 10% for the best scores, reported on the statement date. Everything else is noise.
