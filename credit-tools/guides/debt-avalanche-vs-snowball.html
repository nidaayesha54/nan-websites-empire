Debt Avalanche vs Snowball: Which Payoff Method Wins?

Updated 2026-10-12

Debt avalanche vs snowball: how each payoff method works, the math on interest saved, and which one to pick.

The two methods

  • Avalanche: pay minimums on everything, throw all extra cash at the highest APR debt first.
  • Snowball: pay minimums on everything, throw all extra cash at the smallest balance first.

Both beat minimum-only payments by years. The question is only which priority order fits you.

The math: avalanche wins on interest

Avalanche targets the most expensive debt first, so it almost always minimizes total interest. On $15,000 across three cards with a $300/month extra payment, avalanche typically saves hundreds to thousands vs snowball. See your exact numbers in our side-by-side calculator — on real debt profiles the gap is often $1,000–$5,000.

The psychology: snowball wins on momentum

Killing a small debt in 3 months delivers a dopamine hit that keeps people going; grinding a $12,000 balance for a year can feel hopeless. Studies on debt payoff find completion rates are higher with snowball — the mathematically inferior method people actually finish beats the perfect plan they abandon.

How to choose

  • Pick avalanche if the interest gap is large ($1,000+) and you are disciplined — it is free money.
  • Pick snowball if you have many small debts, have quit payoff plans before, or need quick wins.
  • Hybrid: snowball the tiny nuisance debts first (2–3 months), then switch to avalanche for the big balances.

Prerequisites for either

Minimums must cover interest on every debt (else balances grow forever), and you need a small emergency buffer ($1,000 starter) so one car repair does not restart the cycle.

Bottom line

Run both on your debts in the debt payoff calculator. If avalanche saves little, take snowball's motivation; if it saves thousands, take the money.
