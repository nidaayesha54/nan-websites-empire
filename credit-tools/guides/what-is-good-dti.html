What Is a Good Debt-to-Income (DTI) Ratio?

Updated 2026-10-12

What is a good DTI ratio? Lender thresholds for mortgages, auto loans, and credit cards — plus how to lower yours.

DTI in one sentence

Your debt-to-income ratio is total monthly debt payments ÷ gross monthly income. $2,500 in debts on $7,000 income = 35.7% DTI. Check yours in seconds with our DTI calculator.

The thresholds lenders use

  • Under 36% — good. The classic "healthy" zone; qualifies for the best conventional mortgage terms.
  • 36–43% — caution. 43% is the general ceiling for Qualified Mortgages; approvals get stricter and rates worse.
  • Over 43% — high. Most conventional lenders decline. FHA loans may stretch to 50–57% with strong compensating factors (big reserves, high credit score).

Front-end vs back-end DTI

Mortgage lenders check two numbers: front-end (housing costs only — target ≤28%) and back-end (all debts — target ≤36%). Auto lenders and landlords mostly care about the back-end number.

What counts as debt?

Minimum monthly payments on: mortgage/rent, auto loans, student loans, credit cards, personal loans, child support. Groceries, utilities, insurance, and taxes do not count — only debt payments.

How to lower your DTI fast

  • Pay off small debts entirely — eliminating a $150 minimum moves DTI more than trimming a big balance.
  • Refinance high-payment debts to longer terms (lowers the monthly, the only thing DTI sees).
  • Raise income — a side gig counts once documented.
  • Avoid new debt before applying: that car loan can single-handedly kill a mortgage approval.

Bottom line

Under 36% keeps every door open. If you are buying a home within a year, get DTI down before the application — not after a denial.
