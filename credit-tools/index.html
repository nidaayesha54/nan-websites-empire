Frequently asked questions

What affects my credit score the most?

Payment history (35%) and credit utilization (30%) together drive about two-thirds of a FICO score. Length of history, new credit, and credit mix make up the rest.

What is a good credit utilization ratio?

Under 30% is the standard guideline; under 10% is ideal for the best scores. Both per-card and overall utilization matter.

Avalanche or snowball — which pays off debt faster?

Avalanche (highest APR first) almost always costs less in interest. Snowball (smallest balance first) gives faster psychological wins. Our calculator compares both on your actual debts.

What is a good DTI ratio?

Under 36% is considered good and comfortably qualifies for most mortgages; 36–43% is the caution zone and the upper limit for many qualified mortgages; over 43% is high.

How fast can I build credit?

Utilization improvements can lift scores in 1–2 billing cycles. Late-payment recovery and new positive history take months. There is no legitimate overnight fix — beware anyone selling one.
