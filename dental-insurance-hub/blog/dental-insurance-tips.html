9 Dental Insurance Tips That Save You Real Money

October 2026

Most people buy dental insurance, pay premiums for years, and never use half the benefits they're entitled to. These nine tips — from timing treatments to fighting denials — can save hundreds of dollars a year.

1. Always Get a Pre-Treatment Estimate

Before any procedure over $300, ask your dentist to submit a pre-treatment estimate (predetermination) to your insurer. You'll get a written breakdown of what the plan covers and what you'll owe — before you're in the chair. It takes a few days and prevents the worst surprise in dentistry: a bill triple what you expected.

2. Split Major Work Across Plan Years

Need two crowns and your annual maximum is $1,500? Do one in December and one in January. The maximum resets with the plan year, so timing treatment across the boundary effectively doubles your coverage. Talk to your dentist — most treatment plans can be sequenced this way.

3. Use Every Dollar of Preventive Care

Cleanings, exams, and X-rays are covered at 100% under most plans — and they're use it or lose it. Skipping your free cleanings doesn't just waste money you've already paid in premiums; it lets small cavities grow into $1,100 crowns. Book both visits the day your plan year starts.

4. Stay In-Network

In-network dentists accept negotiated fees that are typically 20–40% below billed charges. Out-of-network, your insurer may reimburse based on "usual and customary" rates — and you pay the difference. One phone call to confirm network status before booking can save hundreds.

5. Know Your Waiting Periods Cold

That crown you need next month may not be covered if your plan has a 12-month wait on major services. Never schedule major work without confirming your waiting periods first — here's how they work and how to avoid them.

6. Appeal Denied Claims — It Works

Insurers deny claims hoping you won't fight back. If a claim is denied, ask your dentist's office to resubmit with additional documentation (X-rays, chart notes, a narrative). A large share of dental denials are overturned on appeal or resubmission. Escalate to your state's insurance department if the insurer stonewalls.

7. Bundle Family Treatment Strategically

Family plans often have a family deductible (e.g., $150) that's less than 3× the individual deductible. If two family members need fillings, scheduling them in the same plan year satisfies the family deductible once — the second person's work then gets fuller coverage.

8. Ask About Cash Discounts Even When Insured

Counterintuitive but real: for small procedures, some dentists offer cash prices below your insured out-of-pocket cost (deductible + coinsurance). Always ask "what's the cash price?" alongside the insured estimate, then pay whichever is less. Use our cost estimator to compare.

9. Re-Shop Your Plan Every Open Enrollment

Premiums, maximums, and networks change yearly. The plan that was perfect last year may be overpriced now. Spend 20 minutes each open enrollment running your options through the Plan Comparator — switching plans is free and takes minutes.
