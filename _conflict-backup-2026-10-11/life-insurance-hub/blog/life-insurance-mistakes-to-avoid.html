7 Life Insurance Mistakes to Avoid in 2026

October 2026 · 6 min read

1. Waiting too long to buy

Every birthday raises your premium. The difference between buying at 30 and 40 can be thousands of dollars over a policy's life — for identical coverage.

2. Relying only on employer coverage

Employer policies (usually 1–2× salary) vanish when you change jobs, and they're rarely enough. Own a personal policy you control.

3. Buying whole life when you need term

Whole life costs 10–15× more per dollar of coverage. Unless you have a permanent need (estate planning, lifelong dependent), term wins. Run the numbers in our comparator.

4. Underestimating how much you need

Guessing "a few hundred thousand" leaves families short. Use the DIME method for a number based on your actual obligations.

5. Not disclosing health details honestly

Insurers check medical records and prescription databases. Misrepresentation can void a policy — exactly when your family needs it most.

6. Naming no contingent beneficiary

If your primary beneficiary dies before you and there's no backup, the payout goes through probate — slow and expensive. Always name a contingent.

7. Never reviewing your policy

Marriage, kids, a mortgage, a raise — life changes should trigger a coverage review every 2–3 years.
