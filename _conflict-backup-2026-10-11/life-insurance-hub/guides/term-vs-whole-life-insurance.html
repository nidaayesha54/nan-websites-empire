Term vs Whole Life Insurance: An Honest Comparison

Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

Agents earn far higher commissions selling whole life, so guess which one gets recommended? Here's the comparison without the sales pitch.

Term Life Insurance

Term life is pure protection: you pay for coverage over a fixed period (10, 20, or 30 years). If you die during the term, your beneficiaries get the death benefit. If you outlive it, the policy expires.

  • Cost: A healthy 35-year-old pays roughly $25–35/month for a $500K 20-year term policy.
  • Best for: Income replacement, mortgage protection, covering kids until they're independent.
  • Downside: No cash value; coverage ends.

Whole Life Insurance

Whole life covers you for your entire life and builds cash value that grows tax-deferred. Part of each premium pays for insurance; the rest goes into the cash value account.

  • Cost: Roughly 10–15× more than term for the same death benefit — $300–500/month for that same $500K.
  • Best for: Estate planning, a lifelong dependent, or final-expense certainty.
  • Downside: Expensive; cash value grows slowly (often 2–4% annually); complex fees.

The Math That Matters

Take the $500K example: 20-year term at ~$30/month costs $7,200 total. Whole life at ~$400/month costs $96,000 over the same 20 years — 13× more. The famous "buy term and invest the difference" strategy says: buy the cheap term policy and invest the $370/month you saved. Over 20 years at 7% average returns, that's roughly $190,000 — far more than most whole life cash values.

When Whole Life Actually Makes Sense

  1. Estate planning: You need guaranteed liquidity to pay estate taxes.
  2. Lifelong dependent: A special-needs child who will always need support.
  3. Maxed-out retirement accounts and you want another tax-advantaged vehicle (niche case).

If none of those describe you, term life plus investing is almost always the better deal.
