How Much Life Insurance Do I Need in 2026?

Updated October 2026 · 8 min read

The most common answer you'll hear — "10× your income" — is a guess, not a plan. It ignores your mortgage, your debts, and your kids' college costs. Financial planners use a better framework: the DIME method.

The DIME Method, Step by Step

DIME stands for Debts, Income, Mortgage, and Education. Add the first four, subtract your assets, and you have a coverage number built from your real life.

D — Debts (excluding mortgage)

Add up everything you owe except the mortgage: credit cards, auto loans, student loans, personal loans. These don't disappear if you die — your family inherits the obligation.

I — Income replacement

Multiply your annual income by the number of years your family would need support. A common choice is 10–15 years, or until your youngest child finishes college. If you earn $80,000 and choose 15 years, that's $1.2 million.

M — Mortgage payoff

Include your remaining mortgage balance so your family can stay in the home without your income.

E — Education costs

Estimate future college costs per child. In 2026, a reasonable planning figure is $100,000–$200,000 per child for a four-year degree, depending on public vs. private.

Minus: assets and existing coverage

Subtract liquid savings, investments, and any life insurance you already have (including employer coverage — but remember that disappears if you leave the job).

Worked Example

Meet a typical family: $80K income, $15K in debts, $250K mortgage, 2 kids, $50K in savings, no existing policy.

  • D: $15,000
  • I: $80,000 × 15 years = $1,200,000
  • M: $250,000
  • E: $100,000 × 2 = $200,000
  • Minus assets: −$50,000
  • Total: $1,615,000 — they'd round to a $1.5M or $2M policy.

Special Situations

Stay-at-home parent

Don't skip coverage. Replacing childcare, housekeeping, and household management can cost $50,000+/year. A $500K–$1M policy is common.

Single with no dependents

You likely need only enough to cover debts and final expenses — often $50K–$100K — unless you want to leave a legacy.

Business owner

Add business debts you personally guaranteed and consider key-person coverage for partners.
