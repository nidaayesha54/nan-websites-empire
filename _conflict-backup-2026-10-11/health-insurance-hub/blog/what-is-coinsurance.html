What Is Coinsurance? (With Examples)

Coinsurance is your share of medical costs after you've paid your deductible. It's written as a percentage — "20% coinsurance" means you pay 20% of the bill and insurance pays 80%.

How It Works, Step by Step

Say your plan has a $2,000 deductible, 20% coinsurance, and a $6,000 out-of-pocket max. You get a $10,000 surgery:

  1. Deductible first: you pay the first $2,000.
  2. Coinsurance next: of the remaining $8,000, you pay 20% = $1,600.
  3. Your total: $2,000 + $1,600 = $3,600. Insurance pays $6,400.

The Out-of-Pocket Max Saves You

If instead the surgery cost $50,000: deductible $2,000 + 20% of $48,000 ($9,600) = $11,600 — but your OOP max caps it at $6,000. That's the ceiling that makes insurance, insurance.

Coinsurance vs Copay

  • Copay: a flat fee ($30 for a doctor visit) — simple, predictable.
  • Coinsurance: a percentage — scales with the bill, kicks in after the deductible.
