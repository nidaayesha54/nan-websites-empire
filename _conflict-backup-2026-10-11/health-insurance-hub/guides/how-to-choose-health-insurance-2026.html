How to Choose Health Insurance in 2026: The 5-Step Framework

Open enrollment gives you a short window and a wall of confusing options. Here's the framework that actually works — used by benefits professionals everywhere.

Step 1: Estimate Your Medical Usage

Look at last year's bills and next year's plans. Are you healthy (a checkup + maybe urgent care), average (a few specialist visits, prescriptions), or high-use (surgery planned, chronic condition, pregnancy)? Your honest answer drives everything else.

Step 2: Compute the TRUE Yearly Cost

Never compare premiums alone. The real number is:

True yearly cost = (monthly premium × 12) + expected out-of-pocket spending

Out-of-pocket = your deductible + coinsurance until the out-of-pocket max. Our plan comparator does this math at low, medium, and high usage so you can see which plan wins in each scenario.

Step 3: Check Your Subsidy

If you buy on the ACA marketplace and your household income is roughly 100%–400% of the federal poverty level, premium tax credits can slash your monthly cost. Run our subsidy estimator before ruling any plan out — a "too expensive" plan may be affordable after credits.

Step 4: Check the Network and Formulary

The cheapest plan is worthless if your doctors are out of network or your prescriptions aren't covered. Before enrolling:

  • Search each plan's provider directory for your doctors and hospital
  • Check the drug formulary for your prescriptions (and their tier)
  • Note referral rules — HMOs usually require PCP referrals; PPOs don't

Step 5: Consider the HSA Option

If you're healthy and can save, a high-deductible plan with an HSA offers a triple tax advantage (deductible contributions, tax-free growth, tax-free withdrawals for medical costs). Use our HSA vs PPO analyzer to see the break-even point for your situation.

The 3 Biggest Mistakes

  1. Picking the lowest premium blindly — then getting crushed by a $7,000 deductible.
  2. Ignoring the network — out-of-network care often doesn't count toward your OOP max.
  3. Not re-shopping yearly — plans, premiums, and your health change. Auto-renewal is how insurers profit.
