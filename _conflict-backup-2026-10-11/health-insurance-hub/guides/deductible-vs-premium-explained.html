Health Insurance: Deductible vs Premium, Explained

Every health plan forces the same tradeoff: pay more each month (premium) or pay more when you need care (deductible). Understanding this seesaw is the key to picking the right plan.

The Definitions

  • Premium: what you pay every month just to have the plan — whether you see a doctor or not.
  • Deductible: what you pay out of pocket each year before insurance starts sharing costs.
  • Coinsurance: your share of costs after the deductible (e.g., 20%).
  • Out-of-pocket max: the absolute ceiling on what you'll pay in a year (premiums excluded).

A Worked Example

Plan A: $450/month premium, $1,500 deductible, $6,000 OOP max, 20% coinsurance.
Plan B: $280/month premium, $5,000 deductible, $9,000 OOP max, 30% coinsurance.

Healthy year ($2,000 in care): Plan A costs $5,400 + $1,600 = $7,000. Plan B costs $3,360 + $2,000 = $5,360. Plan B wins.

Bad year ($40,000 surgery): Plan A costs $5,400 + $6,000 = $11,400. Plan B costs $3,360 + $9,000 = $12,360. Plan A wins.

The Rule of Thumb

Young and healthy? The cheap-premium, high-deductible plan usually wins — and it may unlock an HSA. Expecting a baby, surgery, or managing a chronic condition? Pay the higher premium for the lower deductible and lower OOP max.

The real answer is always: annual premium + expected out-of-pocket. Never compare premiums alone.
