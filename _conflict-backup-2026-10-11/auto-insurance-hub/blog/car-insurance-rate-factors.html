7 Factors That Raise Your Car Insurance Rate

Ever wonder why your neighbor pays half what you do? Insurers price risk with dozens of variables. Here are the seven that matter most — ranked by impact.

1. Your ZIP code

Where you park at night is the #1 factor. Dense cities with more crashes, theft, and lawsuits cost far more than rural towns.

2. Your age

Teen drivers crash the most, so they pay the most — often 2–3× adult rates. Prices drop sharply at 25.

3. Driving record

Tickets, at-fault accidents, and especially DUIs follow you for 3–5 years. A clean record is the best discount there is.

4. Your vehicle

Expensive to repair = expensive to insure. Sports cars, luxury SUVs, and EVs sit at the top; modest sedans at the bottom.

5. Coverage choices

Low deductibles and high limits cost more. Raising a deductible from $500 to $1,000 commonly saves 10–15%.

6. Credit-based insurance score

In most states, insurers use a credit-derived score. Better credit, lower premium — a controversial but legal practice in ~45 states.

7. Miles driven

More time on the road = more risk. Low-mileage drivers should ask about usage-based or pay-per-mile programs.
