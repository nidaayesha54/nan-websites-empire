When Should You Drop Full Coverage?

If your car is old and paid off, you may be paying hundreds a year to protect a car worth a few thousand. Here's how to decide.

The 10% rule

Add up what collision + comprehensive cost you per year (not liability — keep that). If that number is more than 10% of your car's current value, dropping full coverage usually wins the math.

Example: Your 2012 sedan is worth $4,500. Collision + comp costs $720/year. That's 16% — drop it and bank the $720.

Three questions to ask

  1. Could I replace this car out of pocket? If yes without pain, you don't need the insurer to do it.
  2. Is there a loan or lease? If yes — stop. Lenders require full coverage; you can't drop it.
  3. What's the car's actual value? Check KBB. Under ~$4,000–$5,000 is the classic drop zone.

What to keep

Never drop liability — it's legally required and protects your assets. Consider keeping comprehensive alone if it's cheap in your area (it covers theft and hail for often under $15/month).
