Full Coverage vs Liability: What's the Difference?

This is the most important choice in car insurance — and the most misunderstood. Here's the plain-English version.

Liability-only: pays for damage YOU cause to others

Liability covers the other driver's medical bills and property damage when you're at fault. It does not pay to fix or replace your car. It's the legal minimum in almost every state, and costs roughly 55% of full coverage.

Full coverage: liability + your own car

"Full coverage" isn't a single product — it means liability plus collision (crash damage to your car) plus comprehensive (theft, hail, flood, hitting a deer). Lenders and leasing companies almost always require it.

When does liability-only make sense?

  • Your car is paid off and worth under ~$4,000–$5,000
  • You could replace it out of pocket without financial pain
  • You want the absolute lowest legal premium

When do you need full coverage?

  • Any loan or lease (required by the lender)
  • Car worth more than a few thousand dollars
  • You couldn't afford to replace the car tomorrow

The 10% rule of thumb

If full coverage costs more than 10% of your car's value per year, dropping collision/comprehensive is often the smarter math. Example: $800/year for full coverage on a $5,000 car = 16% → consider liability-only.
