Cheapest Car Insurance in 2026

Car insurance is expensive — the average US driver pays roughly $170–$200/month for full coverage. But averages hide huge differences: the cheapest insurers charge half of what the priciest ones do for the same driver. Here's how to find the bottom of the market.

Which companies are cheapest?

Nationally, the insurers that most often appear at the top of "cheapest" lists include USAA (military families), Geico, State Farm, and Progressive. Regional insurers can be even cheaper in their home states. But "cheapest on average" means little — your cheapest company depends on your age, car, ZIP code, and record. Always get at least 3–5 quotes.

State rules, litigation rates, weather, and uninsured-driver rates drive most of the gap. Moving isn't practical advice — but knowing your state's baseline helps you spot a bad quote.

7 ways to get the cheapest rate

  1. Compare every 6–12 months. Loyalty rarely pays; insurers raise rates quietly.
  2. Raise your deductible from $500 to $1,000 — often saves 10–15%.
  3. Bundle home/renters + auto for up to 15% off.
  4. Ask for every discount — good student, telematics, defensive driving, paperless. Use our discount finder.
  5. Keep a clean record. One ticket can raise rates ~15%; a DUI can nearly double them.
  6. Choose a cheaper car to insure. Sedans and small SUVs cost far less than sports cars.
  7. Drop collision on old cars. If your car's value is under ~$4,000–$5,000, full coverage may not pay off. See full coverage vs liability.

Bottom line

The cheapest car insurance is the one you shop for. Run our premium estimator, check your discounts, and compare real quotes — most drivers overpay by hundreds per year simply by not looking.
