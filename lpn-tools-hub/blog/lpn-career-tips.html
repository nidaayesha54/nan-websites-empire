7 LPN Career Tips Nobody Tells New Grads

Published October 12, 2026 — You passed the NCLEX-PN, you have the license, and now the real education begins. Here are seven things experienced LPNs wish someone had told them in year one — the moves that actually raise your pay, strengthen your resume, and set up your long-term career.

1. Your first job should optimize for skills, not salary

A busy med-surg floor or high-acuity nursing home will teach you more in one year than a quiet clinic teaches in three. IV skills, wound care, time management, and clinical judgment compound — and every future employer (including bridge programs) values them. Take the harder first job; the pay catches up fast.

2. Get IV therapy certified in your first year

In most states, LPNs can perform IV therapy with certification — and IV-certified LPNs qualify for hospital, home-health, and infusion roles that pay noticeably more. Many employers pay for the certification course. It's the single highest-ROI credential an LPN can add early.

3. Night shift is a pay raise in disguise

Night differentials of 10–15% are worth $6,000–$9,000 a year on an average LPN salary — tax-advantaged, because it's base-pay-adjacent income, not overtime. New grads who can handle nights for 12–18 months bank a serious head start. Estimate your shift-adjusted pay with our LPN Salary Calculator.

4. Negotiate your first offer (yes, really)

New LPNs assume the posted rate is fixed. It often isn't — especially shift differentials, sign-on bonuses, and start-date flexibility. Research the going rate for your state first (see our LPN Salary by State 2026 guide), then ask. The worst outcome is a polite no; the best is a few thousand dollars a year, every year.

5. Document everything — your career portfolio starts now

Keep a private log of skills, certifications, preceptor feedback, and patient-population experience. When you apply to bridge programs, travel agencies, or specialty roles, this log becomes your evidence. Nurses who track their growth negotiate and advance faster than those who rely on memory.

6. Start RN prerequisites in year one, not year five

The biggest career regret among LPNs is waiting too long to bridge. Take one prerequisite per semester — anatomy, microbiology, statistics — while the study habit is fresh. By year two or three you can enter an LPN-to-RN bridge program with zero delay, and RN pay is roughly 35–40% higher.

7. Protect your license like it's your paycheck — because it is

Scope-of-practice violations, documentation shortcuts, and social-media mistakes end LPN careers every year. Never perform a task outside your state's LPN scope, chart accurately and promptly, and keep patient information off your phone and social accounts. A clean license is the foundation every one of these tips builds on.

The bottom line

Your LPN license is a launchpad, not a ceiling. Certifications, smart first jobs, shift strategy, and an early bridge plan can take you from ~$62,000 to six figures within a few years.
