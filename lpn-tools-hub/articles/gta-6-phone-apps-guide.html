GTA 6 Phone: Every App and What It Does

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and one of the most important things in the game is not a weapon or a car. It is the phone. The in-game phone is a second screen for the whole game: your bank, your camera, your social media, your car service, your fitness tracker, your news and your weather, all in your pocket. Learning it early is learning the game early, because almost every system in GTA 6 runs through it. This guide explains every app on the phone and what each one actually does.

Opening the phone is the first thing to learn: press D-pad Up. That is it — one button, and the whole second screen is in your hands. From there, the apps split into two groups: the ones you start with, and the ones the story adds as you go.

The Apps You Start With

From the start of the game, the phone carries nine apps. WhatUp is the messaging app — the place where the story reaches you, where characters text you, and where your relationships live. Contacts is the address book, and it grows as you meet people. Camera takes your photos, and Gallery stores them. Snapmatic is the social media feed. Fitness tracks your body. News and Weather tell you what is happening in Leonida, and Settings is the phone's own options. That is the starting kit, and it is already more than most games give you in a whole menu.

Two of those apps deserve a closer look. Camera is not just for screenshots: selfies go into the Gallery, and the photos you take matter in ways the game will show you later — including photos you send to your partner. And News is not just flavour: the world reacts to what is in the news, and reading it is one way to understand what Leonida is doing while you are busy elsewhere. The phone is a window, not a menu.

The Apps the Story Adds

As the story moves, the phone grows. The first addition is the most important: BuckMe, the banking app, which arrives in the mission of the same name. The bank refuses Jason and Lucia an account, so they get the app instead — and opening it gives them 100 dollars as a welcome gift. BuckMe is your bank for the rest of the game: deposits, family savings, and the checking-account cap that stops payments when it fills up. Learn it in Chapter 1, because every dollar you earn runs through it.

The other story apps are about the world. WAiNK is the car scanner — point it at a car and learn what it is, which matters when you are choosing what to steal and what to leave. Scooter Bros is the vehicle delivery service, and also how you recover a destroyed car: open the app, pay the fee, get the car back. Vice City Real Estate is the property app, for whatever the game lets you own — so far, garages. And RydeMe appears in Chapter 2: a ride-hailing app that lets you skip a journey for a flat 100 dollars, the game's fast travel when you would rather not drive back.

Snapmatic: The Feed You Can Browse But Not Post To

Snapmatic is the game's social media, and it is modelled on the photo-sharing apps of the real world: a feed you can browse, like and follow, full of people living their lives in Leonida. But there is a twist that defines the whole game: Jason and Lucia cannot post publicly. They are fugitives, and they leave no digital trail — so the feed is something they watch, not something they join.

That does not make Snapmatic useless. Quite the opposite: the feed is a source of information. Snapmatic posts can point to world events, races and robberies happening around the map — places to go, things to intercept, opportunities that only exist because someone posted about them. And the photos you take with the Camera can be sent to your partner privately, which is the game's way of letting you share a moment without leaving a trail. The phone's rule is the story's rule: stay invisible, but keep watching.

WhatUp: Texts That Change Relationships

WhatUp is where the game's relationships live, and it is the app that punishes inattention. Characters text you, and what you do with those texts matters: ignoring a WhatUp message can hurt the relationship with the person who sent it. The game is keeping score of your attention, and the score shows up in how people treat you. Answer your messages. It costs nothing, and it costs more than you think to skip it.

The relationship system goes further than texts: the game tracks what you do together — a gym session with your partner counts as a date — and the things you do side by side build the bond between the two leads. The phone is the ledger for all of it: the texts you answered, the plans you kept, the people still on your side. In a story about two people trying to escape together, that ledger is the story.

The Fitness App and Your Stats

The Fitness app is the phone's quiet workhorse. It tracks how well you are sleeping, so check it if your stats feel off — the game shows tiredness here before it shows anywhere else. It connects to the three core stats that rule the game: Health, Stamina and Focus. Lifting weights increases health, exercising improves stamina, and mindfulness activities such as fishing and playing pool increase focus. The app is the dashboard for all of it.

Use it as a routine check: before a big mission, open Fitness, check your sleep, and ask whether you need a shower, a meal, or a night's rest first. The buffs from eating, sleeping and showering last for the session or until you die, so the check is worth the ten seconds it takes.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I open the phone in GTA 6?

Press D-pad Up. The phone opens with WhatUp, Contacts, Camera, Gallery, Snapmatic, Fitness, News, Weather and Settings, and the story adds BuckMe, WAiNK, Scooter Bros, Vice City Real Estate and RydeMe as you progress.

Can I post on Snapmatic in GTA 6?

No. Jason and Lucia are fugitives and leave no digital trail, so they can browse, like and follow Snapmatic but cannot post publicly. Photos can be sent to your partner privately, and Snapmatic posts can point you to events, races and robberies to intercept.

The Bottom Line

Your GTA 6 phone is the game's second screen: WhatUp for relationships, BuckMe for money, Scooter Bros for cars, WAiNK for scanning, RydeMe for skipping a drive, Snapmatic for watching the world, and Fitness for sleep and stats. Open it with D-pad Up, answer your texts, check Fitness before big missions — and remember the rule: you can watch everything, but you cannot post.
