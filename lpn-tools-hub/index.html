LPN Career FAQ

How much does an LPN make in 2026?
The national mean LPN wage is about $62,000 per year, with the 10th percentile near $45,000 and the 90th near $83,000. State, specialty, and shift differentials move that number a lot — calculate your estimate.
How long does LPN school take?
Typically 9–18 months. Accelerated technical-school programs can finish in about 9–12 months; community-college certificates take 12–18 months; hybrid part-time programs run 14–18 months. Then you sit for the NCLEX-PN.
Can I become an LPN online?
No fully-online LPN program exists — every state requires hundreds of in-person clinical hours. Hybrid programs teach lectures online but clinicals are always in person. Take the program finder quiz to find a flexible fit.
Is LPN school worth the cost?
Usually yes: with typical tuition of $4,000–$22,000 and an average salary lift of $20,000+ over non-licensed work, most students break even within 1–2 years of working. Run your own numbers in the LPN ROI calculator.
What's the difference between LPN and LVN?
They're the same role — Texas and California call it Licensed Vocational Nurse (LVN); everywhere else it's Licensed Practical Nurse (LPN). Same exam (NCLEX-PN), same scope.
