GTA 6 Wanted System: Cops Only Chase What They See

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, thirteen years after GTA 5 — and IGN's review, watched 7.44 million times on launch day, calls the game a masterpiece. One of the biggest changes hiding inside that praise is easy to miss: the wanted system has been rebuilt. In GTA 6, the police act only on what they actually witness. There is no magic GPS, no instant knowledge of where you are. If no cop — and no witness — sees what you did, the law does not magically find you. That single change rewrites how chases work in Vice City, and this guide explains exactly what it means for the way you play.

It matters because the old habit from GTA 5 will get you caught. In GTA 6, the system is built around observation instead — and understanding that difference is the difference between a five-minute chase and a clean getaway.

How the New Wanted System Works

What IGN's review showed is a system built on line of sight. Police respond to crimes they witness, or crimes that witnesses report — they do not simply know where you are the moment you break the law. If you commit a crime where no one can see you, the response is different from committing the same crime in front of a patrol car. The game is simulating observation: the police have eyes, and they have limits, just like real police do.

That is a real change from the older games, where the wanted system often felt like the whole map was watching you at once. Here, the question is always the same: who saw it? A quiet street at night is a different place from a crowded boulevard at noon, and the game treats it that way. The wanted system is not a timer or a meter you fill — it is a reaction to what the world around you actually observed.

What "Witnessed" Means in Practice

In practice, the rule is simple: if a police officer sees you commit a crime, expect a response right away. If a civilian sees you, expect a response shortly after — witnesses report what they see, and the game's world includes an in-game phone and social media layer, so word travels. If nobody sees anything, the crime may pass unnoticed entirely. That is why the same action can have completely different consequences depending on where and when you do it.

This is also why the time of day and the crowd around you matter more than they used to. A busy Vice City street has dozens of potential witnesses; an empty road in the Leonida countryside has none. Before a risky job, it is worth looking around: who is watching, where are the patrols, and is there a quieter way to do the same thing? The players who treat the world as full of eyes will stay out of trouble far more often than the ones who treat it as empty.

Breaking Line of Sight

When the police are after you, the core of the escape is breaking line of sight. Get out of view, and stay out of view — the search is local, not omniscient. Ducking down a side street, slipping into an alley, or putting a building between you and the patrol changes the situation in a way it never quite did in older games. The police are searching an area because they saw you there, not because a marker on the map is telling them exactly where you are.

Changing vehicles helps for the same reason. The police are looking for what they saw — the car, the clothes, the direction you were heading. A different car, a different street, and a few blocks of distance can turn a hot pursuit into a cold search. And distance matters: the further you get from where you were seen, the weaker the search becomes. The old advice of "just drive fast" is less useful now than the new advice: "stop being seen."

Witnesses Are the Real Danger

The most dangerous person in the new system is not always the cop — it is the bystander. A civilian who watches you commit a crime can report it, and that report brings the police to where you were, not where you are. That means the seconds after a crime matter: a witness standing there with a phone is a problem that a fast getaway alone does not solve. The world of GTA 6 is full of people, and people notice things.

The practical lesson is to think about witnesses before the crime, not after it. A quiet approach, a less crowded route, and a quick exit all reduce the number of people who can describe what you did. And if you do get spotted, the priority is the same as with the police: break line of sight and leave the area quickly, before the report turns into a response.

What Not to Do

Do not assume you are safe just because no police car is on screen. A witness may have seen you, and the response may be on its way. Do not hang around the scene after a crime — the longer you stay where you were seen, the easier you are to find. And do not assume the chase is over the moment the sirens fade: if you stay in the same area, a new witness can start the whole thing again.

Above all, do not play the new system with old habits. Charging through Vice City in daylight, in front of everyone, is the fastest way to learn how the system works — from inside a police chase.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is there still a wanted level in GTA 6?

Yes — the police still respond to crime, and the response still escalates. What Rockstar has not published is a full breakdown of how the levels work. What IGN's review confirmed is the principle behind it: the police act on what they witness, with no magic GPS. The details of escalation are still being learned by players, so treat any "complete wanted level guide" you see with caution until it is verified.

Can I lose the cops just by hiding?

Breaking line of sight and leaving the area is the core of it. The police search where they saw you, not where you are — so hiding, changing vehicles, and putting distance between you and the scene all help. Staying in the same area, even hidden, is the mistake: the search is local, but it is thorough.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6's wanted system rewards players who think about being seen. The police only chase what they witness, so quiet jobs, empty streets, and quick exits keep you free — while loud crimes in front of a crowd bring the whole city down on you. Learn the new rule, break line of sight, and Vice City becomes a much easier place to work in.
