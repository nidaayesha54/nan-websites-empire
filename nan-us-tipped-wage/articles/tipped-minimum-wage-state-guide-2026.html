Tipped Minimum Wage by State 2026: Cash Wages, Tip Credits & Your Rights

The full breakdown of tipped minimum wage laws: how the tip credit works, what happens when tips fall short, and the 50-state picture.

In-depth 2026 guide~6 min readFree

A server's paycheck is one of the most misunderstood documents in American labor law. The hourly rate printed on it — sometimes as low as $2.13 — is not the whole story, and in some states it is not even legal. Between the federal tip credit, 50 different state regimes, and a strict employer "make-up" obligation, tipped wage law has more moving parts than almost any other corner of pay regulation.

This guide breaks it all down: how the tip credit works, what the 2026 cash wages are across the states, what happens when tips fall short, and the rights most tipped workers never learn they have. Use it with our tipped wage calculator to look up your state and check a real paycheck.

The federal baseline: $7.25, $2.13, and the tip credit

Under the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA), the federal minimum wage is $7.25 per hour — unchanged since 2009. For tipped employees, employers may pay a lower cash wage of $2.13 per hour and claim a tip credit of up to $5.12 per hour, so that cash wage + tip credit = $7.25. Federal benefit rules have moved more recently — for example, the repeal of WEP and GPO restored full Social Security benefits to millions of public servants.

Two federal conditions must both be met for the $2.13 cash wage to be legal at all:

  • The worker must be a "tipped employee" — someone who customarily and regularly receives more than $30 per month in tips.
  • The employer must inform the worker of the tip credit provisions (the FLSA's Section 203(m) notice requirement). No notice, no tip credit.

But the federal numbers are only the floor. States set their own rates, and where the state rate is higher, the state rate wins. That is why the actual cash wage on a server's pay stub ranges from $2.13 to over $17 depending on the state.

The make-up rule: your employer's safety net obligation

This is the single most important protection in tipped wage law, and the one most often violated. If your cash wage plus your actual tips do not add up to at least the full minimum wage for every hour you worked, your employer must make up the difference. Tips are yours — the employer cannot use the tip credit to leave you below the minimum.

Example: you work 30 hours in Texas at the $2.13 cash wage and earn $120 in tips. Your total is (30 × $2.13) + $120 = $183.90, which is $6.13/hour — below the $7.25 minimum. Your employer owes you the shortfall: 30 × ($7.25 − $6.13) = $33.60 extra on that paycheck. In a state with a higher minimum, the same math applies against the higher number.

The make-up is calculated per workweek, not per shift — a great Friday does not legally offset a dead Tuesday in the same week, but the week as a whole is the measuring period. Run any suspicious paycheck through our paycheck checker to see the math instantly.

Rules employers break most often

1. Tip pooling with non-tipped staff. When an employer takes the full tip credit, tips may only be pooled among employees who customarily receive tips. Managers and supervisors can never take a share — the DOL has been explicit, and courts have awarded large back-pay judgments on this point.

2. The 80/20 rule and "dual jobs." If you are a server who also does substantial non-tipped side work (rolling silverware, deep cleaning), the employer can only pay the tipped cash wage for the tipped work. Extended stretches of non-tipped duties must be paid at the full minimum wage.

3. Illegal deductions. Walkouts, broken dishes, register shortages, and uniform costs cannot be deducted from your pay if the deduction would drop you below minimum wage — and for tipped workers, several courts apply this to the full minimum, not just the cash wage.

4. Overtime on the wrong base. Overtime for tipped workers is 1.5× the full minimum wage, minus the tip credit — not 1.5× the $2.13 cash wage. In a $7.25 state that means at least $10.88/hour before tips, not $3.20.

5. Service charges are not tips. An automatic 18% "service charge" belongs to the restaurant, not to you, unless the employer distributes it. Only amounts the customer designates as a gratuity count as your tips for tip-credit purposes.

What to do if your pay is short

Start by doing the math yourself for 2–3 recent weeks: (hours × cash wage) + tips, divided by hours, compared against your state's full minimum. Our free salary tools can help you convert between hourly, weekly, and annual figures. Keep copies of pay stubs and your own tip records — the DOL's free timesheet app exists precisely for this. If the numbers show a shortfall, raise it with your employer in writing first; payroll errors are common and sometimes innocent.

If that goes nowhere, you can file a confidential complaint with the U.S. Department of Labor's Wage and Hour Division (1-866-487-9243), which investigates and can recover back wages for up to two years (three for willful violations) — or contact your state labor agency, which enforces the often-stronger state rates. Retaliation for asserting these rights is itself illegal.

FAQs

Can my employer pay me $2.13/hour in my state?

Only if your state allows a tip credit down to the federal cash wage. In the seven no-tip-credit states (CA, OR, WA, AK, NV, MN, MT) and many others with higher cash-wage floors, $2.13 is illegal.

Do I have to report all my tips?

Yes — you must report tips to your employer (monthly, if over $20) for tax withholding, and all tip income is taxable whether cash or card. Underreporting can also weaken a future wage claim.

Can a restaurant require tip pooling?

Mandatory pooling among tipped staff is generally allowed, but managers, supervisors, and (when the employer claims a tip credit) non-tipped back-of-house staff cannot participate.

What is the 80/20 rule in simple terms?

If you spend too much of your shift on work that doesn't earn tips, your employer must pay the full minimum wage — not the tipped cash wage — for that time. Brief side work is fine; endless side work is not.

Does the tip credit apply to overtime hours?

Overtime must be at least 1.5× the full minimum wage minus the tip credit — never 1.5× the cash wage alone. Check overtime lines on your stub separately.
