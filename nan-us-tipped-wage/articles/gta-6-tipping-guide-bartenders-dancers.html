GTA 6 Tipping: Who You Can Tip and What It Costs

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

GTA 6 has a tipping system, and it is more detailed than most players expect. You can tip the bartender when you order a drink, tip a dancer during a private dance, and throw money at the stage — and each of those tips does something different. It is a small system, but it is a real one, and it is easy to waste money on it if you do not know the prices. This guide covers every tipping spot the game actually documents, what each tip costs, and what it buys you.

Tipping matters in GTA 6 for the same reason it matters in real life: it is how you get good service in a world that runs on cash. The game tracks your money in three places — pocket cash, your BuckMe bank account and loot in stash bags — and tips come out of the cash you are carrying. If your pockets are empty, the tip prompt does not help you, so the first rule of tipping in Leonida is the same as the first rule of everything else: bank your money, but keep some on you.

Bars: drinks, prices and the $10 tip

The most common place to tip is any bar. Walk up to the counter, order a drink, and the game shows you the prices: rum and cola costs $9, tequila costs $6, and beers run $5 to $6 depending on the bar. Ordering is a separate step from carrying the drink — you order, you pick it up, and you decide what to do with it — so slow down and read the prompts instead of rushing past the bartender.

The tip is a separate option from the drink price. At the bar counter you will see a $10 tip option, distinct from what the drink itself costs. Tipping the bartender is a role-play spend: it buys you goodwill with the person pouring your drinks, and it is the kind of small detail Rockstar built into the world to make its bars feel like real places. It does not unlock a mission or a discount as far as any guide has shown — it is flavour, and it costs a flat $10.

Strip clubs: $300 dances and $50 tips

The biggest tipping in the game happens in Leonida's three strip clubs: Delights Cabaret in Port Gellhorn, Nine1Nine in Vice City, and Jack of Hearts, also in Vice City. Each club has a stage, a bar and dancers you can approach, and the tipping there has real prices attached.

Approach a dancer and you can pay $300 for a private dance — greet them first, then take the private dance prompt. While the dance is going on, you can tip $50 at a time, and each tip pushes the performance further: more of the routine, more interaction, and prompts that only repeat after you tip again. You can also watch the stage show and throw money at the dancers from the floor, which the game calls making it rain. The clubs are expensive by design — a private dance plus a few tips can eat hundreds of dollars in minutes — so keep an eye on your cash before you sit down.

What tipping does not do

It is worth being clear about the limits. Tipping a bartender does not open a secret mission. Tipping a dancer does not let you hire them for anything beyond the dance — the game has cut prostitution entirely, and no amount of tipping changes that. And tipping does not change the story: no character's opinion of you is tracked through tips, and no ending depends on how generous you were at the bar.

What tipping does do is make the world feel alive, and that is the point. The bartender remembers you were polite. The dancer gives you a better show. The game is full of small systems like this that exist to reward players who slow down and treat Leonida like a place rather than a checklist.

The viral cafe clips are not confirmed

You may have seen clips of a waitress at an outdoor cafe demanding a tip for two coffees, then starting a fight when the customer refuses. Those clips are everywhere on social media, usually captioned as proof that GTA 6 has a realistic tipping system that has gone too far. Treat them as unverified. They are short clips shared without context, and nothing in the documented guides confirms a cafe tipping mechanic where refusing a tip starts a brawl.

The tipping the guides do document is bars and clubs, with fixed prices and clear prompts. If a clip shows something else, it is either a staged scene, a modded moment or a misunderstanding of what is on screen. Do not budget for a mechanic you have only seen in a 30-second clip.

The valet rumour

One more rumour worth flagging: a pre-release video claimed that calling for your car sends a valet driver who brings it to you, then rides away on an electric scooter, and asked whether players should tip the valet. That idea comes from a single unconfirmed source, and no guide has documented a valet tipping prompt in the game. Until one does, treat the valet as a rumour, not a feature — and do not expect a tip prompt when your car arrives.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much is a tip in GTA 6?

At a bar the tip option is $10. In a strip club, a private dance costs $300 and tips during the dance cost $50 each. Drink prices are separate: rum and cola $9, tequila $6, beers $5 to $6.

Where can I tip in GTA 6?

Bars and the three strip clubs — Delights Cabaret in Port Gellhorn, Nine1Nine and Jack of Hearts in Vice City. Those are the tipping spots the guides document.

Does tipping unlock anything in GTA 6?

Not as far as any guide has shown. Tips buy better service in the moment — a better dance, a friendlier bartender — but no mission, discount or ending depends on them.

The bottom line: tipping in GTA 6 is real, it is cheap at the bar and expensive at the club, and it buys atmosphere, not advantage. Tip the bartender, enjoy the show, and keep the viral clips in perspective.
