The law: your cash wage + tips must reach the full minimum wage for every hour worked. If tips fall short, the employer must make up the difference — and the cash wage can never be below the state's tipped cash minimum.

What Is the Tip Credit?

Under federal law, an employer may pay a tipped worker a cash wage as low as $2.13/hour and claim a tip credit of up to $5.12/hour toward the $7.25 federal minimum wage — but only if cash wages plus tips reach the full minimum wage for every hour worked. If tips fall short, the employer must pay the difference. Many states set higher cash wages and smaller credits, and seven states ban the tip credit entirely (Alaska, California, Minnesota, Montana, Nevada, Oregon, Washington): there, tipped workers get the full minimum wage plus all their tips.

FAQs

Who counts as a "tipped employee"?

Under federal law, an employee who customarily and regularly receives more than $30/month in tips. Some states set different thresholds (e.g., Pennsylvania $135/month, Vermont $120/month).

Can my employer keep my tips?

No. Tips are the property of the employee. Employers may require tip pooling among tipped staff in some states, but managers, supervisors, and owners can never take a share.

What if my tips don't bring me to minimum wage?

Your employer must make up the shortfall — every pay period, your cash wages plus tips must at least equal the full minimum wage times hours worked. Use the paycheck checker above.

Does my city have a higher minimum wage?

Possibly — cities like Seattle, New York City, San Francisco, and Chicago set local minimums above their state's. The DOL table (and this tool) shows state levels; always check your city too.

Can I be paid the $2.13 federal cash wage in a high-minimum-wage state?

No. When state and federal law differ, the more protective standard applies — you get whichever cash wage is higher.

Are tips taxed?

Yes — all tips (cash, card, and pooled) are taxable income and must be reported to your employer and the IRS. This tool covers wage compliance, not taxes.
