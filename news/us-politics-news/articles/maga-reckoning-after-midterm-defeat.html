MAGA Asks If It's Over After Republicans' Worst Midterm Defeat in Decades

November 8, 2026

Donald Trump's Make America Great Again movement is facing an existential reckoning after the Republican Party's worst electoral defeat in decades, Reuters reported on November 8, based on interviews with more than a dozen MAGA activists, influencers, party strategists, Christian nationalist leaders and researchers who track right-wing politics.

"Trump has cost us"

As Republicans faced a nationwide drubbing on Tuesday, supporters streamed out of a Dallas ballroom where they had hoped to toast a win for US Senate candidate Ken Paxton, a favourite of President Trump. In the hotel bar, Michael Little, a 59-year-old businessman and three-time Trump voter, stared at the president's image on a television screen. "People are pissed off at Trump. Trump has cost us," he said, capturing a sentiment rippling through the movement after voters disillusioned with soaring fuel prices, the Iran war and Washington corruption delivered Democrats decisive congressional majorities. An analysis of 12,850 posts sampled from 4.6 million Trump-related comments on X and Reddit, from 9 p.m. on Election Day to 8 p.m. the following evening, showed a movement turning its anger on itself. The stolen-election claims that have driven MAGA since Democrat Joe Biden beat Trump in the 2020 presidential election have mostly gone quiet.

Doubling down, not changing course

Trump's first major response to the midterms came on Friday. Instead of changing course, he doubled down on the movement's core, promoting Stephen Miller, the main architect of the administration's hardline immigration crackdown, to chief of staff, replacing Susie Wiles. The White House did not directly answer Reuters' questions about why Republicans lost, the defeat of candidates who embraced Trump's false 2020 election claims, or where MAGA goes from here. MAGA survived the fallout from the January 6, 2021, attack on the US Capitol and propelled Trump back to the White House. Now, the Democrats' sweeping victory has forced the movement to grapple with an unexpected question: Is MAGA over? The results left Trump facing large Democratic majorities in both chambers of Congress. In the Senate, Democrats could control as many as 56 of the chamber's 100 seats, more than enough to block Trump's future judicial picks. Republican Florida Governor Ron DeSantis said the morning after the election that Republicans might not be able to recapture the Senate until 2032.

Sources: Reuters, November 8, 2026
