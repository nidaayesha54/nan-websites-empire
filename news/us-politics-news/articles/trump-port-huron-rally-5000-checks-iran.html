Trump Rallies in Port Huron, Defends Iran Stance and Repeats $5,000 Check Pledge

October 26, 2026

President Donald Trump fired up Republicans at a rally in Port Huron, Michigan, on Sunday, using the border-city stop to defend the ongoing war with Iran, repeat a pledge of $5,000 checks for American adults, and predict a favorable end to the trade fight with Canada.

The crowd inside the event was estimated at about 3,000. Trump told supporters he expects the tariff war with Canada to soon come to an end on terms favorable to the United States, saying Canada has been, in his words, ripping off the United States for years and is now willing to give Washington anything it asks for.

Gas prices and the Iran war

Trump also defended the ongoing war with Iran, saying higher gas prices are a small price to pay for ensuring Iran does not obtain a nuclear weapon. He added that he had expected gasoline prices to rise much more than they have.

The remarks come as high gas prices and continued inflation have weighed on the president's approval ratings, which reports describe as historically low. That political backdrop has caused some Republican candidates to try to distance themselves from the president even as he campaigns for them.

The $5,000 check pledge, repeated

Trump repeated an earlier pledge to send $5,000 checks to all American adults, but only if Republicans retain control of the U.S. House and Senate in the midterms. He has repeatedly urged his supporters to vote in the midterms as if he himself were on the ballot, and he did so again on Sunday in Port Huron.

The pledge ties a direct cash promise to the outcome of the November 3 election, giving Republican campaigns a closing argument aimed at voters feeling squeezed by prices.

Why Michigan matters

Michigan, typically a national battleground, has emerged in 2026 as a critical state in determining control of the U.S. Senate. Fighting for an open seat are former U.S. Representative Mike Rogers, the Republican candidate, and Democrat Abdul El-Sayed, a former Wayne County and Detroit public health director.

Trump also wants to boost the chances of U.S. Representative John James, the Republican vying with Democratic Secretary of State Jocelyn Benson to succeed Governor Gretchen Whitmer, who is term-limited. Both Rogers and James spoke at the Port Huron rally before Trump's speech and returned to the stage during his address, with James in particular heaping praise on the president.

Rogers, who spoke for about seven minutes before Trump took the stage, focused mostly on El-Sayed, urging the crowd to vote Republican in opposition to what he described as a Democratic effort to turn the country socialist.

The Port Huron stop was one of several the president has made in the lead-up to the midterms, as both parties pour energy into the final stretch of a campaign that will decide control of Congress.
