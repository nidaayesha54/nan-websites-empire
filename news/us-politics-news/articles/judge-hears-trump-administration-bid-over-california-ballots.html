Federal Judge Hears Trump Administration Bid Over California Ballots

October 27, 2026

A federal judge heard arguments on Tuesday in the Trump administration's bid to compel California to set aside ballots from voters it alleges may not be citizens, adding a courtroom fight over election administration to the final week of the midterm campaign. The hearing comes after an appeals court declined the administration's request to get involved the night before, according to NPR's election coverage.

The dispute centers on ballots the administration argues should be separated out on citizenship grounds. California election officials administer voting under state law, and elections in the United States are largely run by state and local governments rather than federal authorities. The judge's consideration of the request comes as millions of Americans have already cast ballots early or by mail ahead of the November 3 election.

Ballot fights in the final week

Legal disputes over ballots and voting procedures have become a recurring feature of the closing days of recent elections, and this year's contests are no exception. With control of the House and Senate at stake, both parties have been closely watching how courts handle challenges to election rules in battleground and large states alike. The California case is one of the most prominent such disputes to reach a hearing in the final week before Election Day.

More than 29 million early and mail-in ballots had already been submitted nationwide as of October 26, according to figures cited by Nikkei Asia. Because polling hours vary by state and the country spans six time zones, results are expected to arrive piecemeal on election night, and close races may take days to resolve as remaining ballots are counted.

What happens next

For now, the California ballots at issue remain the subject of the court proceedings, and no final ruling on the administration's request had been reported as of Tuesday. Election officials in California and other states have continued to urge voters to check their registration status and polling locations ahead of November 3, when all 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats, and governorships in 36 states will be decided.

The outcome of the California case could influence how similar challenges are handled elsewhere, but analysts note that the broader election landscape will ultimately be decided by voters. With polling averages favoring Democrats in the House race and the Senate rated a toss-up, the final week of campaigning is expected to focus heavily on turnout in the most competitive districts and states.
