Trump Approval Falls to a Record-Low 31% in Reuters/Ipsos Poll as Midterms Near

October 26, 2026

President Donald Trump's approval rating has fallen to a record low as the November 3 midterm elections approach, according to a Reuters/Ipsos poll that closed on Monday, October 19, 2026. Some 31% of Americans approve of Trump's performance in office, down a percentage point from two weeks earlier and the lowest level in either of his presidential terms, the four-day poll showed.

The poll also delivered a warning for Republicans on the ballot question that matters most in a midterm year. Asked which party they would vote for if the congressional elections were held today, some 47% of registered voters picked Democrats and 35% chose Republicans. The 12-point Democratic advantage was the widest over the current election cycle, and it could bolster Democratic hopes of seizing control of the House of Representatives and the Senate.

The economy drives the numbers

The survey points to the cost of living as the central drag on the president's standing. High gasoline prices featured prominently in Reuters' account of the poll, and a related Reuters/Ipsos report found that 68% of Americans disapprove of Trump's handling of the economy, including 37% of self-identified Republicans. Cost of living has emerged as the top issue on voters' minds as they decide how they will vote, ahead of democratic values and immigration, according to the polling.

The latest survey gathered responses from 1,679 U.S. adults, including 1,279 registered voters, with a margin of error of 2 percentage points for all respondents and 3 points for registered voters. It follows an earlier October poll that had put Trump's approval at 32%, unchanged from a September survey that first marked the record low. Hispanic voters have also cooled sharply: just 24% of Hispanic Americans approved of Trump's performance in the earlier October survey, the lowest share in either of his terms.

Why it matters for Congress

For Republicans, losing control of either chamber could complicate Trump's agenda in the last two years of his term, Reuters noted. Generic ballot polls give a national snapshot of sentiment rather than a district-by-district forecast, and Republicans hold some structural advantages in the state-by-state and district contests that actually decide control. Even so, a record-low approval rating this close to Election Day leaves little room for error — and gives Democrats a closing argument built around affordability.

The slide has been steady rather than sudden. A September Reuters/Ipsos survey first marked the record low, and an October survey held at 32% before the latest poll slipped to 31%. Support has fallen across every major demographic group since Trump took office in January 2025, according to Reuters' account of the polling, including double-digit drops among Hispanics, men, rural and suburban voters, people without college degrees and evangelical Christians. One bright spot for Republicans in the data is older voters, who still favor the party by 2 percentage points, 44% to 42% — though that was down from an 8-point advantage in December. The enthusiasm gap, however, remains the party's most immediate problem: its own voters appear less eager to turn out than its opponents'.

Sources: Reuters, Reuters/Ipsos
