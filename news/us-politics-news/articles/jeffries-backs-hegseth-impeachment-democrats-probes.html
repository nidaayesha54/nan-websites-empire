Jeffries Backs Hegseth Impeachment as Democrats Vow Probes of Trump Allies

November 9, 2026

House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries has thrown his support behind impeaching Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, as Democrats prepare to use their incoming congressional majorities to investigate President Donald Trump's administration, Al Jazeera reported on November 9.

A \"hard yes\" on the Hegseth resolution

In a Sunday appearance on ABC's This Week with George Stephanopoulos, Jeffries said Democrats would support the impeachment resolution against Hegseth, which has been spearheaded by outgoing Republican Representative Thomas Massie of Kentucky, a vocal critic of the US war on Iran. \"I'm a hard yes on the Hegseth resolution,\" Jeffries said, adding that Democrats would discuss it when they return to the Capitol this week. Jeffries is poised to become the next Speaker of the House after Democrats' historic victory in the November 3 midterm elections, in which both the House and the Senate flipped to Democratic control. The new Congress is sworn in on January 3.

Probes of Musk, Miller and corporate America

Senior Democrats used the Sunday shows to lay out an aggressive oversight agenda. Representative Robert Garcia of California, who is set to lead the House Oversight Committee, and Senator Elizabeth Warren of Massachusetts, set to lead the Senate Banking Committee, vowed investigations into tech billionaire Elon Musk, newly appointed White House chief of staff Stephen Miller and large companies, The Economic Times reported. Garcia said his committee would investigate what he called the \"Trump crime family.\" Jeffries said the Democratic majority would move \"aggressively\" in its first 100 days, both to address affordability and \"unprecedented corruption\" under Trump. The Trump administration has pushed back: Trump said on social media that investigative requests already made by Democrats amount to \"extortion at the highest level.\" About seven in 10 midterm voters said Congress is not doing enough to provide checks and balances on the presidency, according to The AP Voter Poll of more than 100,000 voters.

Sources: Al Jazeera, November 9, 2026; The Economic Times, November 9, 2026
