Hakeem Jeffries Poised to Become First Black Speaker of the House

November 7, 2026

House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries of New York is on the verge of making history as the first Black speaker of the US House of Representatives after Democrats' decisive victory in Tuesday's midterm elections, Al Jazeera reported on November 7.

A two-decade rise to the top job

For the 56-year-old congressman, the speakership would be the culmination of almost two decades in office, giving him a central role in Democrats' efforts to rein in Republican President Donald Trump in the final two years of his presidency. Jeffries celebrated what he called a "blue tsunami" the day after the election, and no serious challenger to his bid has emerged so far. He appears to have the speakership all but secured, though the formal vote takes place when the new Congress is sworn in this January.

Pressure from the progressive wing

Not everyone in the party is convinced Jeffries is the right man for the moment. An online petition calling on lawmakers to choose another speaker had collected more than 980,000 signatures by Saturday morning, and several newly elected progressive lawmakers have not said whether they will support him. Axios reported on a plan by some incoming progressives — informally known as "The Bloc" and including Claire Valdez and Darializa Avila Chevalier of New York and Melat Kiros of Colorado — to use the upcoming speaker vote to win concessions on issues such as Medicare for All, cutting funding for ICE, and weapons shipments to Israel. Jeffries, known for his cautious approach and longstanding support for Israel, now faces the bigger test of holding a fractious caucus together once he is on the job.

Sources: Al Jazeera, November 7, 2026
