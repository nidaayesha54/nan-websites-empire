Early Voting Surges Past 47.9 Million as Texas Turnout Jumps 50% Ahead of the Midterms

October 31, 2026

Early voting in the 2026 midterm elections has surged well past the pace of four years ago, with nearly 47.9 million ballots already cast before Election Day on Tuesday, November 3, according to data from the University of Florida's Election Lab reported on October 31.

A record early-vote pace

An Associated Press analysis of voting data covering 47 states and Washington, DC found nearly 10 million more early ballots cast than at the same point in 2022. The Election Lab put the national total at 47,922,669 early votes by midday on October 30, already ahead of the previous midterm early-voting record of about 45.9 million ballots cast in 2022.

The increase has been sharpest in states with competitive Senate races. In Texas, more than 6.3 million people had voted by Wednesday, compared with 4.2 million at the same point in 2022, a rise of about 50%. Early voting also rose by more than 30% in Maine, Michigan, North Carolina and Kansas, all home to competitive Senate contests. In states that report party affiliation, about 6.8 million Democrats and 5.2 million Republicans had cast early ballots, though those figures do not cover all early voters and do not show which candidates any voter supported.

What is at stake on Tuesday

Voters will decide all 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships. Republicans currently hold a 53-47 majority in the Senate and a 218-214 edge in the House, and forecasters see a Democratic House as likely while the Senate remains close, with control resting on a handful of toss-up races. Early-vote totals are a measure of turnout, not a result: states certify outcomes only after completing their canvass, and close races can take days to call.

Sources: Associated Press via Times Now, University of Florida Election Lab, Indian Express, October 31, 2026
