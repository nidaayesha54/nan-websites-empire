Midterm Early Voting Nears 27 Million Ballots, Running Ahead of the 2022 Pace

October 26, 2026

Nearly 27 million Americans have already cast ballots in the 2026 midterm elections, pushing early turnout ahead of the pace set four years ago and giving both parties a large pool of completed votes to study in the final days before Election Day.

The national count reached 26,849,689 ballots across 46 reporting states as of October 25, according to a tracker compiled from state election offices. The total includes roughly 13.7 million returned mail ballots and 13.1 million in-person early votes.

Running about 10 percent ahead of 2022

Early voting is running about 10 percent ahead of the same point before the 2022 midterms, based on the current national count. Texas leads the country in ballots cast, followed by California, Florida, Georgia, North Carolina and Virginia.

The figures reflect a mix of mail voting and in-person voting, and reporting practices vary by state. Some states update totals several times a day, while others report on a delayed schedule, so national counts should be treated as a moving snapshot rather than a final measure of turnout.

What the numbers do and do not say

The size of the early vote matters because it shows that a substantial share of the electorate has already made a final choice. What it does not reveal is which candidates those voters selected, since ballot totals are reported without results attached.

That distinction matters for campaign strategy in the closing stretch. With tens of millions of votes already banked, both parties are now competing for a shrinking pool of voters who have not yet cast a ballot, while also working to make sure their own supporters who requested mail ballots return them on time.

More states open polls this week

Early voting is still expanding. In Washington, D.C., for example, early voting for the November 3 election begins on October 26 and runs through November 1, with same-day registration available at voting centers for residents who can show proof of residence.

Voters who have not yet cast a ballot should check their state or local election office for early voting locations, hours and identification requirements, since rules and deadlines differ from state to state. Election Day itself falls on Tuesday, November 3.

Turnout watchers will keep updating the national count daily through Election Day. If the current pace holds, the 2026 midterms could finish with one of the largest early-vote totals ever recorded in a midterm cycle, even before the votes cast on Election Day itself are counted.
