FBI Investigating Suspicious Letters Sent to Three Arizona Democrats

October 26, 2026

Federal officials are investigating after the offices of three U.S. House Democrats from Arizona received suspicious letters containing unknown substances. U.S. Reps. Adelita Grijalva, Greg Stanton and Yassamin Ansari said on October 19 that state and federal law enforcement had informed them over the weekend about the letters.

In a joint statement, the three members of Congress said: “We will not be intimidated, and we’ll keep doing the work that Arizonans elected us to do.” They said the FBI's Phoenix field office and the Arizona Department of Homeland Security had alerted their offices, and that the FBI and the U.S. Postal Inspection Service are investigating. The letters addressed to Grijalva and Ansari were sent to former office addresses, while the letter addressed to Stanton's office was intercepted before delivery.

Part of a wider pattern

The Arizona incidents are not isolated. The Arizona Executive Tower at the state Capitol in Phoenix was evacuated on October 15 after staffers in the Secretary of State's Office opened an envelope containing white powder. The Louisiana Secretary of State's Office in Baton Rouge reported a similar incident two days earlier, and election officials in Ohio and Oklahoma also reported receiving suspicious packages earlier in October. No injuries have been reported in those states, according to local media.

The pattern has grown wider still in the days since. Cronkite News reported that more than three dozen congressional and election offices in 32 states have been sent suspicious mail in recent days, with recipients including lawmakers in both parties, and that the powder in a letter sent to Grijalva tested negative for anything harmful, according to an aide. The House Sergeant at Arms told lawmakers that tests on letters sent to congressional offices have not shown any dangerous substances, according to Roll Call, as cited by Cronkite News.

A tense final stretch

The incidents arrive in the final weeks before the November 3 midterm elections, when threats against election administration have become a recurring flashpoint. Law enforcement has not publicly identified a sender or detailed the contents of the Arizona letters beyond describing unknown substances. For now, the lawmakers' message is defiance: the letters, they said, will not stop them from doing the work they were elected to do — and investigators are working to find whoever sent them.

The FBI's Phoenix field office has described the investigation in careful terms. Brooke Brennan, a spokeswoman for the office, told Cronkite News that the FBI and the U.S. Postal Inspection Service are investigating a series of suspicious mailings across the country, saying: “Some of the letters contained an unknown substance, and we are working closely with our law enforcement partners to respond to each incident and to find whoever sent the letters.” Officials have not released further details about the letters, including what they contained or who may have sent them, and the investigation continues.
