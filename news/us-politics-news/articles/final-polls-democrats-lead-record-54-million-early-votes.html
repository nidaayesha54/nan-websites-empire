Final Polls Point to Democratic Gains as Record 54 Million Vote Early

November 2, 2026

Democrats enter Election Day with polls pointing to at least partial control of Congress, as a record early vote and a string of final surveys shape expectations for Tuesday, November 3, Agence France-Presse reported on November 2.

A record early vote

More than 54 million people have already cast ballots, a midterm record, up from the 51 million reported days earlier. Democrats held final get-out-the-vote rallies on Monday, including star Senate candidates James Talarico in Texas and Adam Hamilton in Kansas. Polls show Democrats near certain to take the House majority and with a real shot at the Senate, which Republicans currently hold 53-47.

What the final surveys say

The final New York Times/Siena poll released Sunday evening showed Democratic candidates leading in all seven seats viewed as most competitive, though five remained within the margin of error. An NBC News poll released Sunday showed 60 percent disapproving of the president's performance, particularly over high prices exacerbated by his eight-month war against Iran. In a sign of Democratic infighting, Michigan congresswoman Haley Stevens endorsed Republican Mike Rogers against progressive candidate Abdul El-Sayed in her state's key Senate race on Sunday. Polls are snapshots, not results — voters decide on Tuesday.

Sources: Agence France-Presse via Philstar, The Source, November 2, 2026
