Polls Give Democrats the Edge One Week Before the 2026 Midterms

October 27, 2026

One week before Americans vote in the 2026 midterm elections, polling averages point to a Democratic advantage in the race for control of Congress, though a batch of closely contested races could still decide the final outcome. Voters will decide all 435 seats in the House of Representatives and 35 of the Senate's 100 seats on November 3, along with governorships in 36 states and a wide range of state and local offices.

Polling averages compiled by RealClearPolitics project Democrats winning 46 Senate seats to 44 for Republicans, with 10 races still too close to call. Of those competitive seats, eight lean Republican and two lean Democratic, according to the averages. If the Senate were to split 50-50, Vice President JD Vance would hold the deciding vote, which means Republicans could still retain control by winning enough of the toss-up contests.

House outlook favors Democrats, Senate remains a toss-up

In the House, where 218 seats are needed for a majority, the same averages project Democrats winning 227 seats to 182 for Republicans, with 26 seats rated highly competitive. Republicans currently hold 218 House seats to 214 for Democrats, with one seat held by an independent and two seats vacant. In the Senate, Republicans currently hold 53 seats to 45 for Democrats, with two independents who generally align with Democrats.

A new NPR/PBS News/Marist poll released this week found Democrats holding an 8-point advantage among likely voters on the generic congressional ballot, a widely watched measure of the national mood. The poll put President Donald Trump's approval rating at 40 percent. Analysts cited by NPR noted that while a Democratic House majority now looks likely, the Senate remains a toss-up, and close races may not be decided on election night as votes are counted.

What voters say is driving their choices

The NPR/PBS News/Marist poll found economic strain weighing heavily on voters, with one in three Americans reporting financial hardship and four in 10 saying their personal finances are worsening. Preserving democracy was reported as a leading concern for Democrats and independents, while immigration ranked as the top issue for supporters of the president's MAGA movement. Analysts also noted that the president's party has historically lost seats in midterm elections, a pattern that has held in most cycles since 1946.

The Senate contests include special elections in Florida and Ohio to fill vacancies left by Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Vice President Vance. The winners will be sworn in on January 3, 2027, and the balance of power they determine will shape the president's legislative agenda for the remaining two years of his term.

Sources: RealClearPolitics polling averages, NPR/PBS News/Marist poll
