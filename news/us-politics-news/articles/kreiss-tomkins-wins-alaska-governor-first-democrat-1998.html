Kreiss-Tomkins Wins Alaska Governor's Race, First Democrat Elected Since 1998

November 14, 2026

Alaska voters have elected a Democrat for governor for the first time since 1998, the Associated Press reported on November 14, calling the race on Friday for former state lawmaker Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins.

A three-way Republican field

Kreiss-Tomkins, 37, defeated three Republicans — Bernadette Wilson, an Anchorage businesswoman backed by President Donald Trump; Dave Bronson, a former mayor of Anchorage; and Treg Taylor, a former state attorney general. He passed 50 percent of first-choice votes late Thursday with about 88 percent counted, so the race did not require a ranked-choice redistribution to determine a winner, NBC News reported.

Democrats reach 30 governorships

His victory means Democrats will control the governor's office in 30 states, up from 24 before the November 3 midterms, after flips in places such as Iowa, Georgia, Ohio and Texas. Kreiss-Tomkins, who returns to politics three years after leaving the state House, will succeed term-limited Republican Governor Mike Dunleavy and be sworn in on December 7, with independent Zac Johnson as lieutenant governor.

Sources: Associated Press, November 14, 2026; Reuters, November 14, 2026
