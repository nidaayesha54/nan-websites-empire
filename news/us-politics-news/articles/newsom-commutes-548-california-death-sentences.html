Newsom Commutes All 548 California Death Sentences to Life Without Parole

November 6, 2026

Gov. Gavin Newsom announced on November 5 that he will commute all 548 California death sentences to life in prison without the possibility of parole, an extraordinary use of his clemency authority that clears the largest death row in the Western Hemisphere, USA Today and The New York Times reported.

What the commutation does — and does not do

The action covers 529 men and 19 women currently sentenced to death in California, and the Death Penalty Information Center described it as the largest mass commutation in US history. In an open letter, Newsom argued that state-sanctioned killing is incompatible with California's values and undermines the foundations of its legal system. The commutations do not repeal the death penalty, which remains on the state's statute books, and they do not overturn convictions, grant pardons or make anyone eligible for release. Restitution orders also remain in place. Newsom imposed a moratorium on executions shortly after taking office in 2019, and California last carried out an execution in 2006.

Why most cases are not final yet

Not every commutation takes effect immediately. Under the California Constitution, the state Supreme Court must recommend clemency for anyone convicted of more than one felony, and that requirement applies to about 382 of the 548 cases — roughly two-thirds. Newsom has asked the court to return its recommendations by the end of 2026 so he can issue commutation certificates before his term ends in January. The remaining cases, about one-third, can take effect on his authority alone. Prosecutors in several counties, including Riverside, criticised the decision, saying district attorneys and victims' families were not consulted in advance. Newsom, who is reported to be considering a run for president in 2028, leaves office in January.

Sources: USA Today, The New York Times, November 5, 2026
