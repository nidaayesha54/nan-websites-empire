Trump Cancels Iowa and Kansas Rallies, Switching to Tele-Rallies Across Nearly 20 States

October 30, 2026

President Donald Trump has abruptly changed his final midterm campaign push, canceling planned weekend rallies in Iowa and Kansas and replacing them with tele-rallies reaching voters in nearly 20 states, according to the Republican National Committee and reporting on Friday, October 30.

From in-person rallies to virtual events

The change is striking because both Iowa and Kansas have unexpectedly competitive Senate races, and Trump had only just campaigned in Ohio on Wednesday before a scheduled stop in South Carolina on Thursday. Instead of traveling to Iowa and Kansas, he will campaign in Florida on Friday, a state he won handily in 2024 but where early voting has tilted toward Democrats, according to Reuters. Former Vice President Kamala Harris is scheduled to rally for Democrats in Atlanta on Friday.

The shift comes with the clock running down: more than 44 million Americans had already voted as of Thursday evening, according to the University of Florida's Election Lab, less than two million short of the total from the 2022 midterms.

What the polls and markets show

With four days until the November 3 election, Democrats enter the final weekend with the advantage nationally and are favored to take the House, while the Senate battle remains much closer. RealClearPolling's generic congressional ballot average puts Democrats ahead by 8.2 percentage points, 51.4% to 43.2%, and a Harvard-Harris survey gives Democrats a 4-point lead, 52% to 48%.

Prediction markets are more bullish still for Democrats: as of Friday morning, Polymarket gave Democrats a 94% chance of winning the House and a 69% chance of taking the Senate, while Kalshi put the House figure at about 92%. Forecasters are somewhat more cautious: Decision Desk HQ puts Democrats' House chances at 80% and projects 52 Senate seats, while the Cook Political Report projects a Democratic gain of 12 to 22 House seats. Prediction-market prices are not polls; they reflect what traders are willing to pay, and they can move quickly.

Why the Senate still matters most

Current polling averages have Democrats narrowly ahead in Iowa and Kansas, while races in Maine, Alaska, Ohio and Texas remain important to the fight for control. Democrats need a net gain of four seats to win back the Senate majority from Republicans, which is why even a small polling edge in a handful of states could decide the chamber — and the final two years of Trump's presidency.

Sources: Reuters, NorthJersey.com, Action Network, October 30, 2026
