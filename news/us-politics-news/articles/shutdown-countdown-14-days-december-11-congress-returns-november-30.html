Shutdown Countdown: 14 Days Left as Congress Returns November 30 With No Spending Bills Passed

November 27, 2026

The federal government is funded through Friday, December 11, 2026, under a stopgap law, and with 14 days left as of November 27, not one of the 12 regular spending bills for fiscal year 2027 has become law, according to the House's government shutdown tracker updated on November 27.

Where the 12 bills stand

The House has passed three of the 12 bills and the Senate has passed none, Congress.gov records show. Congress is away for Thanksgiving until November 30, leaving under two weeks to act when it returns. The stopgap, Public Law 119-103, was signed on September 2, and if Congress has not enacted the regular bills or another stopgap by the end of December 11, a funding gap begins on December 12. In all six midterm lame ducks since 2002, the first deadline after the election was extended, the House tracker notes.

The sticking points

Three disputes are holding up a deal: whether any stopgap should restrict funding for the war in Iran, President Trump's demand for $350 billion more for defence through a separate reconciliation package, and how long any extension should run, with Democrats preferring a short extension into early 2027 and Republicans wanting a longer one or full-year bills. Democrats won control of both chambers in the November 3 midterm elections and take over in January, but until then the December 11 deadline belongs to the outgoing Republican majority. The quickest way out is another stopgap; a shutdown beginning December 12 would start two weeks before Christmas.

Sources: GOV.HOUSE, November 27, 2026; Around Prague, November 27, 2026
