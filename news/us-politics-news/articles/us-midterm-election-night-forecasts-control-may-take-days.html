Election Night: Forecasts Point One Way, but Control May Take Days to Call

November 3, 2026

Forecasts published before Election Day point in the same direction but differ on confidence, and none of them is a result: voters, not models, decide control of Congress, reports on November 3 stressed.

What the models say

Decision Desk HQ's model, updated on October 29, gave Democrats an 80 percent chance in the House, with 238 seats projected, and a 60 percent chance in the Senate, with 52 seats projected. The Cook Political Report projected Democratic gains of 12 to 22 House seats and two to six Senate seats, while prediction markets priced Democratic control higher, at 92 to 93 percent for the House and 68 percent for the Senate. RealClearPolling put Democrats 8.3 points ahead on the generic congressional ballot, and a Reuters/Ipsos poll put President Trump's approval at 32 percent, near a record low.

Why the wait

Results start after the first polls close at 6 p.m. ET, but close races can take days, and final tallies can take days or weeks as mail-in and provisional ballots are counted. In Georgia, a Senate race with no majority winner goes to a run-off on December 1. Ranked-choice counts in states such as Alaska and Maine could also leave control uncertain, and the new Congress will take office on January 3, 2027. Early leads can shift as later ballots are counted, which on its own is not a sign of fraud, Al Jazeera noted.
