Jim Jordan Elected House Minority Leader, Beating Scalise 106-73 After Midterm Rout

November 11, 2026

House Republicans have chosen Representative Jim Jordan of Ohio as their next minority leader, handing the combative Judiciary Committee chairman a decisive victory after the party's sweeping midterm losses, US media reported on November 11.

Jordan beats Scalise 106-73

Jordan defeated House Majority Leader Steve Scalise of Louisiana 106 votes to 73 in a closed-door, secret-ballot election on Tuesday. The Ohio congressman will replace House Speaker Mike Johnson, who announced he was stepping down from leadership after Republicans lost control of both chambers of Congress in last week's elections. The contest was a rematch of their 2023 battle for the speakership following the ouster of Kevin McCarthy. Senate Republicans meanwhile re-elected John Thune as their leader, retaining their existing leadership team despite demands from conservatives for a reckoning over the election losses.

A brawler takes the reins

Jordan, a co-founder of the ultra-conservative House Freedom Caucus and one of President Donald Trump's closest congressional allies, built his career opposing his own party's leadership. He now heads a much smaller Republican conference as Democrats prepare to control the House in January, with Representative Hakeem Jeffries in line to become Speaker. Republicans also kept Representative Tom Emmer of Minnesota as minority whip and returned Representative Lisa McClain of Michigan as conference chair. Both President Trump and Johnson stayed out of the leadership contest.

Sources: New York Post, November 11, 2026; Malay Mail, November 11, 2026
