Election Day: Polls Open Across America After a Record 58 Million Early Votes

November 3, 2026

Americans are voting on Tuesday, November 3, in midterm elections that will decide control of Congress for the final two years of President Donald Trump's second term, with polls opening across all 50 states and no results reported yet, live coverage reported.

A record early vote

More than 58 million people had cast ballots before Election Day, according to the University of Florida Election Lab, cited by CBS News and the Financial Express — a record for a midterm election, up from the 54 million reported on November 2. All 435 House seats, 35 Senate seats and 36 governorships are on the ballot. Republicans currently hold a 53-47 Senate advantage and a narrow House majority, so a handful of competitive races in states including Michigan, Ohio, North Carolina, Georgia and Iowa are expected to decide control.

Polls, monitors and the count ahead

Polls opened as early as 5 a.m. ET in Vermont and at 6 a.m. ET in states including Connecticut, Maine, New Jersey, New York and Virginia. The Justice Department said it would send roughly 1,000 election monitors across all 50 states, describing it as the largest federal monitoring effort in its history. The first polls close at 6 p.m. ET, and close races may take days to call as mail-in and provisional ballots are counted.
