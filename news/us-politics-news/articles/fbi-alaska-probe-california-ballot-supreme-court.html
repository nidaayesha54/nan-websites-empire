FBI Alaska Probe and California Ballot Fight Intensify in Final Midterm Weekend

November 1, 2026

The Trump administration stepped up its legal and investigative efforts on the final weekend of the midterm campaign, as federal prosecutors investigated an alleged candidate-recruitment effort in Alaska's pivotal Senate race and the Justice Department said it would likely take its fight over thousands of California mail-in ballots to the Supreme Court, Reuters reported on November 1.

An Alaska investigation with Senate control at stake

In Alaska, federal prosecutors are investigating whether aides to Democratic challenger Mary Peltola helped recruit a retired schoolteacher, Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., to run because his name is similar to that of the Republican incumbent, Senator Dan S. Sullivan. The closely contested race is seen as crucial to Democrats' hopes of taking the Senate in Tuesday's vote. Alaska's Senate race is one of seven key toss-ups that will likely decide whether Republicans keep their 53-47 majority in the chamber.

California ballots head toward the Supreme Court

In California, a federal appeals court ruled 2-1 this week that the Justice Department had failed to justify its emergency bid to force the state to set aside postal ballots from more than 20,000 registered voters it alleges are likely to be non-citizens. Acting Deputy Attorney General Trent McCotter indicated on Saturday that the administration could take the dispute to the country's highest court. California Governor Gavin Newsom warned the state's top federal judges on Saturday that the Justice Department may seek warrants to seize ballots before results are certified, though he said he did not know of any pending request. Separately, the administration has sued roughly 30 states and the District of Columbia seeking unredacted voter rolls.
