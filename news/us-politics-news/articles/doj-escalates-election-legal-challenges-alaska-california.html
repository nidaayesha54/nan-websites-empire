Justice Department Escalates Election Legal Challenges in Alaska and California Days Before Midterms

October 30, 2026

The Trump administration is intensifying legal action against US states with just days to go until the midterm election, Reuters reported on October 30, with new moves in Alaska and California that election officials say amount to an extraordinary federal intervention in elections administered locally under the Constitution.

Alaska: a bid to block certification in a close race

In Alaska, the Justice Department filed an amended lawsuit on Thursday seeking to block the certification of results in the event of a narrow Republican loss in a hugely consequential Senate race. The suit cites a New York Times report that Democratic operatives recruited a spoiler candidate to siphon votes from the Republican incumbent, Senator Dan Sullivan, who faces Democrat Mary Peltola. The Times reported on Wednesday that operatives recruited a candidate with the same name as the incumbent.

Alaska is home to one of about 10 highly competitive Senate races, and a dispute there could leave control of the chamber unclear for weeks. Democrats need a net gain of four seats to win back the majority.

California: subpoenas in a non-citizen voting probe

In California, the Justice Department is escalating its pressure campaign against state election officials, issuing subpoenas as part of a criminal probe into alleged voting by non-citizens, according to copies of documents seen by Reuters. The department has sued 30 states and the District of Columbia to obtain voter registration lists, saying it needs them to find non-citizens, and has lost more than two dozen of those cases, according to reporting on the broader campaign.

A federal judge rejected a related California request on Wednesday to set aside ballots from about 20,000 people the department suspects are non-citizens, saying it would wreak havoc on an election already underway. The White House says it is protecting election security, and many of the feared scenarios remain hypothetical; federal courts have so far rebuffed the administration's main legal challenges.

What officials are bracing for next

Election officials around the country have told Reuters they are bracing for further legal challenges from Washington, including a planned deployment of around 1,000 federal election monitors to all 50 states — the largest ever. If Democrats take one or both chambers of Congress, they could launch probes into the administration and increase pressure on the president, including over the war in Iran, according to Reuters.
