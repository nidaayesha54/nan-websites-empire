US Prepares New Tariffs on China and Dozens of Countries Over Industrial Subsidies

November 24, 2026

The United States is preparing to raise tariffs on China and dozens of other countries that the Trump administration argues are harming American companies by supporting their own domestic industrial production, the Wall Street Journal reported on November 24, citing people familiar with the plans.

What is reportedly coming

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer is expected as soon as this week to unveil the results of a months-long investigation into the industrial subsidies of China, the European Union and 14 other nations, though the people familiar with the plans warned the timing could change. Bloomberg subsequently reported that the administration could announce the fresh tariffs as early as Tuesday. The new tariffs would cover most products exported from the targeted economies, though steel, aluminum and automobiles are likely to be excluded because they are already tariffed under a separate national-security authority. The European Union, Japan and South Korea will likely face tariffs of 15 percent or lower, reflecting trade pacts they signed with the administration, while countries such as India and Vietnam could face higher rates, and China is likely to receive a higher tariff than many other nations.

Rebuilding the tariff system

The move would help rebuild much of the original tariff system after the Supreme Court struck down the administration's first batch of levies in February 2026. Administration officials have said they intend to impose tariffs similar to those set in 2025, and the new duties would likely be added to separate tariffs of 10 to 12.5 percent announced in July against 60 economies over forced-labor trade concerns. U.S. stock futures slid after the report, with Nasdaq 100 futures down more than 2 percent. The U.S. Trade Representative's office declined to comment, and the White House did not respond to requests for comment, the reports said.

Sources: The Wall Street Journal, November 24, 2026; Bloomberg via Moneycontrol, November 24, 2026
