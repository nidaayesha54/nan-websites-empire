Harmeet Dhillon Resigns as Justice Department Civil Rights Chief After Midterm Defeat

November 6, 2026

Harmeet Dhillon, who led the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division since April 2025, resigned on November 5, becoming the first senior Trump administration official to leave after Democrats retook both chambers of Congress in Tuesday's midterm elections, Reuters and USA Today reported.

A planned exit, delayed until after the vote

Attorney General Todd Blanche announced the departure on November 5, saying Dhillon had led the division with conviction over the past 18 months. Dhillon told Fox News Digital that the decision was her own: she said she had informed White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles in October that she wanted to return to private practice, and that Wiles asked her to wait until after the midterms. She formally submitted her resignation on November 5 and plans to return to her firm, the Dhillon Law Group. She also cited a gruelling schedule following the deaths of her husband and her father in the lead-up to the 2024 election.

What her tenure changed

Under Dhillon, the division was reshaped around Trump administration priorities, including challenges to diversity initiatives at universities, lawsuits over transgender participation in women's sports, Second Amendment cases and election-integrity work. The Associated Press reported that more than three-quarters of the division's lawyers at the start of Trump's second term have since left, and the division dropped proposed agreements that would have put police reforms in Louisville and Minneapolis under court supervision. Her departure comes as the incoming Democratic Congress prepares investigations into the administration, and the NAACP has urged her successor to enforce civil rights laws fairly.

Sources: Reuters, USA Today, Al Jazeera, November 5, 2026
