Hornets 115–110 Heat: Miller's 34 Drops Miami to 0–3

October 26, 2026

Brandon Miller scored 34 points and the Charlotte Hornets beat the winless Miami Heat 115–110 on Saturday, October 24, 2026, bouncing back from one of the worst losses in franchise history a night earlier. The win moved Charlotte to 2–1; the loss dropped Miami, which added superstar forward Giannis Antetokounmpo during the summer, to 0–3.

The turnaround was personal for Miller. Charlotte's 128–81 loss to Atlanta on Friday in its home opener was the fifth-largest in franchise history, and Miller missed 12 of 14 field goal attempts and 10 of his 11 three-point tries in a six-point effort that came four days after he signed a five-year, $209 million contract extension. On Saturday he delivered a game-high 34 points with seven rebounds and four assists. Dennis Schroder added 25 points, Naz Reid had 23 and Grayson Allen 20 for Charlotte.

A third-quarter surge, then a Miami rally

Charlotte, which was 11 of 51 from three-point range on Friday, made 16 of 43 on Saturday. Five of those threes sparked a 24–7 run in the third quarter that turned a 68–63 lead into a 92–70 advantage with 2:55 left in the quarter, to the delight of the sellout crowd of 19,611 at Spectrum Center. But Miami refused to go away: with Antetokounmpo in foul trouble — he would foul out with 1:57 to play — coach Erik Spoelstra's rally forged ties at 100, 102 and 104.

After the final tie with 3:46 to play, Allen turned the momentum back Charlotte's way for good with an acrobatic three-pointer from the left wing while being fouled by Tim Hardaway Jr. at the end of the 24-second shot clock. Antetokounmpo was limited to 14 points — more than 20 fewer than the 35.5 per game he averaged in his first two games after his trade from the Milwaukee Bucks — while Davion Mitchell led Miami with 22 points and 10 assists.

Spoelstra's challenge

After the game, Spoelstra sent a clear message to his stuttering roster: “We just have to find a way. That's what it is right now. We have to be able to sustain that level of effort and focus and discipline for 48 minutes.” He added: “Our system is our system. We can be that active at any point during the game. When we aren't we are choosing not to and paying the price right now.” It is, per ESPN Research as cited by the Daily Caller, the first 0–3 start of Spoelstra's 19 years as Miami's head coach. Three games is far too early to panic — but for a team built to contend now, it is also far too early to look this lost.
