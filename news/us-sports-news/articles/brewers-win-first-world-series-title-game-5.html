Brewers Win First World Series Title in Franchise History, Beating Rays 4-1 in Game 5

October 29, 2026

The Milwaukee Brewers are World Series champions for the first time. Milwaukee beat the Tampa Bay Rays 4-1 in Game 5 on Wednesday night, October 28, at Tropicana Field, capping a 4-1 series victory and ending a championship wait that stretched across the franchise's 58-year history.

How Game 5 was won

Second-year ace Jacob Misiorowski threw 5 2/3 scoreless innings of two-hit ball before leaving with a right calf cramp, departing with a 4-0 lead. Rookie shortstop Cooper Pratt drove in two runs and set a World Series rookie record with six consecutive hits across Games 4 and 5, while Jackson Chourio set a five-game Series record with 11 hits. The bullpen covered the final 4 1/3 innings without allowing a run, and Trevor Megill closed out the ninth after Tampa Bay finally scored.

A dominant October

The Brewers finished October 11-2, reeling off an eight-game winning streak that was only interrupted by Tampa Bay's 12-inning win in Game 4. The club won 114 games including the playoffs, and dethroned the two-time defending champion Los Angeles Dodgers along the way. Misiorowski, who led the majors with 252 strikeouts and a 1.80 ERA in the regular season, won Games 1 and 5 of the Series.

Dedicated to Mr. Baseball

The title carried special meaning for a franchise shaped by broadcaster Bob Uecker, who died of cancer in January 2025 without seeing the Brewers crowned champions. Christian Yelich and manager Pat Murphy dedicated the win to Uecker after the final out, with Yelich saying he wished Uecker could have been there to see it. The club's only previous World Series trip, in 1982, ended in a Game 7 loss to St. Louis.
