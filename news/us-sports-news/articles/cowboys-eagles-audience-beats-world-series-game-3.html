Cowboys-Eagles Draws 18.8 Million Viewers, More Than Double World Series Game 3

October 31, 2026

A regular-season NFL game has beaten baseball's championship showcase by a wide margin in the television ratings, renewing debate about how Major League Baseball schedules its World Series.

The numbers

The Week 7 Cowboys-Eagles game on Monday night, October 26, which Dallas won 38-21, attracted 18.8 million viewers, according to ESPN figures cited by NBC Sports. Game 3 of the World Series between the Milwaukee Brewers and Tampa Bay Rays averaged 8.33 million viewers, making it the fourth-least-watched World Series game ever, behind only Game 3 in 2023, Game 4 in 2023 and Game 3 in 2020.

The gap is striking because the World Series audience was respectable before the NFL arrived: Game 1 between Milwaukee and Tampa Bay averaged 12.26 million viewers and Game 2 drew 10.85 million, trailing last year's Dodgers-Blue Jays audiences by only a modest margin despite the absence of Shohei Ohtani, baseball's biggest television draw.

A scheduling debate, revived

There was once a time when the NFL went out of its way to avoid the World Series — in the 1970s it moved Monday Night Football to Thursday to dodge baseball's championship, and before 2010 it avoided Sunday night games during the Series. Commentators now argue the tables have turned, and that baseball should schedule World Series games on nights when NFL games are not being aired. The Cowboys' win also put Dallas back in the NFC East scramble, with upcoming matchups now carrying a defensive focus against the Arizona Cardinals.

Sources: NBC Sports, Barrett Media, Archynetys, October 30-31, 2026
