College Football Week 8: UCF Stuns No. 2 BYU, Texas Survives Ole Miss, Virginia Tech Wins at Clemson

October 26, 2026

Week 8 of the 2026 college football season, played Saturday, October 24, was one of the wildest weekends of the year — and the Big 12's playoff picture descended into chaos. Texas survived Ole Miss 34–27 after blowing a 21-point lead, UCF stunned No. 2 BYU in double overtime following one of the strangest endings imaginable, and South Carolina walked off Tennessee, according to The Football Corner's weekend recap.

In the Big Ten, No. 6 Indiana went on the road and survived a stiff test from Michigan in a 13–6 overtime victory in Wolverines freshman quarterback Tommy Carr's first career start. It was Indiana's first win at Michigan since 1967, and the Hoosiers won despite a subpar day from a banged-up Josh Hoover, who completed only eight of 18 passes for 81 yards, no touchdowns and an interception. Elsewhere in the conference, Minnesota walked off No. 14 Iowa 13–10, Wisconsin beat No. 15 USC 24–7, and Illinois beat No. 25 Oregon 21–10.

Virginia Tech's late stunner at Clemson

The nightcap produced another upset. Ethan Grunkemeyer threw two touchdown passes, including the game-winner with 1:44 left, as visiting Virginia Tech slipped past Clemson 20–16 in an Atlantic Coast Conference defensive struggle at Memorial Stadium. Both of Grunkemeyer's scoring passes went to Luke Reynolds, and the go-ahead score came on a fourth-and-1 play from the Clemson 5, when Grunkemeyer lobbed a pass to Reynolds, alone in the back of the end zone.

Clemson had reeled off 13 straight points in the third quarter to take a 16–10 lead, but the Hokies responded with the game's final 10 points. Grunkemeyer completed 20 of 32 passes for 156 yards with no interceptions, while Clemson freshman quarterback Tait Reynolds completed 20 of 26 passes for 153 yards and ran for 63 yards and a touchdown. The win was Virginia Tech's second straight after its first two losses under first-year coach James Franklin, and its first win at Clemson since 2007.

What the weekend changed

The results reshuffled the polls: Miami stayed No. 1 in the coaches poll, with Ohio State, Georgia, Indiana and Utah rounding out the top five, while BYU fell to No. 9 and Alabama — which rallied past Texas A&M — climbed to No. 10. Cincinnati took down Texas Tech, LSU crushed Auburn, and UCLA kept its unbeaten record intact with a 28–10 victory against Michigan State. With the season now past its midpoint, Week 8 was a reminder that no lead, no ranking and no home field is safe — and that the playoff race is only getting stranger.
