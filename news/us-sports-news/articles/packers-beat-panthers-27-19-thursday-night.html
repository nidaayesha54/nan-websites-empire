Packers Beat Panthers 27-19 on Thursday Night as Brewers Bring the World Series Trophy to Lambeau

October 30, 2026

The Green Bay Packers beat the Carolina Panthers 27-19 on Thursday Night Football at Lambeau Field, moving to 5-3 and keeping pace in a crowded NFC North, a night after the Milwaukee Brewers captured their first World Series title.

How Green Bay won it

Jordan Love finished 30-of-41 for 288 yards with one touchdown and one interception. Emerging second-year wideout Matthew Golden caught seven passes for 79 yards and a touchdown, tight end Tucker Kraft added six catches for 53 yards and a touchdown, and workhorse running back Josh Jacobs scored twice, his second touchdown giving Green Bay a comfortable 27-10 advantage with 7:30 left in the third quarter.

It was not close to perfect for Green Bay. Downfield threat Christian Watson left in the first half and went to hospital to get his chest checked out, but eventually returned to the stadium to loud cheers from Packers fans — after catching eight passes for 113 yards, including a 53-yard reception that sparked the team in the first half. Carolina trimmed the deficit to eight points with 5:37 left in regulation on a 12-play, 70-yard touchdown drive, but could not complete the comeback.

A Wisconsin celebration

The night carried an extra Wisconsin flavor: members of the Brewers walked onto the field with the World Series trophy in hand for everyone in attendance, a day after Milwaukee's championship. The Packers, just like the Bears did on Thursday, have now also coughed up two first-round picks in a past blockbuster — theirs to acquire star edge rusher Micah Parsons from the Cowboys ahead of the 2025 season — and are a day closer to getting Parsons back on the field.

Green Bay now sits at 5-3, behind the 6-0 Vikings and ahead in a division where the Bears are 5-2 and the Lions 4-2. Carolina, which entered the night leading the NFC South despite its record, falls further into a fight it cannot afford to lose.

Sources: Sports Illustrated, October 30, 2026
