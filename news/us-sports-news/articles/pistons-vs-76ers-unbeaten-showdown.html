Unbeaten 76ers Host Unbeaten Pistons in Early-Season Eastern Showdown

October 26, 2026

The Philadelphia 76ers and Detroit Pistons arrived at their Sunday, October 25, 2026 meeting as the Eastern Conference's early-season surprise package: two unbeaten teams, both expected to contend, meeting in Philadelphia at Xfinity Mobile Arena. The 76ers were 3–0 with a new star-studded lineup; the Pistons were 2–0 after finishing with the best record in the East last season.

Philadelphia's start had already produced drama. The new lineup debuted with a road win over the defending champion New York Knicks, followed that up with a 29-point comeback victory over the Cleveland Cavaliers, and most recently topped the Milwaukee Bucks 127–116 on Saturday behind Joel Embiid's 10th career triple-double — 28 points, 11 rebounds and 10 assists — while Jaylen Brown added 27 points and Tyrese Maxey chipped in with 26.

A test of depth on both sides

The matchup was shaped as much by who would miss it as by who would play. Embiid was expected to rest on the second night of a back-to-back, which could mean a bigger offensive role for VJ Edgecombe, who scored a season-high 17 points on Saturday, and sixth man Kentavious Caldwell-Pope. Detroit, meanwhile, ruled out Jalen Duren with a right hamstring strain and listed Ausar Thompson as questionable after he sprained his right ankle in Friday's 128–111 win over Miami.

Detroit's early wins had quality: a season-opening 121–113 victory over the Boston Celtics on October 20, in which Duren posted 22 points and nine rebounds, and the rout of the Heat. Philadelphia's slow starts were the one concern — the Sixers trailed Cleveland by 24 points at halftime and held only a one-point lead over winless Milwaukee late in the first half before Maxey banked in a half-court shot at the buzzer.

What it means — and what it doesn't

Jaylen Brown kept the perspective blunt after the Bucks win: “We can't be satisfied being 3–0. We can't be satisfied with where we're at right now. We're building for something, so we've got to get better.” Coach Nick Nurse agreed it was early: “As we're going here, it's still really early for us. ... We're trying to learn things that we're going to polish quick.” One October Sunday does not decide a conference — but for two unbeaten teams, it was the first real measuring stick of the season.

Detroit's depth was already being tested before tip-off. Ron Holland, now in his third season, had a productive game on Friday with eight points, five rebounds, four assists, a steal and zero turnovers in 22 minutes, and the Pistons picked up his fourth-year rookie option on Monday, according to the Detroit Free Press. Kevin Huerter was also among the wings in line to start in Thompson's absence. For Philadelphia, the absence of Embiid opened a different kind of test: whether a team built around its star center could keep its offense humming with LeBron James initiating more of the attack, a look coach Nick Nurse said the Sixers had already tried in stretches — including a brief shift at center during the season-opening win over the Knicks.
