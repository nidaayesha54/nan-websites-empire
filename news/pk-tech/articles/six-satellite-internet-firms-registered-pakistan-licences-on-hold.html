Six Satellite Internet Firms Registered in Pakistan — But Licences Still on Hold

November 9, 2026

Six companies have registered in Pakistan to provide satellite-based internet and undertake space-related activities, according to details submitted by the Securities and Exchange Commission of Pakistan (SECP) to a Senate committee — but registration does not allow any of them to start commercial services, ProPakistani reported on November 9.

The six companies

The registered companies are Starlink Internet Services Pakistan, Spacesail Pakistan, Aether One, Flynautix, Galaxyspace Pakistan and Space P. Starlink Internet Services Pakistan and Spacesail Pakistan were registered earlier, while the other four came after the Pakistan Space Activities Regulatory Board (PSARB) was established.

Three hurdles still to clear

Before any firm can sell a connection, it must clear a three-stage approvals chain. First, it needs a no-objection certificate from PSARB, the body that oversees space activities. Then the Ministry of Interior must complete security checks on the company's foreign directors and shareholders. Only after both steps can the Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) issue a commercial licence. So far, only one company has even cleared the first hurdle: Space P, a subsidiary under the National Command Authority, is the only firm to have submitted its PSARB NOC. Security clearances for the foreign directors and shareholders of Starlink, Galaxyspace Pakistan and Spacesail Pakistan remain pending with the Ministry of Interior. Starlink received provisional registration from PSARB in March 2025, but its full PSARB registration remains incomplete. The PTA's Fixed Satellite Services licence framework, which would let low-Earth orbit operators offer broadband directly to consumers with 15-year non-exclusive licences, is close to final — but the licensing queue cannot move until the security and PSARB steps are done.

Sources: ProPakistani, November 9, 2026; PakTechStan, November 8, 2026
