PTA Bars Licensed Operators from Supplying Bandwidth to Unlicensed Providers

November 20, 2026

The Pakistan Telecommunication Authority (PTA) has barred licensed telecom operators from supplying internet bandwidth and related services to unlicensed entities or distributors, restating the condition at a meeting with operators at its headquarters, Profit reported on November 20.

Who can sell bandwidth to whom

The session was attended by licensees holding Long Distance International (LDI), Fixed Local Loop (FLL) and Class Value Added Services (CVAS) licences. The PTA said LDI and FLL licensees may supply bandwidth, including configured internet packages, only to operators that hold a PTA licence. It said IP bandwidth, panel services, E1 connections, Domestic Private Leased Circuits and similar services must not be provided to unlicensed entities or distributors. Internet Services Class licensees sit closer to the customer: under the restated rules they serve end users directly and cannot pass capacity on to others.

Crackdown on unauthorised internet service

The regulator described the directions as part of efforts to strengthen regulatory compliance and deal with unauthorised internet service provision. The meeting also covered the District Class Licence framework, under which cable operators licensed by the Pakistan Electronic Media Regulatory Authority and last-mile service providers can obtain bandwidth from FLL licensees. The PTA pointed to the framework as the route through which cable and last-mile operators are supplied, and said bills and receipts for internet services must be issued by the relevant FLL or Class licensee. No operator or distributor was named in the accounts of the meeting. PTA officials also spoke about possible changes to mobile SIM limits and a new permission regime for satellite in-flight internet over Pakistan.

Sources: Profit via Skardu Times, November 20, 2026; The Express Tribune, November 20, 2026
