Pakistan's 5G Now Reaches 24 Cities After $509 Million Spectrum Auction

October 27, 2026

Pakistan has significantly expanded its digital infrastructure through a major spectrum auction, 5G deployment and nationwide fibre-optic connectivity, Federal Minister for Information Technology and Telecommunication Shaza Fatima Khawaja told a high-level panel at the Global Digital and AI Summit in Seoul. She said Pakistan viewed artificial intelligence primarily as a network challenge under the Digital Nation Pakistan vision.

Pakistan conducted its largest-ever spectrum auction in March 2026, acquiring 480 MHz of spectrum for $509 million and increasing telecom operators' combined holdings from 274 MHz to 754 MHz. Within six months, 5G services had been launched at more than 1,700 sites across 24 major cities, delivering average speeds of 130 Mbps, the minister said.

Fibre, speeds and international links

Download speeds in the country had risen by 68.1 percent over two years, while more than 234,000 kilometres of optical fibre cable had been deployed nationwide, according to the minister. The government had abolished right-of-way charges to reduce costs and accelerate fibre expansion.

Pakistan is connected to international networks through six submarine cables with combined capacity exceeding 17 terabits per second, and the country is developing the Central Asia Regional Economic Cooperation (CAREC) Digital Corridor to connect Central Asia with the wider world.

Satellite and the road ahead

Highlighting connectivity in remote areas, the minister said regulators were finalising a framework for satellite communications and licensing to facilitate direct-to-device services, with satellite technology complementing rather than replacing terrestrial networks. New frameworks for mobile virtual network operators and infrastructure sharing had also been introduced to attract investment.

With telecom subscribers reported to have crossed 200 million and around 70 percent of the population under the age of 30, the minister said demand for quality digital services would keep rising, making reliable and accessible connectivity central to Pakistan's digital transformation.

The rollout matters beyond headline speeds: reliable, high-capacity networks are the foundation on which artificial intelligence services, remote work, digital payments and online learning all depend. Officials have framed the expansion as part of a wider effort to close connectivity gaps, including in remote areas where satellite services are expected to complement terrestrial networks in the years ahead.

Sources: The Express Tribune, October 21, 2026; Mettis Global News, October 20, 2026.
