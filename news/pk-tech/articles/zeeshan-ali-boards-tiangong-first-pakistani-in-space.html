Zeeshan Ali Boards Tiangong, Becoming First Pakistani in Space

November 25, 2026

Pakistan's Zeeshan Ali on Wednesday became the first foreign astronaut to board China's Tiangong space station, marking a historic moment for his country's space ambitions and for Beijing's efforts to extend its space diplomacy, Dawn reported on November 25.

Liftoff, docking and a warm welcome

The Shenzhou-24 spacecraft lifted off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Centre in northwest China at 8:18am Beijing time — 5:18am in Pakistan — aboard a Long March-2F rocket, and separated from the carrier rocket roughly 10 minutes after liftoff before entering its preset orbit. The China Manned Space Agency (CMSA) confirmed that Shenzhou-24 successfully docked with Tiangong three hours and 38 minutes after liftoff, and the three-man crew entered the station at 2:35pm Beijing time, about six hours after launch. The crew was greeted with hugs by the three Shenzhou-23 members already residing at the station, who had also prepared a cake to celebrate. 'As the first foreign crew member to board the China Space Station, I am incredibly excited. China Space Station here I come!' Mr Ali said in Chinese, according to a report from China's state broadcaster CCTV.

A week in orbit

The 40-year-old Pakistan Air Force pilot, who is serving as a payload specialist, is expected to spend five to 10 days aboard the station, conducting joint China-Pakistan science experiments alongside the Chinese crew before returning to Earth aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft. The mission is commanded by Tang Hongbo, with Wu Yi serving as flight engineer. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif congratulated the nation on Mr Ali's departure for Tiangong, posting on X: 'As Shenzhou-24 lifts off this morning, Nov 25, carrying Zeeshan Ali, Pakistan's first astronaut, a new era begins for Pakistan's space programme.' The mission also carries four joint science projects in biotechnology and materials, including an experiment involving cyanobacteria.

Sources: Dawn, November 25, 2026; Al Jazeera, November 25, 2026
