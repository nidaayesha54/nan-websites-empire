Pakistan to Host UN Space Technology Conference on Early Warnings and Climate Action

October 26, 2026

Pakistan is set to host a five-day international conference in Islamabad from October 27 to 31 on how space technology can strengthen early warning systems, improve disaster risk assessment and support climate action. The event is being organised by the Pakistan Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission, known as SUPARCO, and is co-sponsored by the United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs through its UN-SPIDER programme, the Asia-Pacific Space Cooperation Organization and the Inter-Islamic Network on Space Sciences and Technology.

Who will attend

Organisers expect an estimated 200 to 250 participants, including 60 to 70 international delegates and around 150 national participants. The audience will span experts, policymakers, diplomats, educators, researchers, United Nations agencies, development organisations and representatives of the space industry. The conference will take place at a hotel in Islamabad, with the Serena Hotel named in reports as the venue. That mix matters: early warning systems only work when satellite data, government agencies and local communities are connected, and the conference is designed as a meeting point for all three.

The Early Warnings for All focus

A central focus will be the United Nations' Early Warnings for All initiative, which seeks to protect communities and critical infrastructure against climate-induced disaster events through effective early warning systems. Discussions will explore the role of space technologies in disaster risk reduction, climate-resilient development and the monitoring of water resources, agriculture, forests and environmental change. For Pakistan, a country repeatedly hit by floods, heatwaves and glacial hazards, those topics are not abstract. Satellite observations and geospatial data can track river levels, rainfall, crop stress and forest loss in near real time, giving authorities hours or days of extra warning that can save lives.

Why it matters for Pakistan

Hosting the conference also signals Pakistan's ambition to be taken seriously as a space and climate actor, not just a victim of climate disasters. SUPARCO's work on remote sensing already feeds into agriculture, water management and disaster planning, and an international gathering gives Pakistani researchers a platform to share findings and build partnerships. The test, as with many conferences, will be what survives after the closing session: joint projects, data-sharing agreements and practical tools that district officials can actually use when the next flood warning arrives.

What to watch this week

Over the five days, watch for announcements on training, data access and regional cooperation, as well as any concrete commitments on early warning coverage for vulnerable districts. The conference runs until October 31, and its sessions on water, agriculture and forests are likely to be the most directly relevant to farming communities and provincial disaster agencies across Pakistan.
