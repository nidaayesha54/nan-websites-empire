1.5 Million Pakistani Children Faced Online Sexual Exploitation in a Year, UNICEF-Backed Study Finds

November 1, 2026

About 1.5 million internet-using children in Pakistan experienced sexual exploitation or abuse facilitated by digital technology in a single year, according to new research highlighted in coverage on October 31 and November 1, Dawn and IANS reported.

What the study found

The findings come from Disrupting Harm in Pakistan, a study published in September 2026 by the UNICEF Office of Strategy and Evidence (Innocenti), ECPAT International and INTERPOL, based on a nationally representative household survey of children aged 12 to 17 and their parents or caregivers, plus interviews with frontline and justice professionals. Nearly one in 16 internet-using children in that age group — roughly 1.5 million — experienced at least one form of technology-facilitated sexual exploitation or abuse over 12 months. The reported forms included unwanted sexual images or conversations, demands for intimate photographs, non-consensual sharing of children's images, and sexual threats or blackmail, with AI-generated material flagged as an emerging risk.

Most cases went unreported, the study says

In 85% of the incidents children described, the perpetrator was someone they already knew, challenging the assumption that online abuse mainly comes from strangers, and social media was the most commonly reported setting, accounting for 44% of reported instances. More than half of affected children did not tell anyone, and none of those surveyed reported their experience through formal channels such as a helpline, social worker or police, according to summaries of the study. It also identified gaps in Pakistan's legal and institutional response, including the absence of a systematic requirement for child forensic interviews to be conducted by specially trained professionals, and the continued use of the term 'child pornography' in the Prevention of Electronic Crimes Act to refer to child sexual abuse material. Reports note the federal government is considering measures to restrict children's social media use, including a possible minimum age, but no final decision was reported in the cited coverage.

Sources: Dawn, IANS via MENAFN, UNICEF / ECPAT International / INTERPOL, Disrupting Harm in Pakistan (September 2026), October 31 - November 1, 2026
