PM Launches Two-Year Drive to Give 500,000 Chromebooks to Talented Students

October 26, 2026

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif on Sunday launched a programme to distribute 500,000 Chromebooks and provide internet facilities to talented students from schools and religious seminaries across Pakistan over the next two years. Addressing a ceremony organised under the Prime Minister's Youth Programme, he said access to technology and education must not be determined by wealth or poverty.

Merit as the only criterion

The prime minister said intelligence and hard work should not be held captive by wealth and poverty, and that merit and effort should determine which students receive the devices. The selected students are also being provided with bags and MiFi devices to enable them to access the internet, attend classes remotely and use electronic libraries from home. Addressing the students directly, he said that through the Chromebooks they would be able to acquire knowledge and, as they grow up, become some of the most distinguished and capable people in the country.

Building on an earlier laptop programme

Recalling the laptop distribution programme launched under former prime minister Nawaz Sharif, Shehbaz Sharif said 600,000 laptops had been distributed on merit among students across Punjab, Sindh, Balochistan, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Gilgit-Baltistan and Azad Jammu and Kashmir. The new Chromebook drive is presented as a continuation of that approach, updated for a classroom era in which much learning, revision and research happens online rather than on paper alone.

Wider education push

The prime minister also said 27 Daanish schools are under construction as the government expands access to quality education and digital learning. The Daanish school network, aimed at talented children from disadvantaged backgrounds, has long been part of the Sharif family's education pitch, and pairing new schools with devices and connectivity fits that strategy. The government, he said, was determined to ensure that talented children are not left behind in an increasingly technology-driven world.

What will decide success

The real test of the scheme will not be the launch ceremony but the delivery that follows: whether devices reach the students selected on merit, whether internet access works reliably outside major cities, and whether teachers are supported to use the devices for learning rather than leaving them as prestige objects. Two years is a long window, and the programme's credibility will rest on transparent selection lists and steady distribution. If it works, half a million students will gain a direct route into digital classrooms, online libraries and remote learning that many of their peers already take for granted.

Sources: Pakistan Today, October 26, 2026.
