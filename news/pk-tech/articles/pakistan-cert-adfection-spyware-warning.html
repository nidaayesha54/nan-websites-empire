Pakistan's Cyber Emergency Team Warns of Spyware That Can Infect Phones Through Online Ads

October 31, 2026

Pakistan's National Cyber Emergency Response Team has sounded the alarm over a sophisticated spyware threat that can exploit online advertisements to steal private data and secretly monitor smartphones, Daily Pakistan reported on October 31.

What is 'Adfection'?

At the centre of the warning is a cyberattack technique known as Adfection, which turns digital advertising infrastructure into a potential delivery channel for surveillance software. An apparently harmless advertisement appearing on a smartphone could become a gateway for spyware, allowing attackers to access private conversations, pictures, passwords and other sensitive information without the user clicking a single link. The warning raises particular concern for journalists, government officials, researchers and business professionals who handle confidential information on their phones.

What the research found

The advisory follows findings published on October 15, 2026, by Citizen Lab, a research organisation known for investigating digital surveillance. According to the research cited in the advisory, Pakistan was among the countries targeted through an advertising-based spyware campaign, and the investigation identified evidence suggesting certain devices associated with Al Jazeera had been targeted and compromised using Candiru spyware through the same technique. National CERT emphasised that the appearance of an advertisement alone does not confirm that a device has been compromised — the warning is about the technique and the risk, not a claim that every phone showing ads is infected.

Sources: Daily Pakistan, Citizen Lab research cited by National CERT, October 31, 2026
