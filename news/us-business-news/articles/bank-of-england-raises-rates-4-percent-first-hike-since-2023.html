Bank of England Raises Rates to 4% in First Hike Since 2023, Warns More May Follow

November 6, 2026

The Bank of England raised interest rates for the first time in over three years on Thursday, lifting Bank Rate by a quarter point to 4 percent, and warned that at least one more increase was likely if the US-Iran conflict continues, Reuters reported on November 5.

An 8-1 vote, with the dissent for a bigger hike

The Monetary Policy Committee voted 8-1 to raise rates from 3.75 percent, the first rise since August 2023. The lone dissent came not from a policymaker who wanted to hold rates steady, but from Catherine Mann, who voted for a half-point increase to 4.25 percent. The rate had stood at 3.75 percent since December 2025. The Bank said the rise was warranted by higher energy prices stemming from the Middle East conflict, which has pushed up global oil prices and, with them, UK fuel costs. Consumer prices rose 3.5 percent in the year to September 2026, already well above the Bank's 2 percent target.

Inflation could reach 5% next year

The Bank now expects UK inflation to climb to around 5 percent by the middle of 2027 — its central projection puts CPI inflation at 4.9 percent in the second quarter of 2027 — before easing as the energy-price jump drops out of the annual comparison, and not to return to 2 percent until 2028. Governor Andrew Bailey said the decision should not be seen as the first in an inevitable long sequence of hikes, but warned that unless the conflict in the Middle East is resolved, it does not seem likely to be the last increase. The committee's next decision is due on December 17, and the Bank said it would assess the evidence at each meeting rather than commit to a fixed course.

Sources: Reuters, BBC News, November 5, 2026
