Tech Stocks Stage Partial Rebound as Nasdaq Rises 1.1% After Thursday's Rout

November 7, 2026

Tech shares rose on Friday, recovering a fraction of the losses from the prior session's rout, as the dollar rallied after better-than-expected US jobs data, the Manila Times and the BBC reported on November 7.

A partial recovery after a brutal Thursday

After slumping 5.5 percent on Thursday, the tech-heavy Nasdaq clawed back 1.1 percent on Friday to close at 24,579.42. The S&P 500 rose 0.3 percent to 7,376.86, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.5 percent to 51,285.73. Dip-buying buoyed the market as chip stocks rebounded, offsetting concerns that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates in December. The main impetus for the selloff was the US midterm elections: voters said "not in my backyard" to data centres, and investors responded by dumping stocks exposed to the AI buildout, with the Nasdaq 100's 6 percent Thursday fall alone erasing about $2.5 trillion in value, according to Finimize.

The selloff went global

The panic spread far beyond Wall Street. Japan's tech-heavy Nikkei 225 fell 6 percent on Friday — its worst one-day fall since the US announced "Liberation Day" tariffs in April 2025 — and is now down 9 percent this week and 20 percent from its June peak. South Korea's Kospi also dove 6 percent, taking its drop from its June high to 44 percent, with memory-chip makers SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics, which make up nearly half of the index, falling 10 percent and 6 percent respectively. The Cboe Volatility Index, the market's "fear index", is up around 60 percent over the past month. Friday's rebound eased the panic, but with investors still pricing in tighter US regulation of AI and data centres, the market has not yet decided whether the worst of the rout is over.

Sources: Manila Times, BBC News, November 7, 2026
