Nasdaq Enters Correction as Tech Sell-Off Wipes $2.3 Trillion in a Week

November 8, 2026

The Nasdaq Composite fell more than 10 percent below its October 6 record close during Thursday trading, crossing the widely used threshold for a market correction, Invest News Week reported on November 8. The sell-off erased approximately $2.3 trillion from the Nasdaq on November 6 alone, the worst single-day performance since April 2025, according to WSNext.

A week of sharp reversals

Markets delivered several sharp reversals this week. Stocks were mixed to start the week, kicking off November with a continued rotation out of technology stocks in favour of more defensive names. Markets then jumped midweek as investors believed a decisive midterm election result would help reduce political uncertainty, with the Dow Industrials rising over 3 percent — its best day in more than a year and a half. But stocks came under steady pressure on Thursday, with tech leading all three major averages down: the Nasdaq dropped 5.5 percent while the S&P 500 slid 4.4 percent, according to market data cited by GAMSG. For the trading week from November 2 to 6, the Dow fell 722.50 points, or 1.4 percent, to 51,285.73, while the Nasdaq suffered one of its worst weekly falls, dropping 2,613.92 points, or 9.61 percent, to 24,579.42, and the S&P 500 lost 3.07 percent to 7,376.86, Goodreturns reported. The sell-off was led by technology stocks tied to AI infrastructure, with Oracle, Nvidia, Qualcomm and Tesla experiencing declines ranging from 7 percent to 17 percent, WSNext reported.

Oil slides, rate bets rise

One bright spot was the price of oil. Brent crude settled Friday at $98.32 a barrel, down from $117.64 at the end of the previous week — a fall of about 17 percent over the week. Some believe lower prices could help ease inflation concerns if the trend continues. The week wrapped on a positive note on Friday as markets pushed higher on a better-than-expected jobs report. Unexpectedly strong US employment data pushed markets towards an approximately 80 percent probability of another Federal Reserve rate increase in December. With the Cboe Volatility Index above 20, strategists expect volatility to remain elevated into next week.

Sources: Invest News Week, November 8, 2026; Goodreturns, November 8, 2026; WSNext, November 8, 2026
