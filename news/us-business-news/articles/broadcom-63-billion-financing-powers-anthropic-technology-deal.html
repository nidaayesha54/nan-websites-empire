Broadcom's $63 Billion Financing Powers Anthropic Technology Deal

October 27, 2026

Chip stocks got a lift on Tuesday after a group of banks, including Bank of America, Citi and Morgan Stanley, along with Blackstone, announced $63 billion in financing for the purchase of Broadcom technology for Anthropic, according to CNBC TV18's market coverage. The deal underscores how deeply the AI boom now reaches into financing, with lenders and private capital backing the infrastructure behind frontier AI models.

Broadcom shares rose more than 2 percent on the news, and sentiment spread across chipmakers, with Micron Technology up more than 2 percent, Advanced Micro Devices gaining nearly 2 percent and Nvidia rising around 1 percent in early trading. The gains built on the previous session's rally, when major averages ended higher after Anthropic filed to go public on the Nasdaq.

Why the financing matters

Financing packages of this size show how capital-intensive the AI buildout has become. Training and running advanced models requires vast amounts of specialized chips, networking equipment and data-center capacity, and the companies building that infrastructure increasingly rely on large-scale debt and private financing alongside equity. The involvement of major banks and Blackstone signals that mainstream finance now treats AI infrastructure as a core lending market rather than a speculative bet.

For Broadcom, the deal ties its technology directly to one of the AI industry's most prominent model developers. For Anthropic, access to Broadcom technology at this scale supports its push to expand the computing capacity behind its models as it prepares for public markets.

Part of a broader chip rally

The financing news arrived as investors weighed a packed week of catalysts, including the Federal Reserve's policy decision on Wednesday and earnings from several of the largest technology companies. Chip stocks have been among the market's most sensitive barometers of AI sentiment all year, rising on signs of strong demand and falling when investors question whether spending will pay off.

Analysts will be watching whether the financing translates into sustained orders and revenue for suppliers, and whether Anthropic's public listing, once completed, gives the market a new benchmark for valuing private AI companies. For now, the deal has added fresh momentum to a sector that has driven much of Wall Street's gains this year.

Sources: CNBC TV18, Reuters
