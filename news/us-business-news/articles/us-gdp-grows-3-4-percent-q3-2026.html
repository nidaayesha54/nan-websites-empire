US Economy Grew 3.4% in Q3, Fastest in a Year, Beating Forecasts

October 29, 2026

The US economy grew at a 3.4% annual rate in the third quarter of 2026, accelerating from 2.2% in the second quarter and beating economist forecasts, according to the Commerce Department's advance estimate released October 29.

What drove the acceleration

The expansion primarily reflected faster investment and an upturn in government spending, partly offset by a decline in exports and rising imports. Consumer spending grew at a robust 3.8% pace, matching the second quarter for the strongest back-to-back gains since the pandemic rebound, with services including healthcare leading the way. Equipment spending surged at a 20.9% annual rate, its third straight quarter of double-digit gains, riding the AI investment boom.

Inflation data released alongside

The September personal consumption expenditures price index, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, rose 3.6% from a year earlier, slightly below forecasts, while core PCE rose 3.1%. Within the quarter, the core PCE index came in at 2.5%, down from 3.3% in the second quarter. Personal income rose only 0.2% in September and the saving rate fell to 3.8%, its lowest since October 2022, a reminder that household buffers are thinning even as spending holds up.

Why it matters for the Fed

The day before the release, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh had described activity as expanding at a strong pace with resilient domestic spending and robust capital investment. The stronger growth reading, landing a day after the Fed held rates steady, keeps the December decision finely balanced: solid growth argues against urgency, while inflation still well above 2% keeps another hike firmly on the table.

Sources: US Bureau of Economic Analysis, USA TODAY, KPMG, October 29, 2026
