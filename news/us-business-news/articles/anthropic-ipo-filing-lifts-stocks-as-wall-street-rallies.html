Anthropic IPO Filing Lifts Stocks as Wall Street Rallies

October 27, 2026

US stocks rose on Monday after Anthropic filed to go public on the Nasdaq, reviving appetite for artificial intelligence names and breaking a run of back-to-back weekly losses for the major indexes. The S&P 500 closed at 7,676.77, up 0.70 percent, while the Nasdaq Composite ended at 26,169.38, up 0.8 percent, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.3 percent to 52,361.58, according to Zacks and Saxo market summaries.

The filing was reported to disclose $16.29 billion of revenue for the six months to June, a figure that gave investors a fresh datapoint on the scale of the AI industry's leading private companies. The listing would be another major technology IPO of 2026, following SpaceX's blockbuster debut in June, according to CNBC TV18's market coverage.

Tech shares lead the way

Gains were led by large technology names, though the move was uneven. Tesla gained 5.21 percent, Nvidia rose 2.39 percent and Microsoft added 1.54 percent, while Meta fell 1.88 percent, Apple slipped 1.10 percent and Alphabet declined 1.06 percent, according to Saxo's session summary. Nine of the 11 S&P 500 sectors ended in positive territory, with information technology, industrials and consumer discretionary leading the advance.

The CBOE Volatility Index, Wall Street's fear gauge, fell 5.5 percent to 18.39, and advancers outnumbered decliners on both the New York Stock Exchange and the Nasdaq. A retreat in crude oil also eased pressure on stocks, with Brent crude slipping for a second straight day and US West Texas Intermediate futures shedding nearly 1 percent to around $101 per barrel.

Fed decision and big earnings ahead

Investors now turn to a busy week. The Federal Reserve's policy decision is due Wednesday, with markets widely expecting rates to be held steady, and several of the largest technology companies are due to report earnings later in the week. Their results will be parsed for signs of whether hefty AI investments are translating into growth, a question that has driven much of this year's market debate.

On Tuesday morning, US stock index futures ticked higher as crude prices extended their decline, with Dow E-minis up 169 points, S&P 500 E-minis up 15 points and Nasdaq 100 E-minis up 79 points in early trading, according to Reuters. Coca-Cola shares inched up in premarket trading after the beverage giant raised its annual profit forecast, while PayPal fell close to 1 percent despite lifting its annual adjusted profit view.

Sources: Reuters via Kitco, Zacks, Saxo
