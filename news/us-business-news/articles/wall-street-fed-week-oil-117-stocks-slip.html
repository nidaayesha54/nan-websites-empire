Wall Street Heads Into Fed Week as Oil Near $117 and Stocks Slip

October 26, 2026

U.S. stocks slipped on Friday as oil stayed elevated and bond yields climbed, setting up a pivotal week that brings a Federal Reserve decision, major economic data and earnings from three of the world's largest technology companies.

The S and P 500 fell 0.52 percent to 7,623.68 on Friday, its second straight weekly decline, as oil's rally and inflation worries weighed on shares. The Dow dropped 1.23 percent, or 652.82 points, to 52,215.16, though it still finished the week higher. The Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.21 percent to 25,955.34, and the Russell 2000 eased 0.29 percent.

Oil replaces AI as the market's main worry

Brent crude settled at $116.72 a barrel on Friday after Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia pushed prices higher. Energy was the only clear sector gainer, with Exxon, Chevron and ConocoPhillips all closing at record highs, while eight of the eleven S and P sectors ended in negative territory. The Energy Select Sector fund advanced 2.6 percent, while consumer staples tumbled 1.9 percent.

Oil fell in early Asian trade on Monday after reports that Qatari mediators were in Tehran over the weekend, and that President Trump had ruled out an immediate strike on Iran. The ten-year Treasury yield closed Friday at 5.00 percent, a level that keeps borrowing costs high across the economy.

Fed expected to hold on Wednesday

The Federal Open Market Committee meets October 27 and 28, with the decision due Wednesday at 2 p.m. Eastern, followed by a press conference with Chair Kevin Warsh. The target range is 3.75 percent to 4.00 percent, after the Fed raised rates 25 basis points in September, its first hike since 2023.

Market pricing as of October 20 showed about 83 percent odds of a pause and 17 percent odds of a quarter-point hike. Traders will also focus on the ADP employment report and durable goods orders on Tuesday, and the personal consumption expenditures inflation reading and weekly jobless claims on Thursday, alongside third-quarter growth figures.

Big tech earnings take center stage

The earnings calendar is heavy. Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon are among the key names reporting this week, alongside Boeing, SoFi, PayPal, Robinhood, Visa, Intel, Mastercard, Starbucks, Exxon Mobil and Chevron.

Investors are focused on AI spending and profitability after a bruising week for technology shares, and on whether the big spenders can show returns on their massive infrastructure investments. U.S. stock futures were largely steady on Monday morning as oil retreated, with Tesla gaining in premarket trading after Barclays upgraded the stock to overweight from equal-weight.

The fear gauge, the CBOE Volatility Index, rose 2.2 percent to 19.45 on Friday, and decliners outnumbered advancers by a 1.72-to-1 ratio on the New York Stock Exchange. With the Fed, inflation data and the biggest earnings reports of the season all landing in four sessions, volatility is likely to stay elevated.
