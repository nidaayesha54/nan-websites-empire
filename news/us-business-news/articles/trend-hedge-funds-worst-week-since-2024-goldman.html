Trend Hedge Funds Suffer Worst Week Since 2024 as AI Stocks Slide, Goldman Says

October 26, 2026

Trend-following hedge funds suffered their worst weekly performance in two years last week, as concern about the eventual payoff from hefty AI spending ignited volatility across stocks and bonds, according to a Goldman Sachs note seen by Reuters on Monday.

Systematic long-short hedge funds, whose algorithms ride market trends until they peter out, fell 2 percent on the week, their worst showing since 2024. Fundamental long-short strategies drew down another 1.1 percent. Single stocks collectively saw the largest pulling back of positions since late July, and U.S. equities have been net sold for a third straight week, and in four of the last five weeks.

Tech led the slide

The Nasdaq 100, home to the world's largest technology stocks, fell 5.5 percent last week, its biggest weekly fall since April 2025, when President Donald Trump's Liberation Day tariffs roiled world markets. Last week's fall was led in part by a 10 percent weekly slide in shares of Meta, which reported strong earnings but indicated it would need to raise more capital for its AI investments, sending shivers through markets.

Goldman said its prime brokerage desk reported $20 billion in selling of technology stocks over the last two weeks, close to July's major unwind, which saw $23 billion in selling.

Bonds whipsawed too

Bond markets were also volatile, as investors sought the relative safety of U.S. Treasuries while selling the debt of more fragile economies, such as France. The combination of falling stocks and jumping bond yields is exactly the environment that punishes trend-following strategies, which are built to profit from steady, persistent moves rather than sudden reversals.

The damage has not erased the year for these funds. Fundamental long-short strategies are down 3.8 percent for the month so far but still up 8.8 percent this year, while systematic strategies are down 1.1 percent so far in October but up 18.7 percent so far this year, according to the note.

What it signals

The episode is a reminder that the AI trade, for all its momentum, can reverse violently when investors start questioning the returns. Heavy spending announcements that once lifted shares are now being read as warnings about cash burn, and funds that loaded up on the trend are being forced to unwind in a hurry.

Whether the selling marks a lasting rotation or a brief shakeout will depend heavily on this week's earnings from Microsoft, Alphabet and Amazon, and on Wednesday's Federal Reserve decision. For now, the message from Goldman's data is clear: the smart money spent last week heading for the exits, not adding to the trade.

Sources: Reuters, Goldman Sachs prime brokerage note
