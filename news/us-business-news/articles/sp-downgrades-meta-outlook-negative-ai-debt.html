S&P Downgrades Meta's Credit Outlook to Negative After $95 Billion AI Debt Plan

November 1, 2026

S&P Global Ratings downgraded the credit outlook for Meta Platforms from stable to negative on October 31, Financial News reported, adding a new worry for investors already rattled by the cost of the artificial intelligence buildout.

The debt behind the downgrade

Meta had announced on October 26 that it would raise $95 billion to build infrastructure such as AI data centers. The scale of that borrowing, coming alongside a surge in capital expenditure guidance to between $135 billion and $145 billion for 2026, prompted the ratings agency to take a more cautious view of the company's credit profile. Meta's shares had already fallen 7% on October 22 when the spending guidance was announced, and its free cash flow turned negative even as the company beat revenue estimates.

A wider reckoning on AI spending

The outlook change lands in a week when the AI trade came under heavy pressure: Alphabet reported negative quarterly free cash flow, Amazon reported a $42.4 billion free-cash-flow shortfall over 12 months, and Intel plunged 29% on weak guidance. An outlook downgrade is not a rating cut — it signals that a cut is more likely if trends continue — but it underscores how the market's question has shifted from how fast companies can build AI capacity to how they will pay for it.
