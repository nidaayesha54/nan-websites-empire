October Ends in the Red for Tech: Nasdaq Down 5.3% for the Month as Investors Rotate to Defensives

October 31, 2026

US stocks closed sharply lower on Friday, October 30, ending a volatile month in which the artificial intelligence trade — the market's main engine for much of the year — went into reverse, Reuters reported on October 31.

How the month closed

The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 572.25 points, or 1.09%, to 51,770.31, the S&P 500 lost 1.82% to 7,580.07, and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 2.86% to 25,437.00. For the month, the Nasdaq fell 5.3%, its steepest decline since March 2025, the S&P 500 slipped 0.9%, while the Dow gained 1.7%. The S&P 500 technology sector posted its largest monthly percentage loss since March 2025, and communication services registered its biggest monthly drop since April 2022.

Friday's trading turned more disorderly in the afternoon as investors trimmed positions ahead of next week's midterm elections. Defensive names helped limit losses on the blue-chip index: Walmart and Coca-Cola each rose more than 1%, while Merck and Amgen also advanced. CoreWeave shares slumped 37% in October and Nebius nearly 35% as funding costs rose.

Earnings season, in context

Third-quarter earnings season has passed the halfway mark, with 299 S&P 500 companies having reported and 82% beating consensus expectations, according to LSEG data cited by Reuters. Analysts now expect third-quarter earnings growth of 31.6% year on year. The tension for markets is not whether companies are profitable, but whether enormous AI infrastructure spending turns into cash quickly enough — the question that drove October's selloff, and the one investors will carry into November, when Apple reports earnings on November 2.
