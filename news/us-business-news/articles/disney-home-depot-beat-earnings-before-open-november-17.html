Disney and Home Depot Both Beat Earnings Before the Open as Consumer Names Hold Up

November 17, 2026

Home Depot and Disney both beat expectations before the open on Tuesday, and neither looks like a company bracing for a downturn, according to a US open briefing published on November 17. The trouble is that nobody on Wall Street is really trading shops or theme parks this morning, with everything waiting for Nvidia's results after the close.

Disney's clean beat

Disney was cleaner. Revenue rose 16 percent to $25.95 billion and adjusted earnings of $1.77 a share beat the roughly $1.67 expected, with theme parks, streaming and ESPN all growing. Streaming profit doubled to $824 million, the company raised its dividend 33 percent to $2 a share, and it is targeting $3 billion of annual cost savings by 2029, including more job cuts, while expecting double-digit earnings growth next year. The shares rose a little under 1 percent in early trading.

Home Depot names a new chief

Home Depot's third-quarter sales rose 4.9 percent to $43.4 billion, above the roughly $42.8 billion analysts expected, and adjusted earnings of $3.86 a share edged past forecasts. Comparable sales grew 2.2 percent, against expectations closer to 1.7 percent. The company also named its finance chief, Richard McPhail, as chief executive from November 23, with Ted Decker staying on as chair. The detail is less cheerful than the headline: customers made fewer trips, with transactions down 0.5 percent, but spent more each time, and the operating margin slipped to 12.6 percent from 12.9 percent as costs rose faster than sales. Full-year profit growth is now guided at about 2 percent, and the shares fell about 2 percent before the open.

Sources: Samuel and Co Trading, November 17, 2026; Wall St, November 17, 2026
