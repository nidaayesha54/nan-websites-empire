US Adds 165,000 Jobs in October, Smashing Forecasts as Unemployment Eases to 4.1%

November 6, 2026

US employers added 165,000 jobs in October, far above economists' expectations of about 65,000, and the unemployment rate edged down to 4.1 percent from 4.2 percent, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported on Friday, November 6, Reuters and USA Today reported.

Where the jobs came from

The gain was more than three times the average monthly increase of 48,000 recorded over the previous 12 months. Professional and business services added 70,000 jobs, well above that sector's 12-month average of 15,000, while health care added 42,000, transportation and warehousing gained 25,000 and manufacturing added 19,000. The report also revised earlier months upward: September's gain rose from 29,000 to 60,000 and August's from 133,000 to 186,000, a combined upward revision of 84,000 jobs. Average hourly earnings rose 14 cents, or 0.4 percent, to $38.00 in October, the strongest monthly increase since January, and were up 3.1 percent from a year earlier.

What it means for the Fed

The number of unemployed people stood at 7.1 million, little changed from September, and the unemployment rate has remained in a narrow range of 4.1 to 4.3 percent since March. The labour force participation rate edged up to 61.9 percent, and 421,000 people entered the labour force during the month. Long-term unemployment, those jobless for 27 weeks or more, remained near 2 million, or 27.6 percent of all unemployed people. The strong report gives the Federal Reserve more scope to focus on inflation stemming from the Middle East conflict, and most forecasters expect policymakers to raise their benchmark rate by a quarter point in December, according to CME FedWatch. Attention now turns to the October consumer-price report due on Tuesday, November 10.

Sources: Reuters, USA Today, Bureau of Labor Statistics, November 6, 2026
