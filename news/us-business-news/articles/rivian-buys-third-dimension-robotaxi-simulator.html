Rivian Buys UK Startup Third Dimension to Build Its Robotaxi Simulator

October 26, 2026

Rivian Automotive announced on October 22, 2026, that it has acquired Third Dimension AI, Inc., a pioneer in AI-powered 3D spatial generation. The strategic transaction will accelerate synthetic data generation and full-world simulation for Rivian's driver assistance and self-driving systems. Financial terms of the transaction were not disclosed.

Third Dimension AI, founded in 2024 by Tolga Kart and Piotr Sokolski, has developed a simulation platform that transforms real-world data into high-fidelity, controllable 3D environments, enabling complex driving scenarios to be recreated and varied at scale. Its 15-person team, mostly based in the United Kingdom, will join Rivian's Autonomy research and engineering teams. By bringing the team and platform in-house, Rivian said it will further expand its physical and virtual data flywheel.

Why simulation matters

“Simulation is central to how we train and validate our autonomous driving systems,” said James Philbin, Rivian's senior vice president of Autonomy and AI. “Third Dimension's team has built impressive technology that turns real-world data into detailed 3D environments. This allows us to recreate challenging driving situations, vary the conditions, and improve how our systems respond. Their technology and expertise will strengthen and accelerate autonomy across our consumer and robotaxi roadmaps.”

The logic is straightforward: autonomous vehicles need to learn how to handle rare or dangerous situations, but some of those situations may not occur often enough during normal road testing. Simulation lets Rivian recreate these edge cases repeatedly and safely. Philbin said Rivian receives more than 400,000 data sequences daily from customer vehicles whose owners have allowed data transmission from external cameras, and the acquisition is aimed at closing the gap between virtual worlds and actual roads.

The robotaxi angle

The deal supports Rivian's robotaxi plans with Uber, with a launch targeted for 2028, according to TechCrunch's reporting, as well as hands-free surface-street driving software targeted for release by the end of the year. Third Dimension co-founder and CEO Tolga Kart said: “Joining Rivian allows us to deploy our simulation technology at scale, accelerating both the generation of data needed to train autonomous systems and the validation needed to deploy them safely and reliably.” In a race where testing speed is everything, Rivian just bought itself a faster way to practice.

The acquisition lands alongside other autonomy milestones for Rivian. Self Drive News noted the company's Autonomy Early Access Program for point-to-point driving, launched on October 20, 2026, and its longer-term Level 4 autonomy roadmap. Together, the moves sketch a strategy that leans less on any single breakthrough and more on compounding advantages: real-world data from customer vehicles, synthetic data from simulation, and a validation loop that ties the two together. For a company competing with far larger rivals in self-driving, that flywheel — physical and virtual data feeding each other — is the bet the Third Dimension deal is designed to accelerate.
