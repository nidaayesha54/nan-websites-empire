Fed Holds Rates at 3.75%–4% in 10–2 Vote as Warsh Says Politics Played No Role

October 29, 2026

The Federal Reserve held interest rates steady on October 28, leaving its benchmark federal funds rate unchanged at a range of 3.75% to 4% in a 10-2 vote, days before the midterm elections and one month after its first rate hike in three years.

A divided committee

Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack dissented, preferring another quarter-point increase. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh said politics played no role in the decision, telling reporters he had heard no reference to politics or elections during two days of deliberations. The Fed's statement said accumulating more information would help determine the policy stance needed to return inflation to its 2% target.

Inflation still above target

Consumer price inflation was running at about 3.7% in the year to September, roughly a point above the Fed's goal, with limited progress in recent months. Officials described economic growth as strong and job gains as keeping pace with workforce growth, but expressed concern that stronger-than-expected inflation could become entrenched as businesses and households adjust their expectations.

What to watch next

Traders raised their bets on a December hike after the decision, with CME FedWatch pricing roughly 85% odds of another quarter-point increase at the Fed's final meeting of the year. Warsh also announced a structural change: from 2027, one of the Fed's eight annual meetings will be devoted entirely to broad monetary policy strategy, with no rate vote, rising to two such sessions in 2028.
