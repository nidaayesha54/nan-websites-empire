Microsoft Winds Down Oracle AI Cloud Deal as Older Nvidia Chips Lose Their Fit

November 7, 2026

Microsoft is winding down an arrangement to rent AI computing from Oracle as part of a larger reassessment of billions of dollars in cloud partnerships, Bloomberg News reported on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter, Reuters reported on November 6.

Why Microsoft is walking away

Microsoft has been switching off rented Oracle computing capacity in recent months after declining to renew the contract. The rented capacity became less valuable over time because the Oracle servers used an older generation of Nvidia chips that no longer meet Microsoft's evolving computing needs. The arrangement dates to 2023, when Oracle agreed to supply servers supporting Microsoft's AI-powered Bing search engine; the financial terms were never disclosed. Oracle shares fell about 3 percent after the report. Oracle pushed back, saying demand for its AI infrastructure remains strong and that capacity that came up for renewal in its fiscal first quarter was renewed or resold to other customers under long-term contracts at prices 20 percent above the previous agreements. Microsoft declined to comment.

Part of a wider cloud review

The wind-down is part of a broader review of the more than $80 billion Microsoft has committed to renting capacity from outside cloud providers, including CoreWeave, Nscale and Nebius, whose costs and delivery delays have frustrated its leadership. Bloomberg also reported that Microsoft will not exercise an option to more than double its agreement with AI cloud provider Nscale to rent computing power at a Texas data centre. The move follows an earlier report that separate negotiations to lease additional Oracle capacity broke down in June. The episode underlines a new pressure in the AI buildout: as hardware generations turn over faster, long-term rental contracts signed in the rush to secure capacity can quickly become the wrong bet.

Sources: Reuters, Bloomberg News, November 6, 2026
