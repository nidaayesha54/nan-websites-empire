October CPI Rose 0.4% as Fed's Waller Urges a Rate Rise 'Sooner, Rather Than Later'

November 10, 2026

The consumer price index rose 0.4% in October, according to data released on Tuesday morning, cooler than September's 0.6% increase, Investopedia reported on November 10. Core CPI, which excludes food and energy, was 0.3% higher month on month.

Waller raises the stakes

Fed Governor Christopher Waller, a voting member of the central bank's rate-setting committee, said in a morning speech in London — before the inflation data was released — that inflation remained too high and tightening monetary policy was necessary \"sooner, rather than later.\" Waller said he is leaning towards a December increase even if inflation comes in on the cooler side, and that if the numbers are hotter than expected, a 50-basis-point rise should be considered, TipRanks reported. The government did not release year-over-year numbers, citing data collection issues; economists had anticipated CPI rising 3.9% over the 12 months, up from 3.7% in September.

Markets brace for the verdict

Stock futures pointed to a mixed open after Monday's bounce, with futures tied to the Nasdaq 100 down about 0.8% and those associated with the Dow up 0.1%, while the yield on the 10-year US Treasury sat near 4.9%. Oil prices were also falling, with Brent crude down about 3% to $89.88 a barrel. Monday had been Wall Street's best day since April: the S&P 500 rose 2.35% to 7,549.94, the Nasdaq Composite jumped 2.74% and the Dow added more than 1,100 points as cheaper oil and a chip rebound pulled markets out of last week's slide.

Sources: Investopedia, November 10, 2026; TipRanks, November 10, 2026
