White House Names Jay Clayton as AI Czar to Lead 'Super Intelligence Force'

October 26, 2026

President Donald Trump has tapped Jay Clayton, his director of national intelligence, to lead a new government task force on artificial intelligence. Trump is calling it the “Super Intelligence Force,” part of his effort to rebrand AI as “super intelligence,” and the announcement on Sunday, October 4, 2026, came after Trump hosted top executives of AI companies at the White House the previous week.

The task force will coordinate the federal effort “to ensure that America continues to lead the World in Super Intelligence, which many say is bigger than the Industrial Revolution, and the Internet, and will protect the interests, and improve the lives, of all Americans,” Trump said in a social media post announcing the move. It will reach out to consumers, public interest groups, religious organizations, critical infrastructure providers and AI companies.

Who is on the task force

Clayton, who was confirmed as intelligence chief in August after previously serving as U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York and as chair of the Securities and Exchange Commission, replaces former AI and crypto czar David Sacks, who served part time and stepped down in March after reaching his allotted 130 days. Sacks now joins as an external adviser, alongside former Secretary of State Condoleezza Rice, according to Nextgov.

The task force's three vice chairs are Federal Trade Commission Chairman Andrew Ferguson, Pentagon technology chief Emil Michael and Office of Personnel Management Director Scott Kupor. Its members include Vice President JD Vance and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and the group reports directly to Trump and his chief of staff, Susie Wiles.

A 120-day clock — and a security-first signal

Under its charter, the task force has 120 days to report on the risks and opportunities of advanced AI and to set out a federal plan, including plans to handle AI-enabled threats, streamline breach notifications and improve government response capacity while aiming to avoid overregulation. Putting the sitting intelligence chief at the helm signals that the administration treats AI leadership as a national security question as much as an economic one. Clayton framed the stakes bluntly to the Wall Street Journal: “The risk of not being first is high.”

The task force follows an earlier White House step: Trump hosted AI company executives last week and said they had signed a voluntary accord that would effectively self-police the development of the technology, according to the Associated Press. The administration has also begun referring to AI as “super intelligence” after the president signed an executive order late last month calling for federal agencies to use the new phrasing, Nextgov reported. Critics, including Senator Mark Warner, have questioned how much authority the new body actually has — a question the task force's 120-day report will have to answer in practice, not just on paper.
