China's Intelligence Arm Ran Campaign to Acquire Advanced AI Chips, Report Says

November 22, 2026

China's intelligence agency has made acquiring advanced semiconductor chips and gathering intelligence on the global chip industry a priority, running a campaign to overcome Western export restrictions for at least two years, according to a New York Times report published on November 22 and cited by Business Standard.

A campaign run from the top

The Ministry of State Security set up a working group under its chief, Chen Yixin, to lead the effort, which includes helping Chinese companies acquire and smuggle advanced chips into the country, besides collecting intelligence on the semiconductor sector and its supply chains, the report said, citing serving and former security and intelligence officials from Asia, the US and Europe. The effort is being coordinated at the party level by the Central Science and Technology Commission, established by President Xi Jinping in 2023 and led by Ding Xuexiang, a member of the Politburo Standing Committee, according to the report.

The Nvidia connection

The revelations come amid US scrutiny of Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang, whom American officials allege was working with the Chinese intelligence agency to spy on Jensen Huang, Nvidia's co-founder and former chief executive. A separate November 20 report in the New York Times said around $3.6 billion in Chinese government funding had flowed through companies associated with Huang's family or business associates, funding technology projects including data centres that received Nvidia chips, though the available research did not establish whether the Chinese military had actually used computing power from these data centres. The Wall Street Journal reported on November 21 that a Chinese businessman, Li Ming, allegedly ran the operations of Singapore-based Megaspeed, the company at the centre of the chip-diversion allegations, and that Li was charged with fraud in Singapore in February 2025 over allegations he helped divert computer servers powered by Nvidia chips.

Sources: Business Standard, November 22, 2026; The Daily Jagran, November 22, 2026
