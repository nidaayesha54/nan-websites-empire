Apple Passes Nvidia as World's Most Valuable Company at $5.03 Trillion

November 1, 2026

Apple ended Friday, October 30, as the world's most valuable public company, edging past Nvidia as investors moved away from companies spending heavily on artificial intelligence, Bloomberg reported.

The changing of the guard

Apple's shares fell 1.2% on Friday, leaving it worth $5.03 trillion, while Nvidia's shares fell 4.2%, cutting the chipmaker's value to $4.93 trillion. It is the first time Apple has held the top spot since a brief stint in late July, and it comes three days before Apple reports results. The swap is a sign that investors are now rewarding companies for spending less on AI rather than more — a reversal from much of the past two years of the AI boom. Apple shares are up 27% this year, more than twice Nvidia's gain, and Apple has kept its capital spending down while its free cash flow is projected to rise 38% to $142 billion for its fiscal year that ended September 30.

Why the AI trade is wobbling

The rotation follows weak results from the biggest AI spenders: Alphabet reported negative free cash flow of $9.1 billion for the third quarter, and Amazon reported a $42.4 billion shortfall over the past 12 months, while Intel plunged 29% on Friday after weak guidance. Nvidia sells the chips that power AI data centers, so doubts about whether that spending will pay off hit it directly. Whether Apple keeps the crown likely depends on its earnings report on Monday, November 2 — its first under chief executive John Ternus, who succeeded Tim Cook on September 1.

Sources: Bloomberg via The Seattle Times, US Brief, October 30-31, 2026
