AI Giants Pledge a Single 'No Scraping' Tool for Publishers After Years of Complaints

October 31, 2026

OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic pledged on Friday to give media outlets and other websites a single way to prevent their content from being scraped to train artificial intelligence models, amid growing criticism and legal battles from news publishers, AFP reported on October 31.

One instruction instead of many

Publishers currently have to implement multiple blocking commands for every bot that crawls the web to collect data. The new proposed solution will let them enter a single, standardized instruction recognized and honored by any AI company that adopts the protocol. The announcement comes as publishers face a surge in bots scraping their articles to feed AI models, and as some, like the New York Times, have taken the matter to court — the Times sued OpenAI and Microsoft in December 2023, alleging millions of its articles were used without authorization or payment to train ChatGPT.

A slow rollout, with limits

The solution will not fully take effect for months. Microsoft will implement it beginning February 1, 2027, and Google sometime in 2027; OpenAI said it will roll it out in the coming months, while Anthropic said it has already started. The setting will apply only to future training of models, does not prevent bots from crawling otherwise, and content scraping will be allowed by default until a publisher changes the setting, Microsoft said in a blog post.

Sources: AFP via Malay Mail, October 31, 2026
