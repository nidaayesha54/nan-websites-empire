Justice Department Launches Super Intelligence Task Force to Fight AI Fraud and Hacks

November 6, 2026

The Justice Department has formed a new task force to protect taxpayers, critical infrastructure and national security from criminals who use artificial intelligence to commit fraud and carry out hacks, the department announced on November 5, PYMNTS and Nextgov reported.

Who runs it and what it will do

The Super Intelligence Task Force, known as DOJ-SITF, will be co-chaired by Associate Attorney General Stanley Woodward and Associate Deputy Attorney General Stephen Weiss. Its mandate is to combat misuse of the technology by foreign state and state-sponsored actors, transnational criminal organisations, and individual or corporate offenders engaged in fraud or other unlawful conduct. The task force will organise the department's criminal and civil enforcement involving AI, deconflict investigations across Justice Department components and US attorneys' offices, recommend enforcement priorities and develop guidance on prosecuting unlawful AI-related conduct. It absorbs the AI Litigation Task Force announced in January under then-Attorney General Pam Bondi.

The 'super intelligence' label

"Super intelligence," or SI, is the term the executive branch now uses in place of "artificial intelligence" under a September 29 executive order signed by President Donald Trump. The task force will serve as the department's main point of contact with the White House Super Intelligence Force, chaired by Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton, which coordinates the government's engagement with AI companies. The launch announced no new criminal offence and no cases: the founding memorandum, signed by Attorney General Todd Blanche on October 13, creates a coordination mechanism rather than a new statute, and the task force's first cases will show how the department intends to use it.

Sources: PYMNTS, Nextgov, November 5, 2026
