NASA Unveils X-70 Fractal, a New Experimental Aircraft Built to Test Next-Generation Flight

November 6, 2026

NASA unveiled a new experimental aircraft, the X-70 Fractal, on November 4 during the UP.Summit at NASA's Kennedy Space Center in Florida, the agency's latest addition to its long-running X-plane programme, Business Today and The War Zone reported.

A flying laboratory, not a passenger jet

The X-70 Fractal is a crewed, high-performance research aircraft designed to test advanced flight-control systems and explore new levels of manoeuvrability, rather than to serve as a commercial passenger aircraft or a conventional military jet. NASA Administrator Jared Isaacman said the aircraft's modular design and ability to rapidly swap internal and external payloads would let research teams move quickly from one experiment to another. The aircraft can carry up to 2,000 pounds of experimental equipment. NASA said it expects the X-70 to make its first flight under the agency's banner within the next two years, though it has not disclosed the manufacturer, the final airframe configuration, the programme cost or a detailed flight-test schedule.

The numbers NASA has released

According to NASA, the X-70 has a takeoff weight of 10,000 pounds, a cruising speed of Mach 0.75 — about 75 percent of the speed of sound — a ceiling of 45,000 feet and a range of around 1,500 nautical miles. Isaacman described the aircraft as the first in a broader expansion of NASA's X-plane portfolio under his leadership, signalling a renewed focus on aeronautics research. The image NASA released alongside the announcement was described as generated content rather than a photograph of the finished aircraft, so its final appearance remains unconfirmed. A second experimental aircraft is also reported to be in the works, with more details expected at NASA's Max Power air show at the Shuttle Landing Facility this weekend.

Sources: Business Today, The War Zone, Flying Magazine, November 4-5, 2026
