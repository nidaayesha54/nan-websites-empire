Nvidia's China Comeback at Risk as Customers Cancel Orders After Huang's Exit

November 23, 2026

Nvidia staged a comeback in China's booming artificial-intelligence industry by selling less powerful chips and software after US export bans cost it billions in sales, but that business is now threatened by the scandal surrounding former chief executive Jensen Huang, Reuters reported on November 23.

Cancelled orders, unsettled customers

Some Chinese customers have begun cancelling orders amid concerns that the RTX PRO 5500, a graphics card designed for professional computers that can also run AI models, may not be permitted into China, according to two people familiar with the matter. Reuters could not determine the scale of the cancellations or whether the US or China would restrict shipments. The controversy surrounding Huang has also deeply unsettled Chinese suppliers, customers and other partners whose businesses depend on Nvidia, according to two people familiar with the matter, who fear heightened scrutiny could extend to Nvidia's remaining legal chip sales.

A billion-dollar turnaround under strain

Before Huang's troubles, revenue from customers headquartered in China and Hong Kong had more than tripled year over year, rising from $2.97 billion in Nvidia's October 2025 quarter to $11.05 billion in the quarter ended October 25, 2026, according to the company's financial results. Even as the company was reporting growing China revenue, Huang publicly said that Washington's restrictions had decimated the company's chip business in China and that its share of China's AI chip market had plunged to zero. China's reliance on Nvidia is attracting attention in Washington and may attract more after Democrats routed Trump's Republican Party in the November 3 midterm elections, winning control of the US House and Senate from January. Congress is going to press hard, on a bipartisan basis, on chips and chipmaking capabilities, after two years of close to zero oversight, said Mike Kuiken, vice chair of the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission.

Sources: Reuters, November 23, 2026
