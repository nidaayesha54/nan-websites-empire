Google DeepMind's AI Agents Expose Weaknesses in Legacy 1024-Bit RSA Encryption

November 7, 2026

Google DeepMind has used AI agents working under human direction to find two new weaknesses in legacy 1024-bit RSA encryption, the company announced on November 6, Crypto Briefing and Interesting Engineering reported.

What the AI agents found

The research, announced by DeepMind vice president of science Pushmeet Kohli and led by researchers Dj Dvijotham and Abhradeep Thakurta with outside cryptographers Nadia Heninger of UC San Diego and Inria and Emmanuel Thomé of Inria, covers two separate results. The first enables the forgery of digital signatures: the agents' implementation reproduced a known forgery using 3.5 times less CPU time than the previous approach, and for harder cases — where an attacker controls only a small slice of the signed data, such as a 32-byte digital fingerprint — a new algorithm theoretically improves on the best previously known method by roughly 1,600 times for 1024-bit keys. The second result is a new algorithm for batch factoring that cuts the average cost of factoring each additional 1024-bit key by about 30 times compared with earlier methods. DeepMind has published full technical papers on both results so the wider security community can check and build on them.

Modern encryption is not affected

DeepMind stresses that the findings apply only to obsolete 1024-bit RSA, a standard the US standards body NIST stopped accepting for generating new signatures back in 2013. Modern deployments almost universally use 2048-bit keys or larger, and those are unaffected by these specific findings — online banking, messaging apps, software updates and digital currencies are not threatened, and no live keys were broken, since the experiments ran in simulation on a retired standard chosen precisely because attacking it harms nobody. The work is nonetheless a striking demonstration of AI agents doing original cryptanalysis in weeks, and a warning to any organisation still running legacy 1024-bit systems to retire them.

Sources: Crypto Briefing, Interesting Engineering, November 6, 2026
