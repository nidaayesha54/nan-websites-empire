SpaceX Q3 2026: $14.6 Billion Revenue as Starship Flight 15 Targets a Dual-Stage Catch

October 26, 2026

SpaceX delivered revenue of $14.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 161% year over year, the company reported on October 20, 2026, with income from operations of $2.8 billion, net income of $4.4 billion and adjusted EBITDA of $8.8 billion. The quarter marked what the company described as an inflection moment across launch, connectivity and its Super Intelligence business.

Super Intelligence is now SpaceX's largest segment. Revenue reached $7.56 billion, up 830% year over year, driven by the activation of new cloud services agreements, the acquisition of Cursor — which contributed $608 million after its August 14 close — and growing adoption of Grok. Connectivity revenue rose 69% year over year to $5.25 billion, and Starlink ended the quarter with 13.6 million consumer subscribers, up 1.6 million, at $69 a month average revenue per user.

Starship goes commercial

Starship Flight 14 was the company's first revenue-generating Starship mission, deploying 26 Starlink V3 satellites and adding roughly ten times the downlink capacity of a Falcon 9 Starlink launch. SpaceX completed 35 launches in the quarter, including 21 Starlink Falcon 9 flights, two Starship flights, one Falcon Heavy mission and 12 customer missions. Nameplate compute reached 1.7 gigawatts as of September 30, as the company builds out Colossus II in Memphis.

The next milestone is the most ambitious yet. Musk said Starship Flight 15, expected in November, will try to catch both the booster and the ship at the tower — which would be the first time an orbital rocket has been fully recovered. Management said it plans to pursue weekly Starship flights during the first half of 2027 and potentially twice-weekly flights in the second half.

The spending behind the surge

The growth comes with extraordinary spending: capital expenditures totaled $19.6 billion in the quarter, with $16.4 billion allocated to the Super Intelligence segment. Starlink Mobile reached more than 22 million monthly users, and SpaceX received FCC approval to launch 15,000 Mobile V2 satellites. Whether the AI bet pays for itself is now the central question for the world's most valuable private space company — but on revenue alone, the third quarter was its biggest yet.

Two other numbers from the quarter show how broad the company has become. Grok Bot, launched in August, had 800,000 paid weekly users earlier in October, more than doubling in a month, and enterprise deployment is underway. Starlink Mobile reached more than 22 million monthly users, up 37% sequentially, and Starlink was contracted for approximately 12,000 aircraft, nearly one-quarter of the available aircraft referenced by management. The company also quadrupled aviation production capacity from the beginning of 2026 and expected to double it again before year-end — growth that helps explain why connectivity, once the whole story, is now only one of three engines.
