CDC: Flu Rising in 26 States as Illnesses Reach 1.7 Million

November 7, 2026

Flu activity is rising sharply across much of the United States, with health officials warning that cases are increasing earlier than usual, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention said on Friday, Internewscast and USA Today reported.

The latest CDC numbers

In its latest update, the CDC estimated that more than 1.7 million people had contracted influenza by the week ending October 31, with more than 25,000 hospitalizations — a 56 percent weekly increase — and approximately 1,400 deaths, up 55 percent from the prior week. The weekly flu hospitalization rate rose to 2.5 per 100,000 people, compared with 1.8 per 100,000 the week before. Flu activity increased across 26 states spanning the South, West, Midwest and Northeast, and Louisiana and South Carolina are now reporting high flu activity. USA Today reported that, as of November 5, CDC projections found flu infections rising in 40 states and declining in none.

An early season driven by H1N1

The early nationwide increase has been associated with H1N1, the influenza strain behind the 2009 flu pandemic, which sickened 60 million people and resulted in more than 240,000 hospitalizations. The CDC said flu levels were "higher than typically observed at this time of year" and continued to climb in many parts of the country. Health officials continue to urge vaccination: the CDC recommends a flu vaccine every season for everyone aged six months and older, and experts say it is not too late to get a shot, since a partial match to the circulating strain still gives real protection.

Sources: Internewscast, USA Today, November 6-7, 2026
