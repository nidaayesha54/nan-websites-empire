Regeneron Extends US Ebola Supply Deal Worth About $270 Million Through 2031

October 26, 2026

Regeneron said on Monday it extended its agreement with a U.S. health authority to provide additional supply of its Ebola treatment, amid a growing outbreak in Africa that has become the widest geographic spread of any Ebola outbreak on the continent to date.

The new agreement with the U.S. Biomedical Advanced Research and Development Authority, expanding a 2020 contract, will see Regeneron supply doses of its drug Inmazeb through 2031 for about $270 million. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved Inmazeb in 2020 for the treatment of infection caused by Zaire ebolavirus.

An outbreak that keeps spreading

The current outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain, for which there is no approved specific antiviral treatment or licensed vaccine. Regeneron said it is testing its experimental therapy, maftivimab, as a potential treatment for the Bundibugyo virus.

The outbreak, first reported in the Democratic Republic of Congo in May, has now spread to four countries across East, Central and West Africa. The current outbreak has infected nearly 10,000 people and killed more than 4,800 in Congo, according to Reuters.

What Ebola does

Ebola is a severe viral illness that can cause fever, vomiting and diarrhea and, in severe cases, internal and external bleeding. Outbreaks spread through close contact with the bodily fluids of infected people, which is why isolation, protective equipment and safe burial practices are central to containment.

The scale of the current outbreak, combined with its spread across four countries, has raised concern among global health officials that containment will require sustained international effort, not just a local response.

Why the U.S. is stockpiling

The extended supply agreement is part of the U.S. government's preparedness strategy: keeping approved countermeasures on hand before a crisis reaches American shores. Inmazeb's approval for the Zaire strain makes it a cornerstone of that stockpile, even though the current outbreak involves a different strain.

That gap is exactly what Regeneron's experimental work targets. If maftivimab proves effective against Bundibugyo, it could fill one of the most dangerous holes in the world's Ebola defenses. For now, the drug remains investigational, and its testing is at an early stage.

Health officials stress that the immediate priority remains stopping transmission in the affected countries, through contact tracing, community engagement and care for patients. The supply deal, while significant, is a long-term insurance policy, not a substitute for the outbreak response now underway in Africa.

Sources: Reuters, Regeneron
