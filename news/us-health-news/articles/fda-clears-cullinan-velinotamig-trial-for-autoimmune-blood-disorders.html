FDA Clears Cullinan's Velinotamig Trial for Autoimmune Blood Disorders

October 27, 2026

Cullinan Therapeutics announced on Tuesday that the US Food and Drug Administration and Australia's Therapeutic Goods Administration have cleared the company to initiate a global Phase 1b/2a clinical trial of velinotamig in adults with relapsing and refractory immune thrombocytopenia (ITP) and warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia (wAIHA). The open-label, multicenter study will evaluate the safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, immunogenicity and preliminary clinical activity of the subcutaneously administered drug.

Velinotamig is a BCMAxCD3 T cell engager designed to deplete antibody-producing plasma cells. The study expands Cullinan's immunology portfolio into autoimmune cytopenias, evaluating a short-duration subcutaneous regimen in patients whose disease has not responded adequately to existing treatments.

Serious disorders with limited options

ITP and wAIHA are serious, potentially life-threatening autoimmune blood disorders in which pathogenic autoantibodies contribute to the destruction of platelets and red blood cells, respectively. Patients often experience significant disease burden, including debilitating fatigue and reduced quality of life, as well as severe manifestations such as bleeding in ITP and anemia in wAIHA.

Despite available therapies, many patients experience persistent or relapsing disease, remain dependent on chronic treatment, or develop substantial adverse events from current therapies. That gap is what Cullinan says its approach is designed to address, by targeting the plasma cells that produce the harmful antibodies rather than managing symptoms alone.

What the trial will test

The Phase 1b/2a study is designed to establish the safety and dosing of velinotamig while gathering early evidence of clinical activity. As an open-label trial, both researchers and participants will know who is receiving the drug, a standard design for early-stage studies focused on safety and dose-finding. Results from this stage will determine whether the drug advances to larger trials.

The dual clearance in the United States and Australia allows the company to run the trial across both countries, a common strategy for recruiting patients with rare conditions. For patients with relapsing and refractory disease, new trial options represent one of the few paths to treatments that go beyond currently approved therapies.
