'Dark Nutrition' Fans Drink Raw Milk, but FDA and CDC Flag the Catch

November 2, 2026

Raw milk's appeal does not erase the infection risk of skipping pasteurization, Men's Journal reported on November 1, summarizing an October 21 New York Times Magazine investigation into so-called 'dark nutrition' — overlapping communities questioning conventional food advice, rather than one standardized eating plan.

What the agencies say

The FDA says research shows 'no meaningful difference between the nutrient content of pasteurized and unpasteurized milk.' Its shopping advice is specific: 'Read the label. Safe milk will have the word pasteurized on the label.' The CDC puts it plainly: 'While good practices on farms can reduce contamination, they cannot guarantee safety from harmful germs,' identifying Salmonella, E. coli and Listeria among the potential hazards of raw milk.

Why the trend matters now

The renewed spotlight comes as food-safety recalls keep raw dairy in the news — including recent recalls of raw milk cheese linked to illness — and as influencers promote unpasteurized products as more 'natural.' The agencies' position is that skipping the heating step adds risk without a demonstrated nutritional advantage. Readers should not treat social-media claims about raw milk as medical advice; anyone with questions about diet and infection risk should consult a clinician.

Sources: Men's Journal, FDA, CDC, November 1-2, 2026
