CDC Deputy Director Jennifer Shuford to Leave After Midterms, Report Says

October 27, 2026

Jennifer Shuford, the deputy director of the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, will leave the Trump administration after next week's midterm elections, the Washington Post reported on Monday, citing sources. Shuford, a former Texas health commissioner, also serves as the CDC's chief medical officer and was hired in April as part of a team that included former deputy surgeon general Erica Schwartz, healthcare executive Sean Slovenski and FDA official Sara Brenner.

The reason for Shuford's departure was not immediately clear, the paper said, with one person attributing it to a family health issue and another saying she had disagreed with the administration's approach to vaccine policy. The CDC and the White House did not respond to requests for comment outside business hours, according to Reuters, which carried the report.

Departure follows vaccine policy shifts

The reported departure follows changes in the government's vaccine policy, with President Donald Trump unveiling plans to demand that vaccine makers split certain childhood shots into five separate doses. The step would advance a long-standing goal of Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., whose promotion of a link between vaccines and autism has been repeatedly debunked by scientists, according to Reuters.

Shuford's exit, if confirmed, would add to turnover at the top of the nation's public health agencies during a period of significant policy change. The CDC plays a central role in disease surveillance, outbreak response and vaccine recommendations, and its leadership has faced intense scrutiny this year as the administration has moved to reshape health policy.

What is not yet confirmed

Because the report rests on unnamed sources, key details remain unconfirmed, including the exact timing of Shuford's departure and the reasons behind it. The Washington Post's account presents two different explanations, and neither the CDC nor the White House has publicly confirmed the move. Observers will be watching for an official announcement and for news of a successor, as the deputy director role carries significant responsibility for the agency's day-to-day operations.

The timing, just after the midterm elections, also places the departure in a politically charged moment. Health policy has featured prominently in the campaign, and any leadership change at the CDC is likely to draw attention from lawmakers and public health experts alike.

Sources: Reuters, Washington Post
