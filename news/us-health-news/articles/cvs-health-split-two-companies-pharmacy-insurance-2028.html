CVS Health to Split Into Two Companies: Pharmacies in One, Aetna and Caremark in the Other

November 4, 2026

CVS Health confirmed on Wednesday, November 4, that it will split into two independent, publicly traded companies, marking one of the largest shakeups in the US healthcare industry and unwinding the vertically integrated model built over nearly two decades, reports said.

The two companies

One company, to be called CVS Pharmacy, will hold approximately 9,000 retail pharmacies, the mail-order pharmacy business, MinuteClinic including virtual care, and the Health100 technology services business, led by current group president Prem Shah as chief executive. The other, referred to as CVS Health in the interim, will hold insurer Aetna, pharmacy benefit manager CVS Caremark, CVS Specialty, Oak Street Health and Signify Health, led by current chairman and chief executive David Joyner. Shareholders will own shares in both companies in a transaction intended to be tax-free.

Timing, earnings — and what does not change yet

The separation is targeted for completion in the first half of 2028, and until then CVS will continue to operate as it does today, the company said. CVS also reported third-quarter results on November 4: net profit of nearly $2.2 billion, up from a $4 billion net loss a year earlier, and revenue of $108.4 billion, up 5.4 percent. The company said the split would have little impact on how people shop at CVS and would not change what they pay for prescriptions; shares fell about 10 percent in early trading after initially ticking up premarket, CNN reported.

Sources: CVS Health announcement via CNN, Becker's Payer, Fierce Healthcare, November 4, 2026
