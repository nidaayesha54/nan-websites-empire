FDA Floats Draft Peptide Compounding Policy Covering Six Peptides, Comments Open to November 13

November 1, 2026

The Department of Health and Human Services published a draft enforcement-discretion policy on October 30 covering compounding of certain peptide drug substances, with a notice of its availability in the Federal Register, according to a summary of the policy published October 31.

What the draft covers — and what it does not

Under the draft, HHS says it does not intend to take enforcement action against a state-licensed pharmacy, federal facility or licensed physician compounding a drug using one of six peptides in its Appendix A — BPC-157, Epitalon, KPV, MOTS-c and TB-500 as the acetate, and Semax as free base — under the conditions it sets, while the FDA's 503A bulks list is developed through rulemaking. It approves no drug: the notice states that compounded drugs are not FDA-approved. It does not cover 503B outsourcing facilities, nor products sold as 'research use only,' which FDA warning letters have called unapproved new drugs.

A draft, not a rule

The policy is a draft and is 'not binding on HHS or the public,' with comments open until November 13, 2026. HHS says it may revisit the policy if a safety signal or product-quality issue arises. Readers should not treat the draft as approval or as a safety endorsement of any peptide product; the distinction between a compounded drug made under enforcement discretion and an FDA-approved medicine remains the key point of the policy.

Sources: HHS/Federal Register via Kalios, October 30-31, 2026
