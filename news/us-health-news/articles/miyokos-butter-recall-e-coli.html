FDA Recall: Miyoko's Plant-Based Butter Pulled in 10 States Over E. Coli, Yeast and Mold

October 28, 2026

A popular plant-based butter has been recalled in ten states after lab reports identified potential contamination with yeast, mold and E. coli, the US Food and Drug Administration said. Miyoko's Creamery European Style Salted Plant Milk Butter, made from cashew milk and plant oils by Prosperity Organic Foods Inc. of Boise, Idaho, was assigned a Class II risk level, meaning use of the product could cause temporary or medically reversible adverse health consequences.

What to check in your fridge

The recall covers 1,304 cases, totalling 7,824 individual 8-ounce units, shipped in 6-pack cases with lot number 1007871 and a best-by date of November 5, 2026. The affected units were sold in Connecticut, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Maryland, New Hampshire, New Jersey and Wisconsin. The recall began after supermarket chain Market Basket notified customers of high yeast levels that did not meet quality standards; the FDA classified the recall as Class II on October 23, following an initial recall on September 11, according to reports.

E. coli infections can cause severe stomach issues, diarrhoea, nausea and vomiting, and some strains risk hemolytic uremic syndrome, a life-threatening condition that can lead to kidney failure. Young children and older adults are particularly vulnerable to severe illness, according to the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. As a Class II recall, the safest option is to toss the product or return it to the store.
