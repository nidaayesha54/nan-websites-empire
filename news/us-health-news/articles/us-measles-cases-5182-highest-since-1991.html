US Measles Cases Reach 5,182, Highest Annual Total Since 1991

November 7, 2026

US measles cases have climbed past 5,000 for the first time in more than three decades, reaching 5,182 so far in 2026, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention said on Friday, USA Today reported on November 6.

A 35-year high

The CDC's latest weekly update, published November 6, put the national case count at 5,182 confirmed cases as of November 5 — up 330 cases from the previous update, one of the largest weekly jumps of the year. It is the highest annual total since 1991, when 9,643 cases were recorded, and more than double the 2,289 cases reported for all of 2025. The cases span 45 states plus Washington, D.C., with 5,164 cases across 47 jurisdictions and 18 among international visitors to the United States. The CDC reported 43 new outbreaks in 2026, and 96 percent of confirmed cases — 4,993 of 5,182 — are outbreak-associated. The CDC's count reflects four deaths as of October 29, and the agency notes its figures can change as information and reviews are completed.

Elimination status under review

The World Health Organization is expected to review the US measles elimination status on November 10, following the 2025 Texas outbreak that killed three people. Measles was declared eliminated in the United States in 2000, meaning the virus was no longer spreading continuously within the country, but the sustained outbreaks of the past two years have put that status at risk. Health officials say the surge is driven by gaps in vaccination coverage, and the CDC continues to recommend two doses of the MMR vaccine for children and for adults without evidence of immunity.

Sources: CDC, USA Today, November 6, 2026
