CDC and FDA Performance Ratings Hit All-Time Lows in Gallup Poll

October 26, 2026

Performance ratings for the FDA and CDC have hit all-time lows, according to a Gallup poll reported on October 15, 2026. Gallup asked 1,000 U.S. adults to rate the job performance of 12 federal agencies and five Cabinet departments as excellent, good, only fair or poor. The poll was conducted September 1–17, 2026, by telephone, with a margin of error of plus or minus 4 percentage points.

Just 26% of Americans rated the CDC's performance as excellent or good, while a record-high 44% rated it poor. The FDA fared worse still: 25% rated it excellent or good, with 48% rating it poor. The FDA and CDC are the lowest-rated of the 12 agencies surveyed, and the only ones with higher poor ratings than excellent/good ratings. By contrast, the highest-rated agencies were the U.S. Postal Service at 58% and NASA at 53%.

A long fall from 2019

The CDC's decline has been steep. In 2019, 64% of Americans rated the CDC excellent or good. That share fell to 40% in 2021 and now stands at 26%, down five points from 2025 and 14 points from 2024. The FDA has historically been rated lower than the CDC, reaching only 46% in a 2017 survey; its current 25% is statistically similar to last year's 27%, but still the lowest Gallup has measured for the agency.

The partisan split tells much of the story — and it has flipped. Democrats' rating of the CDC dropped 14 points this year, to 20%, following a 31-point decline in 2025, while Republicans' ratings improved from 19% in 2024 to 32% this year. For the FDA, 36% of Republicans rated it excellent or good, up from 23% in 2024, compared with 18% of Democrats, down from 47% in 2024. Since 2019, Gallup notes, the party subgroup aligned with the incumbent president has always rated the FDA significantly more favorably.

Why it matters now

Gallup notes the agencies have been in the news recently — the CDC in connection with measles outbreaks amid attempts to change long-standing vaccine guidance, and the FDA in its coverage of a major cyclospora outbreak linked to lettuce that sickened people in many states. Congress will also need to weigh in again on funding for both agencies in the coming months. With a midterm election weeks away in which voters say health care costs will be a top issue, the poll is less a verdict on any single outbreak than a measure of how far public confidence in the country's health agencies has fallen — and how partisan that confidence has become.

Sources: Gallup, Becker's Hospital Review, Fierce Healthcare
