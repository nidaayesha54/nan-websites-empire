FDA Warns San Francisco Fermented Foods Maker Over Pathogen Risks and Labeling

October 26, 2026

The Food and Drug Administration has issued a warning letter to a San Francisco food manufacturer after inspectors found deficiencies in its food safety plan, sanitation procedures and controls for refrigerated, ready-to-eat fermented cabbage products.

The letter names RDM Express Inc. of San Francisco. The agency also cited labeling violations involving the company's Russian-style kvas drink, sour cabbage and half-sour pickles, according to coverage of the letter by Food Safety News.

What inspectors found

Inspectors flagged problems in the company's food safety plan, its sanitation procedures and its controls for refrigerated, ready-to-eat fermented cabbage products. Refrigerated ready-to-eat foods are a particular focus for regulators because they are eaten without a cooking step that would kill pathogens, so the safety controls at the plant are the consumer's main protection.

The labeling violations cover the company's kvas drink, sour cabbage and half-sour pickles. Labels are not just marketing: for fermented and refrigerated products, accurate labeling tells consumers how to store a product safely and what is actually inside it.

How FDA warning letters work

As part of its enforcement activities, the FDA sends warning letters to entities under its jurisdiction. Business owners have 15 days to respond to a warning letter, laying out how they will correct the violations.

Warning letters are often not issued until companies have been given months or years to correct problems, and some letters are not posted for public view until weeks or months after they are sent. That means a warning letter usually represents the escalation of a long-running compliance conversation, not a first notice.

What it means for shoppers

A warning letter is not itself a recall, and the coverage of this letter does not report illnesses linked to the company's products. But it is a public signal that regulators found the company's safety systems wanting, and consumers who buy refrigerated fermented products should pay attention to storage instructions and expiration dates.

Shoppers who have concerns about a specific product can check the FDA's warning letter postings and the company's own communications for updates. The most important practical step is simple: keep refrigerated fermented foods refrigerated, follow the label, and discard anything that looks or smells off.

The case is a reminder that the fermented foods boom, from kimchi to kvas to craft pickles, sits squarely inside the food safety system. Small producers face the same rules as large ones, and the FDA's letter to RDM Express shows the agency is watching that fast-growing corner of the grocery aisle.
