Graziers Raw Milk Cheese Recall: 13 Sick, 8 Hospitalized as FDA Issues Class I Warning

October 26, 2026

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration has issued its highest-risk warning for recalled Graziers raw milk cheese products after an E. coli outbreak sickened 13 people across nine states, hospitalized eight and left three with hemolytic uremic syndrome (HUS), a serious complication that can cause kidney failure. No deaths have been reported. The FDA classified the recall as Class I on Monday, October 19, 2026 — meaning there is a reasonable probability that consuming or being exposed to the recalled products could cause serious health consequences or death.

The affected products are Sierra Nevada Graziers grass-fed raw milk cheeses, made by California-based Sierra Nevada Cheese Company of Willows, California. Varieties include medium cheddar, sharp cheddar, jalapeño jack and Monterey Jack, sold in 8-ounce and 16-ounce retail packages, 5-pound loaves for food service and wholesalers, and 40-pound cheddar blocks. The products were distributed in Arizona, California, Colorado, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Maryland, New Jersey, Oregon, Texas, Washington and Wisconsin — 13 states of distribution, distinct from the nine states where illnesses were reported.

A recall with no lot code

Most recalls name a lot code and a date range. This one names neither. Sierra Nevada Cheese Company initiated the voluntary recall on September 28, 2026, after being contacted by the FDA regarding the ongoing FDA/CDC outbreak investigation, and the company said the investigation “has not been narrowed to a specific lot, date code, or production date,” so no batch could be cleared. The company immediately stopped production and shipment of all Graziers raw milk cheese, notified customers and issued notices through its website and social media.

According to information provided during the investigation, illnesses have been epidemiologically associated with consumption of Graziers Raw Milk Medium Cheddar, and the outbreak involves E. coli O26:H11, a strain of Shiga toxin-producing E. coli. The CDC reported 13 people infected as of September 25. Because some wholesale cheese may have been cut and repackaged, shoppers holding cheese without its original packaging have little to go on except the Graziers name — which is the practical reason the company chose a blanket recall.

What to do

Consumers who have purchased the recalled Graziers raw milk cheese products should not consume or serve them, and should return the product to the place of purchase for a refund or dispose of it. Check both refrigerators and freezers, because these cheeses may last a long time, and clean and sanitize any containers, shelves, knives, boards or surfaces that may have touched the product. Symptoms of E. coli infection usually begin 3 to 4 days after infection but can start any time between 1 and 14 days, and can include fever, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting and abdominal pain. Contact a doctor if you have concerns after eating a recalled product.
