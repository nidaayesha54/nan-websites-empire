FDA Tentatively Approves First Generic Lenacapavir, Priced at $40 a Year in 120 Countries

November 4, 2026

The US Food and Drug Administration has granted tentative approval to Dr. Reddy's Laboratories for a generic version of injectable lenacapavir, the first generic long-acting HIV prevention injection cleared by the agency, reports said on November 4.

What tentative approval means

Tentative approval confirms the product meets FDA standards for safety, efficacy and manufacturing, but keeps it off the US market while Gilead's patents run; it allows US government programmes to buy it for use abroad. Dr. Reddy's generic is priced at $40 per person per year in 120 low- and middle-income countries, and first supplies should reach countries before the end of 2026, with wider availability in 2027, reports said.

The context

The clearance comes 16 months after Gilead's original lenacapavir won FDA approval in June 2025. Dr. Reddy's filed the prevention and treatment uses together under the FDA's 505(b)(2) pathway, which qualified the application for priority review with a six-month target. Lenacapavir is a twice-yearly injection; the generic approval is a regulatory milestone for HIV prevention access, not a change to US availability, which remains patent-blocked for now.
