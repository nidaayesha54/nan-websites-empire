Salmonella Oyster Recall Expands: What to Know About the Little Wicomico Outbreak

October 26, 2026

Federal health officials are warning consumers not to eat recalled oysters from Virginia's Little Wicomico Oyster Company, linked to a multistate Salmonella outbreak. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention reported on October 19, 2026, that 27 people in 12 states have been sickened with Salmonella Telelkebir infections, five have been hospitalized, and no deaths have been reported. Illnesses began between May 29 and September 14, 2026.

The company voluntarily recalled whole, in-shell oysters on October 9, 2026. The recall covers oysters harvested from September 18 through October 8, 2026, from the Little Wicomico River and Little Wicomico River Lower growing areas in Virginia. The company's certification number is VA-1421-SP. The Virginia Department of Health has also temporarily closed the Little Wicomico River harvest area as a precaution, and the company has stopped harvesting from the affected area and is cooperating with investigators.

Two different numbers — do not mix them up

Readers may see two different sets of figures, and they measure different things. The 27 illnesses in 12 states are the CDC's confirmed case count, with Pennsylvania reporting the most illnesses (eight), followed by New York (four) and Virginia (three). Separately, the recalled oysters were distributed to restaurants and retailers in 15 states and the District of Columbia: California, Colorado, Connecticut, Florida, Georgia, Illinois, Maryland, Missouri, Nevada, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Pennsylvania, Texas, Virginia and Washington, D.C. Distribution is wider than the confirmed case count, and officials say the oysters may have been distributed further.

Food Safety News also reported that the Virginia Department of Health has documented 55 patients across 19 states — a higher tally than the CDC's 27 — reflecting differences in how state and federal counts are compiled and updated. The advice is the same regardless of which count you read: do not eat, sell or serve the recalled oysters.

How investigators linked the oysters

Of the 27 people counted by the CDC, 13 were interviewed, and 10 of them (77%) reported eating raw oysters, against a 1.6% background rate in CDC's FoodNet Population Survey. Traceback was possible for seven of the ten, and Little Wicomico River oysters were the only ones all seven had in common. Pennsylvania officials also sampled oysters at a distributor, and one sample harvested by the company from the Little Wicomico River was positive for Salmonella, with whole genome sequencing to determine whether it matches the outbreak strain still pending. Contaminated food can look, smell and taste normal, the FDA cautioned — so check tags and discard or return any affected oysters, and seek medical attention for severe symptoms.

Sources: CDC, FDA, CIDRAP, ConsumerAffairs, Food Safety News
