FDA Recalls Thousands of Blood Pressure Medicine Bottles Over Excess Levels and Failed Tests

November 3, 2026

The US Food and Drug Administration has announced nationwide recalls of two blood pressure medications over concerns about excess drug levels and manufacturing quality failures, reports on November 3 said.

What was recalled

The FDA announced a Class III recall of Viatris Inc.'s combination telmisartan and amlodipine tablets (40 mg/5 mg) because they may contain more telmisartan than specified — 21,620 bottles in total, in 30-count bottles across lots 3228113, 3228114 and 3228115 (expiry October 31) and lots 3228120 and 3228121 (expiry November 30). Class III means use of the affected product is not likely to cause adverse health consequences, and the FDA did not specify how much additional telmisartan the tablets may contain.

The second recall

The FDA also recalled 3,744 bottles of a Macleods Pharmaceuticals combination of olmesartan medoxomil, amlodipine and hydrochlorothiazide (40 mg/10 mg/12.5 mg) after the tablets failed dissolution specifications — the test of whether a medicine dissolves at the required rate. The company began the recall on October 7, and the FDA classified it as Class II on October 26, meaning use could cause temporary or medically reversible health problems, though serious harm is considered unlikely. Patients should not stop a prescribed blood pressure medicine without speaking to their clinician or pharmacist, and should check lot numbers against the recall notice.

Sources: Health and Me, Dispatch Times, November 3, 2026
