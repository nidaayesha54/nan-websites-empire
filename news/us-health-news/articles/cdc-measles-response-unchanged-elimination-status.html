CDC Chief: Measles Response Will Not Change Even if US Loses Elimination Status

November 11, 2026

The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention will not fundamentally change its response to measles outbreaks even if the US loses its measles elimination status this month, CDC Director Dr. Erica Schwartz told Reuters on Tuesday, CNN reported on November 11.

A decision expected within days

Global health experts convened by the Pan American Health Organization will determine in the coming days whether the US has lost its official measles-free status, a hard-won public health achievement it has held since 2000. Schwartz said she expected a PAHO decision by early next week. The United States is experiencing its highest number of measles infections in more than three decades, with nearly 5,200 confirmed cases reported in 2026, compared with 2,289 cases throughout 2025, and 11 deaths reported by state health departments since early last year.

Same response, whatever the verdict

\"Let's say we did lose our measles elimination status. We're going to be doing the same exact thing that we've been doing today,\" Schwartz said, describing continued work with states, public awareness efforts and promotion of the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine, known as MMR, which the CDC calls the number one way to prevent measles. Schwartz, confirmed as CDC director in August, inherited a raging outbreak that has worsened since last year. Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., who oversees the CDC, has for years cast doubt on the safety and efficacy of the MMR vaccine.

Sources: CNN, November 11, 2026; Reuters via Devdiscourse, November 11, 2026
