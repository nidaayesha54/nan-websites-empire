Flu Hospitalizations Hit Highest Week-42 Rate Since 2010 as H1N1 Dominates

November 8, 2026

The US flu season is running ahead of schedule. The CDC's week-42 data show the highest week-42 hospitalisation rate since the 2010-11 season, nearly all of it H1N1pdm09, with the CDC forecasting continued increases in hospitalisations and emergency department visits through mid-November, according to a November 7 pathogen update citing CDC data.

An early, H1N1-driven season

The early surge is being driven almost entirely by the H1N1pdm09 strain, the update said. The CDC had already reported that flu illnesses had reached 1.7 million this season, with activity rising in 26 states. Health officials are urging vaccination before holiday gatherings, noting that flu season can last from autumn to as late as May, peaking between December and February. A University of Wisconsin Health expert quoted in vaccination coverage published November 7 put it simply: the one gift nobody wants to give or receive this holiday season is the flu or another respiratory illness.

What else is circulating

The same November 7 update, which tracks new GISAID and wastewater data, noted that the COVID lineage PJ.2.1 now accounts for about a quarter of infections — 25.1 percent as of October 19, up from 15.7 percent two weeks earlier — based on World Health Network wastewater estimates. The update cautioned that the latest figures revise the same period as the previous week's file rather than adding new days, so the trend, not the daily number, is the signal to watch.

Sources: Pathogen Update, November 7, 2026; CDC
