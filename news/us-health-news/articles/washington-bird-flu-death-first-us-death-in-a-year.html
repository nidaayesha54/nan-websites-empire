Washington Resident Dies of Bird Flu in First US Death in a Year

November 6, 2026

A person in Yakima County, Washington, has died from complications of bird flu after being exposed to infected dairy cattle, county health officials said, the first US bird flu death in a year, the Associated Press and CNN reported.

What officials have confirmed

The resident, who had previous health conditions, was hospitalised with a severe case of bird flu and died on Sunday, November 1, according to county officials. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention identified the infection as H5N1, the strain detected in dairy cattle that has caused sporadic human illnesses in the United States. It is the third US death linked to bird flu since February 2024, when the virus first jumped into dairy cattle: an elderly person in Louisiana died in January 2025 after contracting H5N1, and a Washington resident died in November 2025 from H5N5, a rarer form of the virus. County officials reported that 33 dairy herds in Washington have recently tested positive for H5N1, and all three human cases in the state since mid-October resulted from exposure to infected cows.

Risk to the public remains low, officials say

More than 70 human infections have been reported in the US since the beginning of 2024, according to the CDC, but there is no known person-to-person spread of the virus and the agency says the risk to the general public is low. The CDC analysed the genetic sequence of the virus from the person who died and found no changes that would make it spread more easily between people or cause more severe illness, a CDC spokesperson said. State health officials have reported all three recent Washington cases to the CDC, though the agency had not yet included them on its bird flu dashboard at the time of the reports. Dairy and poultry workers remain the most at-risk group, and health officials continue to advise protective equipment for people working with infected animals.

Sources: Associated Press, CNN, STAT, November 2-3, 2026
