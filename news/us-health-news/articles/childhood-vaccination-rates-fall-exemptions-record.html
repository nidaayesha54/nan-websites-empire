Childhood Vaccination Rates Fall Across the US as School Exemptions Hit Record High

November 11, 2026

Childhood vaccination coverage across the United States has dropped to levels not seen in over a decade, leaving millions of school-aged children exposed to preventable infectious diseases and fuelling a nationwide resurgence of measles, a joint investigation published on November 11 by NBC News and Stanford University revealed.

The numbers behind the decline

Approximately 83 percent of US counties and jurisdictions have recorded significant drops in routine paediatric immunisation coverage, the investigation found. Data released by the CDC shows that school vaccination exemptions reached an unprecedented 4.2 percent among kindergartners during the 2025-2026 academic year. National coverage for the two-dose measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccine among kindergarten children fell from 95.2 percent during the 2019-2020 school year to 92.4 percent in 2025-2026, and the CDC estimates that roughly 280,000 kindergartners entered classrooms this year without full, documented protection against measles.

A real-world consequence

Coverage for other routinely recommended childhood immunisations — including DTaP, polio and chickenpox — has fallen by comparable amounts, confirming a systemic challenge across routine paediatric prevention. The consequences are already visible: preliminary CDC figures show 285 confirmed measles cases in 2024, 2,289 in 2025 and 5,182 in 2026 through November 5, with about 96 percent of this year's cases linked directly to outbreaks. Health officials say the MMR vaccine remains the most effective protection, and the CDC continues to urge parents to keep their children's vaccinations up to date.

Sources: NBC News / Stanford University via Health and Family, November 11, 2026
