Energy Drinks and Cardiac Arrest: What a New Study Actually Shows — and What It Doesn't

November 3, 2026

A new study has renewed scrutiny of energy drinks and stimulant-containing supplements, finding cardiac arrest appeared about 11 times more often in US Food and Drug Administration adverse-event reports involving caffeine and stimulant-containing products than in reports involving products without those ingredients, Times Now reported on November 3.

What the researchers found

Presented as research to be discussed at the American Heart Association's Scientific Sessions 2026, the analysis examined 28,345 adverse-event reports submitted to an FDA safety database between 2017 and 2025. Of these, 854 involved energy drinks or dietary supplements containing caffeine and other stimulants, while 27,491 concerned products without those ingredients. Reports involving stimulant-containing products were also about 4.5 times more likely to mention heart attacks, and reports of ventricular arrhythmias and heart palpitations were around four times more common. Adults aged 18 to 34 accounted for around 45 percent of the reports linking these products to cardiovascular events.

The crucial caveat

Researchers stressed the findings establish an association, not a direct cause-and-effect relationship. The analysis relied on voluntarily submitted FDA reports, so it cannot establish how frequently cardiac events occur among all energy drink consumers or prove that the products triggered them, and the research is preliminary and has not yet undergone peer review as a full scientific paper. Readers should not treat social-media summaries as medical advice; anyone with concerns about caffeine, stimulants and heart risk should consult a clinician.

Sources: Times Now, November 3, 2026
