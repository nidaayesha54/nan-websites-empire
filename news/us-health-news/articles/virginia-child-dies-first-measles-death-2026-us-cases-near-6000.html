Young Child Dies in Virginia's First Measles Death of 2026 as US Cases Near 6,000

November 21, 2026

Virginia reported its first measles-associated death of 2026 on Friday, involving a child who tested positive for measles, the state health department said, Reuters reported on November 20.

A child younger than four

The Virginia Department of Health said in a statement late in the afternoon on November 20 that a child younger than 4 years old died. On the same day the child was reported sick in Charlottesville, the United States lost its measles-free status by the World Health Organization. Virginia has reported 220 measles cases in 2026, including 40 among children aged 0 to 4 and 75 among those aged 5 to 12, state health department data showed as of November 19.

A worsening national outbreak

The CDC on Friday reported 5,838 measles cases as of November 19, as the country battles its worst outbreak of the viral infection in decades, with cases above 5,000 for the first time since 1991. Pennsylvania, the hardest-hit state, reported an eighth measles-related death and confirmed 1,631 measles cases including 326 hospitalizations as of November 20. Wisconsin has reported two deaths, while New York state recorded its first measles-associated death, involving a child. The CDC has confirmed six measles-associated deaths so far in 2026, though it does not specify the states where the deaths were recorded.

Sources: Reuters via Progress-Index, November 20, 2026; Reuters via KTWB, November 20, 2026
