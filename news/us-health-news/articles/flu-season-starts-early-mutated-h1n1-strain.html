Flu Season Starts Early as Mutated H1N1 Strain Drives a Surge in Cases

November 6, 2026

Flu season has arrived weeks earlier than usual in the United States, driven almost entirely by an influenza A(H1N1) strain carrying a new change that may help it slip past some immunity from past seasons, according to the CDC's latest surveillance report, CNN reported on November 5.

What the CDC data shows

The CDC's FluView report for Week 42, covering the week ending October 24 and published October 30, says seasonal flu activity remains low nationally but is increasing in many parts of the country and is higher than typically observed at this time of year. In that week, 7.6 percent of 53,102 specimens tested in clinical labs were positive for flu, and 98.9 percent of the flu A viruses subtyped by public health labs were A(H1N1)pdm09 — the same subtype behind the 2009 swine flu pandemic. The CDC estimates at least 1 million illnesses, 16,000 hospitalisations and 900 deaths from flu so far this season, and it has recorded the season's first flu-associated death of a child, in the week ending October 10. The strain carries a single change in hemagglutinin, called G155E, which appeared after this year's vaccine strains were selected.

Vaccines still urged despite a partial match

Lab work suggests this year's flu shot is only a partial match to the changed strain, yet experts still urge people to get vaccinated now, because a partial match still gives real protection. The CDC's FluSight forecasts predict flu hospital admissions will likely keep rising nationally through at least mid-November, with a forecast range of 4,800 to 23,200 new admissions for the week ending November 14. The early surge is most visible on the West Coast: in California, flu test positivity reached 9.65 percent on October 10, against about 1 percent in early October in each of the past two seasons. The CDC recommends a flu vaccine every season for everyone aged six months and older, and says an earlier increase does not necessarily lead to a higher seasonal peak or greater overall severity.

Sources: CDC FluView Week 42, CNN, November 5, 2026
