Pakistan Signs $2.25 Billion Deal for Lithium Battery Gigafactory in Rawalpindi

October 27, 2026

Pakistan has signed a $2.25 billion agreement for a lithium-ion battery gigafactory in Rawalpindi, Profit by Pakistan Today reported on October 27. The agreement was signed by MHIL with Chinese and local investors, and the project remains pending policy approval, according to the report.

The development comes as the government moves towards implementing the Automotive and Auto Parts Manufacturing Policy 2026-2031, recently approved by Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif but awaiting clearance from the International Monetary Fund over proposed tax incentives for electric vehicle manufacturing.

Demand outlook to 2030

Engineering Development Board chief Mansoor said Pakistan's demand for battery energy storage systems could reach 100,000 MW by June 2030 if economic growth reaches 5 percent or higher, compared with an estimated 80,000 MW under a growth scenario below 3 percent. He said grid stabilisation alone could require 3,000 to 5,000 MW of battery storage, while meeting overall demand would require four to five gigafactories costing approximately $1 billion to $1.25 billion each.

Pakistan currently assembles around 3,000 to 4,000 MW of batteries, along with components such as motors, controllers and chargers, according to the EDB chief. The agreement would also require manufacturers to export a specified share of their production and comply with international safety and performance standards.

Why local production matters

Mansoor said local battery production would help reduce import costs, support the country's transition towards electric mobility and create opportunities for exports. Battery storage is increasingly central to managing variable renewable energy and stabilising grids, and local manufacturing would reduce dependence on imported cells and packs.

For the project to move from agreement to construction, the pending policy approvals, including the IMF clearance on tax incentives, will be closely watched by investors and the auto industry alike.

The project also fits a broader regional pattern, with countries across Asia investing in battery manufacturing to secure supply chains for electric vehicles and grid storage. For Pakistan, success would mean not only import substitution but a new industrial export line, provided the policy framework, including the pending tax-incentive clearances, is settled in a way investors can rely on.

Sources: Profit by Pakistan Today, October 27, 2026.
