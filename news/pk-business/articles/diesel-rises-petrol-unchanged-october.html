Diesel Raised to Rs393.66 a Litre, Petrol Unchanged at Rs377.71

October 26, 2026

The federal government has increased the price of high-speed diesel (HSD) by Rs1.91 per litre while keeping the price of petrol unchanged, under the latest petroleum pricing review. According to a notification issued by the Petroleum Division, petrol would continue to be sold at Rs377.71 per litre, while HSD would cost Rs393.66 per litre from October 24 to October 26.

The numbers in detail

Following the revision, HSD rises to Rs393.66 per litre from Rs391.75, while the price of motor spirit (petrol) remains unchanged at Rs377.71 per litre. The revised prices remain effective from October 24 to October 26, 2026. The Petroleum Division said the change was necessitated by global developments, including movements in Platts rates, premiums and incidentals. The latest revision comes a day after the government decreased the price of petrol by Rs1.60 per litre while increasing the price of high-speed diesel by Rs1.64 per litre for October 23 — making this the second consecutive diesel increase in two days.

Why prices are moving daily now

On July 17, the government announced a new pricing mechanism under which petroleum product prices would be reviewed and notified daily, replacing the weekly pricing mechanism, as renewed tensions between the United States and Iran continued to drive volatility in global oil markets and raise concerns over fuel supplies. The global backdrop to this revision was stark: Brent crude futures gained $1.40, or 1.2%, to reach $114.57 a barrel on Friday, touching $115 earlier in the session, its highest level since May 4 and a peak of more than five months, amid concerns about further disruption to energy supplies from the Middle East following Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia. The government continues to levy Rs114 per litre in taxes and duties on petrol and Rs100 per litre on diesel, according to published breakdowns of the price.

Who feels a diesel rise most

The increase in diesel prices will raise fuel costs for transporters, farmers and other consumers dependent on diesel-powered vehicles and machinery, while petrol consumers will see no change in prices during the three-day period. Diesel matters especially for freight, agriculture and public transport, so even small increases can pass through to food and goods prices. On September 13, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif announced a special relief scheme offering Rs100 per litre off petrol for motorcycles, three-wheeler rickshaws and cars with engines up to 800cc, in an attempt to shield lower-income consumers from the impact of rising petroleum prices, covering an estimated 11.8 million beneficiaries. For now, though, the diesel-dependent parts of the economy — the trucks, tractors and buses — face another, if modest, cost increase.
