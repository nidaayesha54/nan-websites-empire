FBR Activates Faceless Tax Audit System From October 15

October 26, 2026

Pakistan's Federal Board of Revenue has put into effect a new procedural framework for designated tax cases through the National Faceless Centre, effective October 15, 2026. The move extends across both income tax and sales tax procedures and adds electronic documentation and quality-control checks to the process, in what officials present as a bid to make tax proceedings faster, fairer and more transparent.

What the new framework covers

According to SRO 1828(I)/2026, the FBR has enforced Chapter XX of the Income Tax Rules, 2002, while SRO 1825(I)/2026 brings Chapter VIAC of the Sales Tax Rules, 2006, into effect from the same date. Under the income tax rules, the faceless mechanism applies to cases designated under Section 122E of the Income Tax Ordinance, 2001, rather than automatically covering every taxpayer. The covered proceedings include audits under Sections 177 and 214C, proceedings under Section 111 and assessments under Chapter X of the Ordinance. The sales tax chapter covers audit under sections 25 and 72B of the Sales Tax Act and proceedings under section 11E. The rules also cover proceedings relating to penalties under Section 182 and default surcharge under Section 205 of the Income Tax Ordinance.

Separation of duties, and everything electronic

The rules require that audit, assessment and quality-control responsibilities for the same case and tax year be handled by separate officers or units. FBR says this functional segregation is designed to strengthen transparency and procedural oversight within the faceless system, so that no single officer controls a case from audit to final order. Notices, orders and other official communications must be generated through the FBR's computerised system and carry a Digital Identification Number (DIN). Taxpayers and their authorised representatives will be required to submit replies, supporting documents and evidence electronically through their designated IRIS accounts. Audit reports, adjudication notices and assessment orders will undergo quality-control reviews under the prescribed procedures.

What has not changed — and what to watch

The framework also allows physical verification through the Field Operations Wing where necessary, subject to authorisation, and cases may be transferred to another tax authority in specified circumstances requiring physical records, repeated verification or special handling. One caution is worth stating plainly: the notifications bring the chapters into force, but as of mid-October reporting, no published order had yet listed which specific cases are assigned to the centre, and no transition rule for audits already open had been found. For taxpayers, the practical step is unchanged in spirit but new in form: keep returns and wealth statements reconciled with FBR's own data, and be ready to respond through IRIS rather than in person. Whether faceless audits deliver on the promise of fairness will be judged by the first cases that go through them.
