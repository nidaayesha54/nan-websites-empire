PSX in October: KSE-100 Fell 3,560 Points as War Fears and a Rate Hike Weighed

November 2, 2026

The KSE-100 Index closed October 2026 on a weak note, shedding 3,560.08 points, or 2.09 percent, to settle at 166,409.25, as an escalating Middle East conflict kept energy prices elevated and pushed the State Bank of Pakistan into tightening policy in the final week of the month, Mettis Global reported on November 2.

Five weekly losses, then a bounce

The index opened October around 169,969, its September close, and drifted lower for most of four weeks, logging five consecutive weekly losses before a late-month bounce that proved too shallow. October was the market's third losing month out of four in fiscal 2026-27, taking the fiscal-year-to-date loss to 13,892.45 points, or 7.71 percent, from the June close of 180,301.70. Market capitalisation fell to Rs4.66 trillion from Rs4.78 trillion. Foreign investors remained net sellers in October, offloading equities worth $71.26 million.

What drove the fall — and what held up

Even a staff-level agreement with the IMF early in October — paving the way for $1 billion plus $210 million in disbursements, subject to board approval — could not lift the mood for long. The State Bank raised the policy rate by 50 basis points to 12 percent on October 26, citing higher energy prices and geopolitical uncertainty. Beneath the headline, KSE-100 company earnings rose 26 percent year over year in the first nine months of 2026 to Rs1,638 billion, according to Arif Habib Limited — a reminder, analysts said, that October's fall was driven mostly by worry, not by collapsing profits.

Sources: Mettis Global, Shafaqna, Arif Habib Limited, November 2, 2026
