State Firms Lose Rs342.8 Billion in First Half of FY2026 as Privatisation Push Continues

November 19, 2026

Pakistan's loss-making state-owned enterprises recorded Rs342.8 billion ($1.23 billion) in losses during the first half of the current fiscal year, the prime minister's privatisation adviser said on Thursday, defending plans to transfer government businesses to private investors, Arab News reported on November 19.

Support to state firms surges 31 percent

Speaking at the sixth Pakistan Prosperity Forum, Adviser to the Prime Minister on Privatisation Muhammad Ali said government financial support to state enterprises had risen 31 percent to a record Rs804 billion ($2.88 billion), highlighting the strain poorly performing businesses placed on public resources. He outlined clarity, cooperation and consistency as the principles to drive sustainable economic transformation and attract private investment. Official finance ministry figures show that while loss-making state enterprises recorded Rs342.8 billion in losses, profitable enterprises generated Rs423.3 billion ($1.52 billion) in earnings over the same period, and the government received Rs839.8 billion from state enterprises against Rs804 billion in support, resulting in a positive net fiscal flow of Rs35.8 billion.

PIA and power distribution sales

Pakistan is pursuing its privatisation programme under its $7 billion International Monetary Fund bailout, seeking to reduce the financial burden of state enterprises, improve services and attract investment. The government has already transferred management control of Pakistan International Airlines (PIA) to a consortium led by Arif Habib Corporation, with the initial closing in June 2026 injecting Rs80 billion in new equity alongside Rs10 billion in sale proceeds, and a second closing in June 2027 set to bring a further Rs90 billion. The government is also preparing to sell three electricity distribution companies — IESCO, GEPCO and FESCO — under Batch-1 of the power distribution privatisation. Only a day earlier, the Public Private Partnership Authority board approved construction of the first two sections of the M-6 Motorway (Hyderabad-Sukkur) on a public-private partnership basis at an estimated investment of $672 million under a 25-year concession model.
