Gold Prices Rise by Rs1,400 per Tola in Pakistan on Monday

October 26, 2026

Gold prices edged higher in Pakistan on Monday, October 26, with the price of 24-karat gold increasing by Rs1,400 per tola to Rs423,436, according to market reports from Karachi. The rise continues a pattern in which local gold prices closely track international bullion movements and shifts in the rupee, making the yellow metal one of the most closely watched prices in Pakistani households and bazaars.

Why gold matters in Pakistan

Gold holds a special place in Pakistan's economy and culture. It is bought for weddings, gifted at family occasions and held by many households as a store of value that can be sold quickly in an emergency. Jewellers in Karachi, Lahore and other trading centres adjust their rates daily, and even a modest move of Rs1,400 per tola is noticed immediately by buyers and sellers. For families planning weddings, the timing of a purchase can change the final bill by thousands of rupees, which is why daily price updates draw such a wide audience.

What drives the local price

Local gold prices are shaped mainly by two forces: the international price of gold in dollars and the value of the rupee against the dollar. When global prices rise, or the rupee weakens, the rupee price of gold in Pakistan tends to climb even if nothing has changed in local demand. Regional tensions and uncertainty can also push investors towards gold as a safe haven, adding further upward pressure. Monday's increase should therefore be read as part of that wider picture rather than as a purely local event, although local demand ahead of the wedding season can amplify moves in either direction.

What buyers should keep in mind

For buyers, the practical lesson is familiar: prices move daily, and small daily changes add up over weeks. Jewellers advise checking the day's official rate before buying and confirming the purity and making charges separately, since the headline per-tola rate is only part of the final price. For investors, gold's appeal lies in its long record as a hedge, but it pays no interest or dividend, and its price can fall as well as rise. Monday's rise is a single day's move, not a forecast, and anyone buying gold as an investment should weigh it against other options rather than chasing one day's headline.

The wider market picture

The gold rise comes on a day when other economic signals were mixed, with business confidence reported to have softened in October even as manufacturing capacity use improved. Taken together, the picture is of an economy still finding its footing: households and firms hedging with gold while waiting for clearer signals on growth, inflation and interest rates. Gold's next move will likely follow global prices and the rupee, and traders will be watching both closely through the week.
