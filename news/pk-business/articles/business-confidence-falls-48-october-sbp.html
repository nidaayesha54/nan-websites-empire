Business Confidence Falls to 48 in October, State Bank Survey Shows

October 26, 2026

Overall business confidence weakened further in October 2026, with the Business Confidence Index falling 1.1 points to 48, led by a decline in services sector sentiment, the State Bank of Pakistan's latest survey showed. The survey, conducted by the State Bank in collaboration with the Institute of Business Administration, ran from October 1 to 14, 2026, and covered 491 respondents.

Services drag, industry steadies

The Services sector index slipped 1.6 points to 47.7, while the Industry sector index edged up 0.2 points to 48.7. The Current Business Confidence Index, which reflects views on general economic conditions over the past six months, eased 1.1 points to 45.4. The Expected Business Confidence Index, which captures the outlook for the next six months, fell 1.2 points to 50.5, sitting just above the neutral threshold. A reading below 50 generally signals that more businesses are pessimistic than optimistic, so the overall figure of 48 points to a cautiously negative mood, even though expectations for the future remain marginally positive.

Purchasing managers and inflation

The Purchasing Managers Index also eased 1.4 points to 51.8, though it stayed above the neutral threshold, suggesting that overall business activity was still expanding, if more slowly. Order books rose to 51.9, the one component to improve, while business activity fell to 50.3, total employees to 48.7, raw material purchases to 55 and speed of suppliers' delivery to 53.2. Aggregate inflation expectations eased to 7.1 percent from 7.7 percent in the previous wave, even though the share of businesses expecting higher prices edged up to 70 percent from 69.8 percent. In industry, that share rose to 71.4 percent from 69.5 percent, while in services it fell to 68.9 percent from 70.1 percent.

Jobs and capacity

Employment sentiment weakened. The Current Employment Index fell to 45.4 from 48.9 in September, while the Expected Employment Index eased to 50.4 from 53.1. There was one brighter spot: in manufacturing, average current capacity utilisation rose to 67.9 percent from 64.8 percent in the previous wave, suggesting factories are using more of their installed capacity even as hiring sentiment cools. That combination, busier plants but weaker hiring, is consistent with firms meeting existing orders cautiously while holding back on new recruitment until demand looks more secure.

What it means

For policymakers, the survey paints a mixed picture rather than a collapse. Activity is still expanding according to the purchasing managers' measure, inflation expectations are moderating, and factories are busier. But confidence, especially in services, is slipping, and employment sentiment is softening. The next survey round will show whether October's dip was a pause or the start of a longer slide, and businesses will be watching interest rates, energy costs and demand conditions for the signal that tips sentiment back above 50.
