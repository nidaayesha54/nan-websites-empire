Pakistan's Exports Rise 7.7% to $8.5 Billion but Trade Gap Widens in First Quarter

October 30, 2026

Pakistan's merchandise exports rose 7.7% to $8.5 billion during July–September, the first quarter of fiscal year 2026-27, compared with $7.9 billion in the corresponding period last year, according to figures shared by Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal and the Finance Division's October Economic Update and Outlook.

Imports grew almost twice as fast

The country's imports of goods rose 14.3% to $17.8 billion in the quarter, while combined imports of goods and services increased 14.6% to $21.3 billion. The result was a quarterly trade deficit of $9.3 billion — larger than the entire quarter's exports. September was stronger on both sides: exports grew 15.5% to $3 billion, while imports grew 22.7% to $6.2 billion. Oil explains much of the import side, with Brent crude averaging $116.8 a barrel in September, up 28.4% from August, and petroleum accounting for about a quarter of total imports in fiscal 2025.

Remittances and reserves cushion the gap

Workers' remittances increased 14% to $10.9 billion in the quarter, compared with $9.5 billion a year earlier, while foreign direct investment rose by nearly 20% to approximately $720 million. By October 16, the State Bank's liquid reserves had reached $21.5 billion, with total liquid foreign reserves at $26.8 billion. The widening trade gap contributed to a current account deficit of $874 million, compared with $755 million in the same period last year.

Inflation and interest rates

Consumer inflation stood at 10.3% in September, down from 11.1% in August but above the 5.8% recorded in September last year. Transport prices increased 27.4% year-on-year, while housing, water, electricity, gas and fuel costs rose 12.4%. The State Bank raised its policy rate by 0.5 percentage points to 12%, effective October 27, 2026, and the Finance Division expects inflation to remain between 9.5% and 10.5% in October.

The government expects around 4% growth this fiscal year, after 3.7% growth in fiscal 2025-26, as it attempts to avoid past cycles in which consumption-driven expansion increased imports and pressured foreign exchange reserves.

Sources: Arab News, Finance Division October Economic Update and Outlook, and The Pakistan Daily, October 30, 2026.
