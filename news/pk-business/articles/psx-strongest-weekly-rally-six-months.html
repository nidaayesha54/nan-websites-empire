PSX Stages Strongest Weekly Rally in Six Months as KSE-100 Jumps 7,477 Points

November 8, 2026

The Pakistan Stock Exchange (PSX) staged a strong recovery in the first week of November after October's bearish spell, with the benchmark KSE-100 index gaining 4.5 per cent as falling international oil prices, easing inflation and hopes of reduced geopolitical tensions lifted investor sentiment, Dawn reported on November 8. The index rose 7,477 points to close at 173,887 points on Friday from 166,409 points in the previous week, according to Arif Habib Ltd (AHL). The Express Tribune described it as the market's strongest weekly rally in nearly six months.

What drove the rally

AKD Securities Ltd attributed the rally to a 15.9 per cent weekly decline in Brent crude prices to $98.8 per barrel, following diplomatic efforts to ease tensions in the Middle East and the outcome of the US midterm elections. AHL said positive sentiment after the Democrats' midterm election victory in the US, which led to a sharp decline in oil prices on November 4, supported the market. On the macroeconomic front, inflation, measured by the Consumer Price Index (CPI), slowed to 9.61 per cent year-on-year in October, down from 10.26 per cent in September, although it remained significantly higher than the 6.25 per cent recorded in October last year. Average inflation during the first four months of FY27 stood at 10.1 per cent, compared with 4.7 per cent in the corresponding period last year.

Reserves, rupee and fuel

The State Bank of Pakistan's foreign exchange reserves increased by $40 million week-on-week to $21.6 billion as of October 30, providing import cover of approximately three months. The rupee also edged up 0.03 per cent against the dollar to close at Rs276.66. The government raised around Rs386–400 billion through its latest Pakistan Investment Bonds auction against a target of Rs350 billion. Domestic petroleum prices provided further relief, with petrol prices falling 6.7 per cent to Rs352.59 per litre and high-speed diesel declining 4.6 per cent to Rs382.25 per litre, following a reduction in ex-refinery prices, Dawn reported. Pakistan's trade deficit widened by 20 per cent year-on-year to $3.9 billion in October, according to the Pakistan Bureau of Statistics, with exports rising 2.8 per cent to $2.9 billion and imports up 11.8 per cent to $6.8 billion. Analysts expect market direction to remain sensitive to US-Iran talks and oil prices, while the MSCI review scheduled for November 11 will also be key.

Sources: Dawn, November 8, 2026; The Express Tribune, November 8, 2026
