PSX Recovers 155 Points to Close at 166,564 as Oil Prices Ease

November 2, 2026

The Pakistan Stock Exchange staged a late recovery on Monday to close marginally higher, reversing an earlier decline as investors weighed hopes of diplomatic progress in the Middle East against persistent selling pressure, The Express Tribune reported on November 2.

A volatile session

The benchmark KSE-100 index climbed 154.81 points, or 0.09 percent, to settle at 166,564.07 points, after remaining under pressure for much of the session and falling to 165,776.85 at 12:29 p.m., down 632.40 points from the previous close of 166,409.25. Buying was led by Mari Energies (up 3.15 percent), Fauji Fertiliser (up 0.92 percent) and Engro Holdings (up 1.57 percent), while Attock Refinery (down 10 percent), Oil & Gas Development Company (down 1.66 percent) and Pakistan State Oil (down 2.33 percent) were the biggest drags. All refineries traded down to lower limits after the government shifted higher petrol import costs onto local refineries in the October 31 pricing notification, leaving the ex-depot price unchanged at Rs377.91 a litre.

Why sentiment turned

The positive close came amid a more than 4 percent decline in global oil prices, as Iranian and Qatari officials held a third consecutive day of talks aimed at ending the US-Israeli war on Iran, easing concerns over energy costs and Pakistan's import bill. Trading volume fell to 443.4 million shares from 825.4 million on Friday. October CPI rose 9.6 percent year over year, still above the State Bank's 5-7 percent target range, with resistance at 167,500-168,000 the key level to watch, according to Arif Habib Limited.

Sources: The Express Tribune, Business Recorder, Arif Habib Limited, November 2, 2026
