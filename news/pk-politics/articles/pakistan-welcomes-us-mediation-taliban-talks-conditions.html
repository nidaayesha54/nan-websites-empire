Pakistan Welcomes US Mediation Offer on Taliban Talks — With Conditions

November 2, 2026

Pakistan's Foreign Office said on Sunday, November 1, that it would welcome US efforts to facilitate talks with Afghanistan, attaching a condition that Kabul take verifiable action against militant groups before meaningful negotiations can begin, Pakistan Today reported via KhabarWire on November 2.

The condition

'Pakistan would welcome sincere efforts by friendly countries, including the United States, to facilitate dialogue with Afghanistan,' Foreign Office spokesman Sajjad Haider Khan told Independent Urdu. He said any meaningful dialogue must rest on a 'clear commitment' by the Afghan Taliban authorities to take concrete, sustained and verifiable action against the Tehreek-e-Taliban Pakistan, the Balochistan Liberation Army and other groups. The response followed remarks by US Secretary of State Marco Rubio at the G20 foreign ministers' meeting in Atlanta on Saturday, October 31, that Washington had 'obviously heard' Pakistan's concerns about attacks 'sponsored from the territory of Afghanistan' and that 'if there's a productive role for us to play, we'll play it.'

What is still uncertain

No date, venue or format for talks has been agreed, and Washington has not said whether it will formally take on the role of facilitator, KhabarWire noted, adding that no full official transcript of the Foreign Office remarks has been published. The exchange follows the Taliban saying its final efforts at dialogue had failed and Pakistan's information ministry calling cross-border terrorism a red line. Pakistan's statement is a response to a US remark, not a joint announcement — and Rubio's offer was itself conditional.

Sources: KhabarWire, Pakistan Today, The Express Tribune, November 2, 2026
