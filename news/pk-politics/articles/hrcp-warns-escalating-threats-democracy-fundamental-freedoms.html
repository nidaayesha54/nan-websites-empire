HRCP Warns of Escalating Threats to Democracy and Fundamental Freedoms

November 16, 2026

The Human Rights Commission of Pakistan (HRCP), following the conclusion of its 40th Annual General Meeting (AGM), has raised concerns over what it described as an escalating assault on constitutional democracy and fundamental rights across the country, warning that freedom of expression, judicial independence and democratic institutions are facing growing threats.

Censorship, surveillance and restrictive laws

According to a press release issued by HRCP Chairperson Asad Iqbal Butt, the commission's General Body expressed alarm over the systematic erosion of freedom of expression through censorship, surveillance, restrictive regulations and the misuse of criminal laws. Highlighting the October 2026 sentencing of human rights lawyers Imaan Mazari-Hazir and Hadi Chattha to ten years in prison, HRCP described the verdicts as a chilling example of how individuals exercising their constitutional rights could be targeted.

Disappearances, Balochistan and federalism

The commission also expressed concern over continued enforced disappearances and the detention of activists, including Mahrang Baloch, Ali Wazir and Idris Khattak. Addressing the security situation in Balochistan, the General Body called for an open national debate on the province's challenges and urged the restoration of cross-border trade to safeguard local livelihoods. The press release also highlighted concerns over what HRCP described as the erosion of federalism and democratic representation in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, pointing to the imposition of an emergency and governor's rule, the detention of the province's elected chief executive and the mass arrests of Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf (PTI) workers in October 2026. It further criticised the Federal Constitutional Court's intervention restricting the Peshawar High Court's exercise of its constitutional jurisdiction, saying it undermined access to justice and judicial independence. On constitutional and judicial matters, HRCP said the 26th and 27th Constitutional Amendments had seriously weakened judicial independence and institutional checks and balances.

Sources: Indian Economic Observer, November 16, 2026
