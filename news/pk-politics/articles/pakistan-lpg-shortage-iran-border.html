Pakistan LPG Shortage Worsens as Iran Border Crossings Close

October 26, 2026

Pakistan is facing a worsening shortage of liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) after the closure of two key border crossings with Iran halted imports, traders said, driving up prices for households that depend on gas cylinders for cooking and heating. The Gabd-Rimdan and Taftan crossings were closed on October 17, cutting off a supply route that delivers a large share of Pakistan's daily LPG imports.

Why the closure matters so much

Pakistan imports about 7,000 metric tons of LPG a day from Iran, according to the Pakistan LPG Association. More than 4,000 tons a day enters through Gabd-Rimdan alone, said Shamsul Haq, former president of the Gwadar Chamber of Commerce and Industry. With both crossings shut, that flow stopped. "These imports are at halt," Haq said. The Balochistan government, in the province where the crossings are located, said the temporary closure was intended to address security concerns and curb smuggling, while Iran's foreign ministry cited technical issues. The two sides giving different reasons has left traders and transporters uncertain about when normal crossings will resume.

Prices jump and trucks pile up

Traders said the price of a 12kg LPG cylinder had risen to 7,000 rupees from 4,500 rupees within days, and warned it could climb further if the disruptions persist. For low-income households, that jump is a direct hit on the weekly budget, since a cylinder that lasted a household through its cooking needs suddenly costs more than half as much again. Hundreds of trucks, including LPG tankers, are stranded on both sides of the border, Haq said, and the Quetta Chamber of Commerce and Industry has advised members to stop placing new orders with Iran until further notice. Truck drivers stuck at the crossings said they had been waiting for days in harsh conditions with limited access to basic amenities. "We have been standing here for over a week waiting for the border to open," said Abdul Ghani Baloch, one affected driver.

A partial reopening, and a wider trade stake

There was some relief in the week: the Gabd-Rimdan crossing reopened for Pakistani exports of perishable goods, although more than 1,000 Pakistani containers carrying potatoes, bananas and rice for Iran were also reported stranded. Pakistan LPG Association chairman Irfan Khokhar warned shortages and price increases could worsen if imports do not resume soon. The episode also underlines how much ordinary trade rides on these crossings. Pakistan-Iran trade totalled about $2.8 billion in the 2023-24 fiscal year, including roughly $684 million of Pakistani exports, according to the Federation of Pakistan Chambers of Commerce and Industry. Until the crossings fully reopen, households, traders and transporters alike will be watching the border for the first sign of LPG tankers moving again.

Sources: Reuters, October 23, 2026; Pakistan LPG Association; Federation of Pakistan Chambers of Commerce and Industry.
