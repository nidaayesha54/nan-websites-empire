Pakistan Strongly Condemns Houthi Missile Attacks Near Makkah and Madinah

October 26, 2026

Pakistan on Monday strongly condemned ballistic missile attacks reported on October 24 that targeted Saudi Arabia's Makkah and Madinah regions, including areas near Islam's holiest sites. The Foreign Office said the attacks were reprehensible and constituted a grave violation of Saudi Arabia's sovereignty and territorial integrity.

What the Foreign Office said

Foreign Office spokesperson Sajjad Haider Khan said the attacks demonstrated a blatant disregard for the sanctity of Islam's holiest sites and for the safety of civilians. In a statement shared by the ministry, Pakistan reiterated what it described as its unwavering solidarity with the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia and its firm commitment to the Makkah Alliance. The statement also reaffirmed Pakistan's support for the security, stability and defence of Saudi Arabia, including the protection of the Two Holy Mosques, against any external aggression or threat.

Interceptions reported on October 24

Reports on October 24 said Saudi Arabia's Civil Defence briefly issued alerts warning of potential danger in Taif and Yanbu, as well as in Makkah and Jeddah, before later saying the threat had passed. A Saudi-led coalition fighting Yemen's Houthis said it destroyed two ballistic missiles fired towards Taif and Yanbu. Dawn reported that debris from intercepted missiles fell in the Makkah and Madinah regions, including Wadi al-Mughammas in Makkah and areas of Jeddah, Taif and Rabigh. The Houthis subsequently denied targeting Makkah and Madinah, saying their operations were directed at Saudi oil facilities and military bases, according to Dawn.

Why the condemnation matters

The condemnation comes at a moment of heightened regional tension, with fighting between the Saudi-led coalition and the Houthis having shattered a United Nations-brokered truce reached in 2022 that had largely halted cross-border attacks. Pakistan also commended what it called the vigilance and efficiency of Saudi air defence forces in countering the attacks. Earlier in October, the Foreign Office had separately condemned an attack on a power distribution station in Madinah on September 29, describing an attack on civilian infrastructure supplying electricity to the Prophet's Mosque as a grave provocation. Monday's statement fits a consistent pattern: Islamabad publicly aligning itself with Riyadh's security whenever holy sites or Saudi territory are reported to be under threat, while stopping short of announcing any new military step of its own.

What happens next

For now, the statement is diplomatic rather than operational. It signals solidarity and reaffirms existing defence commitments, but it does not announce fresh deployments or new agreements. Attention now turns to whether regional diplomacy, including reported talks involving Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkiye, can lower tensions before further attacks are reported. Readers should treat claims about the targets and the scale of any attack as reported by the respective sides, since the two sides have given different accounts of what was struck.

Sources: The Express Tribune, October 26, 2026; Dunya News, October 26, 2026; Dawn, October 26, 2026.
