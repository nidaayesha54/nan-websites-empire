Pakistan Tests Indigenous Fa'az Air-to-Air Missile, Its First Locally Developed Weapon of Its Kind

November 4, 2026

Pakistan has successfully tested its Fa'az air-to-air missile, its first locally developed air-to-air missile, in a push to strengthen its indigenous missile capabilities, PTI reported on November 4, citing an armed forces statement.

What was announced

The flight test was conducted on November 3. The missile is equipped with an advanced terminal guidance system and is designed to engage hostile aircraft at longer ranges, the statement said. The armed forces said the new weapon system would significantly expand the operational flexibility of the Pakistan Air Force. The statement did not disclose the missile's operational range, speed, payload specifications or other technical details, and those figures have not been stated here.

Reactions and recent tests

President Asif Ali Zardari, Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif and Army Chief Asim Munir congratulated the scientists and engineers, recognising their contribution to the indigenous weapon system, reports said. The Fa'az test comes amid a series of missile development and testing initiatives in recent months, including the January 2026 test of the indigenously developed Taimoor air-launched cruise missile, which has a reported range of 600 km and is designed to engage land and sea targets.

Sources: PTI via Economic Times, November 4, 2026
