MQM-P Postpones November 29 Rally, Renews Demand for Smaller Administrative Units

November 28, 2026

Muttahida Qaumi Movement-Pakistan (MQM-P) leader Dr Farooq Sattar on Saturday announced the postponement of the party's November 29 rally in Karachi, saying the city commissioner had refused permission citing security threats, The Express Tribune reported on November 28.

Public safety over politics, party says

Addressing a press conference in Karachi alongside Health Minister Mustafa Kamal and others, Sattar called the Sindh government's decision extremely discriminatory and unjust, arguing that if there were security concerns, rallies of all political parties should have been treated the same way. Kamal said the party had decided to postpone the gathering because human life and public safety were more important than holding a political event, and Sattar said a new date for the rally would be announced in December. The PPP had earlier said Sindhi nationalist leader Dr Qadir Magsi had announced his own November 29 rally nearly a month ago and had requested MQM-P not to hold its event on the same day, warning that two simultaneous gatherings could lead to tensions or clashes in Karachi.

Renewed call for new administrative units

Sattar said the controversy over the rally was part of a wider issue concerning the creation of new provinces, adding that the party had suggested seeking public opinion on the issue through a referendum under Article 48(6) of the Constitution. He proposed new administrative units carved out of Sindh for Karachi, Larkana and Nawabshah. The current debate over new provinces began on July 30, when Interior Minister Mohsin Naqvi called for additional administrative units or provinces as part of a broader overhaul of the country's governance system, a proposal the PPP has strongly opposed when it comes to dividing Sindh.

Sources: The Express Tribune, November 28, 2026; Dunya News, November 28, 2026
