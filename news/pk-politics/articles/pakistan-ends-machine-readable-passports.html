Pakistan Ends Machine-Readable Passports and Switches Fully to E-Passports

October 26, 2026

Pakistan has discontinued the issuance of traditional machine-readable passports (MRPs), with all passport offices across the country and Pakistani diplomatic missions abroad switching exclusively to e-passports from October 23, 2026. According to the Directorate General of Immigration and Passports, the move marks the end of the 22-year-old machine-readable passport system as the country completes its transition to electronic passports.

What changes for applicants

Anyone applying for a new passport, or renewing an expired one, will now receive an e-passport. The directorate said all passport offices in Pakistan and diplomatic missions abroad were now issuing only e-passports, completing the transition to the upgraded passport system. The change follows a statement by the interior ministry in the National Assembly, when it informed lawmakers that the issuance of machine-readable passports would be discontinued within a month. Responding to a question raised by MNA Malik Shakir Bashir Awan during the question-and-answer session, the ministry had said that passport offices and Pakistani missions abroad would subsequently issue only e-passports.

What stays the same for existing holders

Citizens holding valid machine-readable passports will be allowed to continue using them until their expiry dates, and do not need to replace a valid document simply because the system has changed. Once expired, these passports will not be eligible for renewal under the previous system, and holders will be required to obtain e-passports instead. That distinction matters for the many Pakistanis living abroad, who often plan travel around passport validity: a valid MRP remains a valid travel document until the date printed in it, but the next document in hand will be an electronic one.

A transition four years in the making

Pakistan introduced e-passports in March 2022, initially making the facility available to diplomats and residents of Islamabad. In August 2023, the facility was extended to all Pakistani citizens, who were given the option of obtaining either a machine-readable passport or an e-passport. Before the introduction of machine-readable passports, Pakistan issued handwritten passports, a practice dating back to the country's independence in 1947. The e-passport carries an electronic chip storing the holder's personal and biometric information, which authorities say strengthens travel-document security and makes identity verification more effective in line with international standards. For applicants, the practical advice is simple: check the expiry date on your current passport, and when renewal time comes, expect the new electronic format. Applicants should also budget for higher fees reported for the new document: Pakistan Observer reported the normal fee for a 36-page e-passport valid for five years at Rs9,000, against Rs4,500 for the machine-readable equivalent, with urgent and fast-track e-passport fees reported at Rs15,000 and Rs22,000 respectively.
