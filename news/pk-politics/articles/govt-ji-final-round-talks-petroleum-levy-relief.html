Government, JI Hold Final Round of Talks as Party Demands End to Petroleum Levy

November 10, 2026

The government and Jamaat-e-Islami (JI) held the final round of their negotiations on the petroleum levy in Islamabad on Tuesday, with the party demanding the immediate abolition of the levy and expanded relief for the public, 24NewsHD reported on November 10.

Tenth round of talks

JI Deputy Emir Liaqat Baloch said the latest meeting was the tenth round of negotiations with the government committee, and that it was particularly important for securing relief for the public. The meeting was held in a committee room of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs under the chairmanship of Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Ishaq Dar. Baloch said the government had acknowledged that the demand to abolish the petroleum levy was legitimate, and had promised to present a future course of action on the issue. JI has proposed extending the relief scheme to vehicles with engines of up to 1,000cc and to the agricultural sector, and Baloch said both sides had agreed on the need to expand existing relief measures.

Rs220 billion collected in two months

The talks come as official figures show the federal government collected Rs220 billion through the petroleum levy in July and August of fiscal year 2026-27, Profit by Pakistan Today reported. The levy is currently Rs80 per litre on both petrol and diesel. Two other fuel-related levies added Rs27.5 billion from the climate support levy and Rs7.225 billion from the new energy vehicle adoption levy, taking total fuel-levy collections to Rs254.7 billion in the first two months of the year. The government has said its budget framework, agreed with the International Monetary Fund under the $7 billion Extended Fund Facility, leaves little fiscal space to cut the levy at this stage, and it remains unclear how it intends to meet JI's demand.

Sources: 24NewsHD, November 10, 2026; Profit by Pakistan Today, November 10, 2026
