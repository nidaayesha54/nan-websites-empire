'Ghost School' Opens in Pakistan Cinemas on October 30 After Censor Court Fight

October 27, 2026

Ghost School, the award-winning Pakistani film by British-Pakistani filmmaker Seemab Gul, will release in cinemas across Pakistan on Friday, October 30, 2026. The film is Gul's first feature; she also wrote and produced it. No streaming deal has been announced, with the film opening as a cinema release.

Despite its title and talk of a jinn, Ghost School is not a horror film. It is a drama about a young girl trying to get her school reopened, with only a touch of the supernatural. Nazualiya Arsalan plays Rabia, the young lead, while Samina Seher plays her mother.

What is a 'ghost school'?

In Pakistan, a ghost school is a government school that exists on paper, with a building, a budget and sometimes salaried staff, but which does not actually teach any children. The film uses that reality as the backdrop for Rabia's fight to restore schooling in her village after the school is shut over rumours of a jinn.

The Sindh censor battle

The film's road to release ran through the courts in Sindh. The provincial censor board first called the film not suitable for public exhibition, then demanded eight cuts. The Sindh High Court overturned all but one of those cuts on October 19, and screenings in Sindh now depend on a fresh decision by the provincial censor board following the court's order.

For Pakistani cinema, Ghost School arrives as another example of a film that made its name abroad first: like Hanging by a Wire, Pakistan's Oscar entry this awards cycle, it built its reputation on the festival circuit before reaching home audiences. Its October 30 opening will test whether a small, story-driven film can find a mainstream cinema audience.

The October 30 release also gives audiences a chance to judge the film on its own terms after weeks of coverage focused on the certification dispute. Festival favourites often struggle to convert critical attention into cinema admissions, and Ghost School's opening weekend will show whether word of mouth around its story of a girl fighting for her village school can carry it beyond the headlines.
