Punjab Plans Stricter Laws for Theatre and Stage Dramas, Replacing 1876 Act

October 30, 2026

The Punjab government has decided to introduce stricter regulations for stage dramas and theatre performances across the province, proposing a comprehensive legal framework to monitor and regulate theatrical activities, according to Dunya News. The proposed Punjab Dramatic and Theatrical Performances Bill 2026 has surfaced and was presented during the ongoing session before being referred to the relevant committee for consideration.

What the proposed law would require

The proposed legislation aims to repeal the nearly 150-year-old Dramatic Performances Act 1876 and replace it with a modern regulatory and administrative system. Under the proposed law, obtaining a licence from the relevant secretary will be mandatory for staging dramas or theatrical performances at any public venue in Punjab. The proposed licence fee is Rs25,000, while scripts will require prior approval at a proposed fee of Rs35,000. Script Review Committees will be established to examine theatrical scripts and will be notified by the Secretary of the Information and Culture Department.

Restrictions and penalties

The proposed law seeks to prohibit performances deemed obscene, immoral, insulting or objectionable, as well as those that hurt religious sentiments. Under the proposed legislation, violations could carry a punishment of up to six months' imprisonment and a fine of up to Rs1 million.

What happens next

Once approved and enforced, the bill would replace the Dramatic Performances Act 1876, introducing a significantly stricter regulatory framework for theatre and stage productions across Punjab. Because the bill has only been presented and referred to committee so far, its final shape — including the fees and penalties — could still change during the legislative process, and theatre practitioners will be watching the committee stage closely.

The proposal marks one of the most significant overhauls of Punjab's theatre regulation in nearly a century and a half.

Sources: Dunya News, October 30, 2026.
