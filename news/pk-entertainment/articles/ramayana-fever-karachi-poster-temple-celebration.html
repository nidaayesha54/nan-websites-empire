Ramayana Buzz Reaches Karachi as Fans Display Film Poster at Temple

October 29, 2026

Excitement around Nitesh Tiwari's upcoming film Ramayana has reportedly travelled beyond India's borders, with a video circulating on social media showing members of the Hindu community in Karachi celebrating the film and displaying its poster at a temple, according to reports published on October 29, 2026.

What the circulating video reportedly shows

According to the circulating account, a local YouTuber visited members of Karachi's Hindu community and recorded their reactions to the upcoming film. The video reportedly shows people putting up a Ramayana poster at a temple, beating drums, singing devotional songs and expressing their excitement about the big-screen adaptation. Several participants are said to have conveyed their best wishes to the actors and director, while some expressed hopes that the film would receive recognition at the Oscars. The original footage and its location have not yet been independently verified, so the celebration is described here as reported rather than confirmed.

The film behind the buzz

Ramayana, directed by Nitesh Tiwari, stars Ranbir Kapoor, Sai Pallavi and Yash, alongside Sunny Deol and Ravi Dubey, among others. A premiere of Ramayana Part 1 was reported to have been held in Los Angeles on October 27, 2026, with early reviews from the screening described as quite positive. The film is reported to be scheduled for release on November 5, with a second part expected the following Diwali.

Why the Karachi reaction caught attention

The reported celebration drew attention because it showed enthusiasm for an Indian epic adaptation among a community in Pakistan, blending film hype with religious devotion to Lord Rama. Participants in the video were reported to have said they would watch the film and wished the cast success, with one temple priest extending best wishes on behalf of Pakistani Hindus.

Whether or not the film releases in Pakistan itself remains a separate question, and the reports note that some participants appealed for a local release.
