World Theatre Comes to Lahore as Punjab MAAS Festival Runs to October 28

October 26, 2026

The Lahore Arts Council Alhamra is hosting the Third Punjab MAAS International Theatre Festival 2026 from October 23 to 28, bringing together theatre artists from Pakistan, Kazakhstan, Indonesia, Norway and France to showcase diverse theatrical traditions and promote cultural exchange. The six-day festival features local and international performances, creative workshops and panel discussions exploring artistic freedom, digital culture, social transformation and the role of theatre in engaging younger generations.

A meeting point for world theatre

The MAAS Foundation, a founding member of ASSITEJ Pakistan, and Alhamra describe the festival as a celebration bringing theatre, cultures, languages and people together on one stage. Organisers say that this year Lahore becomes a meeting point for theatre artists from Pakistan, Kazakhstan, Bangladesh, Indonesia, Norway and France, bringing diverse stories, artistic traditions and powerful performances to Pakistani audiences. The programme includes performances for children and adults, theatre workshops for children and teenagers, and panel discussions focusing on inclusive theatre and productions for young audiences. From different corners of the world, the organisers say, artists are coming together with one shared belief: theatre has no borders.

Highlights of the programme

The international programme began on October 24 with the University of the Punjab's production of 'Ak Boota Ambi Da', followed by 'Gangster Hunting with Daisy Detective' by Norway's ERLER Teater Kompani. On October 25, a French theatre group presented 'Zig Zag', alongside a panel discussion examining the evolving relevance of theatre in an age dominated by social media. Kazakhstan's Ilyas Omarov Kostanay Regional Kazakh Drama Theatre was scheduled to stage 'Sholpan's Sin' on October 26, while a separate discussion focused on the importance of inclusive and accessible theatre. The festival runs until October 28, giving Lahore audiences a full week of international work.

Why a theatre festival matters now

In an age of short videos and streaming, a live theatre festival makes a different case for attention: a shared room, a live performance and an audience reacting together. The festival's panels on digital culture and artistic freedom speak directly to that tension, asking what live performance can do that a screen cannot. For young audiences, the workshops for children and teenagers are perhaps the most important part of the programme, offering a first taste of making theatre rather than only watching it.

Lahore as a cultural stage

For Lahore, a city with a deep theatre tradition, hosting artists from Central Asia, Europe and South East Asia reinforces its role as a cultural meeting point. The MAAS festival does not just import performances; it creates conversations between Pakistani artists and their international counterparts, conversations that can lead to future collaborations long after the final curtain on October 28. For audiences still deciding whether to attend, the remaining days offer a rare chance to see world theatre without leaving the city.
