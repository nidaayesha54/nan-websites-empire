Pakistani Film 'Wakhri' Selected for UK's South Asian Film Season

October 26, 2026

Pakistani film 'Wakhri', inspired by the tragic story of social media personality Qandeel Baloch, has been selected for Channel 4 and Film4's South Asian Film Season 2026 in the UK, where it will be showcased alongside a selection of contemporary films from across South Asia. Directed by Iram Parveen Bilal, the film follows Noor Malik, a widowed schoolteacher who becomes an overnight social media sensation after sharing her unfiltered views about life as a woman in modern-day Pakistan.

A story beyond one person

Although the film draws inspiration from Qandeel Baloch's story, its themes extend beyond one individual or country. 'Wakhri' explores questions surrounding women's voices, social expectations, gender inequality, online identity and the pressures that come with sudden visibility on social media. That broader framing is central to the film's appeal: Noor's journey from a classroom to a phone screen, and from private frustration to public scrutiny, mirrors experiences familiar to many women navigating online spaces in South Asia and beyond.

A festival journey to a UK broadcaster

The film previously travelled to several international film festivals, including the Red Sea International Film Festival, Locarno, Cannes and Busan, before its selection for the UK season. This year's South Asian Film Season features nine films from India, Pakistan and Bangladesh, spanning drama, thriller and social realism and exploring themes including class, racial, political and gender divides. The season was curated by Indian cinema expert and longtime season consultant Nasreen Munni Kabir. 'Wakhri' was scheduled for its UK broadcast on October 5, followed by online availability, according to reports.

Producer calls it a major validation

Speaking to The Express Tribune, 'Wakhri' producer Abid Merchant described the film's inclusion in the season as an important recognition. He called it a huge validation, saying the film is about a widowed teacher who raises her voice on social media, and that while it is inspired by a tragic story, its message is universal. He added that the film's journey from international film festivals to a major UK broadcaster represented an important milestone for the project.

Why the selection matters

For Pakistani cinema, selection for a curated UK season on a major broadcaster is a meaningful marker of reach. Festival acclaim does not always translate into audiences, but a broadcast slot puts the film in front of viewers who might never attend a film festival. For 'Wakhri', whose subject is the cost of speaking out, that wider audience is part of the point: a story about a woman finding her voice online now gets to find its own audience on television and streaming, far beyond the festival circuit where it began.

Sources: The Express Tribune, October 8, 2026; The AZB, October 8, 2026.
