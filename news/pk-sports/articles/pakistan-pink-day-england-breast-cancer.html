Pakistan's Pink Day: Green Shirts Wear Pink Against England for Breast Cancer Awareness

October 26, 2026

Pakistan players donned pink jerseys in the ODI tri-series fixture against England at Gaddafi Stadium, Lahore, on Sunday, October 25, as the Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB) dedicated the match to breast cancer awareness. Pakistan's familiar green kit made way for a striking shade of pink, but there was a meaningful reason behind the team's change of colours — and the gesture extended far beyond the shirts.

A stadium turns pink

The Pakistan Cricket Board has organised Breast Cancer Awareness Day as part of its corporate social responsibility initiatives, during October, which is internationally observed as Breast Cancer Awareness Month. This Pink Day fixture between Pakistan and England serves as a medium to highlight the importance of early detection and adequate treatment. The PCB partnered with Pink Ribbon Pakistan, and the campaign was not limited to the team's playing kit: the stumps used for the game were also pink, the big screens and LED boards displayed awareness messages throughout the day, and players from both sides, match officials and coaching staff wore pink ribbons during the game. Educational segments and survivor stories were also featured on the stadium's big screens and during the broadcast.

Why cricket is the platform

"The Pakistan Cricket Board recognises the responsibility that comes with the immense following and influence of cricket in our country," said the PCB COO Sumair Ahmad Syed. "Through this Pink Ribbon initiative, we want to use the platform of international cricket to raise awareness about breast cancer and highlight the importance of early detection and timely medical attention." The logic is straightforward: a high-profile international fixture reaches an audience that health messaging alone rarely does, and it reaches families watching together. By dedicating an international fixture to the cause, the board hopes to reach a wider audience and highlight the importance of seeking medical guidance and undergoing appropriate screening.

Screenings, not just symbolism

Unlike purely symbolic initiatives, the PCB partnered with the Pink Ribbon Breast Cancer Trust Hospital to provide tangible healthcare resources: free mammogram screenings were offered to female spectators and local residents between October 24 and 26. The board also introduced pink-themed limited edition merchandise, with proceeds going towards the treatment of those who cannot afford it, and held an auction of autographed jerseys from Pakistan stars Shaheen Afridi and Babar Azam, with the proceeds going towards the same cause. This is not the first time Pakistan have worn pink jerseys to support breast cancer awareness — in October 2025, the team wore a special pink kit during a T20I against South Africa in Rawalpindi — but bringing the initiative to an ODI fixture, on the same day as a famous one-wicket win, gave Pink Day its biggest stage yet.
