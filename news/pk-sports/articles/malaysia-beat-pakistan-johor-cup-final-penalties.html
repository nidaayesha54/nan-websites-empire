Malaysia Edge Pakistan on Penalties to Win Sultan of Johor Cup

October 27, 2026

Malaysia staged a remarkable comeback to defeat Pakistan in a penalty shootout and win the Sultan of Johor Cup 2026 after an entertaining final ended 2-2 at the Taman Daya Hockey Stadium. The hosts held their nerve to triumph 4-3 in the shootout, denying Pakistan the title after recovering from a two-goal deficit in regulation time.

Pakistan appeared firmly in control after penalty-corner specialist Rana Waleed opened the scoring in the 19th minute. Usman Muhammad extended their advantage four minutes later, scoring a field goal to put Pakistan 2-0 ahead and leave Malaysia facing an uphill battle.

Malaysia fight back

The hosts responded quickly to reignite their hopes. Adam Asyraf Johari converted a penalty corner in the 25th minute, reducing the deficit to 2-1 and giving Malaysia renewed momentum. Malaysia continued to push forward after the break against a determined Pakistan defence, and their persistence paid off in the 49th minute when Dheeressh Gunaseelan scored from a penalty corner to bring the hosts level at 2-2.

Neither side could find a decisive goal during the remaining regulation time, forcing the final into a penalty shootout. Malaysia proved more clinical from the spot, converting four attempts through Johari, Gagandeep Singh, Rahuul Fazrien Muhammad Nazrien and Ashrafeel Iman Mohd Rizal. Pakistan scored three times, with Ali Hamza converting once and Waleed finding the net twice.

Road to the final

Pakistan's junior team had qualified for the final after holding Japan to a 2-2 draw in their final round-robin match at the same venue. Hamza Fayyaz and Ghulam Mustafa scored for Pakistan, who twice came from behind to secure the draw and book their place in the title clash. Both teams finished the league stage with seven points and an identical goal difference of plus eight, but Pakistan advanced on a superior goals-scored record, having netted 18 goals against Japan's 16.

Pakistan concluded the preliminary round with two wins, one draw and one defeat, having earlier beaten China 8-3 and Canada 6-2 before suffering a narrow 3-2 loss to hosts Malaysia. The final defeat marked Pakistan's second runners-up finish in the Sultan of Johor Cup, having also finished second in the 2016 edition.

Sources: The Express Tribune, October 24, 2026; Dawn, October 23, 2026.
