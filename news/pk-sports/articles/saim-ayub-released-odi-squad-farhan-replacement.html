Saim Ayub Released from ODI Squad as Sahibzada Farhan Named Replacement

October 26, 2026

The Pakistan Cricket Board confirmed on October 26 that opening batter Saim Ayub has been released from Pakistan's men's ODI squad for the remainder of the ongoing tri-series, after he requested a return to domestic cricket to work on his batting form and regain confidence. The national selection committee has named Sahibzada Farhan as Ayub's replacement for the remainder of the series.

An honest conversation

According to the PCB statement, Ayub expressed his desire to return to domestic cricket following a conversation with head coach Mike Hesson. The board said it respected Ayub's decision and appreciated his honest assessment of his current form, and that the team management and the selection committee mutually agreed to accept his request. Ayub, 24, managed scores of 6, 8 and 7 in his three innings in the tournament, according to Cricbuzz, a run of low scores that left his place under scrutiny even before Monday's announcement.

Farhan steps in

Farhan, the newly appointed T20I captain, was originally not picked for the ODI tri-series squad. His inclusion comes as Pakistan seek greater output from their leading batters: reports noted that Ayub, Fakhar Zaman and Babar Azam have collectively averaged just 15.11 in the tri-series, while Pakistan's overall top-order average of 19.81 is the lowest among ODI teams this year. Farhan's arrival therefore carries a clear brief, to steady the top of the order at a moment when the middle order has repeatedly been left to rescue innings, most recently in the dramatic one-wicket win over England on Sunday that secured Pakistan's place in the final.

The road to the final

Pakistan have already qualified for the final after winning their first three matches. They will face Sri Lanka in the fifth match of the tri-series at Gaddafi Stadium in Lahore on Tuesday, October 27, before the final on October 31. Captain Shaheen Afridi had hinted at possible adjustments following the England victory, and Monday's change is the first of them. Pakistan had also added top-order batter Maaz Sadaqat to the squad on Sunday, replacing left-arm wristspinner Sufyan Moqim shortly before the England match.

What it means for Ayub

For Ayub, the release is framed by the PCB as a reset rather than a rejection, a chance to score runs in domestic cricket away from the pressure of an international series. Reports noted, however, that he has no immediate scheduled domestic fixture apart from the President's Trophy final beginning October 27, in which he is not involved, with his next opportunity possibly coming in November at the Quaid-e-Azam Trophy. That gap makes the timing of his return to form an open question, and his international future will depend on the runs he scores when that domestic window opens.
