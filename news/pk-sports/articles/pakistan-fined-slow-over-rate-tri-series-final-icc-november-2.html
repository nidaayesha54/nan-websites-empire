Pakistan Fined for Slow Over-Rate in Tri-Series Final Despite Title Win

November 2, 2026

Pakistan were fined five percent of their match fee for maintaining a slow over-rate during their three-wicket victory over Sri Lanka in the ODI tri-series final in Lahore on Saturday, the International Cricket Council said in a statement on Monday, November 2, Dawn reported.

One over short

The hosts were found to be one over short after time allowances were taken into consideration by match referee Jeff Crowe of the Emirates ICC Elite Panel. Under Article 2.22 of the ICC Code of Conduct, players are fined five percent of their match fee for every over their side fails to bowl in the allotted time. On-field umpires Ahsan Raza and Wayne Knights, third umpire Sharfuddoula Ibne Shahid and fourth umpire Rashid Riaz levelled the charges. Captain Shaheen Shah Afridi pleaded guilty and accepted the proposed sanction, so there was no need for a formal hearing.

A second offence in the same tournament

The Lahore final was not Pakistan's first slow over-rate offence in the tri-series: in the tournament opener at Rawalpindi on October 18, also against Sri Lanka, Pakistan fell two overs short and were docked 10 percent of their match fee. The penalty does not change the result — Pakistan chased 326 with nine balls to spare, Fakhar Zaman scoring 127 off 99 balls — but it closes the disciplinary file on a tournament Pakistan otherwise dominated, losing only one match, against England.
