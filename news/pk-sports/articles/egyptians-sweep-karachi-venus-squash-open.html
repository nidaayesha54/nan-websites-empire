Egyptians Sweep Both Titles at Karachi Venus Squash Open

October 26, 2026

Egypt's Karim El Hammamy and Rowan Elaraby won the men's and women's titles respectively at the Venus ABC Open International Squash Championship in Karachi on Sunday, completing an Egyptian sweep of both trophies at the copper-level event. The finals were played at the Asif Nawaz Squash Complex of the Defence Authority Creek Club in Karachi, with squash legend Jahangir Khan reported to be present for the closing day.

El Hammamy takes the men's title

El Hammamy, the second seed, beat top seed Curtis Malik of England 11-6, 12-10, 11-3 in 48 minutes in the men's final. The scoreline tells the story of a match that was competitive in the second game, which El Hammamy edged 12-10, but otherwise controlled by the Egyptian. Malik, as top seed, had been expected to push hard for the title, but El Hammamy's consistency and movement around the court gave him the edge in the rallies that mattered, and the third game ran away from the Englishman at 11-3.

Elaraby completes the sweep

In the women's final, fourth seed Elaraby beat compatriot and second seed Amina El Rihany 11-6, 11-8, 11-5 in 33 minutes, completing the Egyptian sweep. The all-Egyptian final underlined the depth of Egyptian squash, which has dominated the world game for years across both the men's and women's tours. Elaraby's win, achieved in straight games, was the quicker of the two finals, but it still required her to hold her nerve against a higher-seeded compatriot who knew her game well.

Prize money and prestige

Both winners received 5,415 dollars each in prize money along with the trophy, according to the Sindh Squash Association. Copper-level events on the international circuit may not carry the prize money of the sport's biggest tournaments, but they are vital stepping stones: ranking points earned here help players climb towards larger events, and for Karachi, hosting an international field is itself a statement about the city's squash infrastructure and its history in the sport.

Why Karachi matters for squash

Karachi's place in squash history is inseparable from Jahangir Khan, whose presence at the finals linked the present to the era when Pakistan ruled world squash. Events like the Venus Open keep that tradition alive, giving local fans a chance to watch international squash at home and giving young Pakistani players a benchmark for the standard required at the top level. Egypt's sweep may have disappointed local hopes for a home winner, but the quality of the finals, and the smooth staging of the event, will encourage organisers to bring more international squash back to the city.
