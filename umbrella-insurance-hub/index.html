Frequently Asked Questions

What is umbrella insurance?
Umbrella insurance is extra liability coverage that kicks in when your auto or homeowners policy limits run out. A typical policy adds $1M–$5M of protection against lawsuits, bodily injury, and property damage claims. See our full explainer.
How much does umbrella insurance cost?
The first $1M typically costs about $150–$350 per year — some of the cheapest liability coverage you can buy. Each additional million runs roughly $75–$100/year. Try our free cost estimator.
How much umbrella coverage do I need?
A common rule: carry at least as much coverage as your net worth, since a judgment can reach your assets and future wages. Our coverage calculator gives you a personalized recommendation.
Who needs umbrella insurance?
Homeowners, parents of teen drivers, landlords, dog owners, boat owners, and high-income earners are among the most likely to need it. Read who actually needs umbrella insurance.
Are these estimates financial advice?
No. Everything here is an estimate for education only — not financial, legal, or insurance advice. Confirm details with a licensed insurance agent before buying.
