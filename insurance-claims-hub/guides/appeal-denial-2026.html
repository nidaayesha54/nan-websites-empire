How to Appeal a Health Insurance Denial (2026 Guide)

Millions of health insurance claims are denied every year — and a large share are overturned on appeal. Here's exactly what to do.

Step 1: Read the denial letter carefully

The letter must explain why the claim was denied and how to appeal. Common reasons: "not medically necessary," missing prior authorization, out-of-network provider, or coding errors.

Step 2: Know your deadline

For most employer and marketplace health plans, you have 180 days from the denial to file an internal appeal. Don't wait — start immediately.

Step 3: Gather your documents

  • The denial letter
  • Your policy's coverage provisions (the pages that show the service is covered)
  • Medical records and your doctor's notes supporting medical necessity
  • The itemized bill from your provider
  • A letter from your doctor explaining why the treatment was necessary

Step 4: Write your appeal letter

Keep it clear and factual: state the claim number, dates of service, why the denial is wrong, and reference the specific policy provisions. Use our free appeal letter generator to draft it in seconds.

Step 5: Submit and track

Send it the way your insurer requires (often online portal or fax), keep copies of everything, and log the claim in our claim tracker so you never miss a follow-up.

Step 6: If denied again, request external review

You have the right to an independent external review. An outside reviewer — not your insurer — decides. A meaningful percentage of external reviews overturn the denial.

Disclaimer: General information only, not legal advice. Rules vary by state and plan type. Contact your state's department of insurance for help with your specific case.
