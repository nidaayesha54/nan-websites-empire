How to Read Your EOB — Line by Line

An Explanation of Benefits (EOB) is the statement your insurer sends after processing a claim. It is not a bill. Here's what each part means.

1. Billed amount

What the provider charged. You almost never pay this full number — it's the starting point for negotiations your insurer already did.

2. Allowed amount (negotiated rate)

The price your insurer and the provider agreed on. All your cost-sharing is calculated from this number, not the billed amount.

3. Provider adjustment / write-off

The difference between billed and allowed amounts. The provider writes this off — you do not pay it.

4. Paid by insurance

What your insurer paid toward the allowed amount.

5. Applied to deductible

The portion that counts toward your annual deductible.

6. Copay / coinsurance

Your share. A copay is a flat fee; coinsurance is a percentage of the allowed amount.

7. Patient responsibility

What you may owe: usually deductible share + copay/coinsurance. But wait for the actual bill from your provider before paying — the EOB is not a bill.

8. Denied amounts

If part of the claim was denied, you'll see a denial code. Look up the code, and if it looks wrong, appeal it — many denials are overturned.
