Singapore Baby Bonus: Cash Gift, CDA and Large Families Scheme Explained

Singapore's baby support package can be worth tens of thousands per child — if you know every component. This guide breaks down the cash, the CDA and what is changing in 2027.

Having a baby in Singapore comes with one of the world's most generous support packages — but it's spread across five different schemes with different payout schedules, and the headline totals mix cash, savings matches, and tax rebates. Miss a component and you can leave thousands unclaimed.

This guide breaks down every part of the package for Singapore Citizen children: the Baby Bonus cash gift, the Child Development Account (CDA), the MediSave Grant for Newborns, the Large Families Scheme enhancements, the Parenthood Tax Rebate — and the big changes coming with the SG Child Support Package from 1 April 2027.

The Three Pillars

1. Baby Bonus cash gift — real cash, paid into your Child Savings Account. 2. Child Development Account (CDA) — a co-savings account: an automatic First Step Grant plus dollar-for-dollar government matching on what you save. 3. MediSave Grant for Newborns — S$5,000 into your child's MediSave for healthcare.

Pillar 1: The Baby Bonus Cash Gift

The cash gift is disbursed in tranches over several years — it is not a single lump sum:

At birthS$3,000S$4,000
6 monthsS$1,500S$2,000
12 monthsS$1,500S$2,000
18 monthsS$1,000S$1,000

Total cash giftS$11,000S$13,000

The money lands in the Child Savings Account (CSA) — a joint account with no usage restrictions. Sign up via the LifeSG app around birth registration; the first payout typically arrives within 7–10 working days of registration.

Pillar 2: The Child Development Account (CDA)

The CDA has two government contributions:

  • First Step Grant (automatic): S$5,000 for the 1st and 2nd child; S$10,000 for the 3rd and subsequent child born on or after 18 February 2025 (doubled under the Large Families Scheme). No deposit needed — it lands when you open the CDA at DBS/POSB, OCBC, or UOB.
  • Dollar-for-dollar co-matching: the government matches what you save, up to a cap — S$4,000 (1st), S$7,000 (2nd), S$9,000 (3rd & 4th), S$15,000 (5th+). Save nothing, get nothing matched.

CDA funds pay for approved childcare, kindergartens, early-intervention programmes, medical expenses, and MediShield Life premiums. Unused balances transfer to the Post-Secondary Education Account.

Pillar 3: MediSave Grant for Newborns

S$5,000 is credited to every Singapore Citizen newborn's MediSave account for healthcare costs — MediShield Life premiums, vaccinations, hospitalisation, and approved outpatient treatments. It isn't spendable cash, but it covers real bills from day one. Citizen babies are also covered by MediShield Life from birth.

The Large Families Scheme (from 18 February 2025)

Third and subsequent children born on or after 18 February 2025 get extra layers:

  • Doubled CDA First Step Grant: S$10,000 instead of S$5,000.
  • Large Family MediSave Grant: S$5,000 into the mother's MediSave account.
  • Large Family LifeSG Credits: S$1,000 a year from age 1 to 6 (S$6,000 total).

A third child born today can therefore attract roughly S$69,000 in headline support — but read the next section before treating that as cash.

Cash vs Headline: Read the Total Carefully

Headline totals blend very different things:

  • Real cash: the Baby Bonus cash gift (into your CSA, withdrawable).
  • Conditional: CDA co-matching only pays if you save first — it's a cap, not a gift.
  • Restricted: the MediSave Grant stays in MediSave for healthcare.
  • Not cash at all: the Parenthood Tax Rebate (S$5,000 for the 1st child, S$10,000 for the 2nd, S$20,000 for the 3rd+) offsets income tax payable — worthless if you owe no tax.

The amount that reaches your bank account is always smaller than the headline. Plan around the cash schedule, not the brochure total. And cash support is only half the story — the day-to-day cost of raising a baby is what your budget really needs to cover.

What's Changing: The SG Child Support Package (1 April 2027)

Announced at the 2026 National Day Rally, the new package replaces the Baby Bonus and Large Families Scheme for children born on or after 1 April 2027:

  • S$10,000 Baby Gift in cash, paid in two tranches within the child's first 12 months — the same for every birth order.
  • S$32,000 in Child Credits — S$2,000 a year on each birthday from age 1 to 16, usable for anything.
  • S$5,000 CDA First Step Grant, S$5,000 co-matching cap, S$10,000 PSEA top-up at 17, S$5,000 MediSave Grant, plus Edusave contributions.

Children already receiving Baby Bonus cash gifts keep them until 31 March 2027, with a top-up to S$10,000 if they've received less. The extra S$5,000 First Step Grant and Large Family LifeSG Credits end under the new scheme.

Worked Example: Second Child

  • Baby Bonus cash gift: S$11,000
  • CDA First Step Grant: S$5,000
  • CDA co-matching (if you save S$7,000): S$7,000
  • MediSave Grant for Newborns: S$5,000
  • Headline total: S$28,000 — plus a S$10,000 Parenthood Tax Rebate against tax payable.

More Tax Relief for Parents

Beyond the Parenthood Tax Rebate, working parents should know two more reliefs. Working Mother's Child Relief (WMCR) gives working mothers tax relief as a percentage of earned income per child — the percentages rise with birth order and were enhanced for children born from 2024. Qualifying Child Relief (QCR) gives both parents S$4,000 per child. These stack with the Parenthood Tax Rebate, so a working mother's tax position with three children can improve dramatically — worth modelling at tax season rather than discovering by accident. Parental support looks different everywhere — Canada's maternity leave top-up system is one example worth comparing.

Childcare Subsidies: The Other Big Saver

Cash and CDA aside, the largest ongoing saving for many families is infantcare and childcare subsidies: a Basic Subsidy for all citizen children in licensed centres plus an Additional Subsidy scaled to household income. Combined with CDA funds (which can pay centre fees), out-of-pocket childcare costs can fall sharply. When comparing the "cost of a child," factor subsidies in — they often outweigh the headline baby bonuses over the preschool years.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the Baby Bonus cash gift paid all at once?

No — it's staggered: a larger amount at birth, then tranches at 6, 12 and 18 months, then S$400 every six months until the child turns 6.5.

Do I need to save into the CDA to get the First Step Grant?

No. The First Step Grant (S$5,000, or S$10,000 for 3rd+ children from 18 Feb 2025) is automatic when you open the CDA. Only the co-matching requires your own savings.

My third child was born in January 2025. Do we get the Large Families enhancements?

The enhanced amounts apply to third and subsequent children born on or after 18 February 2025 — a January 2025 birth falls under the previous rates.

What happens to our Baby Bonus if our child is born in May 2027?

The SG Child Support Package applies: S$10,000 Baby Gift plus S$32,000 in Child Credits, instead of the old Baby Bonus structure.

Can the Parenthood Tax Rebate be carried forward?

Unused rebate can generally be carried forward to offset future tax — but it never converts to cash. Confirm current IRAS rules for your filing year.
