What Should Be Included in a Wedding Day-Of Timeline?

Every complete wedding day timeline covers these blocks in order:

  1. Morning prep — hair, makeup, getting-ready photos
  2. Pre-ceremony — first look, wedding party portraits, lineup
  3. Ceremony — processional through recessional
  4. Post-ceremony — family formals, couple portraits, cocktail hour
  5. Reception — entrance, dinner, speeches, cake, dances
  6. Finale — open dancing, last dance, send-off

7 Timing Rules the Pros Swear By

1. Work backwards from the ceremony

Your ceremony time is the anchor. Hair and makeup need 45–60 minutes per person — for a party of 5, that's 4+ hours. Count backwards and add 30 minutes of buffer. If the math says start at 9 AM, start at 8:30.

2. The 20-minute family photo rule

Family formals should take 20 minutes, not 60. The secret: a numbered shot list given to the photographer in advance ("1. Couple + bride's parents, 2. Couple + groom's parents..."). Each grouping takes ~2 minutes when everyone's pre-assigned.

3. Golden hour is non-negotiable

The 30 minutes before sunset produce the most flattering light of the day. Schedule a 15-minute couple sneak-away during the reception. Set a phone alarm — when weddings get busy, this is the first thing forgotten and the most regretted.

4. Speeches go between courses

Never stack all speeches together — guests tune out. One speech per course keeps energy up and gives speakers a natural time limit (the next course arriving).

5. Buffer the transitions

Moving 100+ people from ceremony to cocktail hour takes 15–20 minutes, not 5. Moving from cocktail hour to reception takes 10. Plan for humans, not robots.

6. Confirm vendor arrival times in writing

Two weeks before, email every vendor: "Confirming you arrive at [time] for [duty]." Get a yes in writing. The timeline is only as good as the people following it.

7. Print it and distribute

A timeline in your phone helps you. A printed timeline in the hands of your photographer, DJ, coordinator, MOH, and best man helps everyone. Print 8 copies.

Common Timeline Mistakes to Avoid

  • Skipping the first look but keeping a 4 PM ceremony in winter — sunset at 5 PM means zero daylight couple photos. Either do the first look or move the ceremony earlier.
  • Scheduling portraits during cocktail hour without telling guests — guests feel abandoned. Either do portraits before the ceremony or keep cocktail hour to 45 minutes with great food and drinks.
  • Forgetting to eat — schedule a real 20-minute meal for the couple during prep. Low blood sugar + wedding stress = tears (the bad kind).
  • No plan for the wedding party between photos and reception — give them a room with snacks and drinks, or they wander off and delay everything.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should be included in a wedding day-of timeline?

Hair and makeup start, photographer arrival, getting dressed, first look, ceremony, family portraits, cocktail hour, grand entrance, dinner, speeches, cake cutting, parent dances, open dancing, and send-off — each with exact times and buffers.

How do I make a wedding day timeline?

Start with your fixed ceremony time, work backwards for morning prep, forwards for reception events. Add 15-minute buffers between major blocks, confirm vendor arrival times, then share the final version with everyone 1–2 weeks before.
