GTA 6 Trains: Ride the Rails Across Leonida

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

For the first time in the series, you do not have to drive everywhere in Grand Theft Auto 6. The game has a fully functioning bus and train system, with vehicles that run on timetables, carry passengers between stops and stations, and let you ride as a passenger instead of taking the wheel. It was confirmed in Edge magazine's November 2026 cover feature, and it changes how the map works: Leonida is a place you can travel through, not just a place you drive across.

This guide explains what Rockstar has actually said about the system, how to ride a bus or a train, what it is good for, and what is still unconfirmed — including the one police trick that rests on a single report.

What Rockstar confirmed

The confirmation came from Edge issue 430, in Simon Parkin's cover feature "The state that never sleeps", published October 29, 2026. The feature introduces Phil Hooker, a head of development at Rockstar North, and gives him the transit beat: he spends much of his time waiting at bus stops, checking that buses arrive when expected, then riding them across Leonida and watching the world pass by. His quote, carried by GamesRadar and others: "I'm obsessed with making sure they always turn up. So, anywhere in the middle of playing, I'll just go: right, we're going to get the bus to where we're going. I've memorised all of the routes."

Two things are on the record in those lines. First, the systems exist and function: buses and trains that run on timetables a developer can audit, not background animation. Second, the coverage is state-wide — Hooker rides them "across Leonida", the word Rockstar uses for the whole state, not just Vice City. Co-studio head Aaron Garbut added the other half of the picture, describing the train as part of the slower, roleplay side of the game: buying cigarettes and beer at a shop, or "going on the train for the day", as one way to live a life in Leonida.

How to ride a bus or a train

The system works the way real public transport works. Find a bus stop or a train station, wait for the next one to pull in, and climb aboard. Buses run routes across the state and pick up at bus stops; trains call at stations along their lines. Like the real thing, a train arrives on its own timetable, so you wait on the platform until it comes, and other passengers will be waiting with you. Once you are aboard, sit back and watch Leonida go by on the way to your stop.

Opening up more of the map is the easiest way to spot stops and stations, and the routes are worth learning the way Hooker did: the bus you catch twice a day becomes the fastest way to cross town without touching a car. Rockstar has not published a route map or a list of station names, so the exact lines are something players are mapping themselves in the first weeks — check a fan map if you want the full network before you ride it blind.

The police trick — reported, not confirmed

One more claim travels with this story, and it needs a flag. Beebom, reporting the same Edge interview, quoted Hooker as saying you can use public transport to hide from the police and let a Wanted level cool down: if the police did not see you get on, and you get out of the wanted area, why would they search the bus unless they saw you get onto it? No second publication has carried the quote, and nobody has independently tested it in the finished game, so treat it as reported rather than confirmed. Read it as a rule, not a trick: the bus works as a hideout only while the police's picture of you is broken. If they watched you board, boarding does not help.

Even without the trick, transit has a real tactical use. A bus or train moves you across the map without a car, which means no vehicle to trace, no driving to attract attention, and no crash to start a chase. It is the quiet way to travel, and in a game where the loud way is usually a robbery, quiet has value.

When transit is the wrong choice

Buses and trains are for the journey, not the destination. When you only care about arriving, two faster options exist: taxis, and the RydeMe app on your phone, which lets you call a ride, set a marker on the map, and pay extra to skip the trip and arrive straight away. Transit is what you take when you want to see Leonida — the slow ride past the swamp, the beach and the skyline — or when you want to disappear into a crowd of passengers for a while.

It is also worth knowing the history. Public transport is not entirely new to the series: GTA 4 had a subway network with 26 stations and eight routes, and GTA 5 had a small light-rail system you could ride on demand. What is new in GTA 6 is that the buses and trains run on fixed routes and timetables, like a living network rather than set dressing. That is why Hooker spent his testing time waiting at bus stops: a timetable you can audit is a system, and a system is something you can plan a day around.

FAQ

Are there trains in GTA 6? Yes. GTA 6 has a working train network, and you can catch a train at a station and ride it as a passenger. Buses work the same way at bus stops.

Do buses and trains run on a schedule? Yes. Both keep to their own timetables, so you wait at the stop or on the platform until one arrives — and Rockstar's developers checked the timetables themselves during testing.

Can you hide from the police on a bus? Reported, not confirmed. One outlet quoted a Rockstar developer saying it works if the police did not see you board. Treat it as a lead worth testing, not a guarantee.

The bottom line: GTA 6's buses and trains are real, timetabled and rideable across the whole state. Learn the routes, ride when you want the journey, and call a taxi when you only want the destination.
