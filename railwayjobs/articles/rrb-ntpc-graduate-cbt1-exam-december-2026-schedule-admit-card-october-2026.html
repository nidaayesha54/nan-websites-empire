RRB NTPC Graduate CBT-1 Exam December 2026: Schedule, Admit Card & Last-Month Prep Plan

The RRB NTPC Graduate CBT-1 2026 is indicated for December as per the indicative examination schedule notice issued by the Railway Recruitment Boards. This is the single most important update for the lakhs of graduates who applied under CEN 06/2026 (3,477 posts) during the 8 October – 6 November application window. With roughly six to eight weeks left, this guide lays out the expected timeline, when the admit card will drop, the exam pattern to prepare against, and a focused plan for the remaining weeks.

The December date comes from the indicative schedule — RRBs will confirm the exact exam dates through a separate notice on the official websites. City intimation (around 10 days before) and the admit card (around 4 days before) follow the standard RRB pattern, so keep your registration number and login credentials safe from now.

CBT-1 Exam Pattern to Prepare Against

The NTPC Graduate CBT-1 follows the standard pattern used across recent RRB NTPC cycles:

Duration is 90 minutes, with one-third negative marking for every wrong answer. CBT-1 is a screening stage — only about 20 times the vacancies (category-wise) move to CBT-2 — so accuracy matters as much as attempts. Confirm the exact pattern in the detailed CEN before your exam date.

How to Download the Admit Card (When Released)

1
Wait for the official notice — RRBs announce the admit card release on the regional websites; there is no single all-India download date.
2
Open your regional RRB website — the RRB you applied through (not rrbapply.gov.in).
3
Log in — with your registration number and date of birth or password.
4
Check the free travel pass — eligible reserved-category candidates can download the travel authority along with the admit card.
5
Print and verify — exam date, shift, reporting time and centre; report discrepancies to your RRB immediately.

Focused Preparation Plan for the Remaining Weeks

Mathematics (30 questions)

Prioritise arithmetic — percentages, ratio-proportion, time-speed-distance, time-work, SI/CI and mensuration — since these dominate the section. Practice with a timer: the goal is under 60 seconds per question with high accuracy.

Reasoning (30 questions)

Number and alphabet series, coding-decoding, syllogisms, seating arrangement and non-verbal series carry the most weight. This is the highest-scoring section for most aspirants — drill it daily.

General Awareness (40 questions)

The largest section. Cover static GK (Indian polity, history, geography, economy basics), general science from NCERT level, and current affairs of the last 6–8 months with a railway/budget focus. Daily current-affairs revision beats last-week cramming.

Mock Tests

Take two full-length mocks per week now, moving to alternate days in the final fortnight. Analyse every mock for the same three things: questions you got wrong (concept gap), questions you skipped (speed gap), and questions you got right but slowly (practice gap). Never attempt blindly — the one-third negative marking punishes guessing.
