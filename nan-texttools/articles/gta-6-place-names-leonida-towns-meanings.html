GTA 6 Place Names: What Every Leonida Town Means

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and its map is a joke, a tribute and a geography lesson all at once. Leonida is Florida with the names changed, and every name on the map points at a real place — sometimes by sound, sometimes by shape, sometimes by a pun only a Floridian would catch. This guide explains what the names mean: the state, the counties, the regions and the landmarks, and the real places each one is built on.

A player who knows the real Florida can read Leonida like a translation — and the names themselves say what kind of place you are driving into.

Leonida and Vice City: the state and the city

Leonida is the state's name, and it is a straight lift from Florida's history: the state is named for Juan Ponce de Leon, the explorer who named Florida, and Leonida is the name his story leaves behind. Vice City is the game's Miami, a name the series has used since 2002, and it sits in Vice-Dale County in the southeast — the county's name is a blend of Vice and Dade, the real Miami-Dade County. Vice Beach is the barrier island to the east, the game's version of Miami Beach, and Ocean Beach is the art-deco beachfront where the hotels glow pink and teal at night.

The city's own neighbourhoods carry the translation further. Little Cuba is the game's Little Havana, named for its bakeries and its Cuban community. The Tisha-Wocka flea market sells bootleg brands under a name that sounds like a joke and works like a landmark. The VC Port is the cruise ship capital of the world, the game's version of PortMiami, and Vice City International Airport — VCIA, printed in full on the launch-day map — sits southwest of the city, where the real Miami airport sits. Washington Beach and Gambit Bay, named in the Ultimate Edition material, fill out the beachfront the same way the real city's districts do.

The Keys and the Grassrivers: the south

The Leonida Keys are the Florida Keys, and the name is barely a disguise: a chain of tropical islands south of the city, strung along Route 1A, with loaded bars and a casual dress code, next to dangerous waters. The islands each have their own names on the launch-day map, from Key Lento — the long key, where Jason's starting home sits — down to Mile Zero Air Station at the end of the chain, the game's version of the real Mile Marker 0 in Key West. The Keys are where the game starts, and their names say why: this is the far end of the state, the place the road runs out.

The Grassrivers are the Everglades, and the name is a translation of what the place is: a river of grass, a slow sheet of water moving through sawgrass and mangroves, southwest of the airport and just north of Mariana County. The name tells you the terrain before you see it — wetlands, the famous alligators, and deadlier predators among the mangroves — and it tells you the vehicle too, because the Grassrivers are where the airboats live. Watson Bay and Capri, the Gulf towns in Mariana County, are the small coastal places the wetlands drain into, and their names are as plain as the towns: a bay, and an island name borrowed from the Mediterranean.

Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia and Mount Kalaga: the west and the north

Port Gellhorn sits on the west coast, and it is the game's version of Panama City Beach — Leonida's forgotten coast, all cheap motels, shut-down attractions and empty strip malls. The name sounds like a port and looks like a ruin, which is the point: this is the coast the tourists left, and the Starlet Motel, where Jason and Lucia hide in Chapter 2, is its most famous address. Ambrosia is the factory town in the heart of the state, named for the food of the gods and built on sugar — home to the Allied Crystal sugar refinery and a local biker gang, the game's version of central Florida's industry, where the crop is sugar cane and the air smells of it.

Mount Kalaga National Park is the wild north, up against the state's northern border, and its name is the game's invention for a place built from north Florida and Providence Canyon in Georgia: forests, canyons and rivers, with hunting, fishing and off-road trails, and paranoid radicals in the backwoods. Lake Leonida, in the middle of the state, is the big water the counties are measured against, and Waning Sands, the Atlantic beaches of Leonard County, is the east coast's quiet end — a name that sounds like a beach at low tide, which is what it is.

The stadiums and the landmarks

The landmarks carry the translation into the buildings. Fleeca Field, the football stadium in Rockridge, is modelled on LoanDepot Park, the retractable-roof ballpark in Miami's Riverside neighbourhood, home of the Miami Marlins — a $634 million stadium whose roof, fans have suggested, players may be able to open. The naming rights belong to Fleeca, the banking company that has been a GTA fixture for years, and the home team is the Vice City Mambas, the game's version of the Miami Dolphins. The Sahara Arena is the game's version of the Kaseya Center, the $213 million home of the Miami Heat on Biscayne Bay, home of the Vice City Narcos basketball team.

Hamlet, near the airport, is the little place beside the big city — the name is the joke. Route 1A is the game's version of the real Overseas Highway, U.S. Route 1, which runs the length of the real Keys.

FAQ

What is Leonida based on? Florida. Leonida is the fictional version of the state, named for Juan Ponce de Leon, and its regions — the Keys, the Grassrivers, Port Gellhorn, Ambrosia and Mount Kalaga — are Florida places with changed names.

Is Vice City Miami? Vice City is Rockstar's version of Miami, in Vice-Dale County — the game's Miami-Dade — with Vice Beach as its Miami Beach and the VC Port as its PortMiami.

Where are the Leonida Keys? The island chain south of Vice City, along Route 1A, from Key Lento down to Mile Zero Air Station — the game's version of the Florida Keys and the Overseas Highway.

The bottom line: every name in GTA 6 is a translation, and the translation is the guide. Leonida is Florida, Vice City is Miami, the Keys are the Keys — and once you know the real place behind the name, you know what kind of place you are driving into before you get there.
