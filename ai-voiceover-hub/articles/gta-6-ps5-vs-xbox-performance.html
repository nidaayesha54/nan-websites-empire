GTA 6 on PS5, PS5 Pro, Xbox X and S: Which Runs Best?

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 runs at 30 frames per second on every console, and there is no 60 fps performance mode — Rockstar confirmed that to Kotaku on November 18, 2026, the day before launch. But "30 fps on every console" does not mean the game feels the same on every console. It does not. Base PS5 dips below 30 in heavy combat and explosions, the Xbox Series S is the roughest experience of all, and the fixes that help are different on each platform. GTA 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and if you are choosing which console to play it on — or wondering why your version feels worse than your friend's — this is the honest comparison, built only on verified launch reporting.

The first thing to understand is why the frame rate is capped at all. GTA 6 is an enormously dense game: a living Vice City and the wider Leonida state, with night lighting, real traffic and roadworks, detailed streets, and deep car culture. No console in this generation can push that world to 60 frames per second. Rockstar chose a stable 30 fps target instead of an unstable higher one. That is a design decision, not a bug — and it means the question is not "which console gets 60 fps" but "which console holds 30 fps best, and what can you do when it dips?"

PlayStation 5: Solid, With Dips in Combat

On base PS5, GTA 6 mostly holds its 30 fps target — and then dips below it in the worst moments. Heavy combat, explosions, and dense city traffic at night are where the drops happen: the screen fills with effects, traffic, and physics, and the frame rate falls. The dips are noticeable because they are uneven — a steady 30 fps is easy to adapt to, but a rate that jumps between 30 and the low 20s is much harder to watch, and for some players it causes headaches and nausea.

Two PS5 settings can make the dips less painful, though neither raises the frame rate. First, clear the console's cache: in Safe Mode, choose Clear Cache and Rebuild Database — a full restart, not rest mode, is part of the same routine. Second, turn OFF HDCP in the system settings — disabling it has been reported to smooth out frame drops on base PS5. Turn HDCP back on if you stream or record, since some capture apps require it. And keep the console's system software updated: GTA 6 needs PS5 system software 26.07-14.23.00 to run properly at launch, and an outdated console is the single most common cause of avoidable problems.

PlayStation 5 Pro and Xbox Series X: The Closest Call

The PlayStation 5 Pro is the steadiest console of the four, according to Digital Foundry's launch tests: it holds 30 fps through more than 50 hours of normal play, with only brief dips to 24 fps in explosion-heavy scenes, at 1440p upscaled with PSSR to near-4K with ray-traced shadows. Base PS5 and Xbox Series X are close behind — both render around 1440p upscaled to 4K with Rockstar's temporal anti-aliasing, and both dip into the low 20s in the heaviest scenes: base PS5 to 23 fps in a car-destruction test, Series X to around 21–22 fps in a police shootout. Digital Foundry's verdict is that the difference between them is not huge.

Xbox Series S: The Roughest Experience

The Xbox Series S is the roughest place to play GTA 6, and the verified numbers are blunt: it runs in the mid-20s in busy scenes and can drop to around 18 to 20 frames per second in shootouts. That is a long way from the 30 fps target, and it is the version where the dips are felt most. The game is still the same game — the same Vice City, the same story, the same missions — but the motion is visibly less smooth, and the uneven frame rate is hardest on players who are sensitive to it.

VRR makes the biggest difference on the Series S precisely because the dips are deepest there: with VRR enabled at 120Hz, a drop to 20 fps feels like a small stutter instead of a judder. The other fixes help too — slower camera turns, the Motion Blur Strength slider tried both low and high, the camera pulled back to the far view, sitting further from the TV, a light on in the room, and shorter play sessions. None of these raises the frame rate. All of them make a low frame rate easier to live with. And if the nausea is severe, stop playing and rest — no setting is worth feeling ill over.

The Settings That Help on Every Console

Some fixes are the same wherever you play. Turn the camera more slowly: whipping it around is what makes 30 fps feel worst, because fast camera movement at a low frame rate turns the whole screen into a juddering blur. Slow, deliberate turns give your eyes time to track the motion. Try the Motion Blur Strength slider both ways — some players feel better with more blur smoothing the motion, others feel worse, and there is no universal right answer. Pull the camera back to the far view, which makes the motion on screen feel slower and wider. Sit further from the TV, keep a light on in the room, and play in twenty- or thirty-minute sessions with breaks.

There is one more console-wide truth worth stating plainly: there is no 60 fps mode coming that anyone has announced. Rockstar has not said a future patch will add one, and you should treat 30 fps as permanent. If you are choosing a console for GTA 6 and the choice is open to you, the PS5 Pro is the steadiest of the four in the verified reporting, the base PS5 and Series X are close behind it, and the Series S is the one to avoid if smooth motion matters to you. But whichever console you own, the game is the same masterpiece IGN's review praised — and the settings in this guide are how you make it feel its best.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will a future patch add a 60 fps mode?

Rockstar has not announced one. Treat 30 fps as permanent, and use the settings in this guide — HDCP off and cache cleared on PS5, VRR at 120Hz on Xbox — to make the dips feel gentler.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6 is a 30 fps game on every console, with no 60 fps mode — but the consoles are not equal. The PS5 Pro holds the target best, the base PS5 and Xbox Series X are close behind it, and the Series S is the roughest, dropping to the high teens in shootouts, where VRR at 120Hz helps most. Whichever console you play on, slow the camera, try the motion blur slider both ways, and treat 30 fps as the game it is — not a bug to fix, but a frame rate to live with.
