How Much Does Urgent Care Cost Without Insurance in 2026?

By DoctorClinicHub · Updated October 2026 · 6 min read

No insurance and need care fast? Urgent care is usually the most affordable option outside of a regular doctor's office. Here's what you'll actually pay.

Quick answer: $100–$400

ServiceTypical self-pay range
Basic visit (exam + diagnosis)$100–$250
Visit + X-ray$200–$400
Visit + basic labs$150–$350
Stitches / minor procedure$150–$400
Flu / COVID / strep test$25–$75 (often added to visit)

What's usually included in the base price

  • Provider exam and diagnosis
  • Basic treatment plan and prescriptions
  • Simple in-office tests (strep, flu, urine dip)

Imaging, labs sent off-site, procedures, and medications are typically billed separately.

Urgent care vs ER: the price gap

For the same minor issue, an ER visit typically costs $800–$2,500+ — 5 to 10× more than urgent care. ERs also add facility fees ($200–$1,000+) that urgent care centers don't charge.

5 ways to pay less

  1. Ask for the self-pay rate upfront. Most centers discount 20–40% for cash payment.
  2. Request an itemized estimate before any test or X-ray.
  3. Check retail clinics (CVS MinuteClinic, Walgreens) — often $99–$139 for basic visits.
  4. Look up community health centers (HRSA locator) — sliding-scale fees based on income.
  5. Ask about payment plans if the bill is large — most offer interest-free installments.

When urgent care is NOT enough

Go to the ER or call 911 for chest pain, difficulty breathing, stroke symptoms, severe bleeding, head injuries with confusion, or any life-threatening situation. Cost should never delay emergency care.

Remember: This guide covers costs only — not medical advice. When in doubt about how serious something is, choose the higher level of care.
