ER vs Urgent Care: When to Go Where

By DoctorClinicHub · Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

🚨 Go to the ER or call 911 immediately for: chest pain or pressure, difficulty breathing, signs of stroke (face drooping, arm weakness, speech problems), severe bleeding, serious head injury, or loss of consciousness.

Go to urgent care for

  • Colds, flu, sore throat, ear infections
  • Minor cuts needing stitches, minor burns
  • Sprains and simple fractures
  • UTIs, rashes, pink eye
  • Mild asthma flare-ups (if you have a plan)

Go to the ER for

  • Chest pain, pressure, or suspected heart attack
  • Difficulty breathing or severe allergic reaction
  • Stroke symptoms — remember FAST: Face, Arms, Speech, Time
  • Severe bleeding that won't stop
  • Head injury with confusion, vomiting, or vision changes
  • Broken bones poking through skin, major trauma
  • Severe abdominal pain
  • High fever with stiff neck or rash (possible meningitis)

The cost difference is enormous

A sprained ankle might cost $150–$250 at urgent care versus $1,000–$2,000 at the ER. But never let cost delay emergency care — hospitals must screen and stabilize you regardless of ability to pay (EMTALA federal law).

What about wait times?

ERs triage by severity, so a non-urgent case can wait hours while critical patients go first. Urgent care centers typically see patients in under an hour — another reason to choose them for minor issues.
