How to Read Your Medical Bill (and Spot Errors)

By DoctorClinicHub · October 2026 · 7 min read

Studies suggest a large share of medical bills contain errors — some estimates put it as high as 80%. Here's how to read yours like a pro.

The anatomy of a medical bill

Line itemWhat it means
Date of serviceWhen the care happened — verify it's yours
CPT codes5-digit codes for each procedure/service
ICD codesDiagnosis codes justifying the services
Billed amountThe "sticker price" (almost nobody pays this)
AdjustmentsContractual discounts from your insurer
Patient responsibilityWhat you actually owe

7 common billing errors

  1. Duplicate charges — same CPT code billed twice.
  2. Upcoding — billed for a more complex visit than you had.
  3. Unbundling — services that should be one code split into several.
  4. Phantom charges — services you never received.
  5. Wrong patient info — another patient's charges on your bill.
  6. Balance billing — illegal in many situations under the No Surprises Act.
  7. Math errors — totals that don't add up.

How to dispute a bill (step by step)

  1. Request an itemized bill (not the summary).
  2. Compare it against your Explanation of Benefits (EOB) from your insurer.
  3. Look up unfamiliar CPT codes (the AMA code lookup or fairhealthconsumer.org).
  4. Call the provider's billing department; note names, dates, and reference numbers.
  5. File a written dispute if the phone call fails — keep copies of everything.
  6. Know your rights: the No Surprises Act protects against most surprise out-of-network bills.

Negotiating

Always ask: "Is there a self-pay discount?" and "Can you match the Medicare rate?" Hospitals routinely cut 20–60% for patients who ask. Get any agreement in writing before paying.
