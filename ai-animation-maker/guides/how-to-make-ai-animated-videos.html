🎞️ How to Make AI Animated Videos: The Complete Beginner Workflow

Updated October 2026 · 10 min read

Making an AI animated video isn't "type a sentence, get a movie." The creators getting millions of views follow a repeatable workflow. Here it is — six steps, with the free tools for each.

Step 1: Write a tight script (15 min)

Every animated video starts as words. For a 60-second short, that's roughly 120–150 words of voiceover or dialogue. Write the hook first — the first 3 seconds decide everything. One idea per video, one emotion per scene.

Step 2: Storyboard every scene (20 min)

Break the script into 3–10 second scenes. Each scene needs: a shot description, a camera angle, and timing. AI generators produce short clips, so this planning step is non-negotiable — it's the difference between a coherent story and random pretty clips.

👉 Use our free storyboard planner: add scenes, set durations and camera angles, then export the whole thing as text.

Step 3: Pick your style (5 min)

Your style decides your tool, your prompts, and your audience. 3D cartoon for kids, anime for storytelling shorts, motion graphics for explainers. Still unsure? Take the animation style finder quiz.

Step 4: Write generation prompts (15 min)

Turn each storyboard scene into a prompt: character + action + setting + style + camera. Keep one action per prompt, name the style explicitly, and repeat your character's exact description in every scene so they stay consistent.

👉 The AI animation prompt builder assembles copy-ready prompts for Runway, Kling, Luma, Pika, and more.

Step 5: Generate and iterate (30–60 min)

Generate each scene separately (see our 2026 generator comparison for which tool fits your style). Expect to re-roll: 2–3 generations per scene is normal. Keep the best take of each, and don't chase perfection on background details nobody will notice.

Consistency tricks that work

  • Use image-to-video from a single character reference image instead of pure text-to-video.
  • Keep camera moves slow or static — wild camera motion morphs characters.
  • Reuse the same seed/settings when the tool supports it.

Step 6: Edit and publish (30 min)

Stitch clips in CapCut (free) or your editor of choice. Add voiceover or music, captions, and a strong title. Export in the right aspect ratio: 16:9 for YouTube, 9:16 for Shorts/TikTok/Reels. Then publish — and disclose AI-generated content where the platform asks for it.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Skipping the storyboard. Random clips don't tell stories. Plan first.
  • Overstuffed prompts. One action per prompt, every time.
  • Style-hopping mid-video. Lock one style for the whole project.
  • Publishing without disclosure. YouTube and TikTok both ask about AI-generated content — answer honestly.
