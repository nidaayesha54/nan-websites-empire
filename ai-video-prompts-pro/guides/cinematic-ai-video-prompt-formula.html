The Cinematic AI Video Prompt Formula

Why do some AI videos look like films and others look like screensavers? It's not the tool — it's the shot grammar. Professional cinematographers think in shots: size, angle, movement, light. This formula translates that grammar into prompt language any video model understands.

The formula

[SHOT SIZE + ANGLE] of [SUBJECT + ACTION] in [SETTING], [CAMERA MOVEMENT], lit with [LIGHTING], [TEXTURE/MOTION DETAIL], [LENS/FILM STYLE], [COLOR GRADE], [MOOD].

Fill every slot and the model has no room to improvise badly. Leave slots empty and it fills them with clichés.

Slot 1 — Shot size & angle

This single choice does more for "cinematic" than any adjective. Pick deliberately:

  • Extreme wide / aerial — epic scale, isolation, wonder
  • Wide establishing — place the viewer in the world
  • Medium — human-scale action and dialogue feel
  • Close-up — emotion, detail, intimacy
  • Extreme macro — texture porn; product and food gold
  • Low angle — power, heroism, menace
  • Overhead / top-down — patterns, vulnerability, order

Slot 2 — Camera movement vocabulary

Steal these twelve; they cover 90% of cinema:

  • Slow push-in (growing intimacy or dread)
  • Slow pull-back / crane reveal (expanding context)
  • Lateral dolly / tracking (following action)
  • 360-degree orbit (showcasing a subject)
  • Aerial descent / ascent (god's-eye drama)
  • Handheld (urgency, realism, documentary)
  • Whip pan (energy, transitions)
  • Rack focus (shifting attention between planes)
  • FPV dive (thrill, immersion)
  • Static locked-off (tension, observation)
  • Slow-motion (all of the above, but dreamier)
  • Hyperlapse (time collapsing)

Slot 3 — Lighting recipes

  • Golden hour backlight — romance, nostalgia, beauty
  • Blue hour + practicals — urban melancholy, Blade Runner mood
  • Volumetric god rays — awe, spirituality, nature
  • Chiaroscuro / single source — drama, noir, painting-like
  • Neon-drenched night — cyberpunk, nightlife, energy
  • Overcast diffused — realism, melancholy, documentary

Slot 4 — Texture & motion detail

The difference between "good" and "how is this AI?" lives here. Name surfaces and movement: rain streaking the lens, silk whipping, dust dancing in light shafts, water sheeting, embers drifting, fabric blooming underwater. One vivid physical detail beats five adjectives.

Slot 5 — Lens, film & grade

Finish with the "film stock" layer: anamorphic lens flare, 35mm film grain, shallow depth of field, teal-and-orange grade, 85mm portrait look, IMAX scale, desaturated palette. These tokens push models toward photographic realism.

Assembling multi-shot sequences

For Sora and other long-form models, chain 3 shots with a simple grammar: ESTABLISH → ACTION → PAYOFF. Example: "Wide aerial of storm clouds over savanna. Cut to low-angle: elephant herd silhouetted as lightning forks. Cut to macro: rain striking dust, slow motion." Each beat gets its own camera and light — that's what makes it feel directed, not generated.
