What the Role Involves

The College of Policing sets the standards for policing — it designs the national curriculum, develops training and assesses how officers learn. A Curriculum Development and Assessment apprentice typically works inside that learning machinery: supporting the teams that design training curricula, develop assessment materials and partner with police forces on how learning is delivered. It is a business-partner style apprenticeship — part learning design, part stakeholder work with forces and internal teams — with the apprenticeship framework providing structured training and qualifications alongside the day job. For anyone interested in learning and development, instructional design or education policy, it is a strong entry route with a national-profile employer.

Also Live: Senior AI Engineer (£68k–£75k)

The College is also advertising a PoliceAI: Senior AI Engineer post — £68,680 to £74,930, also remote anywhere in the UK, closing at 11:55pm on Wednesday 21 October 2026 (reference 483742). Policing bodies are investing heavily in AI capability — from digital forensics to demand analysis — and this senior engineering role is part of that push. The deadline is tomorrow at the time of writing, so it is effectively last-call: if you have the engineering background, check the advert immediately.

Who Should Apply for the Apprenticeship

  • Aspiring learning professionals: an interest in training design, assessment or education — formal experience helps but apprenticeships are built for development.
  • Strong communicators: business-partner roles live or die on stakeholder relationships with forces and internal teams.
  • Self-directed remote workers: fully remote means managing your own time and learning — the advert’s regional distance marker confirms anywhere-in-the-UK eligibility.
  • Candidates meeting Civil Service nationality rules: check the advert, as right to work in the UK alone does not automatically qualify you.
