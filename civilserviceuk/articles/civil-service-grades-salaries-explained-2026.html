Civil Service Grades & Salaries Explained 2026

EO, HEO, SEO, Grade 7, Grade 6, SCS — what the grades actually mean, the roles at each level, and real 2026 salary examples from live adverts.

Published 13 October 2026 · NAN Jobs Portal editorial team

Every Civil Service job advert names a grade — EO, HEO, SEO, Grade 7 and so on — and that grade determines your salary band, seniority and the complexity of work expected. If you are new to government recruitment, the grading system can look like alphabet soup. This guide decodes each grade, shows the typical roles at each level, and backs it up with real 2026 salary examples from recently advertised vacancies.

Salary note: Civil Service pay varies by department, profession and location (London weighting applies to many roles). The figures below come from real adverts in the October 2026 cycle — always check the advertised range for the specific role you are considering.

Which Grade Should You Target?

Career changers and school leavers: AO to EO

Operational delivery roles — DWP Work Coaches, HMRC customer service, Border Force — typically sit at EO. These value behaviours and experience over qualifications, making them the most accessible entry point. An EO salary in the mid-£30,000s compares well with many private-sector graduate roles outside London.

Professionals and graduates: HEO to SEO

Policy, analysis, commercial and project-delivery roles usually recruit at HEO or SEO. Recent examples include the Security Industry Authority's Quality, Assurance and Delivery Manager at £44,901 – £48,000 (remote) and UKHSA Delivery Managers at £41,983 – £52,113. These roles expect demonstrable professional experience and stronger behaviour evidence.

Senior specialists and leaders: Grade 7 to SCS

Grade 7 posts lead teams and own significant budgets or policy areas — the Ministry of Justice's Senior DevOps Engineer at £58,511 – £70,725 shows how technical professions command premiums here. Grade 6 and SCS roles carry organisational leadership responsibility, with SCS pay reaching £100,000+ for chief-officer posts like the DBS Chief Information Security Officer role.

How to Choose Your Grade: A 4-Step Check

Match your experience. Compare the advert's person specification with your track record. External candidates can join at any grade — you never have to "start at the bottom".
Check the profession. Digital, commercial, legal and analytical professions publish their own capability frameworks; read the profession-specific criteria alongside the grade.
Weigh London vs national. The same grade can pay thousands more in London. Remote and national roles remove that premium but widen the field.
Look at progression. The Civil Service promotes internally — joining one grade below your ceiling and earning promotion in 18–24 months is a common and successful strategy.

Eligibility and Pay Notes

Pay is set within the advertised range, usually starting at the minimum; exceptions for hard-to-fill specialist roles are possible. Progression pay rises, performance awards and pension benefits (the Civil Service pension is one of the most generous in the UK) sit on top of base salary. Nationality requirements tighten at senior and security-related grades — many SCS and NCA roles require UK nationality and residency.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the main Civil Service grades?

From junior to senior: Administrative Assistant (AA), Administrative Officer (AO), Executive Officer (EO), Higher Executive Officer (HEO), Senior Executive Officer (SEO), Grade 7, Grade 6 and the Senior Civil Service.

Which grade should I apply for?

Apply for the grade matching your experience: operational roles are typically AO/EO, specialist and policy roles HEO/SEO, and team-lead or senior advisory roles Grade 7 and above.

Can you skip grades when joining?

Yes. External candidates can join at any grade if they meet the criteria — you do not need to work up from AO. Specialist professions often recruit directly at SEO, Grade 7 or above.

What is the Senior Civil Service?

The Senior Civil Service (SCS) is the top leadership tier, including directors and deputy directors. SCS roles typically pay £70,000+ and are advertised on Civil Service Jobs and executive search channels.

Do grades mean the same in every department?

Broadly, yes — EO means a similar seniority level everywhere — but salaries and exact responsibilities vary by department, profession and location. Always read the individual advert.
