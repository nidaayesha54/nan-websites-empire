The Grade Ladder: AA to Senior Civil Service

AA and AO — the entry grades

Administrative Assistant (AA) is the most junior grade: entry-level administrative work such as filing, basic customer contact and supporting teams. Administrative Officer (AO) is a step up — roles like caseworkers and contact-centre advisers, often the backbone of big operational departments such as DWP and HMRC.

EO — Executive Officer

Executive Officer (EO) is a classic first professional rung. EO roles involve real responsibility: managing cases, supervising small teams or handling complex customer work. Real 2026 example: the DWP DLA EO Case Manager advert sat in the £28,661–£32,137 range. Many school-leavers and graduates enter here.

HEO — Higher Executive Officer

HEO typically means team leadership or specialist professional work — policy advisers, analysts, and professional-track roles. Real 2026 examples: the HMRC Graduate Valuation Surveyor at £32,500 national / £37,235 London, and the HMCTS Trainee Legal Adviser at £35,335–£42,859. A realistic indicative HEO band from 2026 adverts is roughly £33k–£40k, varying by department and location.

SEO — Senior Executive Officer

SEO is middle management: leading teams, owning projects and delivering policy or operational outcomes. Indicative 2026 advertised examples cluster around £38k–£47k, with specialist and London roles toward the top.

Grade 7 and Grade 6 — senior management

Grade 7 covers senior managers and lead professionals — heads of small teams, senior policy leads and digital delivery managers. Indicative 2026 examples sit around £52k–£60k+. Grade 6 is one step below the Senior Civil Service: assistant directors and senior heads of profession. For a sense of the very top, the Home Office Digital Deputy Directors advertised in 2026 at £86k–£100k — senior-level posts that show where the ladder can lead.

Typical Entry Points

  • School leavers / apprentices: AA and AO posts, plus Civil Service apprenticeship routes.
  • Graduates: the Fast Stream (HEO and above on completion), plus EO-level direct entry and graduate programmes.
  • Experienced professionals: direct entry at HEO, SEO or Grade 7 in specialist professions (digital, legal, finance, surveying, policy).

How Promotion Works

There are two main routes up the ladder. Level transfer lets you move between departments at the same grade to build breadth. Promotion on merit means applying for a higher-graded post and competing fairly — the Civil Service Commission's principles require appointments on merit through open competition, so internal candidates compete alongside external applicants. In practice: deliver strong results at your current grade, gather evidence against the behaviours for the next grade, and apply when ready.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do Civil Service grades like EO, HEO and SEO mean?

They are the standard seniority levels across the UK Civil Service, running from Administrative Assistant (AA) up through Executive Officer (EO), Higher Executive Officer (HEO), Senior Executive Officer (SEO), Grade 7 and Grade 6, to the Senior Civil Service.

Are the salary ranges in this guide official Civil Service pay scales?

No — they are indicative ranges drawn from real 2026 advertised roles. Each department sets its own pay ranges, so always check the salary on the individual advert.

What is a typical EO salary in 2026?

Indicative examples from 2026 adverts sit around £28,661–£32,137 — for example the DWP DLA EO Case Manager advert topped out at £32,137.

How do you get promoted in the Civil Service?

Through level transfer (moving departments at the same grade) or promotion on merit — you apply for a higher-graded post and compete against internal and external candidates under Civil Service Commission principles.

Where are Civil Service jobs advertised?

Almost all permanent roles are advertised on the official Civil Service Jobs portal at civilservicejobs.service.gov.uk.
