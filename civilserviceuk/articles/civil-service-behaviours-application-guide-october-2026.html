Civil Service Behaviours Explained 2026: How to Write Winning 250-Word Behaviour Examples

The complete guide to the Civil Service Success Profiles behaviours framework: how your 250-word behaviour examples are scored, the STAR structure assessors expect, and proven writing tips for EO, HEO and SEO applications.

✅ Updated October 17, 2026
Home » UK Civil Service Jobs » Articles

Civil Service Jobs applications ask for behaviour examples — usually around 250 words each — and candidates find them the hardest part. The formula is consistent: understand the Success Profiles behaviours framework, structure answers around STAR, and match your evidence to the descriptors for the grade (EO, HEO or SEO).

The Success Profiles Behaviours Framework — Key Facts

DetailInformation
FrameworkCivil Service Success Profiles (behaviours, strengths, ability, experience, technical)
BehavioursActions and activities people do that result in effective performance in the role
Common behavioursCommunicating and Influencing; Making Effective Decisions; Delivering at Pace; Leadership; Working Together
Example lengthAround 250 words per behaviour on most adverts
DescriptorsEach advert publishes descriptors for the behaviours at that grade level
ScoringSifters score each example against the descriptors, typically on a 1–7 scale
Note: The behaviours tested, the word count and the scoring approach are set in each individual job advert. Always read the advert’s behaviour descriptors for your grade before you start writing.

How Behaviour Examples Are Scored

Assessors score each example against the published descriptors — commonly 1 to 7 — and the highest scorers reach interview. What wins: specific evidence and alignment with the descriptors.

The STAR Structure Assessors Expect

  • Situation (30–40 words): one or two sentences of context. Keep it brief.
  • Task (30–40 words): what were you asked to do? State the objective and constraints.
  • Action (130–150 words): the heart of the example. Describe specifically what you did, step by step, using “I” — and show your thinking, not just your activity.
  • Result (40–50 words): what changed because of your actions? Include measurable outcomes and what you learned.

The Five Most Common Behaviours

Most adverts draw from the same core behaviours:

  • Communicating and Influencing: clear, audience-appropriate communication that moves people — presenting, persuading, adapting your message.
  • Making Effective Decisions: sound judgement using evidence, even when information is incomplete; owning your decisions.
  • Delivering at Pace: delivering under pressure, prioritising workloads, staying focused when plans change.
  • Leadership: guiding and motivating others, giving direction, taking responsibility — including at junior grades.
  • Working Together: collaborating across teams, sharing information, handling disagreement constructively.

Writing a Strong Example

1
Read the advert’s behaviour descriptors for the grade — these are your marking criteria.
2
Pick a recent example (work, volunteering, university, projects) that matches those exact phrases.
3
Draft in STAR order, keeping Situation and Task tight and most words on Action.
4
Use “I” throughout Action — only your actions can be scored.
5
Close with a concrete Result — outcomes, feedback, or what you learned.
6
Edit for the word count: trim context and make every sentence add evidence.

Common Mistakes That Lose Marks

  • Too much “we”: assessors can only score what you did — rewrite every “we” as an “I” action.
  • All situation, no action: long scene-setting followed by a rushed ending wastes your best word budget.
  • Generic recycled examples: if the example does not match the grade’s descriptors, it will not score.
  • Claims without evidence: “I am a strong communicator” scores nothing; “I presented to 40 stakeholders and secured agreement” scores.
  • Missing the result: always say what changed because of your actions — and what you learned.
  • Copied or AI-generated text: the Civil Service warns against plagiarism and AI-generated content — write in your own words.

Grade-Level Tips: EO, HEO and SEO

Pitch evidence to the grade’s descriptors: EO = reliable delivery and teamwork; HEO = autonomy and influence; SEO = strategic thinking and leadership. The same experience works at any grade if the framing matches.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are Civil Service behaviours?

Behaviours are one part of the Success Profiles framework — the actions that result in effective performance. Each advert lists the behaviours tested, with descriptors for the grade level.

How long should a behaviour example be?

Around 250 words per behaviour on most adverts. Stick close to the limit — examples far over or far under it rarely score well.

How are behaviour examples scored?

Against the published descriptors for the grade, typically on a 1–7 scale. Stronger evidence earns a higher score; the highest-scoring candidates reach interview.

Can I use the STAR structure for behaviour examples?

Yes — Situation, Task, Action, Result. Keep Situation and Task brief, spend most words on Action (what YOU did), and close with a clear Result.

Can I reuse the same example across applications?

Tailor each example to the behaviour and grade descriptors in the advert. Generic examples that don’t match them are a common cause of low sift scores.
