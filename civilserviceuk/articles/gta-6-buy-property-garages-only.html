Can You Buy Property in GTA 6? Only Garages

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

In GTA 5, you could buy businesses that paid you every week — a taxi company, a cinema, a strip club — and build a property empire on the side of the story. In GTA 6, you cannot. That is the honest answer to the most-asked money question about the game, and it is worth hearing before you save up for a mansion that is not for sale. GTA 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and Rockstar has not announced a way to buy houses, apartments or businesses in story mode. The only property with a price tag is a garage. This guide explains what you can buy, what comes with the story, what belongs to the characters, and what the Ultimate Edition shops really are — so you spend your money on the things that exist.

The confusion is understandable, because Leonida is packed with businesses, and every one of them looks buyable. Strip clubs, bars, marinas, recording studios, tattoo parlours, clothing stores — the world is full of front businesses. Seeing a business on screen is not the same as being able to walk in and buy it. Every business Rockstar has named belongs to someone else.

Garages: The Only Property on Sale

Garages are the only property you can buy in GTA 6, as far as Rockstar has said. You start with room for three personal vehicles, according to Rockstar's Rob Nelson, and you can buy garages around the map to own more. When every space is full, sell a car to make room for a new one. Fourteen garages are marked on the map, according to launch-era reporting — but that number, and the buying itself, has not yet been confirmed inside the game, so treat the count as reported rather than settled.

Two garages come with bonus packs rather than a purchase. The Shore Court garage in Ocean Beach comes with the Vintage Vice City Pack, included with all purchases made before November 20. The Paradise Garage in Watson Bay is an Ultimate Edition benefit. Both are useful from the start — an Ocean Beach base and a Watson Bay base, in two of the most useful parts of the map — and both are free with the packs that include them. Beyond those, the garage is the property system: buy space, store cars, sell when full.

Safehouses Come With the Story

Homes work differently in GTA 6: they unlock through the story, not through a shop. Jason and Lucia live where the story puts them, and the safehouses change as the story moves — much like the camps in Red Dead Redemption 2, which moved with the story rather than being bought. There is no real-estate menu, no house-buying system, and no apartment to save up for. The fugitive status of the two leads is part of the reason: a couple on the run from the law does not buy a mansion and settle down, and the game reflects that.

That is a real change from GTA 5, where buying property was a whole system with weekly income attached. In GTA 6, the money goes elsewhere: garages, weapons, clothes, car customization, and everyday spending such as gym fees, fuel and haircuts, with a savings account for whatever is left over. The spending is personal, not property-based — and that fits a story about escape rather than empire-building.

Every Business Belongs to Someone Else

Leonida's businesses all have owners, and the owner is never you. Boobie Ike, the Vice City street legend turned legitimate businessman, holds real estate, the Jack of Hearts strip club in Crosstown and a recording studio — an empire built, in his own words, on club money and drug money. Brian Heder runs a boat yard and marina in the Leonida Keys, where he moves product with his third wife Lori. Dre'Quan Priest runs Only Raw Records, the hip-hop label he co-manages with Boobie, with the rap duo Real Dimez signed to it. Those are places in the story, not things you can own — and Rockstar has not said you can buy or run any of them.

The Ultimate Edition shops are the closest thing to confirmed GTA 6 businesses, and they prove the point. Five shops are only open for business with the Ultimate Edition: Rideout Customs Mod Shop in Vice City, One-Eyed Willie's at Lake Leonida, Sara's Unisex Salon, Stock 305 Clothing Store and the Electric Fang Tattoo Parlor. You shop there; you do not own them. They are access perks, not properties — a place to spend money, not a way to earn it.

What About Online?

There is no online mode at launch, so online apartments and businesses do not apply either. GTA Online, the multiplayer side of GTA 5, keeps running as its own game, with its own businesses — but none of that carries into GTA 6, and Rockstar has announced no online mode for GTA 6 or a date for one. The property question, for now, is a story-mode question, and the story-mode answer is garages only.

That may change. Rockstar has been deliberately vague about purchasable businesses, and creators briefed on the game report that the studio stayed vague on purpose. Until Rockstar confirms a system, file "own a business in GTA 6" under rumour — and spend your money on the garage space, the cars and the customization that actually exist.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you buy property in GTA 6?

Only garages, as far as Rockstar has said. You start with room for three vehicles and can buy garages around the map to own more. Homes come with the story, and no house-buying system has been announced.

Can you buy businesses in GTA 6?

No business system has been announced. The businesses Rockstar names — Boobie Ike's club and studio, Brian's marina, Dre'Quan's label — belong to those characters. The Ultimate Edition's five shops are places you visit, not places you own.

Is there passive income in GTA 6 story mode?

Nothing announced. GTA 5 paid you weekly from owned businesses; GTA 6 has shown no equivalent. Money comes from missions, heists, robberies, hunting and side jobs — all active work, not passive income.

The Bottom Line

In GTA 6, you can buy garages and nothing else. Homes come with the story, businesses belong to the characters, and the Ultimate Edition shops are places to spend money, not own. Save for garage space, spend on cars and customization, and do not chase a property empire that Rockstar has not built — at least not yet.
