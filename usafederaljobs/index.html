How to Apply

Five steps to land your government job.

Create a USAJOBS.gov Profile

Sign up at USAJOBS.gov and build your federal profile with work history, education and eligibility documents.

Build a Federal Resume

Federal resumes are longer and more detailed than private-sector ones — include hours worked per week, supervisor names and exact dates for every role.

Search by GS Pay Scale

Match your experience to the GS grade range (GS-5 to GS-15). The announcement's 'Qualifications' section tells you which grade you qualify for.

Submit Before the Closing Date

Attach transcripts, veterans' preference documents (SF-15) and any required certifications, then submit well before the deadline.

Prepare for Security Clearance

Many federal roles require background checks or clearances (Public Trust, Secret, Top Secret). Be ready for fingerprints, credit checks and interviews.

Frequently Asked Questions

Answers to common questions about government hiring.

What is the GS pay scale?

The General Schedule (GS) is the federal pay system with grades GS-1 through GS-15. Each grade has 10 steps, and your salary depends on grade, step and locality pay for your duty station.

How do I apply for federal jobs?

All federal applications go through USAJOBS.gov. Create a profile, upload a federal-format resume and supporting documents, then apply to announcements before their closing dates.

Do I need a security clearance to work for the government?

Not for every role, but many positions require at least a background investigation or Public Trust clearance, while law enforcement and intelligence roles often require Secret or Top Secret clearances.

What is veterans' preference?

Eligible veterans receive preference points (5 or 10 points) added to passing scores in competitive federal hiring, and some positions are restricted to preference-eligible candidates.

How long does federal hiring take?

Federal hiring typically takes 60 to 120 days from the closing date to a job offer, though direct-hire authorities for shortage occupations like healthcare and IT can move much faster.

Can non-citizens apply for federal jobs?

Most federal positions require U.S. citizenship, though some agencies hire non-citizens for specific shortage roles when no qualified U.S. citizen is available.
