GTA 6 Federal Agents: The FIB, the Feds and 5-Star Heat

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

The first GTA 6 trailer ends with a promise that the whole game is built on: Jason and Lucia get caught in a massive criminal conspiracy that spans the state of Leonida. A conspiracy that big needs someone powerful behind it, and in Grand Theft Auto that someone is usually the federal government. The question every fan has asked since December 2023 is simple: where are the feds in GTA 6?

The honest answer is that no federal agency has been named for GTA 6 yet. Rockstar has not confirmed the FIB, the IAA or any new federal agency for the game, and the nine law enforcement agencies identified in the trailers are all city, county or state forces. But the feds are almost certainly there — and this guide separates what is known from what is only expected.

What trailer 1 actually shows

The first trailer shows local Leonida police and what appears to be federal agents tracking Lucia and Jason. The shots are brief — suited figures, unmarked cars, the kind of presence that reads as federal rather than local — and they sit alongside the trailer's story of a heist gone wrong and a conspiracy reaching across the state. That is the strongest evidence for a federal presence in the game, and it is also the limit of it: appearance, not a name.

It is worth being careful here, because the gap between "federal-style agents on screen" and "the FIB is in the game" is exactly where bad guides are born. Rockstar has named nine agencies across its trailers and screenshots — the Vice City Police, the county sheriffs, the Highway Patrol, the Coast Guard, POACH and more — and not one of them is federal. If a federal agency were confirmed, it would be on that list. It is not.

The FIB and IAA: what GTA 5 taught us

The reason everyone expects the feds is GTA 5. In that game, the Federal Investigation Bureau — Rockstar's version of the FBI — and the International Affairs Agency — its version of the CIA — acted like rival gangs waging a shadow war, using criminals as pawns and forcing Michael, Franklin and Trevor to do their dirty work. It was one of the strongest story engines in the series, and it is the pattern fans expect GTA 6 to repeat.

The pattern is so strong that some fans predict Rockstar will invent a brand-new agency rather than reuse the FIB and IAA, since players have already spent more than a decade with those two. Others argue the Miami setting all but demands a drug-enforcement presence, and point to the DEA as the natural fit for a story built on Keys smuggling. Both are reasonable guesses. Neither is confirmed.

Five stars and the federal question

Guide reporting puts FIB tactical units at the five-star wanted level, arriving in unmarked SUVs with heavy body armour while the response escalates toward roadblocks and air support. That reporting should be treated as reported, not confirmed — it comes from guides, not from Rockstar — but it fits the shape of the wanted system, which escalates from local patrols through county and state forces to something bigger at the top.

The same caution applies to the six-star level, where some guides report military involvement and a state-wide lockdown. Whether the military gets involved at six stars has not been confirmed, and the same is true of any federal role at five. What is confirmed is the escalation itself: six stars are back, the police remember what you look like and what you drive, and the higher you climb, the more of Leonida's law enforcement comes after you.

Why the feds matter to the story

If the conspiracy in GTA 6 works the way Rockstar's stories usually work, the feds are not just a wanted-level tier — they are the plot. Jason and Lucia are small players caught in something much bigger than a bank robbery, and the people running that something are the kind of people who can make a federal agency look the other way, or point it at someone else.

That is the role the FIB played in GTA 5: not a police force, but a power broker, using criminals to settle scores between agencies and burying the evidence afterwards. If GTA 6 follows the same design, the federal agents on Lucia and Jason's trail are not there to arrest them — they are there to use them. And that is a much more interesting story than a chase.

The bottom line: the feds are the biggest unanswered question in GTA 6. The trailers show them, the story needs them, and the wanted system points at them — but until Rockstar names them, every federal claim is a guess.
