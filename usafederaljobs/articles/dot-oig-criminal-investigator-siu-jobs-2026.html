Federal law enforcement jobs rarely open with this much urgency. The U.S. Department of Transportation Office of Inspector General (DOT OIG) has announced vacancies for Criminal Investigator positions in its Special Investigations Unit (SIU), based in Washington, DC. The advertised application period runs October 5 through October 16, 2026, but the recruitment notice warns the announcement may close as soon as 150 applications have been received — so waiting until the last day is a gamble.

What the DOT OIG Special Investigations Unit Does

DOT OIG is the independent watchdog for one of the largest federal departments, covering highways, aviation, rail, transit, pipelines, and maritime programs. Criminal Investigators in the Special Investigations Unit handle criminal, civil, and administrative matters involving fraud and other allegations affecting DOT programs, operating administrations, and grantees. This is classic federal investigative work: following taxpayer dollars, building cases, and working with prosecutors when criminal charges are warranted.

What Background Fits This Role

The recruitment notice points to several kinds of experience that map well to SIU work. You do not need to check every box — but the strongest candidates typically bring several of the following:

  • Criminal investigations experience — conducting or supporting investigations, developing evidence, and preparing investigative reports.
  • Fraud investigations — especially fraud affecting government programs, contracts, or grants, where you traced funds or documented false claims.
  • Contract and grant investigations — DOT spends billions through grants and contracts, so experience auditing or investigating procurement is directly relevant.
  • Evidence analysis and interviewing — collecting and analyzing records, conducting interviews, and writing reports that hold up under review.
  • Investigative reporting — clear, well-organized case documentation is the backbone of OIG work.

Why OIG Investigator Jobs Are Worth Pursuing

Inspector General offices across the federal government offer some of the most mission-driven investigative careers available. You work independently from the agency you oversee, your cases have real consequences for how billions in public funds are spent, and the experience transfers across the entire federal law enforcement community. A posting in Washington, DC at an OIG with DOT's portfolio is a career accelerator — which is exactly why the 150-application cap exists.
