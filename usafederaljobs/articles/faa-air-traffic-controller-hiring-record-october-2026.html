FAA Air Traffic Controller Jobs 2026: Record 2,235 Trainees Hired — 5-Step Process & How to Apply

On October 1, 2026, the FAA announced a record 2,235 air traffic controller trainees hired in Fiscal Year 2026 — the most in agency history — with at least 2,300 more targeted in FY27. Here is who can apply, how the streamlined hiring process works, and what the Oklahoma City Academy involves.

On October 1, 2026, the Federal Aviation Administration announced it had hired 2,235 air traffic controller trainees in Fiscal Year 2026 — the most in FAA history. The record caps a year-long recruitment surge built around a streamlined five-step hiring process, expanded training capacity at the FAA Academy in Oklahoma City, and a headline-grabbing campaign that recruited video gamers into the controller pipeline.

The record is part of the FAA's 2026–2028 Air Traffic Controller Workforce Plan, which targets at least 2,300 new trainees in FY27 and 2,400 in FY28 — a pipeline of at least 8,900 new controllers through 2028.

Why the FAA Is Hiring So Aggressively

The controller workforce has not kept pace with the rebound in air travel. A Government Accountability Office review found the FAA employed 13,164 controllers at the end of fiscal 2025, about six percent fewer than in 2015 — while flights using the air traffic control system rose roughly ten percent over the same period.

The 2026 Gamer Recruitment Campaign

In April 2026, the Department of Transportation launched a recruitment campaign aimed squarely at video gamers, arguing that skills developed through gaming — rapid decision-making, concentration, spatial awareness, and managing multiple information sources at once — translate well to air traffic control.

The campaign worked beyond expectations. DOT said the drive generated more than 12,000 qualified applications in just 24 hours and ultimately brought over 2,000 candidates into the hiring pipeline.

The Streamlined 5-Step Hiring Process

  1. Apply during a hiring window. The FAA posts general-public announcements on USAJOBS and faa.gov/atc-hiring — windows open for only a few days, so set alerts and have your resume ready.
  2. Take the ATSA. The Air Traffic Skills Assessment covers memory, spatial reasoning, logic, math, and simulated multitasking. Scoring in the "Best Qualified" band is effectively required to advance.
  3. Clearances — done concurrently. After a Tentative Offer Letter, medical, psychological, and security clearances are processed at the same time rather than sequentially.
  4. Attend the FAA Academy. Three to five months of classroom and simulation training in Oklahoma City on the Tower or En Route track. It is pass/fail.
  5. Train on the job as a "developmental." At your assigned facility, certify position by position for one to three years until you earn Certified Professional Controller (CPC) status.

Eligibility Basics

The FAA's general-public announcements are open to qualified adults under age 31.

How to Apply for FAA Air Traffic Controller Jobs

  1. Create a USAJOBS profile now. Do not wait for a window to open — build your federal resume today.
  2. Set a USAJOBS saved search. Save a search for "Air Traffic Control Specialist" with email alerts so you catch the short windows.
