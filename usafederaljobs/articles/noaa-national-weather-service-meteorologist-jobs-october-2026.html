NOAA National Weather Service Meteorologist Jobs 2026: Direct Hire, GS-5 to GS-9, First Cut-Off Oct 17

The National Weather Service has opened a Direct-Hire standing register for Meteorologists (GS-5 to GS-9, series 1340) at duty stations nationwide — with the first application cut-off on October 17, 2026. Check the education requirements and how standing registers work.

The National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) is hiring Meteorologists for the National Weather Service (NWS) through a Direct Hire Authority standing register open to the public. The announcement (control number 887393000) lists duty stations across the country — from Anchorage and Fairbanks, Alaska, to Key West, Florida, Honolulu, Guam, and Puerto Rico — and the first cut-off for applications is October 17, 2026, at 11:59 PM Eastern Time, with additional cut-offs to follow.

Positions are permanent, full-time, with promotion potential to GS-12, and pay shown on USAJOBS runs from $40,736 to $90,731 per year depending on grade and location. The announcement remains open through September 30, 2027.

What a National Weather Service Meteorologist Does

NWS meteorologists integrate forecast weather, water, and climate hazards with associated societal risks to produce and communicate life-saving decision-support information — the warnings, forecasts, and briefings that emergency managers, aviation, marine operators, and the public rely on during hurricanes, floods, tornadoes, and winter storms.

It is shift work with real responsibility: the announcement specifies rotating shifts including holidays and weekends, and the positions are not remote.

Eligibility: The 1340 Series Education Rule

Direct Hire Authority removes the usual competitive rating and ranking steps — but it does not remove the education bar. The 1340 Meteorology series has strict coursework requirements: a degree in meteorology (or equivalent coursework) including specified semester hours in meteorological science, plus physics and mathematics requirements, including calculus.

The exact semester-hour breakdown is printed on the USAJOBS announcement — read it line by line against your transcripts before applying, and upload unofficial transcripts with your application to help HR verify you qualify.

GS-5 to GS-9: How the Grades Work

The register advertises GS-5, GS-7, and GS-9 with promotion potential to GS-12. Your starting grade depends on your education and experience: a bachelor's degree in meteorology typically qualifies for GS-5 or GS-7, while a master's degree or directly relevant experience can qualify you for GS-7 or GS-9.

How a Standing Register Works

Instead of a single closing date, this announcement uses cut-off dates. HR reviews all applications received by each cut-off — starting October 17, 2026 — and refers qualified candidates. If you miss a cut-off, your application rolls forward to the next one automatically.

How to Apply for NWS Meteorologist Jobs

  1. Find the announcement on USAJOBS.gov. Search for the National Weather Service Meteorologist (control number 887393000) posting.
  2. Check the 1340 education requirements first. Compare the semester-hour requirements against your transcripts — upload unofficial transcripts with your application.
  3. Build a federal resume. Include month/day/year dates, hours per week, and coursework details for every relevant position.
  4. Select your duty-station preferences. The announcement covers many locations; rank them honestly — accepting a referral commits you.
  5. Submit before the first cut-off. Apply well before October 17, 2026, 11:59 PM ET so document issues don't cost you a cut-off cycle.
