NSA Stokes Scholarship 2026: Up to $30K/Year for College and a Guaranteed NSA Job

The Stokes Scholarship Program gives students up to $30,000 per year for college plus a year-round salary, in exchange for spending summers at the NSA. Graduate, and a full-time NSA job is waiting. Applications close October 23.

Few federal programs are as generous as the NSA Stokes Scholarship Program. It is designed for high school seniors planning to major in STEM or language fields: the program pays up to $30,000 per year toward college, adds a year-round salary while you study, and puts you to work at the National Security Agency during your summers. After you earn your degree, a full-time NSA job is waiting for you. The current application cycle closes October 23, so eligible seniors need to move now.

Who Should Apply

The program targets high school seniors who plan to major in STEM disciplines or foreign languages — the fields where NSA's mission needs are deepest. Ideal candidates are strong students with a genuine interest in national security careers, comfortable with the idea of a clearance process, and ready to commit their summers to government service. If you are weighing expensive college plans against a career in intelligence, cybersecurity, or linguistics, this program effectively removes the financial barrier while guaranteeing the career outcome.

How the Summers Work

Stokes scholars do not just receive money — they join the workforce. During summer breaks, scholars work at NSA facilities on real assignments appropriate to their level, rotating through areas that match their studies. By the time you graduate, you have multiple years of insider experience, a security clearance in progress or completed, and a professional network inside one of the most technically advanced agencies in the world. That is why the program can promise a full-time job: you have already proven yourself on the job.

How to Apply

  1. Confirm eligibility: You should be a high school senior (or equivalent) planning a STEM or language major, and a U.S. citizen able to obtain a security clearance.
  2. Find the announcement: Search USAJOBS.gov for the Stokes Scholarship announcement and read the full eligibility and documentation requirements.
  3. Prepare academics: Have transcripts, test scores (if required), and a clear statement of your intended major ready.
  4. Submit before October 23: Do not wait for the deadline week — assemble materials now and submit early.
  5. Plan for the clearance process: NSA positions require extensive background investigation. Being upfront and organized about your history speeds things up.
October 23 is firm: Scholarship announcements with fixed deadlines do not accept late submissions. If you are a qualifying senior, start your application today — gathering transcripts and recommendations takes longer than most students expect.

Students serious about federal careers should also explore the Pathways Internship Program and the Early Career Talent Network at earlycareers.gov, which matches students with opportunities across the government.
