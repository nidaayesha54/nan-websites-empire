FEMA Jobs 2026: Emergency Management Careers, CORE Hiring & How to Apply

FEMA is rebuilding its disaster workforce after losing 17% of staff in fiscal 2025 — and recruiters are actively hiring now. Here is how FEMA's CORE program, Stafford Act hiring authority, and current USAJOBS postings work.

The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) is hiring. After losing 17% of its workforce in fiscal 2025, the agency has walked back earlier plans to halve its headcount — and FEMA recruiters were recently spotted alongside the U.S. Patent and Trademark Office courting candidates at a federal job fair, per GovExec's October 8, 2026 reporting.

FEMA hires through three doors: the competitive service, the Senior Executive Service, and — most distinctively — the CORE program, a disaster-workforce authority that staffs response and recovery missions far faster than standard hiring.

Current postings show the range: a Deputy Assistant Administrator for Logistics Management (SES) published October 14, 2026, Deputy Regional Administrator roles across Regions 4, 5, 6, and 7, plus emergency management and environmental specialists nationwide.

The CORE Program: FEMA's Fast Hiring Lane

CORE — Cadre of On-Call Response/Recovery Employee — is FEMA's primary disaster workforce. Positions are announced under excepted service via Stafford Act hiring authority, so the agency can bring on specialists for hurricanes, wildfires, and floods without competitive rating and ranking.

The trade-offs matter. Veterans' preference does not apply to CORE selection. But after three years of continuous CORE service, employees earn competitive eligibility — the right to compete for permanent full-time FEMA positions as an internal candidate. For many emergency managers, CORE is the on-ramp to a permanent federal career.

What FEMA Is Hiring For Right Now

Recent USAJOBS postings span the agency's mission. At the top: SES roles like the Deputy Assistant Administrator for Logistics Management (Office of Response and Recovery, Washington, DC) and Deputy Regional Administrators leading regional preparedness, response, recovery, and mitigation.

Below the executive tier: Emergency Management Specialists (Mitigation) reviewing community hazard-mitigation plans, Environmental Protection Specialists handling NEPA compliance, and Supervisory Program Analysts running response-division operations. With a leaner workforce, the agency skews toward technical, analytical, and program-management skill sets.

Which Door Fits You

Want speed and disaster-mission work? CORE is the entry point — especially with emergency management, logistics, environmental compliance, or grants experience. Want a permanent appointment from day one? Target FEMA's competitive-service announcements instead.

Either way, mirror the announcement's specialized-experience language in your resume: FEMA HR screens strictly against stated requirements like mitigation planning, NEPA, and logistics program management.

How to Apply for FEMA Jobs

  1. Pick your door: CORE or competitive. CORE means fast disaster-mission hiring under Stafford Act authority; competitive means a permanent appointment from day one.
  2. Search USAJOBS by agency. Filter to the Federal Emergency Management Agency and scan for your specialty — emergency management, environmental, logistics, program analysis.
  3. Mirror the specialized-experience language. FEMA HR screens against exact requirements — echo the announcement's terminology in your resume.
  4. Understand the CORE trade-off. No veterans' preference in CORE selection — but three years of continuous service earns competitive eligibility for permanent FEMA jobs.
  5. Submit a complete package before the closing date. Include transcripts, SF-50s (current feds), and any licenses the announcement requires.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a FEMA CORE employee?

CORE stands for Cadre of On-Call Response/Recovery Employee — FEMA's disaster workforce, hired under excepted-service Stafford Act authority for response and recovery missions.

Does veterans' preference apply to FEMA CORE hiring?

No. FEMA states veterans' preference does not apply to CORE selection. But after three years of continuous CORE service, employees earn competitive eligibility for permanent FEMA positions.

What is Stafford Act hiring authority?

Authority under the Stafford Disaster Relief Act letting FEMA hire quickly outside the normal competitive process to staff disaster response and recovery operations.

Is FEMA hiring right now?

Yes. FEMA lost 17% of its workforce in fiscal 2025 and recruiters were recently spotted at a federal job fair. Openings span emergency management, environmental compliance, logistics, and SES leadership.

Where do I find FEMA job openings?

All FEMA postings — CORE, competitive service, and SES — are advertised on USAJOBS.gov. Search by agency: Federal Emergency Management Agency.
