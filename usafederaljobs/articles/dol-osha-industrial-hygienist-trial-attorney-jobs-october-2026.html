Industrial Hygienist (GS-9/11) — OSHA

Industrial hygienists protect workers from hazardous exposures — the behind-the-scenes science that prevents occupational illness. This OSHA role calls for experience in workplace safety inspections, identifying and evaluating occupational health hazards, investigating unsafe working conditions, and applying safety and health standards. With openings in Alaska, California, Idaho, Nevada, Oregon, and Washington, it is one of the broader geographic spreads in this month's DOL roundup, and the GS-9/11 ladder gives strong promotion potential.

Trial Attorney (Labor) (GS-12/13/14) — Office of the Solicitor

The Office of the Solicitor is DOL's legal arm, and these Atlanta and Nashville positions put you in court defending workers' rights. The role calls for legal research, persuasive legal writing, litigation experience, and handling complex labor and employment law matters. At the GS-12/13/14 ladder, this is a senior litigation track for attorneys who want their caseload to shape federal labor law rather than billable hours.

Why DOL Enforcement Careers Stand Out

DOL enforcement roles combine mission-driven work with genuine career ladders. OSHA and the Solicitor's office hire steadily because workplace-safety and labor-law enforcement never pauses — these are not one-off postings. Both announcements in this roundup carry multi-grade ladders (GS-9/11 and GS-12/13/14), meaning you can enter at the grade matching your experience and promote non-competitively up the ladder.

Deadline is tight: both announcements close October 26, 2026 — three days from this article's publication. Federal resumes take time to build properly, so start your application the same day you read this.

How to Apply for DOL Jobs

  1. Pick your announcement. Use the DOL Careers link in the Key Details table (Industrial Hygienist) or search "Trial Attorney" on the same portal.
  2. Match the grade. Hygienist applicants should check GS-9 vs GS-11 qualification thresholds; attorneys should verify bar and litigation requirements per grade.
  3. Build a federal resume. Detail inspections conducted, hazards evaluated, or cases litigated — with dates, hours per week, and outcomes.
  4. Attach credentials. Transcripts for the hygienist series; law degree, bar admission proof, and writing samples where requested for attorney roles.
  5. Submit by October 26, 2026. DOL's portal closes announcements at 11:59 PM Eastern on the closing date.
