What an SEC Financial Economist Does

Commission economists provide economic guidance and research expertise across rulemaking, enforcement, and market-risk assessment — and are expected to pursue independent research aimed at top-tier professional journals.

The work spans DERA's specialized offices — asset management, corporate finance, financial intermediaries, litigation economics, and markets — with roles focused on specific policy areas, risk assessment and modeling, or litigation support.

Typical Qualifications

DERA recruits Economic Fellows (two-year term appointments, extendable up to four years, with eligibility to compete for permanent roles as U.S. citizens), permanent Financial Economists, and Visiting Academics (one-year renewable appointments). For all of them, the core qualification is education: a Ph.D. in finance, accounting, economics, mathematics, statistics, or a similarly quantitative field.

Competitive candidates show applied-microeconomics expertise plus a track record of empirical research, and the ability to apply economics and econometrics in support of the SEC's mission: protecting investors, facilitating capital formation, and maintaining fair and orderly markets. Confirm the exact qualifications on the official announcement.

Pay, Benefits, and Career Path

The SEC is on an excepted-service (SK) pay scale, separate from the General Schedule — and the public announcement summary did not confirm a specific pay range, so check the official posting for the current salary band.

How to Apply for the SEC Financial Economist Jobs

  1. Open the official announcement. Visit sec.usajobs.gov and find the Financial Economist — Division of Economic and Risk Analysis posting, or use the announcement link in the Key Details table above.
  2. Confirm the cutoff and appointment type. Check the closing date and whether the vacancy covers fellow, permanent, or visiting-academic appointments before investing time in your package.
  3. Lead with research. Your CV should foreground publications, working papers, and empirical work in finance, securities markets, or applied microeconomics — this is what DERA screens on.
  4. Prepare your documents. CV, transcripts, and any required supporting documentation uploaded before you start the application.
  5. Submit before the closing time. USAJOBS closes announcements at 11:59 PM Eastern on the closing date. Apply early to avoid last-minute technical problems.
