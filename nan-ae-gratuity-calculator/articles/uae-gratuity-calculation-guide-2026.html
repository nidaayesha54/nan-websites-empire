UAE End-of-Service Gratuity 2026: Complete Calculation Guide (Article 51)

Your UAE end-of-service gratuity is often the largest single payment you'll ever receive from an employer — and one of the most miscalculated. Here's exactly how Article 51 works in 2026, the myths that produce wrong figures, and how to check your own number in 30 seconds.

The law: Article 51 in plain English

UAE end-of-service gratuity for private-sector employees is governed by Article 51 of Federal Decree-Law No. 33 of 2021. The essentials:

  • You qualify after one full year of continuous service. Less than a year = no gratuity.
  • Years 1–5: 21 days of basic wage per year of service.
  • Year 6 onwards: 30 days of basic wage per year.
  • Calculated on basic salary only — never allowances.
  • Total is capped at two years' wage.
  • Your employer must pay within 14 days of your contract ending.

Contract type doesn't matter: since the 2022 reforms, private-sector contracts are fixed-term, and the old unlimited-contract gratuity reductions no longer apply. Resignation vs termination also doesn't change the formula.

The gratuity formula, step by step

Daily basic wage = Monthly basic salary ÷ 30
Gratuity = (Daily wage × 21 × first 5 years) + (Daily wage × 30 × years beyond 5)
  1. Take your last basic monthly salary (the one in your contract, excluding housing, transport, and other allowances).
  2. Divide by 30 → your daily basic wage.
  3. Multiply by 21 for each of the first five years of service.
  4. Multiply by 30 for each year beyond five.
  5. Add pro-rata for the unfinished final year (days worked ÷ 365 at the applicable tier).
  6. Exclude any unpaid leave days from the service period.
  7. Cap the total at two years' wage.

Worked example: AED 10,000 basic, 7 years

Daily basic wage10,000 ÷ 30 = AED 333.33
Years 1–5 (21 days each)21 × 5 × 333.33 = AED 35,000
Years 6–7 (30 days each)30 × 2 × 333.33 = AED 20,000
Total gratuityAED 55,000

5 myths that produce wrong figures

Myth 1: "It's calculated on my total salary." No — basic salary only. With basic commonly 50–60% of the package, using the gross figure inflates your estimate by thousands.

Myth 2: "I get nothing if I resign." Wrong. Resignation and termination use the same formula under current law.

Myth 3: "Unlimited contracts get less." Outdated — unlimited contracts were abolished; everyone is on fixed-term now.

Myth 4: "Part-years don't count." They do — fractions of a year are calculated pro-rata after the first completed year.

Myth 5: "My employer can delay payment." The law says 14 days from contract end. Delays are grounds for a MOHRE complaint.

The two-year cap and pro-rata rules

The cap of two years' wage rarely binds ordinary employees — you'd need roughly 28+ years of service to hit it — but it exists. Note the subtlety: the cap is counted on total wage (including regular allowances), while the 21/30-day accrual stays on basic salary. Also remember: unpaid absence days are excluded from the service count, so long unpaid leaves slightly reduce your total.

The alternative savings scheme

Since Cabinet Resolution No. 96 of 2023, employers can voluntarily enroll staff in the Alternative End-of-Service Benefits System — an investment-based savings fund (providers include National Bonds, FAB, Daman Investments, and Ghaf Benefits). If you're enrolled, the employer contributes monthly to the fund instead of the lump-sum gratuity. Key protection: gratuity accrued before enrolment is preserved under the standard Article 51 calculation, and payout is still due within 14 days of termination.

Claiming unpaid gratuity

  1. Calculate your exact entitlement with our calculator — bring the number, not a guess.
  2. Write to HR citing Article 51 and the 14-day payment rule.
  3. File a MOHRE complaint if payment is delayed — keep your contract, salary slips, and joining/exit dates as evidence.
  4. Note the exclusions: domestic workers fall under a separate law (Federal Decree-Law No. 9 of 2022), and DIFC/ADGM free zones have their own regimes — this guide covers mainland private-sector employees.

What exactly counts as "basic wage"?

This is where most disputes start. Your basic wage is the fixed amount stated as "basic salary" in your MOHRE-registered contract — excluding housing allowance, transport allowance, cost-of-living allowances, overtime, commissions, bonuses, and profit shares. As a rough guide, basic salary commonly makes up 50–60% of total compensation in UAE contracts, so the difference between "basic" and "gross" can easily be AED 20,000+ on a 7-year gratuity.

Red flag: some contracts artificially split salary into a tiny basic plus huge allowances to shrink gratuity liability. MOHRE and the courts look at the registered contract, so read the basic-salary line before you sign — it's the number your end-of-service payout will be built on. The gratuity uses your last basic wage, so a late-career raise boosts the whole calculation.

Part-time, job-share and temporary contracts

Part-time employees accrue gratuity proportionally: your contractual hours per year divided by full-time hours, applied to the full-time Article 51 formula. Temporary/contract staff on fixed terms accrue the same way for the contract's duration — but remember the one-year minimum: an 11-month contract renewed once crosses into entitlement territory, so track renewals carefully.

Checklist: before your last working day

  1. Screenshot your MOHRE contract showing the basic salary figure.
  2. Confirm your exact joining date and last working day — a few days can shift the pro-rata.
  3. List unpaid leave days (they're excluded from service time).
  4. Calculate your entitlement with our gratuity calculator and compare with HR's figure.
  5. Get the settlement in writing with the breakdown (21-day tier, 30-day tier, pro-rata) — not just a total.
  6. Check the 14-day clock: payment is due within 14 days of contract end. Mark the date.
  7. Alternative scheme members: confirm your fund balance statement matches the preserved pre-enrolment gratuity.

Beyond gratuity: your full exit package

Gratuity is the headline, but a UAE exit settlement has several other lines — and HR sometimes "forgets" the smaller ones:

  • Unpaid salary through your last working day, obviously.
  • Accrued annual leave payout: unused leave days are paid at your basic wage rate. With 30 calendar days of annual leave a year, even a few unused days are real money.
  • Notice period pay: if either side waives the notice period (30–90 days per contract), the waiving side's entitlement converts to notice pay — a full month's (or more) wage.
  • End-of-service air ticket: many contracts include a repatriation ticket; check whether yours does and whether it's cash-convertible.
  • Overtime arrears: run your unpaid overtime through our overtime calculator before signing any "full and final" settlement — signing usually waives future claims.

Never sign a "full and final settlement" you haven't verified line by line. Once signed, reopening the gratuity figure is extremely difficult.
