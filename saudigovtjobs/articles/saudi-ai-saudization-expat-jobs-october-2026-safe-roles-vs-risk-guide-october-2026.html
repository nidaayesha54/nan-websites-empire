Saudi Government Jobs 2026

Saudi Arabia declared 2026 its Year of Artificial Intelligence — and from October 2026 dozens of administrative roles are reserved for Saudi nationals. Here is the honest guide for expats: which jobs are safe, which are at risk, and how to protect your career.

✅ Updated October 20, 2026
Home » Saudi Government Jobs » Articles

Saudi Arabia declared 2026 its Year of Artificial Intelligence: a third of businesses now use AI tools, and from 4 October 2026 dozens of administrative support jobs are reserved fully for Saudi nationals. The impact is visible in job adverts and work-permit occupation rules.

The useful question for an expat is not whether AI will "empty the Kingdom of foreign workers" — it will not. It is which side of the change your job sits on. AI, alongside Saudization, is squeezing the middle: routine office roles that software can now draft, sort or schedule — and that the government also wants Saudis to hold. At the top end it is doing the opposite: data-centre engineers, AI and data specialists, cybersecurity staff, clinicians and senior technical people are in demand, and the Kingdom is building residency routes to attract them.

Two expats in the same company can have opposite experiences this year. An administrative assistant whose occupation sits on the 100% Saudization list has a real problem to solve. A network engineer who has moved into cloud and AI infrastructure is probably getting calls from recruiters.

Honest framing: Saudization lists are official policy, not rumour — if your work-permit occupation is on a 100%-Saudi list, your employer cannot legally renew you in that role. Check your occupation on Qiwa and confirm with your HR department rather than relying on social-media lists.

Roles Under Pressure (and Why)

  • Administrative assistants and office support: the double hit — AI tools now handle drafting, scheduling and sorting, while the 4 October 2026 rules reserve these occupations for Saudis;
  • Data entry and basic processing: among the most automatable roles in any organisation;
  • Routine customer-service and reception: chatbots and self-service kiosks are replacing first-line human handling;

Roles in Demand

  • AI and data specialists: machine-learning engineers, data scientists, AI product roles — the Year of AI is creating these jobs;
  • Cybersecurity professionals: NCA compliance is driving hiring across government and regulated sectors;
  • Data-centre and cloud engineers: the infrastructure behind the AI push;
  • Clinicians and specialist healthcare staff: structural demand independent of AI trends;
  • Senior technical and project leadership: giga-project delivery still needs experienced international expertise.

How Expats Can Protect Their Position

1
Check your work-permit occupation on Qiwa — confirm whether it sits on any Saudization list and what percentage applies.
2
Move up the skill ladder toward AI-adjacent work: cloud certifications, data skills, cybersecurity credentials — the skills the Kingdom is actively importing.
3
Document your specialist value: employers justify expat hires where Saudi talent is scarce — niche technical expertise is your leverage.
4
Watch residency and premium-residency routes for high-skill professionals, which reduce dependence on employer sponsorship.
5
Build a 6–12 month contingency plan: savings buffer, updated CV, and sector awareness.

The Bigger Picture

Two forces are reshaping Saudi Arabia's labour market: AI automating routine work and Saudization reserving it for nationals. The expat roles that survive are the ones neither force can replace: deep technical skill, clinical expertise and senior leadership. In October 2026, the honest move is to reskill toward the demand list.
