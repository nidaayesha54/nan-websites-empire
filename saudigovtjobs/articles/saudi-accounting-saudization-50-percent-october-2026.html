Saudi Accounting Saudization 50% — From Oct 27

Saudi Arabia raises the accounting Saudization quota to 50% from 27 October 2026 — the next step on the path to 70%. What it means for Saudi accountants, employers and jobseekers.

✅ Updated October 24, 2026
Home » Saudi Government Jobs » Articles

Saudi Arabia will increase the Saudization quota for accountants to 50% effective 27 October 2026, the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (HRSD) has announced alongside the Ministry of Commerce. The move is the second stage of a phased localisation plan for the accounting profession — and it directly creates hiring pressure for Saudi accountants across the private sector.

The timeline is aggressive: an initial 40% mandate took effect on 27 October 2025; the rate rises to 50% on 27 October 2026, then to 60% on 27 October 2027 and 70% on 27 October 2028, according to the reported schedule. As the quota climbs, the permissible share of non-Saudi accountants in covered firms shrinks from the current 50% to just 30% by 2028.

The rules target private-sector establishments employing five or more workers across 44 designated accounting professions. Licensed accounting offices remain exempt under a separate regulatory framework. And the quality bar is explicit: a Saudi national counts toward the quota only if they hold accreditation from the Saudi Organization for Certified Public Accountants (SOCPA) — simply hiring a national is not enough.

Fact conflict flagged: the reported timeline puts the final 70% stage on 27 October 2028, while an earlier analysis of the same Nitaqat accounting decision gives the final target as 70% by 2030. The 50% rate from 27 October 2026 is consistent across sources, but treat the final-stage year as unconfirmed until HRSD publishes it. This article is also single-sourced — verify the rates on HRSD or Qiwa before acting.

What This Means for Saudi Accountants

  • More openings: every covered firm below 50% must hire Saudi accountants — or restructure — by the October deadline;
  • SOCPA matters: accreditation is the counting criterion, so SOCPA-accredited accountants are the most hireable candidates in this wave;
  • Career leverage: with the quota rising annually, Saudi accountants gain negotiating power on salary and role seniority;
  • Graduate pipeline: accounting graduates should complete SOCPA accreditation early — firms will be screening for it in every hire.

What This Means for Employers

  • Compliance risk: firms below the quota face Nitaqat band consequences — restricted visas, hiring freezes and service limitations;
  • Qiwa documentation: only Saudi employees whose contracts are documented on Qiwa count toward the ratio;
  • Planning horizon: the 60% and 70% stages mean workforce planning must look three years ahead, not one;
  • Exemption check: licensed accounting offices operate under a separate framework — confirm your firm's classification before restructuring.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does the 50% accounting Saudization quota take effect?

27 October 2026 — the second stage of the phased accounting Saudization plan, following the initial 40% mandate on 27 October 2025.

What is the full accounting Saudization timeline?

40% from 27 October 2025, 50% from 27 October 2026, 60% from 27 October 2027 and 70% from 27 October 2028, per the reported timeline. One secondary source instead gives 70% by 2030 — verify the final-stage date on HRSD.

Which employers are covered?

Private-sector establishments employing five or more workers across 44 designated accounting professions. Licensed accounting offices are exempt under a separate framework.

What must Saudi accountants hold to count toward the quota?

Accreditation from the Saudi Organization for Certified Public Accountants (SOCPA) — simply hiring a national is not enough; professional benchmarks must be met.

Does this create jobs for Saudi accountants?

Yes — as quotas rise from 50% toward 70%, employers must progressively replace or add Saudi accounting staff, opening roles for SOCPA-accredited Saudi accountants.

Where do I verify the official quota?

On the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Development (HRSD) portal and the Qiwa platform, which publish the binding Saudization rates and profession lists.
