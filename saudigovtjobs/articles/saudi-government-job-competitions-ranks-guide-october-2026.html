Saudi Govt Job Competitions 2026 — Ranks 5–10 Guide

How Saudi government job competitions work: ranked civil-service posts, open musabaqa contests, written tests and Jadarat applications — explained with SASO's live 37-vacancy example.

✅ Updated October 24, 2026
Home » Saudi Government Jobs » Articles

Many Saudi government jobs are filled not by direct appointment but by open job competitions (musabaqa) — a formal, merit-based process in which an entity announces ranked posts, screens applicants, runs written tests and appoints from a ranked list. A live example is running right now: the Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization (SASO) has announced the names of accepted applicants and the written-test dates for its 37 vacancies, for Saudi men, in ranks five to ten.

Understanding how competitions work matters because the process differs sharply from private-sector hiring: there is no negotiation over the rank or the salary band, the written test is eliminatory, and missing a test date ends your candidacy. This guide explains the system step by step, using SASO's competition as a concrete example.

How a Competition Works, Step by Step

  • Announcement: the entity publishes the competition — number of vacancies, ranks, qualifications and the application window — on Jadarat and its own site;
  • Application: candidates apply online, uploading qualifications, experience certificates and national ID details;
  • Screening: the entity's job-competition committee checks eligibility and publishes the list of accepted applicants — as SASO has now done;
  • Written test: accepted applicants sit a test at a published venue and date — SASO runs tests in Riyadh, Jeddah and Dammam;
  • Ranking and appointment: test results (plus any interviews) produce a ranked list, and appointments follow the rank order.

Ranks 5–10 — What They Mean

Saudi civil-service posts are graded on a rank scale, and ranks 5–10 cover the middle professional tier: qualified administrative, technical and specialist roles that require a degree or diploma plus experience. Lower ranks (1–4) are support roles; higher ranks (11–15) are senior management. For most Saudi graduates and mid-career professionals, ranks 5–10 are the realistic entry and progression band — which is why competitions at these ranks attract the largest applicant pools.

Test-Day Rules — Do Not Get Turned Away

  • National identity is mandatory: SASO states explicitly that applicants will not be allowed to enter the test without their national identity;
  • Arrive early: SASO requires arrival half an hour before the test time to register personal information at the test hall;
  • Know your venue: SASO tests run at three sites — Riyadh HQ, Jeddah and Dammam — and the venue is assigned, not chosen;
  • Check the published list: only applicants named on the accepted-applicants announcement may sit the test.

How to Find the Next Competition

1
Register on Jadarat, the national employment platform, and set alerts for your rank band and specialisation.
2
Follow the careers pages of target entities — ministries and authorities publish competitions on their own sites first, as SASO does on saso.gov.sa.
3
Prepare your documents in advance: degree/diploma certificates, experience letters and national ID — competitions have short windows.
4
When accepted, note the test date, venue and arrival time immediately — and bring your national identity.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Saudi government job competition (musabaqa)?

An open competitive process through which a government entity fills ranked civil-service posts — applicants are screened, tested and ranked on merit, rather than appointed directly.

What are ranks 5–10 in the Saudi civil service?

The middle professional grades of the Saudi civil-service rank scale, covering qualified administrative, technical and specialist posts — such as SASO's recent 37 vacancies for Saudis.

Who can apply for Saudi government job competitions?

Saudi nationals meeting the post's qualification and experience requirements. SASO's 37-vacancy competition, for example, was explicitly for Saudi men.

What does the competition process involve?

Application, screening against the post requirements, a written test (with published dates and venues), and then interviews or further selection stages for those who pass.

What must I bring to a Saudi government written test?

Your national identity card, and arrive at least half an hour early to register your personal information at the test hall — SASO states applicants are not admitted without their national identity.

Where are Saudi government vacancies announced?

On the Jadarat national employment platform and on the hiring entity's own website — for example, SASO publishes its competition announcements on saso.gov.sa.
