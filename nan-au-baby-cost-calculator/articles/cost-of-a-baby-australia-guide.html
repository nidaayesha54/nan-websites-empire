How Much Does a Baby Cost in Australia? First-Year Breakdown (2026)

The real first-year cost of a baby in Australia: one-off gear, monthly essentials, childcare, and the government payments that offset it.

In-depth 2026 guide~6 min readFree

"How much does a baby cost?" is the question every expecting Australian couple asks — and the honest answer is that it depends more on your choices than on any price list. Published 2026 estimates put the cost from pregnancy through the first year at roughly $4,500–$12,000 on the public route and $18,000–$35,000 privately, before childcare. Add daycare and the first year can climb another $8,000–$25,000.

But the biggest cost isn't on any list: it's lost income while you're on leave. This guide breaks down the real first-year budget — one-off gear, monthly essentials, childcare, and the government payments that offset it all — so you can plan with real numbers. The two budgets: one-off gear vs. ongoing costs

The planning mistake most couples make is budgeting hard for the nursery and being blindsided by the recurring costs. Separate them:

One-off, front-loaded: cot + mattress, pram, car seat, carrier, nursery furniture, bath, monitor, initial clothing. This is the visible spend everyone worries about — and the most compressible, because so much can be bought secondhand, borrowed, or skipped. A budget setup runs ~$1,500–$3,000; mid-range $4,000–$7,000; premium $8,000+.

Recurring, relentless: nappies, wipes, formula (if used), clothes (they outgrow everything every few months), extra power/water/laundry, and eventually childcare. Budget roughly $150–$400/month for consumables alone in year one — and this is the line that erodes a budget quietly because it's hard to cut.

Childcare dwarfs all of it once it starts: centre-based day care averages around $130–$150/day in the capital cities. Three days a week for six months ≈ $10,000–$12,000 before the Child Care Subsidy — which is why the subsidy section below matters more than any pram comparison.

The income gap: the cost nobody lists

A household going from two incomes to one-and-a-half for 6–12 months will usually find that gap dwarfs every pram, cot, and packet of nappies combined. Model this first, before a single piece of gear:

  1. Your household income during leave (your leave pay + partner's income + any employer top-up)
  2. The point at which each of you returns, and at what hours
  3. The gap between that and your normal household income — that's your true "baby cost"

Paid Parental Leave softens this enormously (see below), but it pays at the national minimum wage — a pay cut for most households. Knowing the size of the gap in advance is what separates a stressful year from a manageable one.

Government support: Paid Parental Leave (26 weeks)

From 1 July 2026, eligible families get 130 days — 26 weeks of Parental Leave Pay, paid at $200.94/day ($1,004.70/week) before tax. The full entitlement is worth $26,122.20 before tax, plus a 12% superannuation contribution (~$3,135) paid by the ATO into your super fund for children born from 1 July 2025. Other countries take different approaches — Singapore, for example, uses a Baby Bonus scheme (see our Singapore Baby Bonus calculator).

Key details:

  • 20 days are reserved for the other parent on a use-it-or-lose-it basis — they can't be transferred.
  • Days are flexible: take them in blocks or single days, including weekends, any time in the first 2 years.
  • It's taxable income — Services Australia withholds 15% by default, which may not be enough.
  • Eligibility is based on work history and income thresholds — check Services Australia's criteria early, ideally before the birth.

Government support: Child Care Subsidy

The Child Care Subsidy (CCS) is the single biggest lever on year-one costs for returning parents. From 6 July 2026:

  • Maximum 90% subsidy for families earning up to $88,520, tapering by 1 percentage point per $5,000 above that, reaching zero at $538,520.
  • The subsidy applies to your provider's fee or the hourly rate cap ($15.19/hour for centre-based day care), whichever is lower.
  • Since 5 January 2026, the 3 Day Guarantee gives every CCS-eligible family at least 72 subsidised hours per fortnight regardless of work or study — the old activity test is gone.

Worked impact: $140/day × 3 days × 26 weeks = $10,920 gross. At an 85% subsidy (typical middle-income family), your out-of-pocket is ~$1,640. At 50%, ~$5,460. The subsidy rate is the variable that matters — model yours before choosing days.

Smart ways to cut the first-year bill

Buy the big three secondhand. Cots, prams, and carriers depreciate brutally; quality secondhand saves 50–70%. (Buy car seats new — you need the full crash history.)

Don't stockpile newborn sizes. Babies outgrow newborn clothes in weeks. Buy minimal newborn, more 0–3 and 3–6 months.

Cloth nappies for part-timers. Even part-time cloth use cuts the nappy bill substantially; the laundry cost is real but smaller than disposables.

Time the return to work around the subsidy. The CCS year and your leave can be sequenced so subsidised care starts the week you return — every unsubsidised week is the most expensive week.

Claim everything you're owed. Beyond PPL and CCS: Family Tax Benefit (Parts A & B), the Newborn Upfront Payment and Newborn Supplement for eligible families. Many couples leave FTB unclaimed for months.

The pre-baby financial checklist

Three months before the due date, work through this list: (1) confirm your Paid Parental Leave eligibility with Services Australia and note your expected payment dates; (2) ask your employer about any paid parental leave top-up and how it interacts with the government payment; (3) price your likely childcare and model the Child Care Subsidy at your income level — this single number shapes your return-to-work timing; (4) build or top up a 3-month buffer for the income gap; and (5) check your private health cover's obstetrics waiting period if you're considering private care. Couples who do this in advance consistently report lower financial stress in the first year — not because babies got cheaper, but because nothing in the budget was a surprise.

FAQs

How much should we save before the baby arrives?

Aim to cover the income gap, not just the gear: 3–6 months of the difference between your normal household income and your on-leave income, plus ~$5,000–$8,000 for setup and surprises. Gear is the smaller half of the equation.

Is private health insurance worth it for pregnancy?

Only if obstetric cover is already active — there's a 12-month waiting period for pregnancy cover, so you can't buy it after conceiving. The public system covers pregnancy and birth at no cost; private buys choice of obstetrician.

Can both parents get Paid Parental Leave?

The 130 days are shared, with 20 reserved for the other parent. Up to 20 days can be taken concurrently. Single parents can access the full entitlement. Separated parents should also understand the legal process costs — see our divorce cost guide for Australia.

When should we join childcare waitlists?

As early as possible — ideally during pregnancy. Popular centres in Sydney and Melbourne have 6–12 month waits, and your return-to-work date depends on securing a place.

Do grandparents providing care affect the subsidy?

Informal grandparent care doesn't attract CCS (it's for approved care). But it can delay or reduce the days of formal care you need — often the biggest saving of all.
