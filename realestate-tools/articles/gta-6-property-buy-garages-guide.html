GTA 6 Property: The Only Thing You Can Actually Buy

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Can you buy property in GTA 6? The question has a clean answer and a messy one. The clean answer is that the only property with a price tag is a garage. The messy part is the flood of lists claiming forty buyable properties with daily income — apartments, nightclubs, warehouses, even an aircraft carrier — none of which is backed by anything Rockstar has published. This guide separates what is confirmed from what is invented, and explains exactly what you can buy, where, and why.

The short version: safehouses come with the story, businesses belong to characters, and garages are the one thing on sale. If a list tells you otherwise, it is guessing.

What you can buy: garages

Garages are the property GTA 6 sells. They arrive in the Vice City Real Estate app, which lands on your phone during Chapter 2 after a job for Moses, the contact who registers your stolen cars, and every garage for sale is bought there. What a garage gives you is room for more cars: each space holds another registered car, so buying garages is how you build up a collection of personal vehicles. You start with room for three personal vehicles, and when every space is full, you have to sell a car before another can become yours.

Two garages come with what you buy rather than with in-game money, and Rockstar describes both the same way: each has a weapon locker plus a secure place to deposit stolen goods to be fenced. The Shore Court personal garage sits a stone's throw from Ocean Beach and comes with the pre-order pack, along with a '55 Vapid Stanier. The Paradise Garage in Watson Bay comes with the Ultimate Edition, along with a '67 Vapid Dominator Buggy. The Ultimate Edition also parks a Dinka Enduro motorcycle and a Crest Kayak at Jason's safehouse.

What you cannot buy: houses

You cannot buy a house in GTA 6. Your home comes from the story, not from a property listing. Rockstar North co-studio head Rob Nelson told Famitsu before launch that the story unfolds chapter by chapter and the characters' safehouses change with it, much like the camps in Red Dead Redemption 2. The first home is not even Jason's: he is living in a house owned by Brian Heder, a classic drug runner from the golden age of smuggling in the Keys, who lets Jason stay rent-free in return for help with local shakedowns.

The pink Vice City apartment shown in the Extended Look is not Jason and Lucia's place either. Nelson said they are staying in someone else's home. Rockstar has not given a number for how many safehouses there are, and has not announced any way to buy a house or apartment in story mode. The story decides where the pair sleep, which is why you cannot buy a home — and it is also why the safehouse feels like a home rather than a menu: the pair share the place, keep daily routines, and start the day with coffee, and the fridge stays stocked.

What you cannot buy: businesses

Leonida is packed with businesses, and the ones Rockstar names all belong to characters. Boobie Ike's empire spans real estate, a strip club and a recording studio, paid for, in his words, by the club money and the drug money. Brian Heder runs his boat yard in the Keys. None of them is for sale. GTA 5's story let you buy businesses that paid every week; GTA 6 has not shown anything like it, and no passive income has been announced for story mode.

The Ultimate Edition's five extra shops are easy to mistake for businesses you own. Rideout Customs in Vice City, One-Eyed Willie's at Lake Leonida, Sara's Unisex Salon, Stock 305 and the Electric Fang tattoo parlour are what Rockstar calls special destinations open for business with the Ultimate Edition: places to customise your cars and your look. You shop there; you do not own them.

Why the 40-property lists are wrong

The lists circulating with forty purchasable properties, daily income rates and return-on-investment tables describe a game that does not exist yet. They borrow the shape of GTA Online businesses — weed farms, cocaine lockups, nightclubs with daily payouts — and paste it onto a story mode where Rockstar has announced none of it. Some of the numbers even contradict each other across lists, which is what happens when the figures are invented rather than measured.

That does not mean businesses will never come to GTA 6. The online mode is not part of the November 19 launch and is not dated yet, so online apartments and businesses do not apply to the launch game at all. What is true today is simple: garages yes, safehouses no, and no business you can own. Spend accordingly — garages and car spaces first, and the rest on gear, gym memberships, car upgrades and the money the story itself asks you to raise.

FAQ

Can you buy property in GTA 6? Only garages, as far as Rockstar has said. Buy them through the Vice City Real Estate app after a Chapter 2 job for Moses. Homes come with the story, and the clubs, marinas and studios belong to characters.

How many cars can you own? You start with room for three personal vehicles. Buying garages adds spaces; when every space is full, sell a car to make room for a new one.

Do the bonus garages cost in-game money? No. Shore Court comes with the pre-order pack and Paradise with the Ultimate Edition, each with a weapon locker and a place to deposit stolen goods to be fenced.

The bottom line: in GTA 6, the property market is one item long — garages. Everything else is the story's, or a character's, and the lists selling you forty properties are selling you a different game.
