The 7 Costliest House Flip Mistakes Beginners Make

Television makes flipping look like a 45-minute transformation. Real flips are won or lost in the spreadsheet before demolition day. Here are the seven errors that turn profits into lessons.

1. ARV fantasy

Beginners pick the nicest comp on the street and call it ARV. Pros use the median of sold comps within half a mile, similar size and age, last 6 months — then subtract for anything their property lacks. Overestimate ARV by $20,000 and you've usually overpaid by $14,000 (70% rule math).

2. Rehab estimates from optimism

Contractor bids routinely run 15–25% over initial estimates once walls open up. Budget a 15% contingency on top of detailed bids, not instead of them. Enter the honest number in the flip calculator — it only helps if the inputs are real.

3. Ignoring holding costs

Every month you own the property costs money: loan payments, taxes, insurance, utilities. A "quick" 6-month flip at $2,500/month carrying cost is $15,000. Delays are the silent profit killer.

4. Forgetting selling costs

Agent commissions, title fees, and concessions run 6–10% of the sale price. On a $280,000 sale, that's $16,800–$28,000 — often the entire expected profit for beginners who "forgot" it.

5. Over-improving for the neighborhood

Quartz countertops in a laminate neighborhood don't appraise. Renovate to the comp standard, not your dream kitchen. The appraisal — not your taste — sets the ceiling.

6. No contractor vetting

The cheapest bid is the most expensive bid if the contractor disappears mid-demo. Check licenses, call three references, visit a finished job, and never pay more than 10% upfront.

7. Skipping the 70% rule

The 70% rule (pay ≤ 70% of ARV minus repairs) exists because mistakes 1–6 are so common. It builds your margin of error into the purchase price. Run every deal through the flip profit calculator before making an offer — it takes two minutes.
