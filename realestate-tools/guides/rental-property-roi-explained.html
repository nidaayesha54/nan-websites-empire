Rental Property ROI Explained: Cap Rate, Cash Flow & Cash-on-Cash

Three numbers run every rental deal: cash flow, cap rate, and cash-on-cash return. They answer different questions, and mixing them up is one of the most common beginner mistakes. Here's what each one means, with a worked example.

On this page
  • Cash flow: can you pay the bills?
  • Cap rate: how the property performs
  • Cash-on-cash: how YOUR money performs
  • Which metric matters most?

Cash flow: can you pay the bills?

Cash flow is the money left each month after everything is paid: mortgage, taxes, insurance, maintenance, vacancy allowance, and management.

Example: A $250,000 property rents for $2,200/month. After a 5% vacancy allowance ($110), taxes ($250), insurance ($100), maintenance ($208), management ($176), and a $1,331 mortgage payment, you're left with about $25/month. That deal barely cash-flows — one water heater replacement wipes out a year of profit.

Positive cash flow means the property pays for itself. Negative cash flow means you feed it every month — only acceptable if you're deliberately betting on appreciation.

Cap rate: how the property performs

Cap rate = Net Operating Income ÷ Purchase Price. NOI is annual rental income minus operating expenses, before the mortgage payment. It measures the property itself, ignoring how you financed it — which makes it perfect for comparing two properties side by side.

Example: The same property has annual NOI of $16,268. Cap rate = $16,268 ÷ $250,000 = 6.5%.

In 2026, typical US cap rates run 5–10%: lower in expensive coastal markets, higher in the Midwest and South where prices are lower relative to rents.

Cash-on-cash: how YOUR money performs

Cash-on-cash return = Annual Cash Flow ÷ Cash Invested. Unlike cap rate, it includes your mortgage payment and down payment — it answers "what is my actual money earning?"

Example: Annual cash flow of $300 ÷ $50,000 down payment = 0.6% cash-on-cash. Terrible — your down payment would earn more in a savings account. This is why leverage cuts both ways: it amplifies returns on good deals and strangles weak ones.

Many investors target 8–12% cash-on-cash return.

Which metric matters most?

  • Comparing properties? Use cap rate — financing doesn't distort it.
  • Deciding if YOU should buy? Use cash-on-cash and monthly cash flow — they reflect your loan and down payment.
