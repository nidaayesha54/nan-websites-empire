How to Analyze a Rental Deal in 2026: The 15-Minute Method

Experienced investors screen dozens of listings a week. They don't spend hours on each one — they run a fast, consistent process and only go deep on deals that pass. Here's that process.

Step 1: Gather the five numbers

Every analysis starts with the same five inputs — get them from the listing, the county tax site, and rent comps:

  1. Purchase price — the asking price (you'll negotiate later).
  2. Market rent — what similar units actually rent for, from Zillow, Rentometer, or local listings. Ignore the seller's rent estimate.
  3. Property tax — from the county assessor; taxes often reset higher after a sale.
  4. Insurance quote — call an agent; landlord policies run $100–$200/month in most markets.
  5. Your financing — down payment, rate, and term you're pre-approved for.

Step 2: Estimate expenses honestly

Beginners consistently underestimate expenses. Use these rules of thumb until you have real quotes:

  • Maintenance: 1% of property value per year (more for older homes).
  • Vacancy: 5–8% of rent — one empty month per year is normal.
  • Management: 8–10% of rent, even if you'll self-manage at first (your time isn't free).
  • CapEx reserve: older roofs, HVAC, and water heaters need a sinking fund — budget extra for properties over 20 years old.

Plug these into the rental ROI analyzer and let it do the math.

Step 3: Run the four checks

  1. Cash flow positive? Monthly cash flow must be positive — ideally $200+/month per unit as a cushion.
  2. 1% rule: Monthly rent ÷ price ≥ 1% is a good sign; below 0.8% needs exceptional appreciation to justify.
  3. Cap rate in range? Compare with similar properties in the same market, not national averages.
  4. Cash-on-cash ≥ 8%? Your invested dollars should beat what they'd earn sitting in an index fund.

A deal that passes all four deserves a deeper look: inspection, lease review, and neighborhood due diligence.

Mistakes that kill deals

  • Trusting pro-forma rents. Verify with real comps, not the listing flyer.
  • Forgetting vacancy. A property rented 11 months a year at $2,000 loses $2,000 — that's real money.
  • Ignoring CapEx. A 25-year-old roof isn't an "if" expense, it's a "when" expense.
  • Falling in love with the kitchen. You're buying a spreadsheet with walls, not a home.
