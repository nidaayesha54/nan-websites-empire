Rent vs Buy Calculator Guide: How the Math Really Works

"Renting is throwing money away" is one of personal finance's most repeated — and most wrong — slogans. Mortgage interest, taxes, insurance, and maintenance are also "thrown away." A fair comparison counts all costs on both sides. Here's how our rent vs buy calculator does it.

The true cost of buying

Buying's net cost over t years is more than the mortgage:

  • Closing costs (~2%): paid on day one, never recovered.
  • Mortgage interest: in the early years of a 30-year loan, most of your payment is interest — roughly 80%+ in year one at 7%.
  • Ownership costs (~4%/year): property tax, insurance, and maintenance, growing as the home appreciates.
  • Opportunity cost of the down payment: $80,000 invested at 6% becomes ~$143,000 in 10 years. The ~$63,000 of foregone growth is a real cost of buying.
  • Selling costs (~6%): counted at the end, since the comparison assumes you eventually sell.

Offsetting these: appreciation (assumed 3%/year) and principal paydown, which build your equity.

The true cost of renting

Renting's cost is simpler: total rent paid, growing at an assumed 3% per year. Renters avoid closing costs, maintenance, and selling costs — but build no equity and face rent increases.

The break-even year

The calculator finds the first year where buying's cumulative net cost drops below renting's. In most markets at 2026 prices and rates, that often lands between year 5 and year 15, depending on the rent-to-price ratio. Before that, renting usually wins; after it, buying's equity building takes over.

The single biggest driver is how long you stay. Moving in year 3 almost always favors renting; staying 15 years almost always favors buying.

Assumptions you should know

  • 3% annual home appreciation and 3% annual rent growth
  • 6% opportunity cost on the down payment
  • 2% closing costs, 6% selling costs
  • 30-year fixed mortgage at your entered rate
  • Property tax + insurance + maintenance ≈ 4% of home value per year

Change any of these mentally for your market: fast-appreciating cities favor buying sooner; high property-tax states push break-even later. Run your numbers in the rent vs buy calculator.
