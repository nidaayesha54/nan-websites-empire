NCLEX Test-Day Tips: The Final 48 Hours Checklist

Updated October 2026 · 5 min read

You've done the work — the questions, the rationales, the practice exams. The final 48 hours aren't about learning more; they're about arriving calm, rested, and ready to execute. Here's the checklist.

Two days before: taper, don't cram

  • Stop new content. Light review only: your error log, lab values, drug antidotes. Nothing new enters the brain after this point.
  • Confirm logistics. Check your ATT (Authorization to Test), test center address, parking, and required ID. Lay out your ID and confirmation the night before.
  • Do a half-length practice set (40–50 questions) in the morning to stay sharp, then stop studying by mid-afternoon.

The day before: protect your sleep

  • No studying after 6 PM. Seriously. One more pharmacology chapter won't move your score; poor sleep will.
  • Exercise lightly — a walk, not a workout. Physical tiredness helps you fall asleep.
  • Eat normally. Don't experiment with new foods or heavy caffeine changes.
  • Set two alarms and plan to arrive 30 minutes early. Rushing spikes cortisol and wrecks focus.

Test-day morning

  • Eat a real breakfast with protein — you'll be testing for up to 5 hours.
  • Dress in layers; testing centers run cold.
  • Bring exactly what the NCSBN requires: valid photo ID and your ATT confirmation. Leave everything else (phone, notes, food) in your car or locker — they're not allowed in.
  • Arrive early, breathe, and remind yourself: you've prepared for this.

Inside the testing center

Pace yourself

You have up to 5 hours for a maximum of 145 questions — that's roughly 2 minutes per question. Don't rush the early questions; the adaptive algorithm uses them to calibrate your difficulty level. If a question takes more than 3 minutes, make your best educated guess and move on.

Don't panic at the shutoff

The exam can end at 75 questions (minimum) or run to 145. Shutting off at 75 does not mean you failed — it means the computer reached 95% confidence in its decision either way. Candidates who pass at 75 and candidates who fail at 75 feel exactly the same walking out. Ignore the shutoff; focus on the next question.

Use your breaks strategically

You're allowed optional breaks. Take at least one: stand up, stretch, drink water, reset your brain. A 5-minute break at question 60 can save your performance on questions 61–145.

Handle SATA and case studies calmly

For select-all-that-apply, judge each option independently — never guess a count. For NGN case studies, read the full scenario before looking at the questions; the six items build on each other.

After the exam

Don't dissect every question with friends — you can't change anything, and second-guessing only creates anxiety. Most states offer quick results within 48 hours through your nursing board.
