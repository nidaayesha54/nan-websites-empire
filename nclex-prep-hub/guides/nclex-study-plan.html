NCLEX Study Plan: The 4-Week Guide That Actually Works

Updated October 2026 · 7 min read

Four weeks is the sweet spot for NCLEX prep: long enough to cover everything, short enough to stay sharp. This plan assumes 3–4 hours of focused study per day — if you have more or less time, plug your numbers into our free study planner and it will rescale this schedule for you.

The ground rules

  • Questions beat reading. Every day includes timed practice questions. Aim for 1,500+ total across the four weeks.
  • Review every rationale. Right or wrong, if you can't explain why, you don't know it.
  • Keep an error log. One line per missed concept. Review it every Sunday.
  • One rest half-day per week. Burnout fails more candidates than content gaps do.

Week 1: Foundations — Safe Care & Health Promotion

Daily target: 50 questions. Cover management of care (prioritization, delegation, legal/ethical), safety and infection control (isolation precautions, restraints, error prevention), and health promotion (growth and development, immunizations, antepartum/intrapartum/postpartum care, newborn assessment).

This week builds your test-taking muscles. Prioritization frameworks — ABCs, Maslow, acute-before-chronic — get drilled daily because they appear in every section. End the week with a 75-question mixed practice set and your first error-log review.

Week 2: The Big One — Physiological Integrity

Daily target: 75 questions. This category is roughly half the exam, so it gets the biggest week. Split it: days 1–2 for pharmacology (cardiac drugs, diabetes meds, psych meds, antibiotics, anticoagulants — learn classes and suffixes, plus antidotes), days 3–4 for adult health (cardiovascular, respiratory, neuro, GI/renal/endocrine), days 5–6 for acute care (fluids and electrolytes, acid-base, shock, post-op care). Day 7 is a full error-log review plus lab-value drills.

Memorize these cold this week: potassium 3.5–5.0, sodium 135–145, calcium 8.5–10.5, magnesium 1.5–2.5, BUN 10–20, creatinine 0.6–1.2, digoxin 0.5–2.0, lithium 0.6–1.2, INR 2–3 on warfarin, aPTT 1.5–2.5× control on heparin.

Week 3: Psychosocial, Peds & Question Stamina

Daily target: 100 questions. Cover psychosocial integrity (therapeutic communication, mental health disorders, crisis intervention, substance use) and pediatrics (emergencies, congenital heart defects, weight-based dosing, communicable diseases). The real goal this week is stamina: do questions in timed blocks of 25 without breaks, exactly like test day.

Take your first full-length practice exam (75–145 questions) under real conditions at the end of week 3 — same time of day as your real exam, no phone, no snacks. Score it, log every error by content area, and let the results dictate week 4.

Week 4: Mastery, Weak Areas & Strategy

Daily target: 75 questions + weak-area review. No new content this week. Every study block targets your two weakest areas from the week-3 exam. Take a second full-length exam mid-week. Drill NGN item types — SATA, bow-tie, trend, case studies — and your time management (about 1–2 minutes per question).

The final two days: light review only. Re-read your error log, skim lab values and antidotes, and stop studying by early evening the day before. Read our test-day tips for the final 24 hours.

Common 4-week study mistakes to avoid

  • Passive re-reading. Highlighting a textbook for three hours feels productive but builds almost no test skill. Every hour of reading should be matched with an hour of questions.
  • Skipping rationale review. The learning happens in the rationales, not the answering. Budget as much time for review as for the questions themselves.
  • Studying only strong areas. It's comfortable to drill topics you already know. Your error log exists to force you into the uncomfortable ones — follow it.
  • No timed practice. Untimed questions teach content; timed blocks teach exam performance. By week 3, everything should be timed.
  • Cramming the night before. Sleep consolidates memory. A rested brain with 95% of the content beats an exhausted brain with 100%.

What if you have 6 or 8 weeks instead?

The 4-week structure scales cleanly. For 6 weeks, stretch each content week: give pharmacology its own full week and add a second full-length practice exam in week 5. For 8 weeks, double the foundation phase and add a dedicated week of mixed review between content and mastery. Don't just do more of the same — use extra time for more timed questions and more practice exams, not more passive reading.
