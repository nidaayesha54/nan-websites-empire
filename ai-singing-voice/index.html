Frequently asked questions

Can AI really sing my lyrics?

Yes. Modern AI singing voice tools turn written lyrics into full sung vocals — some generate the entire song (melody, arrangement and vocals) from a text prompt, while others let you type lyrics and a melody to drive a realistic virtual singer.

What is the best free AI singing voice tool in 2026?

Suno and Udio offer the most generous free tiers for full text-to-song generation, Kits.AI is the best free starting point for voice cloning, and ElevenLabs' free tier is ideal for cloning your own voice. Compare them side by side in our vocal tool comparator.

Is it legal to use AI singing voices commercially?

It depends on the tool's license and how the voice was made. Most paid tiers allow commercial use of songs you generate, but never clone a real artist's voice without permission, and only feed the AI lyrics you wrote or licensed. See our 2026 guide for the full breakdown.

Do I have to disclose that my vocals are AI-generated?

Yes. YouTube, TikTok and Meta all require creators to label realistic AI-generated content, including synthetic vocals. Disclosing protects you from demonetization, takedowns and loss of audience trust.

Can AI clone my own voice for singing?

Yes — tools like Kits.AI and ElevenLabs let you upload recordings of your own voice and create a singable clone. You must have the rights to any voice you clone; cloning someone else's voice without consent can violate publicity rights in many US states.

How long should an AI demo song be?

For pitching and streaming, aim for 2:30–3:30 with the chorus landing before the one-minute mark. Use our free song structure planner to map sections, bars and total runtime before you generate anything.
