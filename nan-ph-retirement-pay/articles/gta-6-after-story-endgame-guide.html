GTA 6 After the Story: What Jason and Lucia Do Next

November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, and the story of Jason Duval and Lucia Caminos takes most players 60 to 80 hours across five chapters. That is a long road — one reviewer took about 90 hours to reach the finale — and when the credits roll, the obvious question is what happens next. Can you keep playing? Is there anything left to do? The answer is yes, and Rockstar built the ending in a way no GTA game has done before.

This guide is spoiler-free: it does not reveal the ending, the choices, or the plot twists. It explains exactly what the game does when the story ends, what stays available, what disappears, and how to plan your save files so you never lose anything you care about.

What happens when the story ends

Before the final mission, the game stops and asks whether you want to go forward with the finale. That choice matters, because of what happens after. Once you finish the finale and see one of the game's endings, the game sends you back in time to that moment just before the finale — Jason and Lucia standing outside a law office, deciding not to go in yet — with everything you collected intact. The finale marker stays on the map, so you can go back and finish the game again, possibly for a different ending.

That is a different design from past GTA games. In GTA 5 the story simply ended and you kept playing in the same world; in Red Dead Redemption 2 you took on a different character after the credits. GTA 6 freezes the story at the edge of the finale instead, which keeps every ending available while leaving the whole map open. The one downside is that the world feels frozen in time — no new major storyline can appear after the finale, because it would clash with the story wrapping up.

What stays open after the credits

Almost everything. Free roam across Leonida stays open: heists, sports, races, collectibles, fishing, hunting, kayaking, basketball, working out and backyard wrestling are all still there. Side mission chains with their own stories stay available, including the ones tied to trophies, as long as their quest givers are still around. The game was designed so that the full 100% checklist can be completed after the story, and any progress you made during the story already counts toward it.

The 100% checklist itself only becomes visible after the story, which is why many players do not realise how much is left. It is closer to 60% true completion than a demand for everything: you need to scan 200 of 277 base-model vehicles, study 100 of 255 animals through the binoculars, and complete 70 of 80 challenges, which lets you skip the hardest ones. There are no mission gold medals this time, so you never have to replay a mission perfectly.

What disappears — and the one missable trophy

Not everything survives the story. Some side missions are tied to specific chapters and disappear later on — the game marks them with black icons and pink letters, and they fade if you leave them too long. Quest givers can also become temporarily unavailable during the story, though every trophy-related character becomes available again later, so that part is safe.

There is one genuinely missable trophy: Positive Mental Attitude, for meditating at every spot across Leonida. The first meditation is missable in Chapter 1, at Jason's house — after the Down to Earth mission, Lucia mentions meditating on the balcony in the morning, and you have to follow her and accept. Miss it, and that trophy needs a new playthrough. Everything else can be cleaned up after the story.

Saves: the 15-slot rule

GTA 6 has no mission replay, which is why the save system matters more than in past games. You get 15 manual save slots plus one autosave slot. The sensible plan is to keep a save at the start and end of each chapter — ten saves — and rotate the remaining five for free-roam cleanup. Before the finale, keep a spare save at the moment the game asks whether to proceed: that save is your doorway back to every ending.

There is no New Game Plus at launch. You cannot restart the story from the beginning while keeping your upgrades and items. If you want a fresh run, it starts from zero. That makes the pre-finale save even more valuable — it is the closest thing the game has to a replay button.

The reward for finishing

Beating the story unlocks two things worth chasing. First, the 100% checklist becomes visible, and with it the path to the Platinum trophy. Second, completing challenges after the story reveals in-game cheat codes that let you go wild in Vice City — a reward for players who keep going after the credits. Take-Two's chief executive was asked on an investor call how Rockstar keeps players engaged after the campaign, and his answer was simple: "you can always keep going — there's so much fun to be had." The post-story game is built on exactly that idea: Leonida as a playground, with mastery replacing mission-chasing.

FAQ

Can you keep playing GTA 6 after the ending? Yes. The game sends you back to the moment before the finale with your progress intact, and the whole map stays open for free roam, side missions and the 100% checklist.

Is there a New Game Plus in GTA 6? No, not at launch. There is no way to restart the story while keeping your upgrades and items, and no mission replay — so keep a save from before the finale.

What should I do first after the story? Open the 100% checklist, scan vehicles and study animals as you travel, and work through the challenges — they unlock cheat codes. And check the Chapter 1 meditation early if you care about trophies.

The bottom line: GTA 6 does not end when the story ends. It hands you the keys to Leonida, keeps every ending on the table, and asks only that you saved your game before the finale.
