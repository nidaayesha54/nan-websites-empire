Philippines Retirement Pay: The RA 7641 Formula Explained

At 60 with five years of service, Philippine law guarantees private-sector workers a minimum retirement benefit. This guide explains the RA 7641 formula and what can raise it above the floor.

Key takeaways:
  • At 60+ with 5 years of service: minimum ½ month salary per year of service.
  • ‘½ month’ means 22.5 days: 15 days + 1/12 of 13th-month pay + up to 5 days SIL.
  • Company plans or CBAs that pay more override the statutory minimum.

Turn 60 in the Philippines with five years at the same company, and the law hands you a right many workers never claim in full: a minimum retirement benefit paid by your employer. Republic Act No. 7641 — the Retirement Pay Law of 1992, now reflected in Article 302 of the Labor Code — guarantees at least one-half month's salary for every year of service.

But "one-half month" is a legal term of art. It doesn't mean 15 days' pay. This guide unpacks the real 22.5-day formula, who qualifies, how company plans interact with the minimum, the tax treatment, and a worked example you can check against your own numbers.

The Law in One Paragraph

In the absence of a retirement plan or collective bargaining agreement, an employee who reaches 60 years old (optional retirement) — and in any case at 65 (compulsory retirement) — with at least 5 years of service in the same establishment, is entitled to retirement pay of at least one-half month salary per year of service. A fraction of six months or more counts as a whole year: 10 years and 7 months credits as 11 years.

The 22.5-Day Formula Explained

The Supreme Court has consistently held that "one-half month salary" comprises three components:

15 days of salary at your latest rate15.0 days
1/12 of your 13th-month pay≈ 2.5 days
Cash equivalent of up to 5 days of service incentive leave (SIL)5.0 days
Total per year of service22.5 days
Retirement pay = daily rate × 22.5 × creditable years of service

The daily rate is typically your latest monthly salary divided by 26. Note the SIL component: it's the cash equivalent of up to 5 days — the commonly used baseline is the full 22.5 days.

Worked Example

Latest monthly salary ₱30,000, 12 years of service:

  • Daily rate: 30,000 ÷ 26 = ₱1,153.85
  • Value per year: 1,153.85 × 22.5 = ₱25,961.54
  • Total: 25,961.54 × 12 = ₱311,538.46 — the statutory minimum.

Company Plans and CBAs: The Floor, Not the Ceiling

RA 7641 sets a minimum. If your employer's retirement plan or CBA pays more — many do, especially for long-tenured staff — the more generous benefit applies. Where both employer and employee contribute to a retirement fund, the employer's total contributions plus interest must at least equal the statutory benefit; otherwise the employer pays the difference.

Who Is Covered — and Who Isn't

Covered: all private-sector employees with an employer-employee relationship — regular, probationary, casual, project, seasonal, and part-time. Service generally counts from your original hiring date, including probationary periods that led to regular employment.

Excluded by statute: national and local government workers (and GOCCs with original charters — they have their own systems), domestic workers (kasambahay), and retail, service, and agricultural establishments regularly employing 10 or fewer workers.

Tax Treatment

Under RA 4917, retirement benefits under a reasonable private benefit plan are income-tax-exempt if received at age 50+ with at least 10 years of service — provided the exemption hasn't been availed in the previous 10 years. Statutory RA 7641 benefits have their own tax considerations; confirm the treatment with your HR or a tax advisor before you retire.

Don't Confuse It With SSS

Employer retirement pay is separate from SSS benefits. On top of what your company owes, you may draw an SSS monthly pension (with at least 120 monthly contributions) or a lump sum. The two systems stack — claiming one doesn't cancel the other. For the bigger picture of retirement income planning, see our retirement hub.

Resignation Before 60 vs Retirement at 60

This distinction matters enormously: resigning at 59 with 20 years of service does not trigger statutory retirement pay — the entitlement is strictly age-linked (60+), unless a company plan or CBA says otherwise. If you're approaching 60 and considering resignation, the timing can be worth hundreds of thousands of pesos. Check your company plan first. Whatever you decide, the retirement benefit forms part of your final pay settlement — make sure the computation includes it.

At 65: Compulsory Retirement

Sixty is the optional retirement age; 65 is compulsory — the employer may retire you, and the same RA 7641 benefit formula applies. Some company plans set an earlier compulsory age (e.g. 60), which is allowed if it's in the plan or CBA. If you love your job and want to work past 65, that requires a new arrangement — typically a fresh fixed-term contract — because the original employment relationship's retirement has triggered.

Early Retirement Packages vs the Statutory Benefit

Many companies offer early retirement programs — voluntary packages for employees below 60, often sweetened to encourage uptake. These are contractual offers, not RA 7641 entitlements: the company can set whatever formula it likes, and you're free to decline. Before accepting, compare the offer against what you'd get by staying to 60 under RA 7641 (or your company plan). An offer that looks generous today can be worse than two more years of service credit — do the 22.5-day math first.

Your Pre-Retirement Document Checklist

  • Service record: hiring date, regularization date, any breaks — get HR to confirm in writing.
  • Latest salary rate: the payslip or contract showing the rate the 22.5-day formula will use.
  • Company retirement plan / CBA: the actual document, not HR's summary — check whether it beats the statutory minimum.
  • SIL balance: your unused service incentive leave, since up to 5 days factor into the formula.
  • SSS records: confirm your contributions are posted and complete, so the pension claim runs in parallel.

Frequently Asked Questions

I'm 62 with 4 years and 8 months of service. Do I qualify?

The 5-year minimum isn't met by service alone — but the 8 months (a fraction of 6+ months) would round the 4 years up to 5 if you had 4 full years plus the fraction. 4 years 8 months → credited as 5 years. You qualify.

My company offers 1 month per year. Can I still claim the 22.5-day computation?

You get the better deal — 1 month per year beats the 22.5-day minimum, so the company plan governs. The statute only guarantees the floor.

Does overtime or commission count in the "salary" base?

The base is generally your latest basic salary rate. Whether allowances and variable pay are included depends on agreement and jurisprudence — the 22.5-day formula itself is computed on the daily rate derived from salary.

I'm a project employee renewed yearly for 8 years. Am I covered?

If the facts show a continuous employer-employee relationship doing work necessary to the business, those years generally count toward the 5-year requirement regardless of the "project" label.

When should retirement pay be released?

The law doesn't fix a RA-7641-specific deadline the way the 30-day final-pay rule does — but it should be settled promptly at retirement. Unreasonable delay is grounds for a DOLE complaint.
