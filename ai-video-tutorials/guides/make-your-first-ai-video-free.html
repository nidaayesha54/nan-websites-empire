Make Your First AI Video — Free, in 30 Minutes

No camera, no budget, no experience needed. This AI video tutorial walks you through the exact steps to create and publish your first AI-generated video today using only free tools. Total time: about 30 minutes.

$0
Cost

3
Free tools used
1

Pick your free AI video generator

For your first video, use Hailuo (MiniMax) or Luma Dream Machine — both have free tiers that generate short clips from text. Sign up with an email address; no credit card is required. Free tiers watermark the video and limit resolution, which is fine for learning.

Tip: Not sure which tool fits your project? Use our free AI video project planner — it picks the right tools for your goal and skill level.
2

Write a strong video prompt

AI video quality is 80% prompt. Describe the subject, action, camera, lighting and style in one detailed paragraph:

"Golden retriever puppy splashing through a sunlit puddle in a park, slow-motion water droplets, low camera angle, warm afternoon light, photorealistic, shallow depth of field."

Weak prompt: "a cute dog." The difference is enormous — be specific about motion and camera.

3

Generate 2–3 short clips

Paste your prompt, set the length to 5 seconds (short clips render faster and cost fewer credits), and generate. Make 2–3 variations — different angles of the same idea. Pick the best two. Don't burn credits regenerating the same prompt ten times; iterate the prompt, not the button.

4

Add voiceover or music

Download your clips, then open CapCut (free). For a silent showcase, add trending or royalty-free music from CapCut's library. For a narrated video, record your voice or generate narration with the free tier of ElevenLabs and import the MP3.

5

Edit: assemble, cut, caption

In CapCut: place your clips on the timeline, trim the slow starts, and add burned-in captions (CapCut's auto-caption tool does this in one click). Captions are non-negotiable — most viewers watch with sound off. Export in 1080p, 9:16 for TikTok/Reels/Shorts or 16:9 for YouTube.

6

Verify quality, then publish

Watch your export on your phone before posting. Look for AI artifacts — warping hands, flickering textures, melting faces. You can also run your finished video through the free AI Video Detector to spot generation artifacts your viewers would notice, then regenerate any bad shots.

Publish to TikTok, Instagram Reels or YouTube Shorts with a keyword-rich caption and 3–5 hashtags. Enable the platform's AI-generated content label where required — it's honest and keeps your account safe.

Account safety: never re-upload someone else's AI video as your own, and don't post misleading "real footage" claims — that's the fastest way to get flagged.
