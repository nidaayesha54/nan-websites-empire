AI Voiceover for YouTube Videos: Monetization Rules & Full Workflow

Faceless YouTube channels run on AI voiceovers — but YouTube's monetization review rejects low-effort AI narration every day. Here's how to stay on the right side of the rules and build a workflow that produces monetizable videos at scale.

Can you monetize AI voiceover videos on YouTube?

Yes — with conditions. YouTube's policy targets "repetitious" and low-value content, not AI voices themselves. Channels get rejected when the video is just a slideshow with robotic narration and no original value. You pass review when you:

  • Hold commercial rights from your voice provider (paid plan, not a free tier).
  • Add original commentary, research or storytelling — not just reading Wikipedia aloud.
  • Use original or licensed visuals edited with intent: b-roll, motion graphics, captions.
  • Disclose AI-generated content where YouTube asks (the "altered content" checkbox).

Best voice style per niche

  • Finance / commentary: calm, authoritative male or female; 150 wpm.
  • History / documentary: deep, measured; 135–140 wpm for gravitas.
  • Scary / mystery: low, slow delivery; 130 wpm with long pauses.
  • Kids / stories: warm, expressive; 140–150 wpm.
  • Tech / explainer: energetic, clear; 160–170 wpm.

The full workflow (per video)

  1. Script — write for the ear; target word count from the table above.
  2. Optimize — run it through the script optimizer (numbers, abbreviations, pacing).
  3. Generate — paragraph-by-paragraph in your chosen tool; re-roll bad lines.
  4. Edit — assemble in CapCut/Premiere; add visuals, captions, music at −22 dB.
  5. Polish — light compression, room tone, loudness to −14 LUFS for YouTube.
  6. Upload — tick the altered/AI content disclosure, write keyword-rich title + description.
