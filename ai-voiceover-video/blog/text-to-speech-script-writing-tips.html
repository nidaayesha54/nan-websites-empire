7 Script-Writing Tips for Text-to-Speech

Published October 2026. The voice engine reads exactly what you write — so write like a director, not an essayist. Seven small changes, each with a before/after:

1. Contract everything

❌ "You will not believe what happened next." → ✅ "You won't believe what happened next." Contractions are the single fastest humanizer.

2. Use punctuation as stage direction

❌ "First we will look at the data then we will draw conclusions." → ✅ "First, we'll look at the data… then — we'll draw conclusions." Every mark is a breath instruction.

3. One idea per sentence

❌ "The stock market which had been rising for three consecutive weeks suddenly crashed on Friday wiping out billions." → ✅ "The market had risen for three straight weeks. Then Friday happened. Billions — gone."

4. Write out every number

❌ "Revenue grew 300% to $5M in Q3." → ✅ "Revenue tripled to five million dollars in quarter three." (Or run it through our script optimizer.)

5. Spell names phonetically

If the AI says "Nye-duh" for "Nida", write "Nay-duh" in the generation script and fix captions separately. Pronunciation beats spelling.

6. Vary your rhythm

Three medium sentences in a row sound robotic no matter the voice. Mix short punch. With longer, flowing explanations that breathe a little.

7. Write the hook like you talk

❌ "In today's video, we shall be discussing…" → ✅ "Okay — this one surprised even me." The first 5 seconds decide retention; never open with formal filler.
