Venue: the $8,000 saving

A traditional venue averages $9,000. Instead: a public park pavilion ($200–$500 permit), a family backyard (free + $800 tent rental), or a restaurant private room with a food-and-beverage minimum instead of a rental fee. Friday and Sunday dates cut venue costs 20–40%.

Food: feed 60 guests for $3,000

  • Brunch wedding: 11am ceremony, breakfast spread — half the price of dinner, twice the charm.
  • Food trucks: $15–$25 per head, built-in entertainment factor.
  • Heavy appetizers + signature cocktail instead of a seated dinner and full bar.
  • Sheet cake in the back: display a small decorated cake, serve kitchen-cut sheet cake. Nobody knows.

Photos: don't skip, spend smart

Photography is the #1 regret category — but you don't need $4,000. Find a talented photographer building their wedding portfolio ($1,200–$1,800 for 4–6 hours). Check their full galleries, not just Instagram highlights. Book 4 hours (getting ready through first dance), not 10.

Decor that looks expensive (but isn't)

  • Candles everywhere: $100 of candles transforms any room more than $1,000 of flowers.
  • In-season grocery flowers arranged the morning of — enlist 3 friends, assembly-line style.
  • Repurpose: ceremony arch becomes the photo backdrop; bridesmaid bouquets become table centerpieces.
  • Fairy lights: $60 of string lights beats $600 of uplighting.

What NOT to cut

  1. Food quality — guests forgive simple decor, never bad food.
  2. A day-of coordinator (even a $300 friend-with-a-clipboard) — so YOU aren't solving problems in your dress.
  3. Marriage license & legal bits — obvious, but don't forget them in the excitement.
