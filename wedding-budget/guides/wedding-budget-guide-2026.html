The 3 biggest cost drivers

1. Guest count

Every guest adds roughly $100–$180 in catering, rentals, favors and stationery. Cutting from 150 to 100 guests can save $5,000–$9,000 — the single most powerful budget lever you have.

2. Location

A major-city wedding costs 40–70% more than the same wedding in a small town. Destination weddings look pricey but often land in the middle because guest lists shrink naturally.

3. Style expectations

Luxury isn't a 20% premium — it's roughly double. Plated dinners, premium bar packages and designer florals compound fast. Decide your style tier before booking anything.

Hidden costs nobody warns you about

  • Service charges & gratuities (18–25%): that $95/plate quote is really $115+ after service fees and tax.
  • Alterations ($300–$800): rarely included in the dress price.
  • Overtime fees ($200–$500/hour): for venue, photographer and DJ when the party runs long.
  • Vendor meals ($50–$100 each): most contracts require feeding your vendors.
  • Marriage license ($30–$100) and officiant tips.
  • Postage: 150 invitations at $0.73+ each adds up — budget $150–$250.
  • Day-of coordinator ($1,000–$2,500): the expense couples skip, then desperately wish they hadn't.
The solution: Add a 10% contingency line to your budget on day one. When (not if) surprises appear, they come from the contingency — not from panic or credit cards.

How to save $5,000+ without a "cheap" wedding

  1. Trim the guest list first. It's math, not emotion — every 10 guests is ~$1,500.
  2. Pick an off-peak date. Friday, Sunday or winter weddings often discount venues 15–30%.
  3. Choose in-season flowers and repurpose ceremony florals at the reception.
  4. Limit the bar: beer, wine and one signature cocktail instead of full premium bar saves thousands.
  5. Digital invitations for save-the-dates; paper only for the formal invite.
  6. Negotiate bundles: photo+video from one team, or venue packages that include catering.
