5 Logo Color Mistakes That Kill Trust

Great brands rarely have ugly palettes — because they avoided these five traps. Check your logo against each one.

Mistake 1: Too many colors

Five-color logos look like confetti and fail at small sizes. Fix: cut to 2–3 colors using the 60-30-10 rule — primary, secondary, neutral.

Mistake 2: Unreadable contrast

Light gray text on white is invisible on phones. Fix: run every pairing through our contrast checker and hit WCAG AA (4.5:1).

Mistake 3: Random, clashing hues

Colors picked "because they look nice" often fight each other. Fix: build harmonies in the palette generator — complementary, analogous, or triadic.

Mistake 4: Chasing trends over fit

A neon gradient looks 2026-fresh but wrong for a law firm. Fix: trends inform, personality decides — see color psychology and how to choose brand colors.

Mistake 5: No documented palette

When "brand blue" means three different hex codes across your team, trust erodes. Fix: lock exact hex codes and usage rules in a one-page brand sheet.
