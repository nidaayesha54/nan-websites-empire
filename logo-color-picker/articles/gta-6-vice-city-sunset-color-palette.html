GTA 6 Colors: The Vice City Sunset Palette

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and it has one of the most recognisable colour schemes in games. You know it the moment you see it: a pink and purple sunset over dark navy, neon signs buzzing over wet asphalt, palm trees cut black against a burning sky. Rockstar and its partners used one Vice City sunset across almost everything — the trailers, the posters, the pre-order campaign, the merchandise — and that single sunset is now the visual shorthand for the whole game. This guide breaks down the palette, gives you the hex codes designers are actually using, and explains how to use the look without overdoing it.

One thing to say up front: Rockstar has not published a palette. Every hex code in this guide is a fan match — a designer reading the mood of the trailers and matching it to proven values. That is honest, and it is also the point: the palette works because it is a mood, not a specification, and you can check it yourself with an eyedropper on any trailer still.

One Sunset, Everywhere

The discipline of the campaign is remarkable. Almost every post Rockstar made in the five months before launch wears the same Vice City sunset — pink and purple over dark navy — and the partners wore it too, from Netflix's billboard to the Miami HEAT's game poster. The one post that drops it, a plain typed letter, is the campaign's most-viewed. The lesson for designers is the discipline itself: one sunset, used everywhere, beats ten colours used once.

The pre-order film is the clearest example: a large VI draws itself as a pink-to-amber outline on purple, fills with a sunset gradient and palm silhouettes, and sits at the centre of a three-by-three grid of illustrated panels — a helicopter, a couple, an alligator, a yellow sports car, a speedboat with a flamingo. The only copy is the date, in white with a magenta offset edge on navy. Sunset, navy, white, magenta: four colours, and the whole game is in them.

The Pink: #FC94AF and the Fan Match

The colour at the centre of the palette is a soft flamingo rose, matched by fans as #FC94AF — in RGB 252, 148, 175, and in HSL near 344 degrees, 94 percent saturation and 78 percent lightness. The case for it is simple: for the golden-hour side of Vice City — the skyline glow, the sunset burning on the water — it is warm enough to feel like heat, soft enough to cover a whole background, and clearly pink the second you see it.

For the nightlife side — the signs and neon outlines — the call is a brighter pair: neon pink #FF6EC7 or deep pink #FF1493 as accents. The rule is just as important as the codes: #FC94AF takes the big areas and the gradients, and the neon pinks are your muzzle flash — small and sharp, never the whole scene. Set against deep purple and a black sky, pink here signals danger, heat and nightlife: the glow you see right before the sirens start.

The Gradient: Purple to Pink to Orange

The Vice City sky is not one colour. It runs from bruised purple to hot pink to burning orange, and the gradient designers keep coming back to is this one: a vertical blend from #3B1C6E at the top, through #FC94AF at 55 percent, to #FF8A3D at the bottom. Purple sky on top, a pink glow in the middle, an orange horizon at the bottom. #FC94AF sits dead centre, the bridge between cold night and warm heat — pull it out and the whole sky falls apart.

That gradient is the single most reusable piece of the palette: use it as a background, a hero banner, or the sky behind a logo, and the result reads as Vice City in half a second. No logo, no HUD, no minimap — just colour doing the work. The trick is the order: purple first, because the night is the frame; pink in the middle, because the sunset is the subject; orange last, because the horizon is where the heat lives.

Six Palettes to Steal

Designers have already turned the look into named palettes worth borrowing as starting points. "Vice City Sunset" runs Electric Blue, Neon Violet, Vice Pink, Sunset Coral and Golden Hour. "Golden Hour Escape" runs Sunset Pink, Coral, Sunny Peach, Purple and Midnight Blue. "Neon Nights" runs a lip-gloss pink, Electric Purple, Indigo, Cyan and a criminal-night black. "Tropical Vice" runs Peachy Pink, Sunburn, Mango, Fresh Mint and Lagoon. "Miami Marina" runs Sunlit Sand, Sky Blue, Marina Teal, Ocean Blue and Palm Green. And "Vice City by Night" runs Hot Pink, Lilac Haze, Violet Rush, Nightfall and Deep Night — with "Midnight Pursuit" covering the police side: Street Blue, Police Blue, Siren Red, Neon Violet and Cool White.

Each palette is a mood, and the mood is the choice: sunset for warmth and arrival, neon for night and danger, tropical for day and beach, midnight for pursuit and police. Pick one per design, not one per element — the palette that works is the one you commit to.

Where the Look Comes From: 1980s Miami

None of this is new, and that is why it works. Pink has been the colour of 1980s Miami since Art Deco hotels, flamingos and sunset-lit boulevards made it the city's calling card. The TV series Miami Vice, which ran from 1984 to 1989, made pastel colour, palm trees and neon signage the visual shorthand for that city, and the same palette later became the base of synthwave and "outrun" art. GTA 6 is not inventing the look; it is inheriting it, and the inheritance is why it feels familiar the first time you see it.

The partnerships prove it: Netflix's teaser for the Extended Look shows a lit billboard reading "I LOVE VICE CITY", with the heart filled by the game's sunset gradient; the Miami HEAT's event post writes the city in pink neon script across a dark court; and PlayStation's limited-edition DualSense controllers cut between glitter-purple shells and pink face buttons. Everyone is wearing the same sunset, because the sunset is the brand.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the GTA 6 pink hex code?

The fan-matched pink is #FC94AF, a soft flamingo rose — not a colour Rockstar has published, just the closest match to the trailer mood, checkable with an eyedropper. Use #FF6EC7 or #FF1493 for neon accents.

What is the GTA 6 sunset gradient?

A vertical blend from deep purple #3B1C6E at the top, through pink #FC94AF in the middle, to sunset orange #FF8A3D at the bottom. Purple sky, pink glow, orange horizon — that is the whole Vice City sky in three stops.

The Bottom Line

GTA 6's palette is one sunset used everywhere: pink #FC94AF as the driver, neon pinks #FF6EC7 and #FF1493 as accents, a purple-to-pink-to-orange gradient for the sky, and dark navy as the frame. It comes from 1980s Miami, Art Deco hotels and Miami Vice — and it works because it is a mood, not a specification.
