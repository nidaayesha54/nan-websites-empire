Brand Color Psychology: What Every Logo Color Says

Up to 90% of snap judgments about a brand are based on color alone. Here is what each major hue communicates — and when to use (or avoid) it in your logo.

Red — Energy, Urgency, Appetite

Red raises heart rates and demands attention. It works for food (McDonald's), entertainment (Netflix), and sales-driven brands. Avoid it for finance or healthcare, where it reads as "danger" or "loss".

Blue — Trust, Security, Competence

The most-used logo color in the world. Blue says "you can rely on us" — perfect for tech, finance, and healthcare. The risk: blending in. Add a distinctive accent.

Green — Growth, Health, Calm

Green signals nature, wellness, and money. Organic brands, fintechs, and eco-startups thrive here. Darker greens read premium; bright greens read energetic.

Yellow — Optimism, Warmth, Attention

Yellow is the happiest color — and the hardest to use alone. Best as an accent on dark backgrounds (think McDonald's golden arches on red).

Purple — Creativity, Luxury, Wisdom

Purple combines blue's trust with red's energy. Beauty, spirituality, and premium creative brands own it. Use deep purples for luxury, bright violets for playfulness.

Orange — Friendly, Affordable, Bold

Orange feels approachable and adventurous — great for startups, sports, and youth brands. It can read cheap in luxury contexts.

Black — Luxury, Power, Sophistication

Black logos never go out of style for premium brands (Chanel, Nike's wordmark). It needs excellent typography — color can't hide weak letterforms.

White — Simplicity, Cleanliness, Space

White works as negative space or on dark backgrounds. Apple and minimalist brands prove restraint is a strategy.
