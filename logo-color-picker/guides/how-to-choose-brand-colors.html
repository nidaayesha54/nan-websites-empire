How to Choose Brand Colors: A 7-Step Framework

Choosing logo colors doesn't have to be guesswork. Follow these seven steps to land on a palette that fits your brand and survives real-world use.

1. Define your brand personality in 3 words

Bold or calm? Premium or playful? Traditional or disruptive? Write three adjectives — these map directly to hues in our color psychology guide.

2. Audit your competitors

List the 5 brands you compete with and note their primary colors. If four use blue, a different hue buys you instant shelf distinction.

3. Pick ONE primary color

Your primary carries ~60% of brand recognition. Choose the hue that matches your #1 personality word. Shortcut: take the brand color quiz.

4. Add a secondary + neutral

Use the palette generator — analogous for calm brands, complementary or triadic for bold ones. Add one neutral (charcoal, cream, or slate) for text and backgrounds.

5. Test contrast (WCAG)

Run every text/background pair through the contrast checker. Aim for AA (4.5:1) on logo text so it reads at favicon size.

6. Test in the real world

View your palette on a phone screen, printed in grayscale, and at 16px. If it fails any of these, adjust lightness before you commit.

7. Lock it and document it

Record exact hex codes, usage ratios (60-30-10 rule), and don'ts. Consistency is what turns colors into brand equity.
