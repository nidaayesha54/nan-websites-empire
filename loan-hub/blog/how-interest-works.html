How Interest Really Works on Loans (Plain English)

Every Payment Splits in Two

Each monthly payment has two parts: interest (the lender's fee for that month) and principal (what actually shrinks your balance). Interest is calculated on your remaining balance each month — so early on, when the balance is big, most of your payment is interest.

A Real Example

$15,000 at 12% APR for 5 years = $333.67/month. In month 1, about $150 goes to interest and only ~$184 to principal. By month 50, it's flipped: ~$30 interest, ~$304 principal.

The One Move That Saves the Most

Pay extra toward principal early. Because interest is charged on the remaining balance, every extra dollar paid in year one saves roughly a dollar of interest in later years. Even $50/month extra on the example above saves over $700 and cuts 8 months off the loan.

Why Shorter Terms Win

A 3-year loan at the same rate has higher payments but far less total interest — you're simply exposed to interest for fewer months. If the payment fits your budget, always prefer the shorter term.
