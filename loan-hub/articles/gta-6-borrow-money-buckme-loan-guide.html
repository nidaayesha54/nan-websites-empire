GTA 6: Can You Borrow Money? Buck Me Explained

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Grand Theft Auto 6 launched on November 19, 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and money matters more in this game than in any GTA before it. Story missions can ask you to earn a set amount before they start, cash in your pocket is lost if you die, and the whole plot is about two people trying to escape a life where they never have enough. So the question comes up fast: can you borrow money in GTA 6? The answer is a story in itself — and it starts with the bank saying no.

That no is not a bug or a missing feature. It is one of the first things the story tells you about who Jason and Lucia are: people the financial system does not want. Understanding why the bank turns them down, and what they get instead, explains most of how money works in the game.

The Buck Me Mission: Why the Bank Says No

The story puts the question front and centre in the mission called "Buck Me". After "Coming Home", Lucia's parole officer insists they set up a bank account — a much better way to be paid than cash in an envelope from the mailbox. So Jason and Lucia go together to the local Fleeca bank, and the scene that follows is a small masterclass in how the game treats money.

First, the bank cannot give them an account at all. Then Lucia brings up a line of credit, so they can get a house — and the manager suggests a veteran's loan. That goes nowhere too: Jason did not leave the Army on good terms. Then comes the credit check, and the bad news: their credit level is entirely too low even to set up a bank account. Two people, one bank, and every door closes. The mission's answer to "can you borrow money" is a flat no, delivered by a manager behind a desk.

BuckMe: The App That Replaces the Bank

What they get instead is an app. BuckMe — the game's in-game banking app, a parody of the real world's cash-transfer apps — becomes their bank account, and opening it gives them 100 dollars as a welcome gift. It is a small, telling detail: the system that rejected them in person will happily hand them an app and a hundred dollars, because an app does not have to look them in the eye.

BuckMe is also where the game's money rules start to bite. Cash in your pocket is lost if you die while carrying it, so deposit at an ATM and keep your money in the app. You can carry up to a reported 20,000 dollars, and your checking account has a cap too: if it fills up, further payments stop until you spend some money or move it into Family Savings. The app is your bank, your wallet and your ceiling, all at once.

Family Savings: Money You Cannot Take Back

Family Savings is the part of BuckMe that catches the most players out, so it deserves its own warning. Inside the app, you can move money into a family savings account — and once you move it, you cannot retrieve it. It is not money you can ever touch again. The mission walks you through moving 100 dollars into savings, with 4,900 dollars still to go until the next goal is met, and the game is clear that the transfer is one-way. Make certain before you put money there.

That one-way rule is not a trap; it is a goal — the savings are useful for the "Save for the Future" mission, and the game's way of asking you to commit to the future Jason and Lucia are trying to build. But a commitment made by accident is still a commitment, so check the goal, check the amount, and only then press the button.

Payday Loan Stores Are Not Lenders

Here is the other half of the borrowing question: the places in Vice City that look like lenders are not lenders at all. Payday loan stores appear all over the game, and the game's 100 percent completion checklist includes seven payday loan jobs — robberies, not loans. They unlock after the story mission "Credit Check" in Chapter 3, and the job is to take the cash and rob the safe in the back room. The name is a joke the game is playing on you: the only way to get money out of a payday loan store in GTA 6 is to rob it.

The same logic runs through the rest of the game's money: 22 bank jobs in the completion checklist, and marked bills from banks and payday loan stores that have to be fenced — 250,000 dollars' worth for the "Dime on the Dollar" trophy. In GTA 6, the financial system is something you work around or work against. You do not borrow from it.

The Real-World Way to Spread the Cost: O2 Financing

There is one place borrowing is real, and it is outside the game. In the United Kingdom, the mobile network O2 offers GTA 6 on a pay-monthly plan, the way you would finance a phone: 20 pounds upfront, then 1.39 pounds a month for 36 months, running to 2029, at 0 percent interest. The total is 70.04 pounds against an outright price of 69.99 pounds — five pence more, spread over three years — and the offer includes the Vintage Vice City pre-order pack. It is open to non-customers too.

It is also a real consumer credit agreement, and it should be treated like one: a credit check, an 18-plus age requirement, and a 36-month direct debit, with missing a 1.39-pound instalment carrying the same formal credit-score penalties as defaulting on a phone loan. The game is a digital download code, not a disc. So no, you do not need a three-year loan to play GTA 6 — but if 69.99 pounds in one go stings, O2 will let you dribble it out until 2029, interest-free.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you get a loan in GTA 6?

No. The bank refuses Jason and Lucia an account and a line of credit in the "Buck Me" mission, and there is no borrowing system in the game. The BuckMe app replaces the bank, and payday loan stores are robbery targets, not lenders.

What is the BuckMe 100 dollars in GTA 6?

It is a welcome gift for opening the BuckMe app during the "Buck Me" mission. Move it into Family Savings only if you mean it — savings transfers are one-way and the money can never be retrieved.

The Bottom Line

You cannot borrow money in GTA 6: the bank turns Jason and Lucia down, the veteran loan is a dead end, and the payday loan stores exist to be robbed, not borrowed from. What you get instead is the BuckMe app, a 100-dollar welcome gift, and a family savings account that only moves one way. In the real world, O2 will spread the game's 69.99-pound price over three years at 0 percent — a real credit agreement, worth reading properly before you sign.
