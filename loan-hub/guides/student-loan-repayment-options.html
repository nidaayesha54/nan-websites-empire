Student Loan Repayment Options Explained (2026)

Federal student loans offer more repayment options than any other debt in America. Choosing wrong can cost you tens of thousands — or save you from default.

1. Standard Repayment (10 years)

Fixed payments over 10 years. The default plan — and the cheapest overall because interest accrues for the shortest time. Use our payoff planner to see your payment.

2. Graduated Repayment (10 years)

Payments start low and rise every 2 years. Good for new graduates expecting salary growth. Costs more than Standard overall.

3. Extended Repayment (25 years)

Available for balances over $30,000. Lower payments, much more total interest. Only makes sense if Standard payments are unaffordable.

4. Income-Driven Plans (SAVE, PAYE, IBR)

Payments set at roughly 10% of discretionary income (income minus 150% of the poverty guideline), recertified annually. Key facts:

  • Lowest monthly payments — sometimes $0
  • Remaining balance forgiven after 20–25 years
  • Forgiven amount may be taxable as income
  • You pay far more total interest over time

Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF)

Work full-time for a qualifying employer (government, nonprofit) and make 120 qualifying payments — the rest is forgiven tax-free. The single best deal in student loans if you qualify.
