How to Get a Loan With Bad Credit (2026 Guide)

A credit score under 670 doesn't mean you're out of options — but it does mean higher rates and fewer lenders. Here's what's realistic.

Your Realistic Options

1. Credit Unions

Credit unions are nonprofits and often approve members with scores in the 580–640 range at better rates than banks. Membership is usually open to anyone in a region or profession.

2. Secured Personal Loans

You pledge collateral (savings account, vehicle). Lenders take less risk, so approval is easier and rates are lower than unsecured bad-credit loans.

3. Co-Signed Loans

A creditworthy co-signer dramatically improves approval odds and rates. Warning: missed payments damage their credit too.

4. Bad-Credit Online Lenders

Some online lenders specialize in scores as low as 560–580. Expect APRs of 25–36% — expensive, but sometimes necessary.

What to Avoid

  • Payday loans: 400%+ APR traps. Almost never worth it.
  • "No credit check" loans: legitimate lenders always check; these are usually predatory.
  • Upfront-fee scams: real lenders deduct fees from the loan — they never ask for payment before funding.

Improve Your Odds Before Applying

  1. Check your reports free at AnnualCreditReport.com and dispute errors (about 1 in 5 reports has one).
  2. Pay down credit card balances below 30% utilization — this can lift your score in weeks.
  3. Avoid new hard inquiries in the 3 months before applying.
  4. Get pre-qualified (soft pull) with multiple lenders to compare real offers.
