Personal Loan vs Credit Card: Which Costs Less in 2026?

You need $10,000. Should you take a personal loan or put it on a credit card? The answer matters more than most people think — the wrong choice can cost you thousands.

The Numbers: $10,000 Compared

Personal Loan (12% APR, 3 yrs)Credit Card (24% APR, min payments)
Monthly payment~$332~$200 (minimum)
Time to pay off3 years (fixed)~9+ years
Total interest~$1,957~$11,600+
Total paid~$11,957~$21,600+

The credit card costs nearly 6× more in interest if you only make minimum payments.

When a Personal Loan Wins

  • Large, one-time expenses (medical bills, home repairs, debt consolidation)
  • You want a fixed payoff date and predictable payments
  • Your credit qualifies you for 8–15% APR

When a Credit Card Wins

  • Small amounts you can pay off in 1–2 months
  • 0% intro APR offers (if you pay it off before the promo ends)
  • You need flexibility and rewards on everyday spending

Key Differences

  • Rate type: personal loans are usually fixed; cards are variable and can rise.
  • Fees: loans may have origination fees (1–8%); cards have annual fees, late fees, cash-advance fees.
  • Credit impact: a loan adds an installment account (good for credit mix); high card balances hurt utilization.

The Bottom Line

For anything over a few thousand dollars that takes more than a couple months to repay, a personal loan almost always costs less. Run your numbers before you borrow.
