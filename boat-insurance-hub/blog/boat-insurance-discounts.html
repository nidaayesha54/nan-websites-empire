11 Boat Insurance Discounts That Actually Work in 2026

October 2026 · 8 min read

Boat insurance is already cheap compared to auto — but there's no reason to leave money on the water. These 11 discounts are the ones insurers actually offer and owners actually get, with realistic savings for each. Stack three or four and a 30–40% cut is very achievable.

1. Take a boating safety course — save 5–15%

A NASBLA-approved course (US Power Squadrons, US Coast Guard Auxiliary, or your state's course) is the single most reliable discount. It costs $20–$50, takes a weekend, and many insurers apply 5–15% off for three years. It also makes you a genuinely safer boater.

2. Lay-up endorsement — save 40–60% on hull (winter months)

If you store the boat for winter, a lay-up endorsement discounts the hull portion of your premium during storage months — while liability stays active. A 5-month lay-up at 50% off the hull portion can cut your annual bill by ~25%. Run your exact numbers in our lay-up vs year-round calculator.

3. Raise your deductible — save 10–20%

Moving from a $250 to a $1,000 deductible typically saves 10–20%. Do the math: if it saves $90/year and you claim once a decade, you come out ahead. Keep an emergency fund for the deductible.

4. Bundle policies — save 5–15%

Insuring your boat with the same company as your home or auto usually earns a multi-policy discount. Ask your current insurer first — it's the easiest quote you'll ever get.

5. Shop 3+ quotes — save up to 30%

Marine insurers price risk very differently. The same boat and profile can get quotes hundreds of dollars apart. Get at least three: a specialty marine insurer, your current home/auto carrier, and an independent agent who shops multiple carriers.

6. Good experience and clean record — save 10–25%

Insurers reward years of claim-free boating. If you're new, the safety course (see #1) is your shortcut. Avoid claims for minor repairs you can afford — a claim history raises premiums for years.

7. Anti-theft devices — save 5–10%

GPS trackers, kill switches, locked storage and trailer locks reduce theft risk. Some insurers give explicit discounts; all of them like lower risk. A $100 GPS tracker pays for itself fast.

8. Pay annually instead of monthly — save 3–8%

Monthly billing often includes installment fees. Paying the annual premium in one shot avoids them — ask whether your insurer charges a billing fee per installment.

9. Store smart — save 5–15%

Trailered at home or in indoor storage is cheaper to insure than left in the water year-round. If you have the option, how you store the boat is a real rating factor.

10. Choose a slower, smaller boat — save 20–50%+

Not a discount exactly, but the biggest lever: pontoons, sailboats and modest runabouts cost dramatically less to insure than high-performance boats and yachts. If you're boat shopping, insurance cost should be part of the budget — see 2026 cost by boat type.

11. Ask about disappearing-deductible and loyalty perks

Some marine insurers reduce your deductible for each claim-free year, or offer renewal loyalty discounts. They're rarely advertised — ask directly when you quote.

Putting it together

A realistic stack: safety course (10%) + lay-up (25% of annual) + $1,000 deductible (15%) + bundling (10%) + annual pay (5%). On a $600 policy, that's roughly $200–$250 back in your pocket every year. Start with your baseline in our boat insurance cost estimator, then apply the discounts when you shop quotes.
