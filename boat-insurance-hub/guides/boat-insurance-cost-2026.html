How Much Does Boat Insurance Cost in 2026?

Updated October 2026 · 8 min read

Boat insurance is one of the more affordable insurance products out there — but "affordable" spans a wide range. A pontoon on a Midwest lake might cost $250 a year to insure, while a high-performance boat in Florida could run $2,000 or more. Here's how pricing actually works in 2026.

The quick answer: 1.5–2% of boat value

Most marine insurers price full coverage at roughly 1.5% to 2% of the boat's agreed value per year. So a $30,000 runabout typically costs $450–$600 annually, and a $100,000 cruiser runs $1,500–$2,000. 7 factors that set your price

1. Boat value. The single biggest input. Insurers quote "agreed value" policies — you and the insurer agree the hull's worth upfront, and premiums scale with it.

2. Boat type and horsepower. Anything over 25 feet or with high-horsepower engines costs more. High-performance boats and PWCs are the most expensive to insure per dollar of value.

3. Length. Premiums jump at roughly 26 ft and again at 36 ft, as boats get harder to handle and more expensive to repair.

4. Your state and navigation area. Florida, Texas and Louisiana are the priciest states — hurricane exposure drives rates. Inland northern states are cheapest. Offshore navigation areas add surcharges.

5. Your experience and record. Beginners pay more; a boating safety course can earn a 5–15% discount. Prior claims or DUIs push rates up sharply.

6. Usage. Pleasure cruising is cheapest. Fishing, water-sports towing and especially rental/charter use cost more.

7. Coverage choices. Liability-only is roughly half the price of full coverage. Higher deductibles ($1,000 vs $250) typically save 10–20%.

Liability-only vs full coverage: the price gap

For a $30,000 runabout, liability-only coverage often runs $150–$300/year, while full coverage (hull + liability) runs $450–$600/year. Whether the extra is worth it depends on your boat's value and your finances — our coverage quiz walks you through the decision in 60 seconds.

How to lower your boat insurance bill

Shop at least three quotes — marine insurers price risk very differently. Take a NASBLA-approved boating safety course. Raise your deductible to $1,000. Add a lay-up endorsement if you store the boat in winter (typically saves 40–60% on hull coverage — try our seasonal calculator). Bundle with your home or auto policy, and install anti-theft devices. State snapshots: cheapest vs priciest states

Geography is one of the strongest pricing signals. Florida is the most expensive state for boat insurance — hurricane exposure, a huge registered-boat population and year-round boating season push premiums 40–60% above the national average. Texas and Louisiana follow for similar storm and flooding reasons. At the other end, inland states like Minnesota, Michigan, Ohio and Pennsylvania are typically 10–20% below average: shorter seasons, freshwater lakes, and less catastrophic-weather exposure. New York and California sit above average thanks to high boat values and dense, claim-heavy waterways. If you boat in multiple states, tell your insurer — the navigation area on your policy determines the rate.

Boat insurance vs auto insurance: why the price feels different

Many first-time buyers are surprised that boat insurance is cheaper than car insurance. The reasons are simple: boats are used fewer days per year, claims per insured boat are lower, and — critically — boat insurance usually isn't mandatory, so the insured pool self-selects toward responsible owners. But don't let the low price fool you into under-insuring: a single at-fault collision on the water can produce liability claims just as large as a car crash, and unlike roads, there are no traffic cameras or standardized fault rules to protect you.

Getting quotes: what to have ready

To get accurate quotes quickly, have this information on hand: the boat's year, make, model and hull identification number (HIN); purchase price or current market value; length, engine type and total horsepower; how and where you store it; your boating experience and any safety courses completed; your claims history for the last 5 years; and your desired liability limits and deductible. With this list, an independent marine agent can quote you from multiple carriers in one call — and you'll avoid the back-and-forth that makes comparison shopping feel like work.
