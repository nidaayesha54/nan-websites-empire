FAFSA Tips That Can Mean More Financial Aid

The FAFSA is the gateway to federal grants, work-study, subsidized loans, and most institutional aid. Small, legal moves in how and when you file can change your Student Aid Index (SAI) — and your aid offer. Here's what matters.

1. File as early as possible

Some state and institutional aid is first-come, first-served. The FAFSA opens October 1 for the following academic year — file in October, not April.

2. Understand what counts (and what doesn't)

The FAFSA assesses parent assets at up to 5.64% but student assets at 20%. Retirement accounts and home equity are excluded. That means money in a 529 owned by the parent is assessed far more favorably than the same money in the student's savings account.

3. Time big financial moves

The FAFSA looks at "prior-prior year" income — for the 2026–27 school year, that's 2024 income. Realizing capital gains or taking a big bonus in a base year can inflate your SAI two years later. Plan accordingly.

4. Don't skip it because you "earn too much"

There's no income cutoff for federal aid eligibility, and many schools require the FAFSA for merit scholarships. Filing takes most families under an hour. Skipping it leaves money on the table.

5. Avoid the classic errors

  • Listing the wrong schools (add every school you're considering — up to 20).
  • Confusing parent and student sections.
  • Forgetting to sign with the FSA ID (both student and parent, if required).
  • Not updating after major income changes — you can appeal aid offers.

6. Appeal if circumstances changed

Job loss, medical bills, or divorce after the base year? Contact the financial aid office and ask for a "professional judgment" review. Schools can and do adjust aid.
