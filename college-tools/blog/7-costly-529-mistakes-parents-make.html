7 Costly 529 Mistakes Parents Make (and How to Avoid Them)

A 529 is simple in concept — but the details trip up smart families. Here are the seven mistakes that cost the most, and the fix for each.

1. Picking the wrong state's plan

If your state offers a tax deduction or credit, its plan usually wins. If it doesn't, shop nationally for the lowest fees — expense ratios vary from 0.05% to over 0.50%, and that gap compounds for 18 years.

2. Choosing the wrong portfolio

Age-based portfolios automatically de-risk as college nears — the right default for most families. A static 100%-stock portfolio at age 16 is gambling with tuition money.

3. Waiting to start

Every year of delay costs roughly a year of compounding. Starting with $100/month at birth beats $300/month starting at age 10. Use the 529 projector to see your own curve.

4. Raiding the account

Non-qualified withdrawals trigger income tax plus a 10% penalty on earnings. Treat the 529 as untouchable — emergencies come from the emergency fund.

5. Overfunding without a plan

Excess 529 money isn't trapped — you can change beneficiaries or roll up to $35,000 into a Roth IRA — but funding retirement first is usually smarter. Don't starve your 401(k) to overfill a 529.

6. Ignoring fees

A 0.40% fee difference on $100,000 over 10 years costs roughly $5,000. Compare expense ratios before you commit.

7. Not coordinating with grandparents

Grandparent-owned 529s used to hurt aid; under current FAFSA rules, grandparent distributions no longer count as student income. Coordinate timing anyway — and read how 529s work together.
