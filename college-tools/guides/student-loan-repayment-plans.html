Student Loan Repayment Plans Compared (2026)

Federal student loans offer several repayment plans, and the default isn't always the best. Here's how the main options compare — run your own numbers in the payoff calculator as you read.

On this page
  • Standard Repayment Plan
  • Graduated Repayment Plan
  • Income-driven repayment (IDR)
  • How to choose

Standard Repayment Plan

What: fixed monthly payments over 10 years. Who it's for: borrowers who can afford the payment and want the lowest total interest. This is the default — and the cheapest way to repay if you can swing it.

Cost: least interest of any plan, because the term is shortest.

Graduated Repayment Plan

What: payments start low and rise every two years over 10 years. Who it's for: new graduates expecting salary growth. Trade-off: you pay more interest than Standard because early payments barely touch principal.

Income-driven repayment (IDR)

What: payments capped at a percentage of discretionary income (often 10%), recalculated yearly; remaining balance forgiven after 20–25 years.

  • Who it's for: borrowers whose debt is large relative to income, or pursuing Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF).
  • Trade-off: much more total interest; forgiven amounts may be taxable as income under current law.
  • PSLF: public servants can get forgiveness after 120 qualifying payments — the fastest legitimate path to forgiveness.

How to choose

  • Can afford Standard? Stay on it — it's the cheapest.
  • Expect raises? Graduated bridges the early-career gap.
  • Struggling or in public service? IDR or PSLF-track plans.
  • Have extra cash? Extra payments on the highest-rate loan first (avalanche) beats any plan switch.
