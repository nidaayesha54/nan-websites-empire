How Much Does College Really Cost? Sticker vs Net Price

Headlines scream that college costs $80,000 a year. The truth is more nuanced — and for many families, much less scary. The key is understanding the difference between sticker price and net price.

On this page
  • What sticker price includes
  • Why net price is the real number
  • 2026 average costs
  • Finding YOUR net price

What sticker price includes

"Cost of attendance" isn't just tuition. It bundles tuition and fees, room and board, books, transportation, and personal expenses — the full cost of being a student for a year. That's why the number looks so large.

Why net price is the real number

Net price = sticker price minus grants and scholarships (money you don't repay). The average grant aid at private nonprofits exceeds $20,000/year, and many wealthy schools meet 100% of demonstrated need. A $64,500 sticker can become a $25,000 net price — below many public universities.

Rule: never rule out a school based on sticker price. Apply, get the aid offer, then compare net prices.

Finding YOUR net price

  1. Use each college's Net Price Calculator (federally required on every college website).
  2. File the FAFSA — it's the gateway to federal grants, work-study, and most institutional aid.
  3. Compare aid letters, not brochures: separate grants from loans.
  4. Project all four years with inflation in the true cost estimator.

Once you know the real number, plan how to cover it: project your 529 growth and read how 529 plans work.
