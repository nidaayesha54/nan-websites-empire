Companies House is recruiting a Senior Technical Architect (Data) — a senior digital role at the agency that maintains the UK’s register of companies. The post pays £42,923 to £58,044, is advertised as remote working (anywhere in the UK), and closes at 11:55pm on Sunday 1 November 2026 (reference 482871).

Technical architects are a core profession in the government Digital and Data (DDaT) framework: they set the technical direction for services used by millions of people and businesses. At Companies House, that means the data architecture behind company incorporation, filings and public search — some of the highest-traffic digital services in UK government.

The Role: Senior Technical Architect (Data)

As a Senior Technical Architect with a data focus, you will own the technical direction of data platforms and services: how company data is modelled, stored, integrated and exposed to users and downstream systems. Companies House is in the middle of a long-term digital transformation, and data architecture decisions made now will shape the register for a decade.

Expect to work across multidisciplinary teams — product managers, delivery managers, engineers, data scientists — providing design authority on data solutions, setting standards, and assuring that implementations meet government technology and security standards.

  • £42,923–£58,044 — plus the Civil Service pension, one of the most generous defined-benefit schemes available in the UK
  • Fully remote — advertised as remote working anywhere in the UK, a genuine draw for senior technologists outside London
  • DDaT profession — technical architect is a recognised DDaT role with a published capability framework and clear progression
  • Reference 482871 — quote it in any correspondence

Who Should Apply

This suits experienced data or solution architects with a track record of designing data platforms at scale — data modelling, integration patterns, cloud data services, and governance. Public-sector experience is useful but not essential; what matters is evidence of senior-level architectural decision-making and the ability to influence non-technical stakeholders.

Civil Service applications are sifted against published criteria, so map your examples to the advert’s skills and behaviours explicitly — a strong CV alone will not carry you through.
