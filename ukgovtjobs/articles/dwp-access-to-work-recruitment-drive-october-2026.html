DWP Access to Work Recruitment Drive 2026: 480 Staff Hired to Clear Backlog by September 2027

DWP is recruiting around 480 additional staff for Access to Work caseworker and case manager roles, with a September 2027 target to clear the backlog. Here is what jobseekers need to know.

✅ Updated October 19, 2026
Home » UK Government Jobs 2026 » Articles

The Department for Work and Pensions (DWP) is hiring around 480 additional staff for the Access to Work scheme — processing disability employment grants, with a formal target of clearing the application backlog by September 2027.

What DWP Announced

In May 2026, the Secretary of State for Work and Pensions announced that almost 500 extra staff would be recruited to clear the Access to Work backlog. The new intake is being employed as case workers and case managers, given what the department calls 'extensive' training to handle complex cases, and deployed alongside disabled people and their organisations to improve how the scheme works.

Why the Backlog Needed Clearing

Access to Work is the grant scheme that funds practical support — from specialist equipment to support workers — for disabled people and people with health conditions to start or stay in work. But demand has nearly doubled in five years, and processing delays were questioned in Parliament, prompting the department to set a firm September 2027 clearance date. Announcing the recruitment push, the Work and Pensions Secretary said: “Access to Work is a lifeline for disabled people and those with health conditions, helping them to start and stay in work… That’s why I’m taking action to clear the backlog, because we know that the right support can change lives.”

What the Roles Involve

Access to Work case workers and case managers handle the grant pipeline end to end: assessing applications, deciding eligibility and agreeing support packages with individuals and employers. The department says new staff receive extensive training for complex cases — so the roles suit applicants comfortable with detailed evidence and vulnerable customers.

Who Can Apply

  • Nationality and right to work: DWP roles are advertised through Civil Service Jobs and follow standard Civil Service nationality rules — check each advert, as right to work in the UK alone does not automatically qualify you.
  • Skills: case-working, customer service or benefits experience is an advantage, but DWP runs large-scale caseworker intakes that train from scratch.
  • Checks: successful candidates undergo criminal record and security checks before appointment.
  • Working pattern: office-based or hybrid depending on the team; check the advert for the specific location and pattern.

How to Apply

Applications go through the official portal:

1
Search Civil Service Jobs for “Access to Work” or filter by the Department for Work and Pensions.
2
Read the job description, salary, working pattern and nationality requirements on the advert.
3
Prepare behaviour-based examples — DWP caseworker roles are assessed against Civil Service behaviours and strengths.
4
Apply before the stated closing date; set up job alerts — a 480-post drive advertises in waves.

The Bottom Line

A 480-person recruitment drive with a public September 2027 deadline means sustained hiring in DWP’s disability teams. If you want Civil Service casework, watch Civil Service Jobs — adverts close on their own schedules.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many staff is DWP recruiting for Access to Work?

Around 480 additional staff — announced in May 2026 as ‘almost 500’ — employed as case workers and case managers with extensive training for complex cases.

When will the Access to Work backlog be cleared?

DWP has set a target of September 2027 to clear the application backlog, after delays were questioned in Parliament.

What are Access to Work helpline wait times now?

Down to 15 minutes and 1 second, according to ministerial figures reported in October 2026, alongside a net backlog reduction of 2,980 cases.

Where are Access to Work jobs advertised?

On the Civil Service Jobs portal, filtered by the Department for Work and Pensions — a 480-post drive advertises in waves, so set up job alerts.

Can I appeal an Access to Work decision?

Yes — DWP says anyone unhappy with their original decision should exercise their right of reconsideration.
