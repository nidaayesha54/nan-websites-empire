HM Courts & Tribunals Service (HMCTS) is recruiting Trainee Legal Advisers — a structured training route into one of the most respected advisory roles in the justice system. Adverts show salaries of £35,335–£37,847 (with a second advert listed at £40,014–£42,859), and applications close on 21 October 2026.

Legal advisers sit at the heart of the magistrates’ court: they advise benches on law, practice and procedure, guide court staff, and make sure hearings run fairly and efficiently. The trainee route gives aspiring legal professionals a paid, supported pathway into that role — an opportunity that rarely appears outside the formal Civil Service recruitment cycle.

About the Role

Trainee Legal Advisers learn to provide independent legal and procedural advice in court. The training covers criminal and family court procedure, evidence, sentencing and case management, combined with court-observation placements where trainees watch experienced legal advisers in live hearings before advising themselves under supervision.

The role suits candidates with strong legal foundations, sound judgement and the communication skills to advise confidently in a formal setting. Because legal advisers support the judiciary directly, the post carries real responsibility from the start of training — and comes with the Civil Service pension, standard civil service leave and development support towards full legal adviser status.

Candidates should expect security vetting as part of the process — standard for justice-system roles — and should check the advert for whether there are additional requirements such as a period of court observation before training begins.

Who Can Apply

  • Legal background: the route targets people training towards legal advisory work — check the advert for the required level of legal qualification, degree or study.
  • Judgement & communication: you must be able to give clear, impartial advice under pressure — assessments typically test analysis, decision-making and written communication.
  • Nationality & vetting: standard Civil Service nationality requirements apply, plus security vetting for court roles.
  • Location: posts are linked to court centres — check the advert for where the trainee placements are based and whether travel is required.
