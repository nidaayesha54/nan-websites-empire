GTA 6 Safehouses: All 4 Homes and What They Do

November 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Jason and Lucia never buy a house in GTA 6. Across the whole game they live in four homes, and every one of them arrives through the story — a stilt house in the Keys, a roadside motel, a Vice City apartment and a beach house. None of them can be purchased, none of them is permanent, and each one marks a chapter of the story. This guide covers all four safehouses, what each one does, and why the home you start in is the one you lose.

Safehouses matter because they are where the game saves, where you change clothes, where you rest, and where the story breathes between jobs. They are also where the relationship between the two leads grows — quite literally, since fixing up the first house improves how Jason and Lucia feel about each other. Know your homes, and you know where the story is taking you.

1. The Leonida Keys safehouse: home, until it burns

The first home is Jason's green stilt house in Key Lento, out in the Leonida Keys, and GTA 6 begins here. It is not Jason's own property. The house belongs to drug runner Brian Heder, who lets Jason live there rent-free in return for help with his business — collecting payments from his other tenants and handling local shakedowns. No safehouse in GTA 6 can be bought, so every home Jason and Lucia have reaches them through the story in a similar way.

The house is yours for nearly all of Chapter 1, and it is the most lived-in home in the game. Jason can look after it with his own hands: four jobs are open while he waits for Lucia's release — the bedroom wall, the broken stairs, the roof and the railing of the workout balcony — and every repair also improves his relationship with Lucia. His bedroom hides a cash stash under the floorboards, the snake tank in the living room is home to his pet snake Dolores, who can be fed, and a basketball hoop stands at the foot of the staircase — playable basketball, back in the series for the first time since San Andreas. Jason's Vapid Ganado is parked outside, and owners of the Ultimate Edition also find the Dinka Enduro and the Crest Kayak waiting at the house.

It does not survive the chapter. In the mission "Home Sweet Home" the pair are about to skip town, Lucia goes back for some of Jason's things, finds two robbers who have torn the place apart and set it on fire, fights them off and gets out through the front door just before the house explodes, with the cash still inside. The safehouse is gone after that, and Chapter 2 opens at the Starlet Motel.

2. The Starlet Motel: Port Gellhorn

The second home is the Starlet Motel in Port Gellhorn, Kelly County, unlocked at the start of Chapter 2 in the mission "Overnight Charges". It is a gritty roadside motel, the kind of low-profile temporary shelter you take when the heat is on and the last home just burned down. Its cramped, weathered interior emphasises how far the duo still have to go — this is not a home, it is a hiding place, and the game treats it that way.

The motel is also where the relationship work continues. The couple are on the run together, and the motel is where they regroup, plan and recover between jobs. It is the humblest of the four homes, and that is the point: the story starts at the bottom, in a house that is not yours and a motel that is barely a roof, and climbs from there.

3. Bayside: Vice City, at last

The third home is the Bayside safehouse on Catalan Boulevard in Vice City, unlocked in Chapter 3 when Jason and Lucia move to the city in the mission "Welcome to Vice City". This is the move the whole game has been building toward: out of the Keys and the motels, into the neon heart of the map. The apartment is a proper home in a proper city, and it marks the moment the duo stop running and start building.

Bayside is the base for the middle of the game — the heists, the clubs, the garages, the city itself. Save here often, bank your cash, and treat the apartment as the centre of your Vice City life, because the game does.

4. Ocean Beach: the beach house

The fourth home is the Ocean Beach safehouse on Vice Beach, unlocked in Chapter 4 when Jason and Lucia set up in Ocean Beach in the mission "Ocean Beach". It is the endgame home — the beach house, the view, the payoff for everything the story has put the pair through. By this point the big jobs are behind them or ahead of them, and the safehouse is the place the story comes back to between the final missions.

Four homes, four chapters, four stages of the same escape: a borrowed stilt house, a motel, a city apartment, a beach house. None of them is owned, none of them is permanent, and that is the story of GTA 6 in miniature — two people moving through homes that are never quite theirs, toward a life that might finally be.

What about garages and buying property?

Property purchases in GTA 6 are garages only. The documented catalogue lists 15 garages: eight can be bought with in-game money after the Daily Driver mission, four unlock through the story and three are edition or GTA+ bonuses. The 12 base-game garages provide 16 registered vehicle spaces. Safehouses are used to save, change clothes and rest — their outside parking does not add registered garage spaces, so do not park a car you love outside a safehouse and expect the game to keep it safe.

The rule is simple: buy garages for your cars, use safehouses for your saves. Bank your cash before you lose it, keep a manual save from before the risky missions, and remember that the first home burns — so do not leave anything in it you cannot afford to lose.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you buy a house in GTA 6?

No. Property purchases are garages only. The four safehouses unlock through the story and cannot be bought.

How many safehouses are there in GTA 6?

Four: the Leonida Keys stilt house, the Starlet Motel in Port Gellhorn, Bayside in Vice City and Ocean Beach. Each unlocks in its chapter and each is free.

What can you do at a safehouse?

Save your game, change clothes and rest. The Keys house also lets you stash cash under the floorboards, feed the pet snake Dolores, do repair jobs and play basketball.

The bottom line: four homes, four chapters, none of them yours. Start in a borrowed stilt house, lose it in a fire, climb through a motel and a city apartment to a beach house — and buy garages, not houses, for the cars you want to keep.
